# Crown & Colony — Player's Manual

Welcome, Governor. This manual explains how to play **Crown & Colony**: what you are trying
to achieve, how to start a game, the systems you will spend your time in, the keys you press,
and how a game ends. It assumes no prior knowledge of the original *Colonization* — if you
have played it, everything here will feel familiar; if you have not, read on and you will be
running a thriving New-World empire in no time.

> This is the full static, written guide. A concise version of it is also available **in-game**:
> the **Help** button on the title screen and the in-game pause menu opens a Help screen covering the
> goal, the core gameplay loops and a controls reference. A step-by-step interactive tutorial is
> planned separately; the game's **About** screen also links to this document.

---

## 1. The goal of the game

You command one of the European powers sending settlers across the ocean to the New World.
Over the course of the game you will:

- **explore** an unknown continent, tile by tile;
- **found colonies** and grow them into productive towns;
- **work the land** — turn raw goods (furs, tobacco, sugar, cotton, ore, lumber) into finished
  products (coats, cigars, rum, cloth, tools, muskets) and sell them;
- **trade** with Europe and with the native nations for gold;
- **win your people's hearts toward liberty** by producing *liberty bells*, recruiting
  *Founding Fathers*, and raising your colonies' *Sons of Liberty* membership;
- and finally **declare independence** and defeat the King's army to become a free nation.

There is no single "you must do this" path. You can play as a peaceful trader, a ruthless
conqueror of native cities, or a single-minded revolutionary — the score rewards all of it,
and **winning the War of Independence is the headline victory**. (Optional alternative
victory conditions exist; see [Section 12](#12-victory-and-defeat).)

## 2. Starting a game

### The main menu

When you launch the game you arrive at the **title screen** with these choices:

- **New Game** — start a fresh game (opens the setup options described below).
- **Load Game** — open the save-slot dialog and resume a saved game.
- **Settings** — open the options screen (video, audio).
- **Help** — open the in-game guide (the goal, the core loops and a controls reference).
- **About** — show the version and licence.
- **Quit** — close the game.

### The New Game setup

Choosing **New Game** opens a setup panel where you pick how this game will be shaped. Every
option has a sensible default, so you can simply press **Start** for a classic game.

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Scenario** | The ruleset / variant that defines the world. Currently *Colonial America (Classic)*. (Future variants, such as an Australia scenario, will appear here.) |
| **Map** | *Random New World* (a freshly generated continent) or *America (fixed)* (a hand-drawn map of the Americas). |
| **World size** | Small, Standard, Large or Huge. A bigger map means more room to expand — and a stronger King's army to face at the end. |
| **Land mass** | How much of a random map is land (sparse → dense). More land means larger continents and fewer islands. |
| **Landmass** | The shape of the land on a random map: one *Continent*, a few large *islands*, or many small *islands*. |
| **Difficulty** | The five classic levels, from the gentlest to the harshest. Higher difficulty means more interference from the King, costlier recruits and a tougher Royal Expeditionary Force. |
| **Nation** | The European power you play — Dutch, French, English or Spanish, each with its own advantage — or *No nation* for the classic nation-less start. |
| **Victory conditions** | Which of the three win checks are active (see [Section 12](#12-victory-and-defeat)). |
| **Map options** | *Fog of war* on or off (see [Section 11](#11-the-map-fog-of-war-and-turns)). |
| **Colony options** | Whether a custom house will sell goods that are under boycott. |

> *Tip:* the world-size, land-mass and landmass-shape options only apply to a **random** map,
> so they are greyed out if you choose the fixed America map.

Press **Start** and the New World appears with your first ship and colonist, on turn 1.

## 3. Exploring

You begin with a ship carrying your first settlers. **Click a unit to select it**, then click
an adjacent tile to move it (or use the *go-to* and *next-unit* keys — see
[Section 10](#10-controls-and-keybindings)).

- Each unit has a number of **movement points** per turn. Open terrain is cheap to cross;
  forests, hills and mountains cost more; roads and rivers are fast.
- The map starts black. As your units move, they reveal the tiles around them. A colonist sees
  the ring of tiles immediately around it; a colony sees further; a **scout** (a colonist on a
  horse) sees further still.
- Watch for **lost-city rumours** — mysterious sites marking ruins, burial mounds or strange
  rumours. Move a land unit onto one to investigate (see [Section 9](#9-natives-rumours-and-treasure)).
- The land is divided into named **regions**. The first power to explore a region earns score.

To reach the New World from Europe (and to return), a ship sails to the **high seas** — the
deep water at the edges of the map. A voyage in either direction takes a few turns.

## 4. Founding and running colonies

### Founding

Move a colonist onto a suitable land tile and press **B** (Build colony). Your colonist becomes
the colony's first inhabitant. If the tile belongs to a native nation, you will first be asked
whether to **buy** the land for gold, **take it by force** (free, but it angers the natives), or
**abandon** the attempt.

### The colony screen

Click a colony on the map to open its full view. Here you manage everything the colony does:

- **Working the land** — assign colonists to the eight tiles surrounding the colony to gather
  food and raw goods (furs, tobacco, cotton, sugar, ore, lumber, and so on). The yield depends
  on the terrain and any special resource on the tile.
- **Buildings** — put colonists to work *inside* the colony to convert raw goods into finished
  products: a weaver turns cotton into cloth, a distiller turns sugar into rum, a blacksmith
  turns ore into tools, a gunsmith turns tools into muskets, and so on.
- **Construction** — queue up new buildings (warehouses, churches, schools, fortifications,
  workshops), or build units such as wagon trains and artillery. The colony completes work as
  its building materials (hammers, and sometimes tools) accumulate.
- **Food and growth** — colonies eat food each turn. Surplus food is stored; once enough is
  banked, a new colonist is born. A colony that runs out of food will starve.
- **Defence** — building a stockade, then a fort, then a fortress makes the colony progressively
  harder to capture.

### Colonists and experts

A plain *free colonist* can do any job, but an **expert** does its specialty far better (an
expert farmer grows more food, a master distiller makes more rum). Colonists become experts in
three ways: by **working a job long enough** to learn it, by being **taught in a school** (see
below), or by **learning a skill from a native settlement**.

You will also meet weaker colonists — **indentured servants** and **petty criminals** — who
work less effectively until they earn their way up to free colonists and then experts.

### Schools

Build a **schoolhouse** (then a college, then a university) and station an expert in it. The
expert teaches the colony's least-skilled colonist, raising it step by step until it becomes an
expert too. This is how you mass-produce specialists at home instead of buying them in Europe.

## 5. The economy and trading with Europe

### The Europe screen

Press **E** (or use a ship in a colony) to open the **Europe** screen. It shows the recruits
waiting on the docks, your ships in port, your treasury, and your immigration progress. From
here you:

- **sell** the goods your ships have carried back, for gold;
- **buy** goods, ships, artillery and trained specialists;
- **recruit** colonists from the dock and load them onto a ship to sail home.

### Prices and the market

Europe's prices move with supply and demand. **Selling a lot of a good drives its price down**;
goods nobody is flooding the market with stay valuable. Each good has its own price band, so
plan which products to specialise in.

A share of every sale is taken as **tax** by the King — the tax rate climbs over the game
(see [Section 8](#8-the-king-and-the-monarchy)).

> *National advantage:* the Dutch feel the market move only half as far when they trade, so they
> can sell larger quantities without crashing the price.

### Transport, wagons and trade routes

- **Ships** carry goods and colonists across the sea; bigger ships have more cargo holds.
- **Wagon trains** haul goods overland between your colonies.
- **Trade routes** automate all of this: define a route (for example *colony → colony → Europe*),
  assign a ship or wagon, list which goods to carry, and the carrier will haul and trade on its
  own every turn — no manual clicking required.

### The custom house

Once you have the right Founding Father, you can build a **custom house** in a colony. It
**automatically sells your surplus goods to Europe each turn** without a ship ever leaving port.
Set a keep-level for each good and everything above it is exported. (Depending on a game option,
a custom house can even sell goods that are under boycott.)

---

## 6. Immigration

New colonists also arrive from Europe through **immigration**, driven by religious unrest:

- Build **churches and chapels** to produce *crosses*. The more crosses you produce, the faster
  new immigrants make their way to your docks.
- When enough interest has built up, a colonist appears on the **Europe dock**, free for the
  taking — you just need a ship to carry them home.
- You can also **recruit immediately** for gold instead of waiting, choosing from the colonists
  currently on the dock. Each paid recruit costs a little more than the last.

A rare bonus — the **Fountain of Youth** — sends a whole wave of fresh colonists to your docks
at once.

## 7. Sons of Liberty, liberty bells and Founding Fathers

This is the political heart of the game and the road to independence.

### Liberty bells and Sons of Liberty

Your colonies' **town halls** produce **liberty bells**. Bells do two things:

1. They raise each colony's **Sons of Liberty** membership — the share of the colony that
   supports the rebellion. Higher membership gives your colonists a **production bonus**. (A
   colony crowded with loyal royalists, on the other hand, suffers a production penalty until
   the rebels win them over.)
2. They accumulate nationally toward recruiting **Founding Fathers**.

Your **national** Sons of Liberty figure — the share of *all* your colonists who are rebels —
is the gate to independence: you need it to reach **50%** before you may declare.

### Founding Fathers

As liberty accumulates you can recruit **Founding Fathers** into your Continental Congress.
Each round offers one candidate from each of five fields — **trade, exploration, military,
political and religious** — and you choose whom to pursue. Each father grants a lasting benefit,
for example:

- **Thomas Jefferson** — greatly boosts liberty-bell production.
- **Adam Smith** — unlocks the top tier of factories.
- **Peter Stuyvesant** — unlocks the custom house.
- **Benjamin Franklin** — keeps the King from declaring war on you and smooths diplomacy.
- **Pocahontas** — calms every native nation and keeps them calmer thereafter.
- **Hernando de Soto** — extends your units' sight and makes every lost-city rumour a good one.
- **Hernán Cortés** — treasure is shipped home for free, with no royal cut.
- **Simón Bolívar** — raises Sons of Liberty across all your colonies.

## 8. The King and the monarchy

Your mother country is not a silent partner. As the game goes on, the **King** intervenes:

- **Tax demands** — the King periodically demands you accept a tax rise. You may **pay** (your
  tax rate climbs, eating into every future sale), or **refuse** in a "tea party" — you dump the
  offending goods into the sea, that good is **boycotted** (you cannot sell it until you settle
  the back taxes), but your colonists' rebel sentiment surges in response.
- **Mercenaries** — in wartime the King offers you veteran soldiers for hire at a discount. You
  can accept (paying gold) or decline. Decline an offer and the King may stop helping you.
- **Free support** — sometimes the King sends free troops or gold, or makes war or peace on your
  behalf with a rival power.
- **The Royal Expeditionary Force** — quietly, every so often, the King adds ships and soldiers
  to a great army back home. This is the **Royal Expeditionary Force (REF)** — the army that will
  cross the ocean to crush you the moment you declare independence. The longer the game runs and
  the more you provoke the Crown, the larger it grows.

## 9. Natives, rumours and treasure

### The native nations

Several **native nations** live on the map, in settlements ranging from small camps to great
cities. They are not simply enemies:

- **Meet a chief** — move a unit beside a settlement and speak with its chief. The first meeting
  with each nation usually brings a gift of gold and reveals some of the surrounding map.
- **Trade** — sell them goods they want and buy what they produce.
- **Learn a skill** — a colonist visiting a settlement can be taught that settlement's
  speciality, becoming an expert for free.
- **Missions** — station a missionary in a settlement to convert its people into colonists over
  time.
- **Land** — when you found a colony or work a tile a native nation claims, you must buy the land
  or take it by force.

Each settlement has a **mood** toward you, from happy down to hateful. Attacking braves, stealing
land and crowding their territory raises their alarm; peaceful trade and the passage of time calm
them. An angry nation will send **braves** to raid your colonies and attack your units.

### Lost-city rumours

Move a land unit onto a **rumour** tile to investigate. Outcomes range from wonderful to
disastrous: a chief's gift of gold, a learned skill, a free colonist, a Fountain of Youth, or a
**treasure train** — but also nothing at all, an angered burial ground, or the loss of the unit
that went looking. **Scouts** never vanish, and Hernando de Soto guarantees only good outcomes.

### Treasure trains

When you destroy a native settlement or strike it rich in a rumour, you may receive a
**treasure train** — a slow, defenceless unit carrying a fortune in gold. Get it to a colony and
the King will ship it home for you, taking a cut; or carry it home yourself aboard a galleon to
keep every coin (and Hernán Cortés waives the cut entirely). Beware: an enemy can capture an
unescorted treasure train.

## 10. Combat and war

You will sooner or later fight — natives, rival European powers, or eventually the King's army.

- **Attacking** — select an armed unit and click an enemy unit or colony. Combat resolves at
  once; the winner holds the ground and the loser is destroyed or captured.
- **Soldiers and dragoons** — arm a colonist with **muskets** to make a soldier, and add
  **horses** to make a faster, harder-hitting **dragoon**. **Artillery** is powerful but
  vulnerable in the open.
- **Terrain and fortification** matter — defenders gain bonuses from forests and hills, from
  being **fortified** (dug in), and from a colony's stockade, fort or fortress.
- **Ships** fight at sea; a badly damaged ship must limp back to Europe to be repaired before it
  can sail again.

### Rival European powers

Other European powers settle the same New World. You can meet them, trade and negotiate, make
peace or go to war. The King may also drag you into (or out of) a war with a rival on your behalf.

## 11. The map, fog of war and turns

### Fog of war

With **fog of war** on (the classic default), tiles you have explored but cannot currently see
are shown dimmed and "remembered" — you see the land as you last left it, and enemy movements
there are hidden until you look again. Turn fog off at setup to keep every explored tile fully
visible for the rest of the game.

### Turns

The game advances in **turns**. On your turn you move your units, manage your colonies and trade.
When you are finished you **end the turn**, and the world resolves: every other power and native
nation takes its turn, your colonies produce and grow, immigrants arrive, ships at sea advance,
native tempers cool, and the calendar moves on. The status bar always shows the current year (and,
later in the game, the season) and turn number.

## 12. Victory and defeat

### Declaring independence

Once at least **half your colonists are rebels** (national Sons of Liberty ≥ 50%) and you hold a
**port** — and provided it is not yet too late in history (the classic cut-off is the year 1800)
— an **Independence** button appears. Pressing it opens the **Declaration of Independence**
screen, which spells out the consequences and offers **"Liberty or Death!"** or **"Maybe Later."**

Declaring independence is momentous:

- everything you had waiting in **Europe is lost**, and Europe closes its doors — no more
  recruiting or trading with the mother country;
- some of the **veteran soldiers** in your most fervent colonies rise up as **Continental
  regulars**;
- the native nation that most resented the old Crown **comes over to your side**;
- the King makes one parting offer of **mercenaries** you may hire;
- and the **Royal Expeditionary Force sails in** to crush the rebellion.

### The War of Independence

From then on it is war. The King's army lands near your ports and marches on your colonies —
the coastal ports first — while his warships hunt your ships. If you can hold out, a sympathetic
**foreign power eventually lands an allied force** to fight alongside you, and the longer the war
drags on the larger that help becomes.

- **You win** by defeating or driving off the King's army and securing your independence.
- **You lose** the war if the King takes your last port.

### Other victory conditions

Depending on the options chosen at setup, you can also win by becoming the **last European power
standing** (every rival's colonies and armies wiped out). A "last human standing" condition exists
for multiplayer and is off by default in the classic single-player game.

### Score

Throughout the game a **score** is tallied from your units, your banked liberty, your Founding
Fathers, your gold, and the regions you were first to explore. Winning the War of Independence
multiplies your final score — the sooner you win, the bigger the bonus. The end-of-game screen
breaks the score down, and a high-score table records your best games.

## 13. Saving, loading and settings

- **Quick save / quick load** — press **F5** to quick-save and **F9** to load your quick-save at
  any time.
- **Save / Load slots** — from the **Pause menu** (or the main menu's *Load Game*) you can use
  five named save slots; each filled slot shows the turn it was saved on.
- **Pause menu** — press the pause key (**Esc**) during play to pause and bring up Resume, Save,
  Load, Settings and Quit options.
- **Settings** — adjust window mode and VSync, and set the master / music / sound-effects volumes.
  Your settings are remembered between sessions.

## 14. Controls and keybindings

Crown & Colony is played mostly with the **mouse** — click a unit to select it, click a tile to
move it or give it an order, and click a colony to open its management screen. The keyboard
provides shortcuts for the most common actions.

The following keys are active in the game view (you never have to memorise them — press **F1**
in-game to pop up an on-screen legend at any time):

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| **Left-click** | Select a unit / move it / open a colony / pick a tile (e.g. a *go-to* destination) |
| **Right-click** | Open the tile menu (pick a stacked unit, centre here, go to here) |
| **Right / middle drag**, **mouse wheel**, **arrow keys** | Pan the view / zoom / slide the camera |
| **Enter** | End the turn |
| **Space** | Skip the selected unit for the rest of this turn |
| **W** | Wait / select the next unit that still needs orders this turn |
| **G** | Go to — then click a destination tile for the selected unit to travel to over future turns |
| **B** | Build (found) a colony with the selected unit |
| **D** | Disband the selected unit (asks you to confirm first) |
| **E** | Open the Europe screen |
| **L** | Open the *Find settlement* panel (locate one of your colonies) |
| **F** | Open the Founding Fathers panel |
| **C** | Open the Colopedia (the in-game reference of units, goods and buildings) |
| **Ctrl+C** | Centre the camera on the selected unit |
| **N** | New game (new map) |
| **Ctrl+S** / **Ctrl+O** | Save / Load (named save slots) |
| **F5** / **F9** | Quick-save / Quick-load |
| **F1** | Toggle the on-screen key legend |
| **Esc** | Open the pause menu |
