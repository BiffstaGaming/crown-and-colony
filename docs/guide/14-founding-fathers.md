# Founding Fathers & the Continental Congress

Behind every great colonial power stands a **Continental Congress** — a gathering of famous statesmen, explorers, soldiers, priests and merchants who lend their genius to your cause. Each one you recruit is a **Founding Father**, and each grants a permanent, nation-wide benefit that can quietly reshape how your whole empire works. Choosing whom to court, and in what order, is one of the most important strategic threads in the game.

## Where Founding Fathers come from

Founding Fathers are earned with **liberty**, and liberty comes from **liberty bells**. Your colonies' **town halls** ring bells every turn, and those bells serve two purposes at once (see [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md)):

- Locally, they raise each colony's **Sons of Liberty** membership and its production bonus.
- Nationally, the same bells accumulate as **liberty** toward your next Founding Father.

You do not spend goods or gold on a father — you spend accumulated bells. A brand-new colony's town hall rings roughly one bell a turn on its own, but that is slow going. Staff the town hall with colonists working as **statesmen** and it rings several bells a turn, and your Congress fills far faster. Fathers are the single best reason to invest early in bell production.

!!! tip
    Liberty bells are the currency of Congress. If you want Founding Fathers quickly, put people in the town hall — ideally trained statesmen — long before you feel you need them. A **printing press** and later a **newspaper** multiply a colony's bell output, compounding everything.

## Recruiting a father

Every round, Congress offers you **one candidate from each of five fields**:

| Field | Nature of the fathers |
|---|---|
| Trade | Commerce, markets and manufacturing |
| Exploration | Sight, movement and the map |
| Military | Combat, defence and warships |
| Political | Liberty, diplomacy and the natives |
| Religious | Immigration, missions and converts |

You pick **which candidate to pursue**. From then on, your accumulated liberty banks toward that father's cost. The turn your banked liberty reaches the cost, he joins your Congress, the cost is subtracted, and a fresh slate of candidates is offered. You then choose again.

**Each successive father costs more than the last.** The first is comparatively cheap; every father you already hold pushes the price of the next one higher. Early fathers arrive quickly, but a large Congress makes each new addition a serious investment of bells — so the order you recruit in genuinely matters.

!!! warning
    Once you **declare independence**, the Continental Congress closes its doors — you recruit **no new fathers** for the rest of the game. The fathers you already hold keep working for you throughout the [War of Independence](19-war-of-independence.md), but you cannot add to them. Recruit everyone you want *before* you declare. See [The Road to Independence](18-road-to-independence.md).

## Notable Founding Fathers

There are many fathers across the five fields; the in-game **Colopedia** lists every one with its portrait, field and exact effect. Here are some of the most consequential:

- **Thomas Jefferson** (political) — greatly boosts your **liberty-bell production**. Because bells feed both Sons of Liberty and Congress itself, Jefferson accelerates *every* future father. He is often the first father worth chasing.
- **Adam Smith** (trade) — unlocks the top tier of **factories** (iron works, textile mill, cigar factory, rum factory, fur factory) and the arsenal. Combined with a high Sons of Liberty membership, factories dramatically out-produce ordinary workshops. See [Buildings](11-buildings.md).
- **Peter Stuyvesant** (trade) — unlocks the **custom house**, which automatically sells your surplus goods to Europe every turn without you sailing a ship. It transforms the logistics of a large empire.
- **Benjamin Franklin** (political) — a master diplomat. While Franklin sits in your Congress, the **King can no longer declare war on your behalf**, a rival at war with you always accepts your offer of **peace**, and a power already at peace with you is **less likely to break it**. See [Rival European Powers](16-rival-powers.md).
- **Pocahontas** (political) — instantly **calms every native nation** to friendly, and permanently **slows how quickly they take offence** at your expanding colonies thereafter. Invaluable if you are hemmed in by native settlements. See [Natives & Diplomacy](15-natives-diplomacy.md).
- **Hernando de Soto** (exploration) — extends your **land units' line of sight**, and makes **every lost-city rumour a good one** — never a disaster. See [Exploration & Discovery](06-exploration.md).
- **Hernán Cortés** (military) — when you sack a native settlement you gain a **treasure train**, and Cortés ships that treasure home **for free, with no royal cut**. A conquest-minded player keeps every coin. See [Combat: Land & Naval](17-combat.md).
- **Simón Bolívar** (political) — raises **Sons of Liberty membership across all your colonies** at once, lifting production empire-wide and bringing you closer to the 50% you need to declare independence.

Other fathers you will meet include explorers who speed your ships, military men who grant free warships or arm your privateers, religious fathers who supercharge your missions, and trade fathers who open dealings with rival nations or wipe away boycotts. The Colopedia is the definitive, up-to-date list.

## Sequencing to your strategy

Because you only bank toward one father at a time, and each costs more than the last, **the order you recruit in is a strategic choice**. Match it to how you intend to win:

- **A bell-and-liberty rush toward independence** — take **Jefferson** early so every later father (and your Sons of Liberty) grows faster, then **Bolívar** to push national membership toward the 50% independence gate.
- **An economic powerhouse** — prioritise the trade fathers: **Adam Smith** for factories and **Peter Stuyvesant** for the custom house turn raw materials into a self-running money machine.
- **A peaceful, native-heavy map** — **Pocahontas** buys you room to expand without constant raids; **de Soto** makes exploration safe and rewarding.
- **A conquest game** — **Cortés** and the military fathers turn plunder and privateering into a war chest.
- **Diplomatic insulation** — **Franklin** keeps the King and rival powers off your back while you build toward the Declaration.

There is no single correct order — but there is an order that fits *your* plan. Decide how you intend to win, then court the fathers who get you there fastest. For the bigger picture of turning liberty into a nation, see [The Road to Independence](18-road-to-independence.md).
