# Combat: Land & Naval

Sooner or later you will fight — a native war party raiding your frontier, a rival European power's soldiers marching on your ports, or, at the end, the King's own army. This chapter explains how a battle is decided, how to arm your colonists for war, and how ships duel on the open sea. Combat in Crown & Colony is fast and decisive: you pick a target, you see the odds, you commit, and the dice are rolled at once.

## Arming your units

A bare colonist cannot fight — it has no attacking strength at all. War begins in your warehouse, not on the battlefield. Standing inside one of your colonies, a colonist trades goods from the stores for a military role:

- **Muskets** turn a colonist into a **soldier** — better at both attack and defence.
- **Muskets and horses together** make a **dragoon** — faster and harder-hitting than a soldier.
- **Horses alone** make a **scout**, which is fast and far-seeing but only lightly armed.

You equip colonists through the colony screen (see [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md)); the goods come out of that colony's warehouse, so keep muskets and horses stocked in any town near trouble. A weak colonist can still be armed — an indentured servant or petty criminal fights as a soldier just fine, and can earn its way up the ranks by winning battles (see below).

**Veterans** are the finest musketmen, trained soldiers who fight better than an armed free colonist. **Artillery** is the heaviest land weapon of all — a devastating siege engine against a colony, but with a fatal weakness in open country, covered below.

!!! tip
    Build up muskets and horses *before* you need them. Arming a colonist is instant, so a colony with a full arsenal can raise a defender the moment an enemy appears — but only if the goods are already on the shelf.

## How a battle is decided

Every fight pits the attacker's **offence** against the defender's **defence**. Each side's strength is its unit plus its equipment plus any bonuses from terrain, fortification or walls. The rule for who wins is simple:

> Your chance to win is your share of the total strength. A strength-3 attacker against a strength-1 defender wins three times out of four.

Before any dice are rolled you get a **pre-combat odds preview**. With a unit selected, click an adjacent enemy and a dialog appears showing **your strength**, **the enemy's strength**, and **your chance to win**. Press **Attack** to commit, or **Cancel** to back out — cancelling costs you nothing: no turn spent, no war declared. Attacking always uses up the attacker's turn.

To attack, click an adjacent enemy just as you would click an empty tile to move onto it — the same click attacks whatever holds the tile.

### Bonuses and penalties

Many things tilt the odds. The main ones:

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Rough terrain (forest, hills, marsh) | Boosts the **defender** — hills give the biggest bonus, forest a solid one, plains none |
| Fortified (dug in) | +50% defence, and immunity to ambush |
| Sheltering in a settlement | Terrain no longer matters; walls apply instead |
| Stockade / fort / fortress | +100% / +150% / +200% defence to whoever holds the colony |
| Artillery in the open | Strength cut to a quarter (attacking **or** defending outside a colony) |
| Ambush from forest or hills | Attacker ignores the defender's terrain cover |

**Ambush** deserves a word. Forest and hills are concealing ground, and a native war party striking from (or onto) such terrain fights as if the defender stood in the open — so never trust a forest to protect a colonist against a raid. The one escape: a unit that has **fortified** cannot be ambushed and keeps its full defence.

!!! warning
    **Artillery is a siege weapon, not a field unit.** Battering a colony it fights at full power — it is the colony-buster, and dug in behind walls it doubles its defence against a native raid. But caught in the open field, both attacking and defending, its strength is slashed to a quarter. Keep your guns inside your colonies or under escort; never let them get jumped on open ground. A cannon beaten in the open is downgraded to *damaged artillery* rather than destroyed.

## What happens to the loser

Results are **graded** — a clear win is worse for the loser than a narrow one — which decides how badly things go. In order:

- A unit **doomed by any loss** (a native brave, a scout) is **killed**.
- An **armed** loser is **disarmed** one step (a dragoon drops to a soldier, a soldier to an unarmed colonist) — and the **winner can capture that equipment**. A brave that beats your soldier walks away carrying his muskets. A disarmed unit usually survives.
- An **unarmed but capturable** colonist is **taken prisoner** and switches sides.
- **Artillery demotes** — a beaten cannon becomes damaged artillery, and damaged artillery that loses again is destroyed.
- Otherwise the loser is **killed**.

**The winner** may be **promoted** one rung up the soldier ladder: petty criminal → indentured servant → free colonist → veteran soldier → colonial regular. So a hard-luck conscript can fight his way to a respectable soldier. Normally only a decisive victory promotes, and only by chance — but with **George Washington** in your Continental Congress (see [Founding Fathers](14-founding-fathers.md)), *every* win promotes.

## Colony defence and Paul Revere

Walls make a colony far harder to take. A **stockade**, then a **fort**, then a **fortress** each add a large defensive bonus that protects whoever is defending — a garrisoned soldier, or the colonists themselves if no soldier stands there. A walled colony can shrug off an assault that would overrun an open one.

Even an undefended colony has one last card. If natives attack a colony whose last colonist is unarmed and you have **Paul Revere** in your Congress, that colonist snatches muskets from the warehouse to fight. If the colonist wins, the muskets go back on the rack; if beaten, those muskets are used up. It is an automatic last-ditch defence — but no substitute for a real garrison.

!!! warning
    A colony with **no unit standing on its tile** can be walked into and captured by a rival you are at war with. Keep a soldier garrisoned in any colony near an angry neighbour — a garrison forces the enemy to beat it in the field first.

## Naval combat

Armed warships — frigates, privateers and men-o-war — can attack an adjacent enemy ship. Sea battles play a little differently from land:

- There is **no terrain** to hide behind on open water.
- A ship riding low with a **full hold fights worse** — every cargo load is a handicap, so warships fight best empty.
- Only warships can initiate; transports such as caravels and galleons have no attacking strength.

A beaten ship suffers one of three fates. A weaker attacker may simply be **evaded** — the target slips away unharmed. Otherwise the loser is **damaged**: it loses everything aboard, cargo and passengers alike, and limps off to repair. Only a heavy defeat **sinks** a ship outright.

A damaged ship repairs at the nearest of your colonies with a **drydock** (or shipyard), sitting in the water beside it for a few turns before it is fit to sail; with no such colony it limps all the way back to **Europe**. While under repair it cannot move, trade or take on cargo. Foreign powers you are at war with will hunt your ships, and you are told when one is sunk.

**Privateers** are the pirates of the age. A privateer can raid rival shipping **without declaring war** — no stance changes, and it flies no flag, so a raided victim never learns whose ship struck. (A privateer preying on your commerce, however, gives the King an excuse to send you a free warship.)

**Shore batteries.** A coastal colony with a **fort or fortress** and **artillery parked on it** automatically fires on enemy ships that stray onto an adjacent sea tile, at the start of your turn, with no risk to itself — the ship cannot fire back. More guns hit harder; the ship is damaged or sunk. Batteries fire on ships you are at war with, and on **privateers** even at peace. An inland colony, or a fort with no artillery, never bombards.

The end-game siege by the King's army follows these same rules, with a few special twists — see [The War of Independence](19-war-of-independence.md). For a full table of every unit's exact attack and defence values, consult the in-game **Colopedia** (press `C`).
