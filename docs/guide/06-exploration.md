# Exploration & Discovery

You do not arrive in the New World with a map. Beyond the tiles right around your ship the whole continent is black, and it stays black until one of your units walks or sails close enough to peel back the fog. Exploration is how you find the good land, the native nations, the rival powers — and the scattered fortunes that can bankroll an empire.

## Why explore

Every step into the unknown pays off in more than one way:

- **You find land to settle.** You cannot plant a colony on ground you have never seen. The sooner you scout the coast and the interior, the sooner you can pick the richest tiles before a rival grabs them. (See [Founding a Colony](07-founding-colony.md).)
- **You earn score.** The land is divided into named **regions**, and the first power to explore a region is rewarded. Being first matters.
- **You meet the neighbours.** Exploring reveals native settlements and rival European colonies — the start of trade, diplomacy, and sometimes war.
- **You stumble on treasure.** Ancient ruins, cities of gold, and helpful strangers are all out there waiting — if you go looking.

How far a unit sees depends on what it is. A plain colonist reveals only the ring of tiles immediately around it; a colony sees further; and a **scout** — a colonist mounted on a horse — sees furthest of all. A scout is your dedicated explorer: fast, far-sighted, and (as you will see below) far safer around ruins.

!!! tip
    Use the **go-to** order (`G`, then click a distant tile) to send a scout journeying across many turns without babysitting it each turn. It will pick its own path and keep moving while you tend to your colonies.

## Lost-city rumours

Scattered across the wilderness are **lost-city rumours** — mysterious sites marking ruins, burial mounds, and old legends. You will not see them through the fog until you get close. **Move a land unit onto a rumour tile to investigate it** — and that is the moment the dice are rolled. Only your own colonists explore; a native brave walking over a rumour leaves it untouched, and a ship can never reach one (they sit on land). Whatever happens, a rumour is a one-shot: investigating it clears it from the map for good.

The outcomes range from wonderful to disastrous:

- **A tribal chief's gift** — a handful of gold lands in your treasury.
- **A learned skill** — the exploring colonist emerges a trained **seasoned scout**.
- **A free colonist** — a new settler joins you, standing on the tile.
- **A Fountain of Youth** — a rush of immigrants crowds onto your Europe dock (see below). This is rare.
- **Ancient ruins** — a modest gold find, or, on a richer strike, a **treasure train** to escort home.
- **A city of gold (Cibola)** — the dream: a huge treasure train worth thousands. These are the rarest find, and there are only **seven** such cities in the whole game — the legendary Seven Cities of Gold. Once all seven are discovered, later "city of gold" rumours turn out to be nothing more than ordinary ruins.
- **Nothing at all** — just an empty ruin.
- **The expedition vanishes** — the exploring unit is lost for good. This is the rare bad outcome.

Whatever you find, a one-line message in the status bar tells you the result — the outcome is never silent.

!!! warning
    Investigating ruins with an ordinary colonist risks losing that colonist forever. Two things protect you:

    - **A seasoned scout never vanishes** — so use a scout to explore ruins and you can never lose the unit.
    - **Hernando de Soto**, once in your Continental Congress, makes **every** rumour a good one and extends your units' sight (see [Founding Fathers & the Continental Congress](14-founding-fathers.md)). A scout exploring under de Soto digs almost fearlessly.

### Strange mounds

On land a native nation owns, a rumour may turn out to be **strange mounds**. Instead of resolving at once, the game asks you whether to **investigate** them or **leave them be**. Walk away and you lose nothing — but gain nothing. Dig in and you take your chances: the mounds may prove a chief's gift, ancient ruins, an empty dig, a vanished expedition — or a **desecrated burial ground**. Defile the dead and the owning nation turns **hateful**: every one of their settlements leaps to maximum alarm, and you gain no loot for it — only a furious neighbour. Weigh the risk before you dig on sacred ground.

## Meeting the native nations

The New World is already inhabited. As you explore, the fog peels back to reveal native settlements — camps, villages, and great cities. Move a unit up beside one and you can **speak with its chief**.

The **first meeting** with each nation is the valuable one: the chief usually hands over a **gift of gold** and tells **tales of nearby lands**, revealing a swathe of the map around the settlement — a free burst of exploration. First contact is counted separately for each European power, so a rival meeting a chief before you does not use up *your* first audience.

A scout is especially good at this. Beyond meeting chiefs and learning skills, a scout can visit settlements to gather their tales and gifts as it ranges across the map, turning goodwill into revealed terrain and coin. There is much more to native relations — trade, missions, learning skills, land, and war — covered in full in [Natives & Diplomacy](15-natives-diplomacy.md).

## The Fountain of Youth

The rarest and most cheering rumour outcome is a **Fountain of Youth**. Where a normal immigrant arrives one at a time, the Fountain sends a whole **wave of fresh colonists** to your Europe dock at once, ready to be shipped across the ocean. They are a windfall of manpower — you still need ships to carry them home, but the recruiting is done for free. It is worth keeping a scout probing ruins for exactly this kind of surprise.

## Treasure trains and cashing in

When you strike it rich in a rumour — or when you sack a native settlement — you may receive a **treasure train**: a slow, defenceless wagon piled with gold. The gold is not banked yet. You must escort the train back to safety and cash it in, and because the train cannot fight and **can be captured**, an enemy who beats it takes the whole haul. A rich find is a prize you have to *get home*.

There are two ways to bank the gold:

| Route | What happens | The cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Let the King ship it** | Bring the train to one of your **coastal** colonies and the King carries the gold to Europe for you. | The King takes a cut equal to your **current tax rate** — the higher your taxes have climbed, the more he skims. |
| **Carry it home yourself** | Load the train onto a **galleon** (it fills the whole hold), sail to Europe, and cash it in there. | Nothing — you keep **every coin**, no cut and no tax. |

So the trade-off is convenience versus cost: the King's route is instant and safe but costs you your tax rate; sailing it home yourself keeps the full fortune but ties up a galleon for a round trip and risks the treasure to pirates on the way. An **inland** colony has no sea route home, so the King will not ship from it — move the treasure to a coastal town or carry it yourself.

!!! tip
    If **Hernán Cortés** sits in your Continental Congress, the King ships your treasure home for **nothing** — full value even from a colony, whatever your tax rate. With Cortés, the King's route becomes strictly the better deal.

To cash a train in on the map, select it on a colony tile and use the **Cash in treasure** order; the game shows you exactly how much you will bank and asks you to confirm. Once you have carried a train home to Europe, you cash it in from the Europe screen instead. For everything else about buying, selling, and the market, see [Trade & the Europe Screen](12-trade-europe.md); for how taxes climb over the game, see [The King, Taxes & Your Monarch](13-king-taxes.md).
