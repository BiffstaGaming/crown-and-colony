# Colonists, Professions & Education

Every colony runs on the backs of its colonists. Who works a tile or a workshop matters enormously: the same forest tile hands one colonist a trickle of furs and another a steady stream, and the difference is *expertise*. This chapter explains the kinds of colonist you will command, how experts multiply your output, the three roads to expertise, and how to run your schools and your labour pool.

## The kinds of colonist

Not all colonists are equal. When people arrive from Europe, are born in your colonies, or fall into your hands another way, they come in several grades:

- **Free colonists** are your everyday workers. A free colonist can do *any* job — farm, mine, weave, preach, soldier — competently. They are the backbone of a young colony and the raw material every specialist is made from.
- **Indentured servants** work, but less effectively than a free colonist. They are cheaper labour that has not yet earned its way up.
- **Petty criminals** are the least productive of all. They still contribute, but a criminal on a job pulls in noticeably less than a servant or free colonist.

Servants and criminals are not a dead end. Both sit at the bottom of an **education ladder** and can climb it:

> petty criminal → indentured servant → free colonist → expert

Every rung up makes them more useful, and the top rung turns them into a true specialist.

## Experts and specialists

An **expert** (also called a specialist) is a colonist who has mastered one profession and performs it far better than a plain free colonist — but only that profession. Put a Master Distiller on the sugar-to-rum job and rum pours out; put that same Master Distiller on a fur tile and they are no better than anyone else. Experts fall into two broad groups:

- **Gatherers** work the land around a colony — an Expert Farmer grows more food, an Expert Ore Miner digs more ore, and their kin each produce more of their raw good on the tiles they suit.
- **Crafters** work inside colony buildings, turning raw goods into finished products: a Master Distiller turns sugar into rum, and other masters weave cloth, forge tools or make muskets.

Beyond the economy there are also specialists you will lean on for politics, faith, war and exploration — an **Elder Statesman**, for example, produces far more liberty bells when placed in the town hall, and a **missionary** can live in a native settlement, converting its people into new colonists over time. A **veteran soldier** likewise fights harder than an armed free colonist. For the full roster of experts, and the exact production figures of each on each job, consult the in-game **Colopedia** (press `C`).

Because an expert only shines at their own trade, the art of running colonies is matching the right specialist to the right slot — a subject taken up in [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md) and [Goods & Production Chains](10-goods-production.md).

## Three ways to gain expertise

You do not have to buy every specialist in Europe. A colonist earns expertise in three ways:

1. **By working the job.** Leave a colonist on the same task long enough and they eventually learn it, upgrading to the matching expert on their own. This is slow but free — the land trains your people while they work.
2. **By being taught in a school.** Station an expert in a schoolhouse and they teach your other colonists at home (see below). This is how you mass-produce specialists without a sea voyage.
3. **By learning from the natives.** A colonist who visits a native settlement can be taught that settlement's speciality and walks away an expert for free. See [Natives & Diplomacy](15-natives-diplomacy.md).

## Schools: teaching at home

Build a **schoolhouse** in a colony (later a college, then a university), and station an expert inside it. That expert becomes a **teacher**, lifting your least-skilled colonists up the ladder one rung at a time until they become experts too.

A few rules govern who teaches, who learns, and how long it takes:

- **The teacher always trains the least-skilled colonist first.** A petty criminal is taught before a servant, a servant before a plain free colonist — anywhere in the colony. You do not choose who learns; the game picks automatically, exactly as in the classic game.
- **You do choose who teaches.** Open the colony and each school shows a **Teach: <expert>** button for every idle expert qualified to teach there. Click it to seat that expert. A plain free colonist has nothing to teach and is not offered; an expert too advanced for the building is likewise refused.
- **Each rung takes a few turns**, and learning the final expertise takes longer for harder trades. A colony under **good government** — high Sons of Liberty — learns faster, its production bonus shaving turns off each step.

The three school tiers grow in both what they can teach and how many teachers they seat at once:

| School | Teachers at once | Teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Schoolhouse | 1 | The simpler experts |
| College | 2 | Up to the more skilled masters |
| University | 3 | The most learned, including the Elder Statesman |

**Each teacher trains its own student in parallel.** A college with two teachers graduates two colonists over the same span, not one after the other — so a busy university can churn out specialists three at a time.

!!! tip
    A **colonial regular** placed in a school teaches the **veteran soldier** skill rather than its own — a handy way to raise home-grown veterans.

!!! warning
    An expert who is too advanced for the school it sits in simply cannot teach there — an Elder Statesman in a mere schoolhouse trains no one. Match each teacher to a school that can hold them, and build up to a college or university before expecting to raise your most valuable specialists at home.

## Managing your labour pool

Colonists are your scarcest resource; keeping them in the right jobs is a constant balancing act.

- **Fill the high-value slots with experts** and leave routine gathering to free colonists, servants and criminals.
- **Rotate the weak up the ladder.** A schoolhouse turning criminals and servants into free colonists steadily upgrades your whole workforce; put your least useful colonists where a teacher will reach them.
- **Clear a speciality when you need flexibility.** Sometimes a specialist is worth more as a general worker. The colony screen offers a **Clear speciality** button for any expert standing on or beside the colony, reverting them to a plain free colonist who can do anything (or be re-trained into something you actually need). Only a genuine expert can be cleared — a free colonist, servant or criminal has no speciality to shed.

Growing the pool itself — through food-driven births, immigration and recruiting — is covered in [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md) and [Trade & the Europe Screen](12-trade-europe.md). The skill here is not just getting bodies, but making sure each one is doing the job it does best.
