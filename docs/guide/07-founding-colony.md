# Founding a Colony

Your first colony is the moment your expedition stops wandering and starts building an empire. Everything that follows — production, trade, liberty bells, and eventually the war for independence — flows from the towns you plant on the map. This chapter shows you how to found a colony, how to read a piece of land before you commit to it, and where a beginner should sink that all-important first settlement.

## How to found a colony

Founding is deliberately simple:

1. Move a colonist onto a suitable land tile (see [Units & Movement](05-units-movement.md) for how to move).
2. With that colonist selected, press `B` (Build colony).

Your colonist settles down and becomes the town's first inhabitant — it leaves the map and reappears as a small house bearing the colony's name. Names are drawn from a classic list (Jamestown, Plymouth, and so on) in the order you found them. Click the colony to open its management screen; see [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md) for what to do next.

Not every tile will accept a colony. The game refuses to build on:

- **water or impassable terrain** (you cannot found in the sea, or on mountains);
- **a tile that already has a colony**;
- **a tile immediately next to another colony** (see spacing, below).

If the `B` action does nothing, one of these rules is stopping you — move to a different tile and try again.

### When the land belongs to a native nation

Much of the New World is claimed by native nations. If the tile you choose sits on land a native nation holds, the game pauses and asks how you want the land:

- **Buy** it for gold — the peaceful, friendly route.
- **Take** it by force — free, but it angers the nation whose land you seized.
- **Abandon** the attempt — back out and choose elsewhere.

!!! warning "Taking land has a price"
    Seizing native land costs nothing in gold but raises that nation's alarm toward you. An angry nation sends braves to raid your colonies and attack your units. Early on, when you have no soldiers and no stockade, buying the land (or founding somewhere unclaimed) is almost always the wiser move. See [Natives & Diplomacy](15-natives-diplomacy.md).

## Reading a site before you build

A colony works the ring of **eight tiles surrounding it**, plus the **centre tile** it sits on. That block of nine tiles is your town's whole workable world, so choosing where to plant it is the single most important decision you make about a colony.

When you hover over a candidate tile, look at what that centre tile and its eight neighbours can offer:

- **Food.** Colonists eat every turn, and surplus food is what grows your population and breeds horses. A site needs a reliable food supply — plains and grassland grow grain, and coastal or lakeside tiles can be fished once you build the Docks. Aim for a spread of tiles that comfortably feeds a growing town.
- **A cash crop or resource.** Beyond food, you want the ring to hold at least one valuable raw good you can turn into money: furs from forest, tobacco or cotton from open land, sugar from swampy ground, ore from hills. A tile carrying a **special resource deposit** (a mineral vein, prime fur, prime sugar) yields far more of its good — those tiles are worth building next to.
- **Coastal or inland.** A **coastal** site (touching the sea) lets ships dock, load goods, and carry recruits straight to your town — and, once you build a Docks, lets you fish the surrounding water. A ship can never sit on the colony's land tile, so a coastal colony is what connects you to Europe. **Inland** sites can be safer and richer in land goods, but everything must be hauled overland by wagon train to reach a port. Your first colony should almost always be coastal.
- **Rivers and high ground.** Rivers speed movement; hills and other high ground give defenders a bonus in battle, which matters once rivals or angry natives come calling.

!!! tip "Check the Colopedia for exact yields"
    Every terrain type, resource, and good has precise numbers behind it. Rather than memorise them, press `C` in the game to open the Colopedia — the in-game reference — and look up what any tile or good actually produces.

## Spacing your colonies

The game enforces a **minimum distance between colonies**: you cannot found on a tile next to an existing colony, so no two colonies' centre tiles ever touch. That rule exists for a reason worth understanding even where the game does not force your hand.

Because each colony works the eight tiles around it, two colonies planted close together will **fight over the same tiles** — only one colony can work any given tile at a time. Overlapping rings waste good land: tiles that could be feeding or enriching two full towns end up serving one. When you scout out your second and later sites, leave a comfortable gap so each colony has its own untouched ring of eight to work.

Spread out, and a handful of well-placed towns will out-produce a cramped cluster every time.

## Your first colony: a beginner's checklist

For your very first settlement, keep it simple and safe:

1. **Found on the coast.** You need a port to trade with Europe from turn one. A coastal centre tile lets your ship dock, unload, and sail home for more colonists.
2. **Make sure the centre feeds itself.** Every settleable tile grows at least enough food for one colonist, so a lone starting colony never starves — but you want room to grow, so favour a ring with plenty of grain and, later, fishable water.
3. **Look for one good money good nearby.** A forest for furs, or open land for tobacco or cotton, gives you something to sell in Europe straight away. A special resource in the ring is a bonus worth chasing.
4. **Prefer friendly or unclaimed land.** If a native nation owns the tile, buy it rather than take it — you cannot afford enemies before you can defend yourself.
5. **Do not agonise.** A decent coastal spot now beats a perfect spot ten turns of exploring away. You can always found more colonies later, and each one teaches you to read the land better.

Once your town is on the map, open it up and put your colonist to work. Head to [Managing a Colony](08-managing-colony.md) to turn that first house into a thriving town.
