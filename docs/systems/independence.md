## 1. How it works (plain English)

Once your colonies are fired up enough — at least **half your colonists are rebels** (Sons of Liberty ≥ 50% nationwide) and you hold a **port** — you can **declare independence**, as long as it isn't already too late in history. There's a **last year** after which the mother country will no longer let a colony break away — in the classic game that's **1800**. That cutoff year isn't a number baked into the code: it's a **ruleset setting** read from the game's data, so a different scenario (or the Australia variant) can move it without touching any code. When you do declare:

- your nation becomes a **rebel** fighting for freedom;
- everything you had **waiting in Europe is lost**, and Europe closes its doors (no more recruiting or trade with the mother country);
- the **loyalists desert you**: in each colony, some of the colonists who never came round to the rebellion (the "Tories") pack up and leave rather than fight their King — and the **less behind-you a colony was, the more of them go**. A colony where everyone's a fervent rebel loses no-one; a half-hearted one loses a chunk of its people. (This is how the original 1994 game worked; FreeCol dropped it, so we've put it back.)
- the earlier you break away, the **bigger the bonus to your final score** — declaring well before the historical date (around 1780) is worth more points than waiting until the last moment;
- in each fired-up colony (over half rebels), some of the **veteran soldiers stationed there rise up as Continental regulars** — the bigger and more fervent the colony, the more of its own veterans muster;
- the **natives who most resented the old Crown side with you** — the native nation you'd angered the most (among those you've met) cools off and throws in with your rebellion;
- the **King makes one parting offer**: a force of professional **Hessian mercenaries** you can hire for gold to help face his army (you accept or decline — nothing is forced on you, and you're never charged unless you say yes);
- and the **King sends his army** — the Royal Expeditionary Force he's been building all game — to crush you. From now on it's war.

From the next turn the **King's army sails in and lands near your ports**, then fights with a clear, single-minded plan rather than wandering about. Its **soldiers go straight for your colonies** — your **coastal ports first** (to cut you off from any foreign ally), and the weakest-defended of those before the well-fortified ones. They deliberately **ignore your loose troops in the field until they've actually captured one of your colonies** — the King wants your towns, not a scattered chase — and only once a colony has fallen do his regulars also start hunting down your stray units. His **men-o-war hunt your ships** and, when there's no rebel vessel nearby, sail in to support the landings. Winning means defeating or driving off that army; losing means being driven from your last port.

**There's more than one way to win.** The game's rules can switch on alternative victory conditions: by default you win the moment you **defeat the King's army and secure independence**, *and* you also win if you become **the last European power left standing** (everyone else's colonies and armies wiped out). A third optional condition — being the last **human** player — exists for multiplayer and is **off** in the classic single-player game. Which of these count is a rules setting read from the game's data, so a variant can turn them on or off without touching code.

**A rival can rebel too.** It isn't only you who can break away. A computer-run colonial power that has grown strong enough — at least half its colonists are rebels, it holds a port, it's still early enough in history, *and* its army is comfortably bigger than the King's expeditionary force (half again as large — the same edge that would let it actually win the war) — will declare its own independence, becoming a rebel nation the King then sends his army against. In practice this is rare: no rival builds an army that dominant in a normal game, so you'll almost never see it, and a default game plays out exactly as before. (Historical note: the real FreeCol game's computer players never do this on their own — we've added it in the same spirit, gated so it only triggers for a genuinely dominant rival.) For now a rival rebel mainly **defends** itself — it digs its troops into its colonies and arms its colonists against the King's army — rather than marching out to hunt the King's forces down; a rival's *offensive* war is a later addition.

**A foreign ally comes to your aid.** While the war drags on, your rebellion keeps producing liberty (bells). A sympathetic foreign power is watching, and the longer and harder you fight, the closer it comes to committing: every bell you generate during the war counts toward a hidden tally. Once that tally crosses a threshold (the classic game's medium setting is **5000**), the foreign power lands a small **Intervention Force** — a couple of colonial regulars, some dragoons, artillery, and a brace of men-o-war — right beside one of your ports, fighting on your side. The tally then resets, so a truly drawn-out war can draw a second wave. **And each wave is bigger than the last:** the ally builds up its standing force the longer the war runs, so a rebellion that holds out for years draws progressively larger landings (every ~52 turns of war the ally commits one more of each kind of soldier, with extra men-o-war to ferry them). It's the game's way of rewarding a stubborn rebellion: hold out long enough and you won't face the King alone — and the longer you last, the heavier the help that arrives.

*Worked example (classic medium, growth period 52 turns):* land before turn 52 and the ally sends its base force — 2 soldiers, 2 dragoons, 2 artillery, 2 men-o-war (8 units). Hold out past turn 52 and the next landing is 3 + 3 + 3 land units; past turn 104 it is 4 + 4 + 4; past turn 156, 5 + 5 + 5 — and once the land force outgrows the two men-o-war's holds, more men-o-war arrive to carry it.

**What the player sees and does:** once your nation is ready to rebel — at least half rebels and holding a port, in time — an **"Independence" button** appears among the HUD controls. Pressing it opens the **Declaration of Independence** screen: it shows your current rebel-sentiment reading and spells out the consequences (Europe closes and any units waiting there or at sea are seized, the King's Royal Expeditionary Force takes the field, and a Continental Army musters from your veterans), then offers **"Liberty or Death!"** or **"Maybe Later"**. Choosing Liberty actually declares independence and shows the **signed Declaration** (the historic resolution text); Maybe Later just closes it, changing nothing. The button is hidden the rest of the game (it only appears while you may legally declare), and the screen reads the same rules-engine gate the button does — if you somehow open it when ineligible, it tells you why instead of declaring. The rest of the war UI (a REF-arrival warning, the defeat screen) is still to come.

## 2. Detailed rules

- **The gate** (`CheckDeclareIndependence`, FreeCol `model.event.declareIndependence` limits): still a plain **colonial** power, **national Sons of Liberty ≥ 50%** (rebels across all colonies ÷ total population), **≥ 1 connected-port colony**, and the **year ≤ the last colonial year** (`Ruleset.LastColonialYear`, the spec `model.option.lastColonialYear` integer game option in the `gameOptions.years` group; classic **1800**). The cutoff is read from the ruleset, not hardcoded — a variant spec moves it by data alone (a missing option falls back to 1800).
- **The AI declaration gate** (`ShouldAiDeclareIndependence`/`MaybeDeclareIndependence`, kanban `86d3e49jp`): a **non-human** colonial power declares on its own turn when it (a) passes the same `CheckDeclareIndependence` gate above **and** (b) its land strength (`LandPowerOf`) is **≥ 1.5× the King's amassed REF land strength** (`RefLandStrength` = `Player.calculateStrength(false)` over the un-spawned `EnsureRefForce()` blocks — `Σ (unitType.OffenceAdditive + role.Offence) · unitType.OffenceMultiplier · count`, the same per-unit `OffenceBase` the live `LandPowerOf` sums, comparable because the REF holds no Founding Fathers). The 1.5× multiple is `AiDeclareStrengthRatio = RefDefeatPowerRatio` — the **same** yardstick the `CheckForRefDefeat` win uses — so an AI only rebels when it already holds a war-*winning* army. Run once per non-human colonial power in `RunPlayerTurn`'s `!IsHuman` block, **before** the economy/turn, so a power that declares this turn then runs the colonial path and defends the same turn. **Wholly RNG-free** — it reads SoL, ports, the year and two strength sums, drawing no stream (least of all the human's stream 0), so the default game (no AI amasses a 1.5×-REF army in the soak window) never fires it and stays byte-identical (ADR-009). **This is a faithful-SPIRIT addition, NOT a literal FreeCol port:** vanilla FreeCol's AI does **not** autonomously declare independence — `Player.getRebelStrengthRatio` appears only in a debug log line in `EuropeanAIPlayer.startWorking` (declaration is human/Monarch-only). We reuse that latent strength-ratio yardstick to let a dominant AI break away. The human is never auto-declared (it pulls its own trigger from the UI).
- **Declaring** (`DeclareIndependence`):
  - the player becomes a **`Rebel`** and records the turn (drives the score and the intervention force later);
  - every unit **in or bound for Europe is removed**; the **recruit dock is cleared**;
  - **Tory (loyalist) expulsion** (`ExpelTories`, a **Col1-faithful reconstruction** — FreeCol's `csDeclareIndependence` has **no** such step): each colony loses `floor(toryCount · (100 − SoL%) / 100)` of its **non-rebel** colonists (`toryCount = Population − RebelCount`, already exposed as `Colony.ToryCount`), **capped to leave ≥ 1 colonist** (a colony is never emptied by the exodus). The fraction scales with disloyalty — a **100%-SoL colony loses none**, a 0%-SoL colony loses every loyalist it can. Worker assignments are re-trimmed to the new size (`TrimAssignments`), and a `ToryExpulsionNotice(ColonyName, Position, ToriesLost, PopulationAfter)` is emitted per affected **human** colony (read once by the presentation right after declaring). **Wholly RNG-free** — the loss is a deterministic fraction (ADR-009) — so it draws no stream and the only state it touches (`Colony.Population`, already persisted) needs no save bump. *Formula rationale:* Col1 removed a SoL-scaled share of the Tory population at the Declaration; the original per-tenth tables aren't recoverable, so the linear `(100 − SoL)/100` share is a documented faithful-spirit reconstruction (the harsher-the-lower-SoL behaviour is the load-bearing fidelity);
  - **early-declaration score bonus** (FreeCol `csDeclareIndependence`): the `DeclaredIndependence` history event is recorded with score `max(0, Ruleset.IndependenceTurn − Turn)` (`Ruleset.IndependenceTurn` = spec `model.option.independenceTurn`, classic **468**) — the further **ahead** of the historical independence turn the nation breaks away, the larger the bonus, **0** at or after that turn. It folds into the player's final score through `HistoryEventScore` → `ScoreComponents.HistoryPoints` (see [scoring](scoring.md)); RNG-free, and the history log already persists (save v58) so it needs no save bump;
  - **continental-army muster** (`MusterContinentalArmy`): **per colony**, for each colony with SoL > 50 the cap is `(unitCount + 2) · (SoL − 50) / 100` — `unitCount` being *that colony's own* units (its population of workers + the units garrisoning its tile, FreeCol `Settlement.getAllUnitsList`) — and up to that many of *that colony's own* garrisoned **veteran soldiers** rise to **colonial regulars** (earliest first by id; colonies processed in id order). A veteran in the open field, or in a colony that hasn't crossed 50% SoL, is not mustered;
  - the **Royal Expeditionary Force** enters play as a new `RoyalExpeditionaryForce` AI player **at war** with the rebel, its amassed [force](royal-expeditionary-force.md) realised into real units mustering in Europe;
  - **native re-stancing** (`ShiftNativeStanceOnDeclaration`, FreeCol `csDeclareIndependence` native block): among the native nations the rebel has **contacted** (spoken with a chief, `NativeSettlement.HasBeenVisitedBy(rebel)`), the **most-hostile** one (highest peak alarm **toward the rebel** — `AlarmFor(rebel.PlayerId)` since the `86d3e4bfb` per-player refactor; id-tie-broken) **swings behind the rebel** — every one of its settlements is calmed (in the rebel's channel only) to at most FreeCol's CONTENT band (`NativeAllyCalmedAlarm` = 600). RNG-free. *Faithful-subset:* FreeCol also turns that ally **hateful toward the REF** and makes the *least*-hostile contacted nation turn on the rebel; alarm is now tracked per colonial power, but there is still **no native↔REF relationship and** (in single-player) **no second human**, so only the representable half — the angriest contacted nation backing the rebel — is implemented (the other two halves have no analogue);
  - **war-time mercenary offer** (`OfferWarMercenaries`, FreeCol `loadMercenaryForce` + `csMercenaries(HESSIAN_MERCENARIES)`): the King makes a one-off **Hessian** force offer, surfaced as a `PendingMonarchDemand(HessianMercenaries, Offer, Price)` — the *same* accept/decline seam as the in-game monarch mercenary offers — built from the existing affordability-trimmed `LoadMercenaries` generator on the **monarch RNG** (ephemeral, off the human's live state + turn, never stream 0). It is **applied only on accept** via `RespondToMonarch` (units spawn on the rebel's Europe dock, gold deducted); decline charges nothing. No offer is made when none is affordable. Surfaced for the **human** rebel only (an AI rebel auto-fights). *Faithful-subset:* FreeCol draws from the Monarch's fixed pre-built `mercenaryForce`; we reuse our veteran-soldier mercenary generator (the same documented simplification the in-game offer makes);
  - a **`DeclaredIndependence` history event** is recorded, carrying the **early-declaration bonus score** above (the history log is persisted from save v58, so the bonus survives a save/load round-trip).
- **The war** (`RunRefTurn`, item 8 + the **specialised REF combat doctrine** `86d3drn5a`): each REF turn, units still in Europe **land** (`LandRefUnits`) — the King's fleet makes landfall at its **fixed entry tile** (a water tile near the human's start, chosen deterministically at game creation and persisted, FreeCol `Player.entryTile`/`86d3c9w5n`): each unit takes the nearest empty tile to that beachhead (`FindLandingTileNear`, land ashore / ships on water), falling back to the ring around the rebel's connected-port colonies when the beachhead is full or unset (a pre-v47 save). Then the REF **prosecutes the war with a bespoke doctrine mirroring FreeCol `REFAIPlayer`** (no longer the generic foreign-power war AI), each unit on the REF's own stream:
  - **the rebel is found explicitly** (`RefRebel` = the `Rebel`/`Independent` player the REF is at war with), not assumed to be "the human" — they coincide in single-player, but the doctrine targets the rebel directly;
  - a **REF land unit** (`RunRefLandUnit`, FreeCol `findColonyTargets`/`adjustMission`): captures an **adjacent undefended rebel colony** outright; else marches on the **best-scored rebel colony in seek range** (`PickRefColonyTarget`) — the shared `ScoreColonyTarget` heuristic (value − distance, attacker strength, loot, −200·stockade) **plus +500 for a connected port** (`RefConnectedPortBonus`, FreeCol's connected-settlement bonus), so coastal/port colonies and the weakest-defended ones come first — fighting a garrison as a field unit when adjacent; **it does not chase loose rebel field units until the REF has captured a colony** (`holdsAColony` gate, FreeCol "Do not chase units until at least one colony is captured" / "The REF is more interested in colonies"), after which `PickRefUnitTarget` lets it seek-and-destroy field units too; with nothing scored in range it closes on the **nearest rebel colony at any distance** (`NearestRebelColony`) so the army besieges rather than idling;
  - a **REF man-o-war** (`RunRefNavalUnit`, FreeCol `REFNavyGoalDecider` + the navy block in `initialize`): seek-and-destroys the **rebel's warships** (`PickRefUnitTarget`, same-domain — ships hunt ships), engaging when adjacent, and — with no rebel ship in reach — escorts toward the invasion (`NearestRebelColony`), supporting the landings;
  - a REF unit standing in a **just-captured colony presses on** to the next objective (it has no defend-and-dig-in branch — FreeCol's "garrison thinly").

  Combat resolves through the REF-stream `AttackRebelUnit`/`CaptureRebelColony` (siblings of the generic `AttackHumanUnit`/`CapturePlayerColony`), recording the same `CombatNotice`/`ColonyLossNotice` for the presentation. The REF draws entirely from **its own RNG stream**; the rebel attacks the REF through the normal stream-0 combat. The REF↔rebel war stance never cools (the colonial-tension decay touches only `Colonial` players).
- **Foreign Intervention Force** (`AccrueInterventionBells`/`SpawnInterventionForce`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csNewTurn` rebel branch + `Monarch.getInterventionForce`): each turn during the war the rebel banks the **net liberty it produced that turn** into `Player.InterventionBells` (the same figure FreeCol adds in `Player.modifyLiberty` while `isRebel()`). When the running total reaches the ruleset's `interventionBells` threshold (`Ruleset.InterventionBells`, classic medium **5000**), a friendly foreign power sends its `Ruleset.InterventionForce` (classic medium **2 colonial-regular soldiers + 2 colonial-regular dragoons + 2 artillery + 2 men-o-war**): the **men-o-war make landfall on the water off a randomly-chosen connected port** of the rebel, **carrying the land units aboard as passengers** (FreeCol `loadShips` — the ally fleet arrives offshore with the troops embarked, and the player disembarks them where they choose), all **owned by, and fighting for, the rebel** (FreeCol creates the force on the rebel itself). Because it only needs sea room for the ships — not an open ring of beach — the ally can still reach a **besieged** port, which is the whole point. The counter then **resets to 0**, so a long war can draw a second — and bigger — wave. **The force grows with the war (`GrownInterventionForce(Turn)`, FreeCol `Monarch.updateInterventionForce`):** before each landing the base force is scaled for the current turn — `updates = Turn / interventionTurns` (classic medium `interventionTurns` = 52; integer division, 0 before the first interval) extra units are added to **each land block** (so the 2 + 2 + 2 land force becomes 3 + 3 + 3 after one interval, 4 + 4 + 4 after two…), and extra **men-o-war** are added (FreeCol `Force.prepareToBoard`) whenever the enlarged land force needs more hold slots than the standing fleet provides, so no reinforcements are stranded for want of a berth. The growth is **pure** (it reads only the ruleset force and the turn — no RNG, no new persisted state, derived from the existing turn counter so there is **no save bump**). The port and the exact sea tiles are the only random choices, drawn from a **dedicated reserved RNG stream** (`InterventionStreamId = 103`), never the human's stream 0 (ADR-009) — so even a human rebel's economy stream is untouched. If the rebel holds no connected port, or no sea room for even one man-o-war, nothing lands.
- **Winning** (`ResolveWarOfIndependence`, item 9): each turn, the rebel wins (FreeCol `checkForREFDefeat`) when the REF **holds no colonies**, the rebel's **land power ≥ 1.5×** the REF's, and the REF is reduced **below 7 land or 2 naval** units (counting its whole force, so the win can't fire before the army even lands). On winning (`GiveIndependence`): peace with the King, the rebel becomes **`Independent`** with **no tax**, the **surviving redcoats on the map surrender** to the new nation (the fleet sails home).
- **Victory conditions** (`Game.Winner`, FreeCol `ServerGame.checkForWinner`): the `Winner` read is now a **pure, ruleset-gated** evaluation of the three FreeCol victory toggles, checked in FreeCol's order:
  1. **Defeat the REF** (`Ruleset.VictoryDefeatRef`, classic **on**) — the first `Independent` nation wins (the headline War-of-Independence win, unchanged behaviour);
  2. **Defeat all Europeans** (`Ruleset.VictoryDefeatEuropeans`, classic **on**) — when exactly **one** live European power (a `Colonial`/`Rebel`/`Independent` player still holding a colony or a non-native unit; the REF and natives excluded) remains, it wins;
  3. **Defeat all humans** (`Ruleset.VictoryDefeatHumans`, classic **off**) — when exactly one live **non-AI** European power remains, it wins.
  Each toggle is parsed from the spec `gameOptions.victoryConditions` group (`model.option.victoryDefeat{REF,Europeans,Humans}`, a `<booleanOption>` read by `Ruleset.ParseBooleanOption`); a spec without them falls back to the classic defaults (true/true/false). All three are **pure reads** (like the old `Winner` and `IsRebelDefeated`); `EndTurn` never short-circuits on them (ADR-009 byte-stability — the presentation reads `Winner` for the victory screen). The default game's outcome is unchanged: with the human + 3 living foreign powers no Europeans/Humans condition fires, and `victoryDefeatHumans` is off.
- **Spanish Succession** (`RunSpanishSuccession`, a separate event): once, from **1600**, a fading European AI (SoL < 50) is **absorbed** by the dominant one (SoL > 50) — its colonies and units change hands. (Not the win condition; a late-game consolidation.)
- **Losing** (`IsRebelDefeated`, item 10): once you've declared, holding **no connected port** (`GetNumberOfPorts == 0` — the REF has taken them all) means you've **lost** the War of Independence. This is a flag the presentation reads for the defeat screen; the turn loop keeps running regardless (a defeated human must not freeze stream 0 — ADR-009). A plain colony with no port is never "rebel-defeated".

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** the gate (SoL ≥ 50, ≥ 1 connected port, year cutoff), the **per-colony continental muster** (cap + veteran→colonial-regular draw), the win thresholds (1.5× / 7 land / 2 naval) and the REF realisation match FreeCol. **Faithful-subset simplifications (documented, not yet faithful):** (1) the **continental muster** now caps and draws **per colony** (FreeCol `csDeclareIndependence`): each colony with SoL > 50 gets cap `(unitCount + 2) · (SoL − 50) / 100` over *its own* units and upgrades up to that many of *its own* veterans. The cap term faithfully counts the colony's population (workers) **plus** the units garrisoning its tile (FreeCol `getAllUnitsList` = worker units + tile units). The one residual deviation: because our colony workers are a `Population` **count**, not `Unit` objects, the veterans actually drawn can only be taken from the **tile garrison**, not from worker colonists — a veteran serving *as a colony worker* isn't mustered until taken out as a unit (FreeCol can upgrade a worker veteran in place). In practice veterans garrison the colony tile rather than working a job, so this rarely bites. (2) the **Spanish Succession** ranks the absorbed/absorbing pair by **Sons-of-Liberty** (we have no overall game-score yet) rather than FreeCol's score, and only an AI (not the human) can satisfy its strong-limit trigger. (3) the REF now runs a **bespoke combat doctrine** (`RunRefTurn`/`RunRefLandUnit`/`RunRefNavalUnit`, `86d3drn5a`) mirroring FreeCol `REFAIPlayer`'s priorities — **colony-first targeting with connected ports favoured** (+500), **no field-unit chase until a colony is captured**, and **men-o-war hunting the rebel's ships / supporting the landings** — rather than the generic foreign-power war AI. The modelled subset is the *target selection and the colony-first gate*, not FreeCol's full mission/transport scheduler: there is **no dedicated amphibious transport doctrine** (REF land units march overland from the beachhead rather than being re-embarked and ferried colony-to-colony), the score's drydock/military-supplier multipliers and the ±20% random twiddle from `findColonyTargets` are omitted (we keep the SoL + stockade terms), and the REF combat ambush-penalty is still deferred. (4) the **Intervention Force** now *grows* with the war (`GrownInterventionForce`, FreeCol `Monarch.updateInterventionForce`): each landing adds `Turn / interventionTurns` to every land block (plus transport men-o-war via `prepareToBoard`), so a long rebellion draws progressively larger waves. **Faithful-spirit timing deviation:** vanilla FreeCol grows the force **once**, at the declaration (`updateInterventionForce` is called from `csDeclareIndependence`), and the IVF then lands a **single** time (`interventionBells` is set to `Integer.MIN_VALUE`, never re-firing); our model instead lands **repeatedly** (the counter resets to 0 on each landing) and recomputes the growth from the **current** turn at each landing — the same `turn / interventionTurns` formula, but applied per-landing rather than once. This produces the spec's intended outcome (a longer war ⇒ a larger ally force) within our repeating-landing model. The fleet arrives FreeCol-faithfully — the men-o-war on the water carrying the land units aboard (FreeCol `loadShips`), troops over the holds disposed — so a besieged port still gets its ally. The bell accrual is recomputed from the rebel's liberty gain via a transient per-turn snapshot rather than hooked into `modifyLiberty`; a turn that straddles a save/load, or a turn the rebel elects a Founding Father (liberty spent), can drop that single turn's contribution — negligible against the 5000 threshold. (5) **Native re-stancing** on declaration implements only the representable half of FreeCol's rule — the angriest *contacted* native nation is calmed to back the rebel — since our single-player native model has no native↔REF relationship and no second human to turn hostile. (6) The **on-declaration Hessian offer** reuses our affordability-trimmed mercenary generator rather than FreeCol's fixed pre-built `mercenaryForce`, and is surfaced to the human rebel only (an AI rebel auto-fights). (7) The **alternative victory conditions** (defeat all Europeans / humans) are faithful pure reads; "live European power" is approximated as *holds a colony or a non-native unit* (FreeCol's full `isAlive`/death model is richer, but this matches in practice). (8) **AI self-declaration is a faithful-SPIRIT addition, not a literal port** (`86d3e49jp`): vanilla FreeCol's AI never declares independence on its own (`getRebelStrengthRatio` is debug-logging only); we let a *dominant* AI (army ≥ 1.5× the amassed REF, the win yardstick) break away through the same `DeclareIndependence` machinery. A fresh AI rebel currently runs only **passive defence** — it digs its garrisons in and arms its colonists (A1's fortify/arming, via the colonial-economy path) against the invading REF — because the rebel's *offensive* war AI in `RunForeignPowerTurn` is keyed to the human, not the REF; generalising it to make an AI rebel hunt the REF is a deliberately **descoped follow-up** (the REF is the aggressor, so a defended garrison is a coherent, shippable slice). See §5.

## 3. Technical design

- `PlayerType` gains `Rebel`, `Independent`, `RoyalExpeditionaryForce` (the lifecycle Colonial → Rebel → Independent; the REF is its own type). `RunPlayerTurn` routes Rebel/Independent through the colonial economy path and the REF through `RunRefTurn` (`Game.Independence.cs`), which lands the in-Europe units (`LandRefUnits`) then drives the **specialised REF combat doctrine** (`RunRefLandUnit`/`RunRefNavalUnit` + the targeting helpers `PickRefColonyTarget`/`PickRefUnitTarget`/`AdjacentCapturableRebelColony`/`NearestRebelColony` and the REF-stream combat `AttackRebelUnit`/`CaptureRebelColony`) — see item 8 above and FreeCol `REFAIPlayer`. The colony-score reuses the shared `ScoreColonyTarget`/`ScoreUnitTarget` (`Game.cs`) plus the `RefConnectedPortBonus` (+500); no new state, no save change.
- `Game.NationalSonsOfLiberty(player)` = `sum(colony.RebelCount) · 100 / max(1, sum(Population))`.
- `CheckDeclareIndependence`/`DeclareIndependence` (ADR-006). The year branch reads `Ruleset.LastColonialYear` (no longer a `LastColonialYear` const). `MusterContinentalArmy` iterates the rebel's colonies with SoL > 50 (in id order); per colony it computes `unitCount = colony.Population + |on-map non-native units owned by the rebel standing on the colony tile|` (FreeCol `getAllUnitsList().size()`), `limit = (unitCount + 2) · (SoL − 50) / 100`, then upgrades up to `limit` of the rebel's veteran soldiers garrisoning that tile (ordered by id) via the existing `UpgradeUnitType` swap (units are immutable in their type). The per-colony loop and tile-scoped draw replace the earlier nationwide `Sum`/whole-map `Take`. `CreateRefPlayer` adds a non-human `RoyalExpeditionaryForce` player with its **own RNG stream** (seeded off the human's current state, read non-destructively — stream 0 untouched), sets the WAR stance both ways, and realises `_refForce` into units via `SpawnInEurope`.
- **AI self-declaration** (`Game.Independence.cs` + `Game.cs`, `86d3e49jp`): `RunPlayerTurn`'s `!IsHuman` block now calls `MaybeDeclareIndependence(power)` **before** `RunForeignPowerEconomy`/`RunForeignPowerTurn`. `MaybeDeclareIndependence` calls `DeclareIndependence(power)` iff `ShouldAiDeclareIndependence(power)` — `power` is non-human, `CheckDeclareIndependence(power).Allowed`, and `LandPowerOf(power) ≥ AiDeclareStrengthRatio · RefLandStrength()` with `RefLandStrength() > 0`. `AiDeclareStrengthRatio` is a `const double = RefDefeatPowerRatio` (1.5). `RefLandStrength()` (`internal`) sums the amassed `EnsureRefForce().LandUnits` blocks by `(Ruleset.Unit(e.UnitTypeId).OffenceAdditive + Ruleset.Role(e.RoleId ?? DefaultRoleId).Offence) · Ruleset.Unit(e.UnitTypeId).OffenceMultiplier · e.Count` — the un-spawned mirror of `OffenceBase`/`LandPowerOf` (the REF has no Congress, so the father factor is 1.0 and the two are directly comparable; pinned by `RefLandStrength_EqualsTheLandPowerOfTheSpawnedRefAfterDeclaration`). Once the AI declares, the existing `DeclareIndependence` runs (it flips to Rebel, spawns the REF on its own stream, etc.); `OfferWarMercenaries` self-gates to the human so the AI rebel draws no monarch stream. **No new state, no save change** — the gate is a pure read, and the resulting Rebel/REF rows already persist (v41). Descope: the rebel's offensive in `RunForeignPowerTurn` stays human-keyed (`atWarWithHuman = StanceBetween(power, _human) == War`), so an AI rebel — at war with the REF, not the human — falls through that block to the **FP-5 garrison-fortify + colonist-arming defence** (A1); offensive rebel-vs-REF AI is a follow-up (§5).
- **Military-report oracles** (`86d3e4buh`, ADR-006 read-only accessors — no behaviour change): two `public` strength readers for the empire **Military** report. `Game.HumanMilitaryStrength()` returns `(LandPower, LandUnits, NavalUnits)` — the human's combined land attack power (`LandPowerOf(_human)`, the same War-of-Independence yardstick) plus its land/naval unit counts. `Game.ExpeditionaryForceStrength()` returns the REF `Force`'s `(LandUnits, NavalUnits)` counts, reading `_refForce ?? BuildBaseRef()` so a report read **does not** materialise/store the base force (`RefForceOrNull` stays the saved truth — pinned by `ExpeditionaryForceStrength_MatchesTheBaseRefCounts_WithoutMaterialisingIt`). Both are RNG-free and mutate nothing; the presentation's Military tab reads them directly.
- **Tory expulsion + early-declaration score** (`Game.Independence.cs`, `86d3fq0e0`/`86d3fq0dc`): `DeclareIndependence` calls `ExpelTories(player)` (before the muster) and records the `DeclaredIndependence` history event with score `Math.Max(0, Ruleset.IndependenceTurn − Turn)`. `ExpelTories` clears `_toryExpulsionNotices`, then for each colony (id order) computes `lost = colony.ToryCount · (100 − colony.SonsOfLiberty) / ToryDesertionPerSolPoint` (`ToryDesertionPerSolPoint = 100`), clamps it to `Population − 1`, and if positive reduces `Colony.Population`, calls `TrimAssignments(colony)`, and (human colony only) adds a `ToryExpulsionNotice`. Per-colony losses read each colony's *pre-mutation* `ToryCount`/`SonsOfLiberty`, so colonies are independent and order-free. `Ruleset.IndependenceTurn` is newly surfaced (delegates to `GameOptions.IndependenceTurn`, spec `model.option.independenceTurn`, classic 468 — mirrors `LastColonialYear`); the score flows through the existing `HistoryEventScore` sum into `ScoreComponents.HistoryPoints` (`ScoreComponents.cs` doc updated). Both are **RNG-free**; the only persisted state touched (`Colony.Population`, the v58 history log) was already saved → **no save bump**. The new `ToryExpulsionNotice` (`Colonies/ToryExpulsionNotice.cs`) is transient UI scratch surfaced via `Game.ToryExpulsionNotices`, refreshed each declaration (a one-off player action) so no central `EndTurn` clear is needed.
- **Native re-stancing + Hessian offer** (`Game.Independence.cs`): `DeclareIndependence` now also calls `ShiftNativeStanceOnDeclaration` and `OfferWarMercenaries`, then `RecordHistory(DeclaredIndependence, …)`. `ShiftNativeStanceOnDeclaration` groups the rebel-contacted settlements by `NationTypeId`, picks the group with the highest peak `Alarm` (id-tie-broken), and `ChangeNativeAlarm`s each of that nation's settlements down to `NativeAllyCalmedAlarm` (= `NativeSettlement.AlarmContentMax` = 600) when above it — RNG-free, no save change (the per-settlement `Alarm` already persists; a nation-scope tension is not modelled). `OfferWarMercenaries` (human rebel only) builds a `PendingMonarchDemand(HessianMercenaries, Offer, Price)` from `LoadMercenaries(rng)` on the ephemeral monarch stream (`MonarchStreamId`, seeded off the human's live state + `Turn`); the existing `RespondToMonarch` mercenary branch applies/declines it. `HistoryEventKind` gains `DeclaredIndependence` (in-memory history; not persisted).
- **Victory** (`Game.Winner` in `Game.Independence.cs`): generalised from "first `Independent`" to FreeCol's three-condition `checkForWinner`, gated on `Ruleset.VictoryDefeatRef`/`VictoryDefeatEuropeans`/`VictoryDefeatHumans`. `LiveEuropeanPowers()` is the private helper — `Colonial`/`Rebel`/`Independent` players that still hold a colony or a non-native unit (REF + natives excluded). `Ruleset` gains the three `bool` properties, parsed in `Ruleset.Load` via `ParseBooleanOption(root, "model.option.victoryDefeat{REF,Europeans,Humans}", fallback: true/true/false)` (the `gameOptions.victoryConditions` group; classic uses `defaultValue`). Not persisted (re-derived from the ruleset on load, like the other game options) → **no save-version bump**; the default classic values leave `Winner`'s default-game result unchanged (ADR-009).
- **`Ruleset.LastColonialYear`** is parsed in `Ruleset.Load` by `ParseIntOption(root, "model.option.lastColonialYear", fallback: 1800)` — a top-level `<integerOption>` reader (mirrors `ParseBooleanOption`; reads `value` then `defaultValue`). It's a **game option, not a difficulty option** (it doesn't vary by level, so it lives on the ruleset root beside the `gameOptions.years` calendar, not in `DifficultyOptions`). Not persisted in the save (re-derived from the ruleset on load, like the calendar) → **no save-version bump**; the default classic spec value is 1800, so the default game's gate is byte-identical to the old const.
- **Intervention options** (`Ruleset.InterventionBells`/`InterventionTurns`/`InterventionForce`): parsed by `Ruleset.ParseIntervention(root, difficultyLevelId)`, which — like `ParseDifficulty` — first selects the chosen difficulty level's `optionGroup` subtree (the options are restated under every level, so reading over the whole doc would pick `veryEasy`), then reads `model.option.interventionBells`/`interventionTurns` (`<integerOption>`) and the `model.option.interventionForce` `<unitListOption>` of `<unitOption>` (`unitType`/`role`/`number`) blocks. A missing level — or any missing option — falls back to the classic-medium values (`InterventionForceComposition.ClassicMedium` = 5000 / 52 / the 8-unit ally force), so the default game and a spec without the options are unchanged. `InterventionForceComposition`/`InterventionForceUnit` are records declared alongside `Ruleset`; the land/naval split is decided at spawn time from each unit type's `IsNaval` flag (ruleset-agnostic).
- **Intervention accrual + spawn** (`Game.Independence.cs`): `ResolveWarOfIndependence` (per turn, in `EndTurn`'s world-advance band) calls `AccrueInterventionBells(rebel)` for each rebel. It banks `max(0, rebel.Liberty − snapshot)` into `rebel.InterventionBells` (a transient `_interventionLibertySnapshot` dict seeds from the rebel's current liberty on first sight — so nothing pre-rebellion is counted, and a save/load straddle costs at most one turn's bells), then if `InterventionBells ≥ Ruleset.InterventionBells` it resets the counter to 0 and calls `SpawnInterventionForce(rebel)`. The spawn first computes the **grown** composition for the current turn via `GrownInterventionForce(Turn)` — `updates = Turn / Ruleset.InterventionTurns` (integer division; 0 when `InterventionTurns ≤ 0` or before the first interval) extra units added to each **land** block, then `prepareToBoard`-style transport men-o-war added when the enlarged land force's required hold slots (`Σ CarrySlots·count`) exceed the fleet capacity (`Σ Space·count` over carrier ships): `more = (deficit / shipSpace) + 1` of the first unit-carrying ship type. `GrownInterventionForce` is **pure and `internal`** (testable at a chosen turn). The spawn then picks a random connected port, drops the **men-o-war** of the grown force on the nearest free sea tiles around it (`FindLandingTileNear(naval: true)`), then **boards the land units onto that fleet as passengers** (round-robin by free `CargoSlotsFree`, `CarrierId` set to the carrying ship; land units that exceed the holds are left behind, FreeCol-style) — all owned by the rebel. With no ship able to make landfall, nothing lands. The RNG is a fresh `Pcg32Random(seed, InterventionStreamId)` seeded off the REF's (or rebel's) persisted stream state + `Turn` — deterministic, reproducible across save/load, and never stream 0. `ResolveWarOfIndependence` is `internal` so a test can land the ally in isolation (the REF's own units are still mustering in Europe at that point, so the fleet arrives unmolested — a full `EndTurn` would land the REF and sink the just-arrived fleet the same turn).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** the REF runs on its own stream (like a foreign power); the human/rebel on stream 0; the monarch tick stops once the player is no longer `Colonial`. The **Intervention Force** lands on the reserved `InterventionStreamId = 103` stream (high id, like LCR/resource-quantity), so a human rebel's economy stream 0 is untouched — the soak (which never reaches the war) stays byte-identical.
- **Victory options** (`Ruleset.VictoryDefeatRef`/`VictoryDefeatEuropeans`/`VictoryDefeatHumans`): parsed in `Ruleset.Load` by `ParseBooleanOption(root, "model.option.victoryDefeat{REF,Europeans,Humans}", fallback)` — the same top-level `<booleanOption>` reader used for `enableUpkeep` (reads `value` then `defaultValue`; classic ships these with `defaultValue`). They live in the `gameOptions.victoryConditions` game-option group (not a difficulty option). Fallbacks are the classic defaults (true/true/false). Not persisted (re-derived from the ruleset on load) → **no save-version bump**; the default game's `Winner` result is unchanged.
- **Per-game override at New Game** (`86d3drn64`, `Ruleset.WithVictoryConditions(defeatRef, defeatEuropeans, defeatHumans)`): the New-Game dialog's **Victory conditions** checkboxes let the player flip the three toggles for a session. The seam mutates the three `VictoryDefeat*` props (now `{ get; private set; }`) on the **freshly-loaded** ruleset in place — safe because every `Ruleset.LoadEmbedded`/`Load` is an unshared parse — and returns `this`; `GameController.StartNewGame` calls it between load and `Game.New` (threaded via the `GameController.PendingVictoryConditions` static, presentation-only). A **configuration** change of which already-implemented `Winner` checks fire, **not** a rules change (the win logic is untouched, ADR-006). Leaving the picks at the spec defaults makes the override a no-op (byte-identical, ADR-009). **Session-only** — the override is **not** persisted (consistent with the parsed options above), so a reloaded game re-derives the conditions from its variant's spec defaults → **still no save-version bump**.
- **Save v41 (unchanged — no bump):** `SavedPlayer.DeclaredIndependenceTurn` + `InterventionBells` (both omitted before independence); the `PlayerType` ordinals (Rebel/Independent/REF) and the REF **player row** persist for free, and the REF's + the landed intervention force's units via `SavedUnit.OwnerId`; a pre-independence game is byte-identical to v40. The Intervention Force **reuses the existing `Player.InterventionBells`** field (no new field, no version bump); the per-turn liberty snapshot is transient (reseeded on load). The native re-stancing, Hessian offer, victory options, and the `DeclaredIndependence` history event add **no new persisted field** (native alarm already persists; the pending offer rides the existing transient `PendingMonarchDemand`; the victory options are ruleset-derived; history is in-memory) → **still v41, no bump**.
