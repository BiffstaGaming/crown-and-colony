## 1. How it works (plain English)

You sell your colonies' goods to Europe for **gold**, and Europe charges a **sales tax** on every sale. Prices follow supply and demand: the more of a good Europe already holds, the less it pays for more of it — so dumping 600 sugar drives the sugar price down as you sell. The reverse is true when you **buy**: every load you take off the market makes the next one **dearer**, so buying up a lot of one good steadily pushes its price up. Each good has a **sell price** (what you receive) and a higher **buy price** (what you'd pay to buy it back); the gap between them is fixed per good.

**Worked example:** sugar starts selling at 2 gold each. Sell 600 sugar with no tax and you get 1,200 gold — but the price has dropped to 1 gold by the end, because Europe is now glutted with sugar. Add a 50% tax and you'd keep only 600 of that.

**National trade advantage (the Dutch):** the Dutch are the traders of the New World — Europe's market reacts to their selling **half as strongly**. When the Dutch dump 600 sugar, the market behaves as if only 300 arrived, so the price slides half as far and they keep selling at a good rate for longer. (It's a per-nation trait: the human only gets it by playing the Dutch; every other nation moves the market normally.)

**Seeing the prices at a glance:** the empire-reports screen (the **Reports** button) has a **Market** tab listing every tradeable good with its current sell and buy price, and flagging any good that's under **boycott** (one you can't trade until you pay off its back-tax). It's a read-only board — you still buy and sell on the Europe screen — but it lets you scan the whole market before sailing.

**Keeping the books (the Trade report):** the game also keeps a running tally, per good, of how much you've **traded over the whole game** — total units sold, total income **before tax**, and total income **after tax**. These are the three columns of the classic **Trade report**. They're *net* figures: selling adds to them and buying the same good back subtracts (a purchase is the opposite of a sale, so buying back 40 of the 100 furs you sold leaves "60 sold" on the books). Income-before-tax shows what your trades earned gross; income-after-tax shows what actually reached your treasury once the Crown took its cut — so the gap between the two columns is the total tax you've paid on that good. The figures persist across save/load, so the report shows your whole trading history, not just this session.

## 2. Detailed rules

- Every tradeable good seeds from the spec's `<market>`: `initial-amount` (Europe's starting stock), `initial-price` (sell/bid), `price-difference` (the spread → buy/ask = bid + difference).
- **Initial prices (verified against the classic spec):** food 1/9, sugar 2/4, tobacco 2/4, ore 4/7, silver 16/18, rum 10/12, muskets 1/3.
- **Price formula** (FreeCol `MarketData.price()`): `bid = round(initialPrice × initialAmount ÷ amountInMarket)`, `ask = bid + spread`. Selling **adds** the goods to `amountInMarket`, lowering the bid; **buying removes** them, raising the ask (FreeCol `addGoodsToMarket(type, ±amount)`).
- **Chunking:** sales **and purchases** are processed in batches of 100; each batch is priced, then moves the market, so one huge trade can't be dumped/bought at the opening price.
- **Buy floor:** the market never holds less than **100** of a good (FreeCol `MINIMUM_AMOUNT`), so even a colossal purchase can't drive the supply to zero (the price clamps at the 19 ceiling instead).
- **Hard bounds:** bid never below 1, ask never above 19.
- **New World cap:** sugar/tobacco/cotton/furs and the goods made from them can't have their sell price pushed above `initialPrice + 2`.
- **Jump clamp:** a single recompute can't move the price by more than the spread (anti-exploit); the inventory is nudged back to stay consistent.
- **Tax:** `goldCredited = (100 − taxRate) × revenue ÷ 100`, integer-truncated (the player can lose a gold to rounding). Tax starts at 0%.
- **Trade accounting (the Trade report data):** each good carries three cumulative counters — `Sales` (net units), `IncomeBeforeTaxes` (net pre-tax gold), `IncomeAfterTaxes` (net after-tax gold) — mirroring FreeCol `MarketData.sales`/`incomeBeforeTaxes`/`incomeAfterTaxes`. **A sell adds** the chunk's units + its pre-tax revenue + its post-tax gold (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.sellInEurope` → `modifySales(+a)`/`modifyIncomeBeforeTaxes(+revenue)`/`modifyIncomeAfterTaxes(+afterTax)`). **A buy subtracts** the chunk's units + its cost off *all three* (FreeCol `buyInEurope` negates the same three; the Crown charges no tax on a buy, so the same cost comes off both income totals). So all three read **net** trade. A non-mutating price quote (`BuyCost`) never touches them. Declaring independence resets them to 0 (FreeCol `reinitialiseMarket` builds a fresh market). The counters are **read-only data** for the Trade report (E4) — they never feed the price model, so adding them leaves the market's evolution (and the soak's determinism) untouched.
- Non-tradeable goods (grain, fish, bells, crosses — anything without a `<market>`) cannot be sold. **Food *is* tradeable** in the classic spec (1/9) — only grain/fish (its unstorable raw forms) are not — so a seller that wants to keep its colonists fed must exclude food deliberately; the foreign-power AI does (see [players.md](players.md)).
- **Boycott (arrears).** A good with non-zero `Arrears` is under **boycott** — `CanTrade(goodsId)` is false and a manual sale (`SellColonyGoods` / `SellShipCargo` via the UI) is refused with an `InvalidMoveException`. The boycott is set by a Boston Tea Party (see [monarchy](monarchy.md)) and lifted by paying the back-tax (`PayArrears`) or electing Jacob Fugger (`LiftAllBoycotts`). **The one exception is the custom house:** it consults FreeCol's `Market.Access.CUSTOM_HOUSE` trade gate, which **ignores boycotts** when `model.option.customIgnoreBoycott` is on (classic default) — it smuggles the boycotted good out at the ordinary price/tax with no penalty, leaving the arrears intact (see [custom-house](custom-house.md)).
- **Per-player markets (ADR-019):** every `Player` owns its **own** `Market` instance. A sale moves only the seller's market — the human's and other powers' prices are unaffected. From FP-5 the foreign powers trade on their own markets each turn (selling their colonies' surplus), so the per-player price model is exercised in real games; each market persists independently in the save.
- **Dutch trade advantage (`tradeBonus`, FreeCol `Modifier.TRADE_BONUS`):** the trade nation type (`model.nationType.trade` — the Dutch) carries `model.modifier.tradeBonus` **−50%**, so each chunk of one of their sells adds only **half** as much to `amountInMarket` — the price falls half as fast as they sell (they still pocket the full chunk revenue; only the price-moving inventory is scaled). The 4th nation-type advantage to fold through the shared `NationTypeModifiers` seam (after the English religious-unrest, French native-alarm, Spanish offence). A player with no such nation type (the human's default) is unaffected. It now applies to **both** buying and selling (FreeCol-faithful): the same `volumeFactor` scales each buy chunk's inventory move, so a Dutch player's buying lifts the price half as fast too.

**Deviations from FreeCol:** `SellColonyGoods` still sells straight from a colony warehouse to Europe (a convenience abstraction kept from slice 1); since slice 3 a ship can also carry cargo and sell it in Europe (`SellShipCargo`) and **buy goods** in Europe (`BuyEuropeGoods`) — buying now **moves the market** (the ask rises as it drains, `86d3dkffz`), so a large purchase costs more than a flat ask × amount — and since slice 10 both are on the **Europe screen** (per-ship Sell buttons + a Buy-goods dropdown), see [europe.md](europe.md).

## 3. Technical design

- `GoodsType` gains `IsNewWorldGoods` and a `GoodsMarket?` (InitialAmount/InitialPrice/PriceDifference; null = untradeable), parsed from `<market>`.
- `Market` (engine-free, `Trade/Market.cs`): per-good `{ amountInMarket, bid, ask }`; `BidPrice`/`AskPrice`/`AmountInMarket`; `Sell(goodsId, amount, taxPercent, volumeFactor = 1.0) → SaleResult(beforeTax, afterTax)` chunks by 100 and recomputes per chunk. **`volumeFactor`** scales each chunk's `amountInMarket` delta (`+= round(chunk × volumeFactor)`) — 1.0 normally, 0.5 for the Dutch trade advantage; the default keeps every existing caller byte-identical. **`Buy(goodsId, amount, volumeFactor = 1.0) → int cost`** is the mirror: it chunks by 100, prices each chunk at the current ask, then **subtracts** `round(chunk × volumeFactor)` from `amountInMarket` (floored at `MinimumAmountInMarket = 100`) and recomputes, so the ask climbs across the buy. **`BuyCost(...)`** runs the same loop on a throwaway copy of the datum — a non-mutating price quote for the affordability check (the cost rises across chunks, so a flat ask under-quotes). `Recompute` is a faithful port of `MarketData.price()` (float supply formula, new-world cap, jump clamp, [1,19] bounds). `SaveDeltas`/`LoadDeltas` persist only inventories that moved from their seed. **Trade accounting:** the per-good `Datum` carries `Sales`/`IncomeBeforeTaxes`/`IncomeAfterTaxes`; `Sell` increments all three per chunk (units, pre-tax revenue, post-tax gold) and the shared `ChunkedBuy` loop decrements them (units + cost off all three) — so `Buy` accumulates on the live datum while `BuyCost` accumulates on the throwaway copy it discards. Read via `SalesOf`/`IncomeBeforeTaxesOf`/`IncomeAfterTaxesOf` (0 for an untraded/untradeable good); `Reinitialise` zeroes them; `SaveCounters`/`LoadCounters` persist the sparse `TradeAccount(Sales, IncomeBeforeTaxes, IncomeAfterTaxes)` triple for every traded good. `Game.Market.{SalesOf,IncomeBeforeTaxesOf,IncomeAfterTaxesOf}(id)` is the clean read surface the Trade-report panel (E4) consumes.
- `Game`: `Gold`, `TaxRate`, `Market`; `SellColonyGoods(colony, goodsId, amount)` / `SellShipCargo(...)` deduct stores and credit the treasury, passing **`MarketVolumeFactor(player)`** into `Market.Sell`. The internal `SellColonyGoods(player, colony, goodsId, amount, bool ignoreBoycott = false)` overload skips the `!CanTrade` boycott guard when `ignoreBoycott` is true — the only caller that passes `true` is the custom-house auto-sell (`AutoSellExports`, gated on `Ruleset.GameOptions.CustomIgnoreBoycott`); every manual/UI sale uses the default `false` and keeps the strict refusal. This also removed a latent End-Turn crash where the auto-sell threw on a boycotted custom-house good (see [custom-house](custom-house.md)). `CheckBuyEuropeGoods` quotes `Market.BuyCost(…, MarketVolumeFactor(player))` and `BuyEuropeGoods` calls `Market.Buy(…, MarketVolumeFactor(player))` (debiting the returned cost). `MarketVolumeFactor(player)` folds the player's nation-type `model.modifier.tradeBonus` onto 1.0 via `NationTypeModifiers` (Dutch → 0.5; no nation → 1.0); `tradeBonus` is nation-type-only (no founding father carries it). No RNG, no save change — the factor is read live from the player's nation type. `Game.New` takes optional `startingGold`/`startingTax` (default 0 = classic).
- **Persistence:** save v9 stores `Gold`, `Tax`, and the sparse moved-market map; pre-v9 saves load with 0 gold/tax and a ruleset-seeded market. **Save v56** adds the per-good trade accounting: `SavedPlayer.TradeAccounts` (goods-id → `SavedTradeAccount(Sales, IncomeBeforeTaxes, IncomeAfterTaxes)`), written via `Market.SaveCounters()` and replayed via `LoadCounters` in `Game.BuildPlayer`. **Additive + omit-when-default:** only goods with a non-zero counter are written, so a never-traded market omits the map and a default (un-traded) game round-trips byte-identically to v55; pre-v56 saves load with every counter 0.
- **Market report tab (`86d3dmn6d`):** `ColonyReportPanel`'s **Market** tab is pure presentation (ADR-006) — it enumerates `Game.Ruleset.GoodsTypes.Where(IsTradeable)` and, per good, reads `Game.Market.BidPrice`/`AskPrice` and derives the boycott flag from `Market.Arrears(id) > 0` (equivalently `!CanTrade(id)`). No write members are touched. Sales/income columns (FreeCol `ReportTradePanel`'s `getSales`/`getIncomeBeforeTaxes`/`getIncomeAfterTaxes`) now have their **data** behind them: `86d3e4bdm` (save v56) added the per-good trade accumulator (`Market.SalesOf`/`IncomeBeforeTaxesOf`/`IncomeAfterTaxesOf`). The report **panel** that surfaces those columns is **E4** — this slice only wired the data; the current Market tab still renders the price + boycott subset until E4 lands.
