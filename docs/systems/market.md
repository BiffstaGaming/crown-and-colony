## 1. How it works (plain English)

You sell your colonies' goods to Europe for **gold**, and Europe charges a **sales tax** on every sale. Prices follow supply and demand: the more of a good Europe already holds, the less it pays for more of it — so dumping 600 sugar drives the sugar price down as you sell. The reverse is true when you **buy**: every load you take off the market makes the next one **dearer**, so buying up a lot of one good steadily pushes its price up. Each good has a **sell price** (what you receive) and a higher **buy price** (what you'd pay to buy it back); the gap between them is fixed per good.

**Worked example:** sugar starts selling at 2 gold each. Sell 600 sugar with no tax and you get 1,200 gold — but the price has dropped to 1 gold by the end, because Europe is now glutted with sugar. Add a 50% tax and you'd keep only 600 of that.

**National trade advantage (the Dutch):** the Dutch are the traders of the New World — Europe's market reacts to their selling **half as strongly**. When the Dutch dump 600 sugar, the market behaves as if only 300 arrived, so the price slides half as far and they keep selling at a good rate for longer. (It's a per-nation trait: the human only gets it by playing the Dutch; every other nation moves the market normally.)

**Seeing the prices at a glance:** the empire-reports screen (the **Reports** button) has a **Market** tab listing every tradeable good with its current sell and buy price, and flagging any good that's under **boycott** (one you can't trade until you pay off its back-tax). It's a read-only board — you still buy and sell on the Europe screen — but it lets you scan the whole market before sailing.

**Keeping the books (the Trade report):** the game also keeps a running tally, per good, of how much you've **traded over the whole game** — total units sold, total income **before tax**, and total income **after tax**. These are the three columns of the classic **Trade report**. They're *net* figures: selling adds to them and buying the same good back subtracts (a purchase is the opposite of a sale, so buying back 40 of the 100 furs you sold leaves "60 sold" on the books). Income-before-tax shows what your trades earned gross; income-after-tax shows what actually reached your treasury once the Crown took its cut — so the gap between the two columns is the total tax you've paid on that good. The figures persist across save/load, so the report shows your whole trading history, not just this session.

**Your trades ripple into your rivals' markets (and theirs into yours):** the European nations trade in the same ports, so a big sale doesn't just move *your* prices. Whenever any European power sells (or buys) in Europe, every **other** European power's market feels a random **5–30%** of it: dump 600 sugar and each rival's Europe quietly gains roughly 30–180 sugar of supply, softening the price *they* would get for sugar too. Buying works in reverse — a rival stockpiling muskets makes muskets a little scarcer (and so dearer) for everyone else. Tiny trades (3 or fewer of a good) are too small to register, and only the *other* powers feel the ripple — your own market still moves only by your own full trade, exactly as before. (A FreeCol behaviour with no dial: always on there, always on here; the original 1994 game had nothing like it because it had only one visible market.)

**Markets slowly heal themselves (drift):** Europe's appetite isn't fixed — once you've traded a good, its market drifts a little **back toward normal every turn**. Flood Europe with 600 sugar and the glut doesn't last forever: each turn a small random amount of that surplus is consumed, so the sugar price gradually climbs back toward its starting level. Likewise a good you've bought out slowly restocks. The drift is gentle and grows with the game's age — nothing at all before turn 10, then up to `turn ÷ 10` units per good per turn (one randomly-picked good each turn gets roughly double that), so late-game markets recover faster than early ones. Only goods you have **actually traded** drift — untouched markets sit still — and each nation's market drifts independently. One quirk inherited from FreeCol: the drift never quite "arrives" — a market sitting exactly on its normal level gets nudged just below it rather than resting there (harmless in practice; the nudges are tiny).

**Price-change alerts:** you no longer have to open the Trade report to notice a price has shifted. Whenever one of your goods' Europe price moves over a turn — because you flooded the market selling sugar, your custom house auto-sold tobacco, or a trade route delivered furs — the game tells you at End Turn: *"⚖ The price of Sugar fell to 1/3 (sell/buy)"*. One line per good that moved, so a busy trading turn gives you a quick read of what changed without doing the arithmetic yourself. (It's purely informational — the alert doesn't change any price, it just reports what already happened.)

## 2. Detailed rules

- Every tradeable good seeds from the spec's `<market>`: `initial-amount` (Europe's starting stock), `initial-price` (sell/bid), `price-difference` (the spread → buy/ask = bid + difference).
- **Initial prices (verified against the classic spec):** food 1/9, sugar 2/4, tobacco 2/4, ore 4/7, silver 16/18, rum 10/12, muskets 1/3.
- **Price formula** (FreeCol `MarketData.price()`): `bid = round(initialPrice × initialAmount ÷ amountInMarket)`, `ask = bid + spread`. Selling **adds** the goods to `amountInMarket`, lowering the bid; **buying removes** them, raising the ask (FreeCol `addGoodsToMarket(type, ±amount)`).
- **Chunking:** sales **and purchases** are processed in batches of 100; each batch is priced, then moves the market, so one huge trade can't be dumped/bought at the opening price.
- **Buy floor:** the market never holds less than **100** of a good (FreeCol `MINIMUM_AMOUNT`), so even a colossal purchase can't drive the supply to zero (the price clamps at the 19 ceiling instead).
- **Hard bounds:** bid never below 1, ask never above 19.
- **New World cap:** sugar/tobacco/cotton/furs and the goods made from them can't have their sell price pushed above `initialPrice + 2`.
- **Jump clamp:** a single recompute can't move the price by more than the spread (anti-exploit); the inventory is nudged back to stay consistent.
- **Tax:** `goldCredited = (100 − taxRate) × revenue ÷ 100`, integer-truncated (the player can lose a gold to rounding). Tax starts at 0%.
- **Trade accounting (the Trade report data):** each good carries three cumulative counters — `Sales` (net units), `IncomeBeforeTaxes` (net pre-tax gold), `IncomeAfterTaxes` (net after-tax gold) — mirroring FreeCol `MarketData.sales`/`incomeBeforeTaxes`/`incomeAfterTaxes`. **A sell adds** the chunk's units + its pre-tax revenue + its post-tax gold (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.sellInEurope` → `modifySales(+a)`/`modifyIncomeBeforeTaxes(+revenue)`/`modifyIncomeAfterTaxes(+afterTax)`). **A buy subtracts** the chunk's units + its cost off *all three* (FreeCol `buyInEurope` negates the same three; the Crown charges no tax on a buy, so the same cost comes off both income totals). So all three read **net** trade. A non-mutating price quote (`BuyCost`, and the sell-side `SaleValue`) never touches them. Declaring independence resets them to 0 (FreeCol `reinitialiseMarket` builds a fresh market). The counters are **read-only data** for the Trade report (E4) — they never feed the price model, so adding them leaves the market's evolution (and the soak's determinism) untouched.
- Non-tradeable goods (grain, fish, bells, crosses — anything without a `<market>`) cannot be sold. **Food *is* tradeable** in the classic spec (1/9) — only grain/fish (its unstorable raw forms) are not — so a seller that wants to keep its colonists fed must exclude food deliberately; the foreign-power AI does (see [players.md](players.md)).
- **Boycott (arrears).** A good with non-zero `Arrears` is under **boycott** — `CanTrade(goodsId)` is false and a manual sale (`SellColonyGoods` / `SellShipCargo` via the UI) is refused with an `InvalidMoveException`. The boycott is set by a Boston Tea Party (see [monarchy](monarchy.md)) and lifted by paying the back-tax (`PayArrears`) or electing Jacob Fugger (`LiftAllBoycotts`). **The one exception is the custom house:** it consults FreeCol's `Market.Access.CUSTOM_HOUSE` trade gate, which **ignores boycotts** when `model.option.customIgnoreBoycott` is on (classic default) — it smuggles the boycotted good out at the ordinary price/tax with no penalty, leaving the arrears intact (see [custom-house](custom-house.md)).
- **Per-player markets (ADR-019):** every `Player` owns its **own** `Market` instance. A sale moves only the seller's market — the human's and other powers' prices are unaffected. From FP-5 the foreign powers trade on their own markets each turn (selling their colonies' surplus), so the per-player price model is exercised in real games; each market persists independently in the save.
- **Dutch trade advantage (`tradeBonus`, FreeCol `Modifier.TRADE_BONUS`):** the trade nation type (`model.nationType.trade` — the Dutch) carries `model.modifier.tradeBonus` **−50%**, so each chunk of one of their sells adds only **half** as much to `amountInMarket` — the price falls half as fast as they sell (they still pocket the full chunk revenue; only the price-moving inventory is scaled). The 4th nation-type advantage to fold through the shared `NationTypeModifiers` seam (after the English religious-unrest, French native-alarm, Spanish offence). A player with no such nation type (the human's default) is unaffected. It now applies to **both** buying and selling (FreeCol-faithful): the same `volumeFactor` scales each buy chunk's inventory move, so a Dutch player's buying lifts the price half as fast too.
- **Rival-market trade propagation (`86d3fpyx3`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.propagateToEuropeanMarkets` :1734–1755):** every completed Europe trade propagates a random share of itself into **every other European player's market**. Rules, all faithful to FreeCol: (a) only **storable** goods propagate (`isStorable()` gate at :1736; in the classic spec every good with a `<market>` is storable, so this is belt-and-braces); (b) the propagated amount is the **raw** traded amount — *not* the Dutch `TRADE_BONUS`-halved amount (`sellInEurope`:1323–1327 applies the modifier to its own market but propagates the raw chunk), so a Dutch sale ripples at full size; (c) the trade is processed in **≤100 chunks** (the propagation is called per chunk in `sellInEurope`/`buyInEurope`) and each chunk rolls its own **uniform 5–30%** (26 values, :1740–1744), scaled with **integer truncation toward zero** — so ≤3 units always propagate 0 and e.g. a 50-chunk at roll 15 lands 7; the roll happens *before* the zero cutoff; (d) each chunk's one scaled amount is applied identically to **all** recipients via `addGoodsToMarket` (`Market.java:214–222`): inventory ± amount floored at 100, price recomputed, **no** sales/income counters (no sale happened on the recipient's market); (e) recipients are the **live European** players (`isEuropean()` = Colonial, Rebel, Independent **and the REF** — `Player.java:761–766`) excluding the trader; natives and Retired players never receive (we have no player-death model yet, so "live" is type-based). Sign: a sell propagates **positive** (rivals' supply grows, their prices sag), a buy **negative** (`buyInEurope`:1261). **Documented divergences (FreeCol bugs not copied):** FreeCol's server layer *double*-propagates a human buy (`InGameController.buyGoods`:1050 re-propagates the whole total after `buyInEurope` already propagated per chunk — asymmetric with the sell path) and a custom-house sale (`ServerColony.java:853–855` queues the sale total as an extra trade on top of `sellInEurope`'s per-chunk propagation). We propagate every trade **exactly once**. Col1: **No** (single visible market); FreeCol: **Yes**, always on — ours is always on too.
- **Per-turn market drift (`86d3fpyyq`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csYearlyGoodsAdjust` :1767–1800):** every **European** player's market drifts back toward its ruleset baseline at the start of that player's turn, **every turn** — FreeCol calls it from `csStartTurn`:1813 with no once-a-year gate ("yearly" is the method's historical name, a documented misnomer). Per tick: one **extra type** is drawn uniformly over the goods list; then for each good the player **has traded** (`hasBeenTraded`, :1781–1782), in spec order: direction = **add** if `amountInMarket < initialAmount` else subtract (at *exactly* the initial amount it **subtracts** — FreeCol's `<` at :1783, a faithful quirk, so the drift dithers just under the baseline rather than resting on it); magnitude bound = `turn / 10` (integer division — **zero before turn 10**), or `2 × (turn/10) + 1` for the extra type; bound ≤ 0 skips the good; the adjust is a uniform draw `0..bound−1` (can be 0), negated when subtracting; it lands via `addGoodsToMarket` (inventory + reprice, **no counters**). Each queued adjust then propagates onward to the other European markets with **one** 5–30% roll each (`flushExtraTrades` :366–371 → `propagateToEuropeanMarkets`, unchunked — even a 0 adjust rolls, since the roll precedes the cutoff). **`hasBeenTraded` is derived**, not stored: any non-zero trade counter *or* an inventory off its seed — anchored on FreeCol's own save fallback (`MarketData.java:478`: `traded = sales != 0`) and widened to counter-free movement (propagation/drift set FreeCol's live flag too); divergence: a good that was only ever propagated/drifted and lands back on exactly its seed with zero counters reads untraded again where FreeCol's sticky flag stays true — cosmetic (it just skips ticks until it moves again). **The REF's market does not drift** (our `RunPlayerTurn` REF branch returns before the colonial path) — a documented divergence from FreeCol's `isEuropean()` gate; inconsequential, as the REF never trades or reads prices. Col1: **No** (single static market baseline); FreeCol: **Yes**, always on — ours is always on too.
- **Price-change notices (`86d3fpz0p`, FreeCol `PRICE_CHANGE` + `ServerPlayer.csFlushMarket` → `Market.makePriceChangeMessage`):** every `EndTurn`, the engine compares each of the **human** market's goods' buy (ask) price to a per-turn **baseline** — the prices the player last saw (recorded at the end of the previous turn, FreeCol's `oldPrice`). Each good whose price moved emits a `PriceChangeNotice(goodsId, oldPrice, newPrice, sellPrice)`; the baseline is then reset to today's prices (FreeCol `flushPriceChange`). The compare keys off the **ask** price (FreeCol `MarketData.getCostToBuy`) and carries the current bid alongside for the message. The watched window spans the **whole turn** — the human's own Europe sells/buys made before End Turn *and* any custom-house / trade-route market activity during the turn. **Transient:** the notices are rebuilt each `EndTurn` and **never saved** (`Game.PriceChangeNotices`, like the other per-turn notices). The baseline is seeded at game creation (the ruleset seed prices, so a turn-1 trade is caught) and re-seeded on load to the restored (already-moved) prices, so a reload does **not** re-announce a change the player already saw. Only the human market is watched (foreign powers' markets move silently). RNG-free + deterministic (the notices are emitted in the market's stable goods order); the soak never makes a human-market trade in its window, so it stays byte-identical.

**Deviations from FreeCol:** `SellColonyGoods` still sells straight from a colony warehouse to Europe (a convenience abstraction kept from slice 1); since slice 3 a ship can also carry cargo and sell it in Europe (`SellShipCargo`) and **buy goods** in Europe (`BuyEuropeGoods`) — buying now **moves the market** (the ask rises as it drains, `86d3dkffz`), so a large purchase costs more than a flat ask × amount — and since slice 10 both are on the **Europe screen** (per-ship Sell buttons + a Buy-goods dropdown), see [europe.md](europe.md).

## 3. Technical design

- `GoodsType` gains `IsNewWorldGoods` and a `GoodsMarket?` (InitialAmount/InitialPrice/PriceDifference; null = untradeable), parsed from `<market>`.
- `Market` (engine-free, `Trade/Market.cs`): per-good `{ amountInMarket, bid, ask }`; `BidPrice`/`AskPrice`/`AmountInMarket`; `Sell(goodsId, amount, taxPercent, volumeFactor = 1.0) → SaleResult(beforeTax, afterTax)` chunks by 100 and recomputes per chunk. **`volumeFactor`** scales each chunk's `amountInMarket` delta (`+= round(chunk × volumeFactor)`) — 1.0 normally, 0.5 for the Dutch trade advantage; the default keeps every existing caller byte-identical. **`Buy(goodsId, amount, volumeFactor = 1.0) → int cost`** is the mirror: it chunks by 100, prices each chunk at the current ask, then **subtracts** `round(chunk × volumeFactor)` from `amountInMarket` (floored at `MinimumAmountInMarket = 100`) and recomputes, so the ask climbs across the buy. **`BuyCost(...)`** runs the same loop on a throwaway copy of the datum — a non-mutating price quote for the affordability check (the cost rises across chunks, so a flat ask under-quotes). **`SaleValue(goodsId, amount, taxPercent, volumeFactor = 1.0) → int`** (`86d3f6…`) is the **SELL analog of `BuyCost`**: `Sell` and `SaleValue` now share one per-chunk loop (`ChunkedSell`, mirroring `ChunkedBuy`) — `Sell` runs it on the live datum, `SaleValue` on a throwaway copy and returns only `GoldAfterTax`, so the quote equals the real after-tax credit byte-for-byte (the bid *slides down* across chunks, so a flat `bid × amount` over-quotes a big sell) while moving neither the price nor the trade counters. It backs `Game.CheckSellShipCargo`'s after-tax `Cost` for the Europe sell display (see [europe.md](europe.md)). `Recompute` is a faithful port of `MarketData.price()` (float supply formula, new-world cap, jump clamp, [1,19] bounds). `SaveDeltas`/`LoadDeltas` persist only inventories that moved from their seed. **Trade accounting:** the per-good `Datum` carries `Sales`/`IncomeBeforeTaxes`/`IncomeAfterTaxes`; `Sell` increments all three per chunk (units, pre-tax revenue, post-tax gold) and the shared `ChunkedBuy` loop decrements them (units + cost off all three) — so `Buy` accumulates on the live datum while `BuyCost` accumulates on the throwaway copy it discards. Read via `SalesOf`/`IncomeBeforeTaxesOf`/`IncomeAfterTaxesOf` (0 for an untraded/untradeable good); `Reinitialise` zeroes them; `SaveCounters`/`LoadCounters` persist the sparse `TradeAccount(Sales, IncomeBeforeTaxes, IncomeAfterTaxes)` triple for every traded good. `Game.Market.{SalesOf,IncomeBeforeTaxesOf,IncomeAfterTaxesOf}(id)` is the clean read surface the Trade-report panel (E4) consumes.
- `Game`: `Gold`, `TaxRate`, `Market`; `SellColonyGoods(colony, goodsId, amount)` / `SellShipCargo(...)` deduct stores and credit the treasury, passing **`MarketVolumeFactor(player)`** into `Market.Sell`. The internal `SellColonyGoods(player, colony, goodsId, amount, bool ignoreBoycott = false)` overload skips the `!CanTrade` boycott guard when `ignoreBoycott` is true — the only caller that passes `true` is the custom-house auto-sell (`AutoSellExports`, gated on `Ruleset.GameOptions.CustomIgnoreBoycott`); every manual/UI sale uses the default `false` and keeps the strict refusal. This also removed a latent End-Turn crash where the auto-sell threw on a boycotted custom-house good (see [custom-house](custom-house.md)). `CheckBuyEuropeGoods` quotes `Market.BuyCost(…, MarketVolumeFactor(player))` and `BuyEuropeGoods` calls `Market.Buy(…, MarketVolumeFactor(player))` (debiting the returned cost). `MarketVolumeFactor(player)` folds the player's nation-type `model.modifier.tradeBonus` onto 1.0 via `NationTypeModifiers` (Dutch → 0.5; no nation → 1.0); `tradeBonus` is nation-type-only (no founding father carries it). No RNG, no save change — the factor is read live from the player's nation type. `Game.New` takes optional `startingGold`/`startingTax` (default 0 = classic).
- **Persistence:** save v9 stores `Gold`, `Tax`, and the sparse moved-market map; pre-v9 saves load with 0 gold/tax and a ruleset-seeded market. **Save v56** adds the per-good trade accounting: `SavedPlayer.TradeAccounts` (goods-id → `SavedTradeAccount(Sales, IncomeBeforeTaxes, IncomeAfterTaxes)`), written via `Market.SaveCounters()` and replayed via `LoadCounters` in `Game.BuildPlayer`. **Additive + omit-when-default:** only goods with a non-zero counter are written, so a never-traded market omits the map and a default (un-traded) game round-trips byte-identically to v55; pre-v56 saves load with every counter 0.
- **Market report tab (`86d3dmn6d`):** `ColonyReportPanel`'s **Market** tab is pure presentation (ADR-006) — it enumerates `Game.Ruleset.GoodsTypes.Where(IsTradeable)` and, per good, reads `Game.Market.BidPrice`/`AskPrice` and derives the boycott flag from `Market.Arrears(id) > 0` (equivalently `!CanTrade(id)`). No write members are touched. Sales/income columns (FreeCol `ReportTradePanel`'s `getSales`/`getIncomeBeforeTaxes`/`getIncomeAfterTaxes`) now have their **data** behind them: `86d3e4bdm` (save v56) added the per-good trade accumulator (`Market.SalesOf`/`IncomeBeforeTaxesOf`/`IncomeAfterTaxesOf`). The report **panel** that surfaces those columns is **E4** — this slice only wired the data; the current Market tab still renders the price + boycott subset until E4 lands.
- **Rival-market propagation (`86d3fpyx3`, `Game.MarketDynamics.cs`):** `Market` gains `internal AddGoodsToMarket(goodsId, amount)` — the faithful `Market.java:214–222` port (inventory ± amount floored at `MinimumAmountInMarket = 100`, `Recompute` with the normal jump clamp, counters untouched). `Game.PropagateTradeToRivalMarkets(trader, goodsId, signedRawAmount)` (internal, in the new `Game.MarketDynamics.cs` partial) skips non-storables, splits |amount| into ≤`CargoChunk` chunks and, per chunk, rolls `rng.Next(26) + 5` and applies `chunk × r / 100` (C# int division == Java's truncate-toward-zero) to every recipient from `RivalEuropeanMarketsOf(trader)` (PlayerType ∈ {Colonial, Rebel, Independent, RoyalExpeditionaryForce} − trader). **ADR-009:** all rolls come from a **transient** per-event `Pcg32Random` on the new reserved **stream 105** (`MarketDynamicsStreamId`; 0 = human, 1 = natives, playerId+1 per player, 100 LCR, 101 Monarch, 102 ResourceQuantity, 103 Disaster+Intervention, 104 NativeTrade+Survival), seeded disaster-style from persisted inputs only: the trader's own stream *state* (`SaveState()`, read without advancing — the human's stream 0 is never touched), the turn, the trader id, an **FNV-1a** hash of the goods id (never `string.GetHashCode()`), and the good's **post-trade** `SalesOf`/`IncomeBeforeTaxesOf` counters folded under two different odd multipliers (they disambiguate repeat same-good trades in one turn; two multipliers because a bid-1 good keeps `Sales == IncomeBeforeTaxes` and a shared hash would XOR-cancel). **No save change:** the rival deltas land in the already-persisted per-player `SaveDeltas`/counters/RNG states, so propagated games round-trip byte-identically. **Call sites:** the three trade seams in `Game.cs` (`SellColonyGoods` after the gold credit; `SellShipCargo` after the gold credit; `BuyEuropeGoods` after the cargo load, negated) — the custom house flows through `SellColonyGoods`, matching FreeCol's `ServerColony:851` route.
- **Per-turn drift (`86d3fpyyq`, `Game.MarketDynamics.cs`):** `Market` gains `internal InitialAmountOf(goodsId)` (the spec seed) and `internal HasBeenTraded(goodsId)` (derived: `Sales != 0 || IncomeBeforeTaxes != 0 || IncomeAfterTaxes != 0 || AmountInMarket != InitialAmount` — no new save field). `Game.RunYearlyMarketAdjust(player)` (internal) mirrors `csYearlyGoodsAdjust` exactly — extra-type draw over `Market.TradeableGoods` (in the classic spec **identical** to FreeCol's storable-goods pool: every `<market>` good is storable and vice versa; a ruleset with storable-but-marketless goods would shrink the draw pool — documented), per-traded-good bound/draw/direction as §2, `AddGoodsToMarket`, then one 5–30% roll per queued adjust onto `RivalEuropeanMarketsOf(player)` (shared `PropagateToMarkets` core). **ADR-009:** one transient `Pcg32Random` per player per turn on the shared **stream 105**, disaster-seeded (`playerStreamState ^ Turn<<1 ^ playerId<<32`) — read-only against the player's persisted stream, so nothing advances and a reload replays the identical tick. **Seam (integrator-wired):** `RunPlayerTurn` immediately after `BombardEnemyShips(player)` — FreeCol's `csStartTurn` order (bombard → yearly adjust → fathers); runs for Colonial/Rebel/Independent, not the REF (early return) and not natives.
- **Price-change notices (`86d3fpz0p`, `Game.PriceNotices.cs` + `PriceChangeNotice`):** `Game` holds a transient `Dictionary<string,int> _priceSnapshot` (the per-good ask baseline) and a transient `List<PriceChangeNotice> _priceChangeNotices` (exposed read-only as `Game.PriceChangeNotices`). `SeedPriceBaseline()` fills the snapshot from the human `Market.AskPrice` for every `TradeableGoods` — called at the end of `Game.New` (ruleset seed prices) and `Game.Restore` (restored prices). Each `EndTurn`: the notice list is cleared at the top; `DetectMarketPriceChanges()` near the bottom walks `Market.TradeableGoods` (stable order), and for each good whose `AskPrice` differs from its snapshot emits `PriceChangeNotice(goodsId, oldAsk, newAsk, BidPrice)`, then writes the live ask back into the snapshot (re-baselining for the next turn — FreeCol `flushPriceChange`). Neither method draws RNG (it only reads/copies prices), so the human's stream 0 is untouched. **No save field:** the snapshot is rebuilt from the live (persisted) market on load and the notice list is per-turn scratch, so save v66 adds nothing for this feature. Presentation (`GameController.FormatPriceChangeNotice` + a `.Concat(...)` line tagged `MessageCategory.Economy`) renders each notice as a turn-message row — no panel edit (the generic `TurnMessagePanel`/`MessageLogPanel` consume it).
