## 1. How it works (plain English)

A **custom house** is a colony building that quietly sells your surplus to Europe **for you, every turn — even when your ports are blockaded or you've declared independence**. Once a colony has built one, you can mark individual goods as **"export this"** and set how much of each you want to **keep in the warehouse** before the rest is shipped off. The custom house then drip-sells anything above that line at the going market price (minus tax), so you stop babysitting galleons just to offload spare sugar or ore.

You control all this **from the colony screen**: a colony with a custom house grows an **"Custom house — exports"** section listing every good you can trade in Europe, each with a **tick-box** ("export this good?") and a **keep-amount** box. Tick a good and it auto-sells its surplus over the keep-amount from then on; untick it and the custom house leaves it in the warehouse. The section only appears once the colony actually has a custom house — a colony without one shows no export controls (there'd be nothing to control). **This screen is the only way to switch a good on for export** — and because nothing exports until you tick it (the default is "export nothing"), it's what makes a freshly-built custom house start earning.

The custom house now *grants* a colony the export ability, each colony remembers **which goods are flagged for export and the keep-level for each** (default: keep 50), and the game has an **auto-export mode** that's **off by default** ("per-good" — only goods you explicitly flag). Each turn — after the colony has produced, **eaten and grown** — the custom house **sells every flagged good's surplus over its keep-level to Europe** at the market price (after tax, nudging the price down just like a manual sale) — no ship required. (Selling happens last so that even if you flag *food* for export, this turn's new colonist is still born first.) A colony without a custom house, and a game with no export toggles, plays exactly as before (nothing ships).

There's also a global mode for players who don't want to flip switches colony-by-colony: **"export everything over the keep-level"**, which auto-exports every sellable good above its line in every colony that has a custom house — **except food**, which it always leaves alone so colonies keep growing. The default stays **per-good** so nothing sells unless you ask.

Because the custom house sells quietly behind the scenes, the game now keeps a **note of every sale your custom houses make each turn** — which colony sold what, how much, and the gold it earned — so after you hit **End Turn** the game can tell you ("your custom house in Jamestown sold 40 sugar for 132 gold") instead of leaving the income a mystery. These notes are just for that turn's summary: they're rebuilt fresh each turn and never saved. Only **your** colonies' sales are noted — rival powers' custom houses still sell, you just don't get a report on them.

You can also set a **"max" level per good** — a ceiling that says *"don't let automatic deliveries pile this good up past here."* This is the **import level**. It doesn't change selling at all; it caps the *other* direction — the goods a **trade route** drops off into the colony. If you run a trade route that hauls, say, tools into a frontier colony, setting tools' max to 100 means the route's wagon stops unloading once the warehouse holds 100 tools (it keeps the rest aboard to carry on to the next stop), instead of dumping its whole load and spilling the overflow. Leave the box at its top value (the warehouse's capacity) and there's no extra limit — deliveries fill the warehouse exactly as they always did, so by default nothing changes. *(In the original game this "import level" lives on the custom house alongside the export settings; we keep it in the same place — the colony screen's custom-house section — but because our custom house only ever **sells** (it has no automatic **buy-from-Europe**), the cap currently bites on the one automatic-delivery path we do have: trade-route drop-offs. If an automatic-buy custom house is ever added, it reads the very same level.)*

The custom house is also a **smuggler**. If the king **boycotts** a good (for example after a Boston Tea Party), you normally can't sell it in Europe until you pay off the back-taxes — but in classic Colonization the custom house **ignores the boycott and sells it anyway**. The sale is otherwise completely ordinary: the usual tax is still withheld and the price still drifts down, and the boycott itself stays in place (smuggling doesn't lift it). This is on by default (the "custom house ignores boycotts" rule); a variant can turn it off, and then a boycotted good is simply **left in the warehouse** until you lift the boycott — the custom house quietly skips it rather than choking on it.

There's a **second** way a custom house can sell a boycotted good even when that "ignore boycotts" rule is **off**: if you've elected the founding father **Jan de Witt** and you're **at peace with at least one rival European power**, your custom houses may sell boycotted goods to those foreign markets. So a de Witt custom house keeps moving your goods to neighbours you're on good terms with, regardless of the king's boycott — but only while that peace holds. If you're at war with everyone (or haven't met anyone), de Witt grants nothing here, and a boycotted good waits in the warehouse as usual.

**Where you can build a custom house.** In classic Colonization you can raise a custom house **anywhere** — even a landlocked inland colony can smuggle goods to market (the only requirement is having elected Peter Stuyvesant). There's an **optional realism rule** — "custom houses only on the coast" — that, when switched on at New Game, says a custom house can only be built in a colony that touches the sea (a real port), exactly like docks. With the rule **on**, trying to build a custom house in an inland colony is refused ("A customHouse can only be built in a coastal colony"), and it simply doesn't appear in that colony's build list. With the rule **off** (the default), nothing changes — inland colonies build custom houses as they always did. This is a setup choice, not something that changes mid-game.

## 2. Detailed rules

- The **custom house** (`model.building.customHouse`) carries the **export ability** (`model.ability.export`). A colony "has export" when it contains **any** building whose type grants that ability (today only the custom house). No other building grants it. The custom house has **no work slots** (`workplaces` 0) — it's infrastructure, not a workshop.
- Each colony holds a set of **per-good export/import settings**, each a triple: **exported** (bool) + **export level** (the warehouse amount to keep before selling the rest) + **import level** (the ceiling an automatic delivery will not stock the good past). The **default** for any good not explicitly set is **not exported, keep 50** (`Colony.DefaultExportLevel`), **import level unset** (`Colony.ImportLevelUnset` = −1). Setting a good back to exactly that all-default state **forgets** it (keeps saves clean) — so a good with *only* an import level is still remembered.
- The **import level** (FreeCol `ExportData.importLevel`) caps **automatic delivery**: an automatic drop-off (today, a **trade-route** stop unloading at this colony) delivers a good only up to `effective import level − amount already here`, leaving the surplus on the carrier. The **effective import level** (FreeCol `getEffectiveImportLevel(capacity)`) is the good's set level, or — when **unset** (−1) — the colony's **warehouse capacity**; so an untouched good is bounded only by the warehouse, exactly as before this concept existed. A negative set value normalises to unset. *(We model the cap on trade-route delivery, the only auto-delivery path in the engine; the custom house itself has no auto-**buy**, so there is nothing else to cap yet — see Deviations.)*
- Export can be flagged for any good that is **storable and tradeable** (has a European market) — **food included** (FreeCol's custom house can export food; it just defaults *off*). You can't export hammers/bells/crosses (not goods you trade in Europe). Flagging anything non-tradeable is refused. *(The bulk "export everything over the keep-level" mode below deliberately leaves food alone — auto-dumping food would stop colonies growing — so food only ever auto-sells if you turn it on for that good by hand.)*
- The game carries a single **auto-export mode**:
  - **PerGood** (default) — only goods individually flagged in a colony are eligible (FreeCol behaviour; food is eligible if you flag it).
  - **ExportAllOverLevel** — every sellable good above its keep-level in any custom-house colony is eligible, regardless of the per-good flag, **except food** (protected, so the colony keeps growing).
- **Per-turn auto-sell.** During each colony's turn — after production, the warehouse-overflow spill, **and the eat/grow step** (FreeCol's customs sale runs **last** in `csNewTurn`, after food consumption and birth) — a colony **with a custom house** ships each eligible good's **surplus above its keep-level** to its owner's European market. The sale is the ordinary market sale: the owner is credited the after-tax proceeds and the good's price ticks down. Eligibility is per the mode (PerGood: flagged goods; ExportAllOverLevel: every sellable good but food). Running **last** matters when food is flagged for export in PerGood mode: the colony eats and grows first, so an exported-food setting can never starve this turn's growth. A colony **without** a custom house ships nothing; with the default PerGood mode and no toggles, nothing sells — so the save bytes and a normal game are unchanged.
- **Boycott smuggling (`model.option.customIgnoreBoycott`, classic default `true`, OR Jan de Witt's foreign-trade ability).** A good under boycott (non-zero market arrears — see [market](market.md)) is normally un-sellable. The custom-house auto-sell consults FreeCol's `Player.canTrade(type, Market.Access.CUSTOM_HOUSE)` gate, which sells a boycotted good when **either** of two conditions holds: (a) the `customIgnoreBoycott` option is on (classic), or (b) the owner holds Jan de Witt's `customHouseTradesWithForeignCountries` ability **and** is at peace/alliance with at least one rival European power (see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)). When **either** allows it the boycotted good is still sold — tax is withheld and the price still moves, with **no extra smuggling penalty** (classic), and the boycott (arrears) is left intact. **When neither holds**, a boycotted good is **skipped** — the auto-sell never enters the sell path for it, so End Turn never errors and the good simply waits in the warehouse until the player lifts the boycott. (A manual `SellColonyGoods` from the UI keeps the strict gate — only the custom-house path smuggles, matching FreeCol's `Market.Access` split.)

- **Coastal-build gate (`model.option.customsOnCoast`, classic default `false`).** The custom house type declares the `model.ability.coastalOnly` ability (classic value **false**). FreeCol's `Specification.clean` flips that value **true** when `customsOnCoast` is set, and `Colony.getNoBuildReason` then returns **COASTAL** for a building with `hasAbility(COASTAL_ONLY)` in a non-coastland colony. We model the same **effective** value: a building "requires coast" when it either declares `coastalOnly=true` directly (a variant may) **or** declares the ability at all while the `customsOnCoast` option is on. The build gate (`BuildRefusal`, after the `required-ability` gate) refuses such a building in a colony that is not sea-connected (`IsColonyCoastal`, the same port test docks use — an inland lake does **not** count), with the message "A {shortName} can only be built in a coastal colony." Classic data declares the custom house's `coastalOnly=false` and the option defaults off, so no classic building is coastal-gated and an inland custom house builds as before (ADR-009). The gate is **data-driven**: the Australia variant inherits it for free (set the ability true, or turn the option on).

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** FreeCol stores the export level, a per-good "exported" flag **and a per-good import level** in `ExportData`, plus high/low water marks; we model the export pair **and the import level** and defer the high/low-water niceties. **Import level — where the cap bites:** FreeCol's `getImportAmount` (which consults `getEffectiveImportLevel`) is used by **trade-route delivery** to cap how much a stop drops off, and FreeCol gates that consult behind the `ENHANCED_TRADE_ROUTES` game option (classic default off). We **always** apply the cap on trade-route delivery rather than gating it on that option, because the import level is **opt-in per good** (unset ⇒ the cap is the warehouse capacity ⇒ no effect), so a default game is unaffected without needing the option gate — and honouring an explicitly-set "don't deliver above N" regardless of an unrelated cargo-sorting option is the player-meaningful behaviour. FreeCol additionally consults the import level in an **automatic-buy** custom house (delivering bought goods up to the level); our custom house has **no auto-buy**, so that path does not exist here — the level is modelled, persisted and exposed, and applied at the one auto-delivery point we do have (trade routes). An auto-buy custom house, if added, reads the same level. **Boycotts:** FreeCol's custom house **ignores boycotts** when `customIgnoreBoycott` is on (the classic default) — modelled faithfully (smuggle on, skip off); classic applies no smuggling penalty, so we add none. FreeCol **also** lets a custom house with the `customHouseTradesWithForeignCountries` ability (granted by Jan de Witt) sell boycotted goods to a foreign power at peace even with the option off — **now modelled**: `AutoSellExports` consults `CustomHouseTradesWithForeignCountries(owner)` (the de Witt oracle, which already encodes "ability elected AND at peace/alliance with ≥1 rival European power") alongside the option. (No classic *nation* grants the ability, so a de-Witt-less default game is unchanged; the ability is reached only once de Witt is elected.) The **auto-export mode** ("export all over level") is our own convenience switch over FreeCol's per-good model — it defaults off so it changes nothing until used; in that bulk mode we **exclude food** (FreeCol has no such mode, and auto-dumping food would halt colony growth). **Food per-good:** FreeCol's custom house *can* export food (food is storable and has a market; the only gate is the export ability + boycotts), and our PerGood mode matches that — food is exportable if explicitly flagged, default off. The original 1994 game never auto-sold food; that behaviour is what our ExportAllOverLevel mode's food exclusion preserves. *(Flagged for Chris: PerGood follows FreeCol here, not the 1994 original — see the session's needs-you note.)*

## 3. Technical design

- **Parse:** `BuildingType.GrantsExport` (`model.ability.export`, parsed in `Ruleset` alongside `BombardsShips`). The custom house inherits nothing relevant from a parent chain, so the ability sits directly on its type.
- **State (colony):** `Colony.ExportSetting(bool Exported, int ExportLevel, int ImportLevel = ImportLevelUnset)` (a `record struct`; `ImportLevelUnset` = −1, FreeCol's `importLevel = -1`); a private `Dictionary<string, ExportSetting> _exports` exposed read-only as `Exports`; `ExportOf(goodsId)` returns the stored triple or `new(false, DefaultExportLevel, ImportLevelUnset)`. Internal `SetExport(goodsId, exported, level)` writes the export pair **preserving** the current import level; internal `SetImport(goodsId, level)` writes the import level **preserving** the export pair (a negative value normalises to `ImportLevelUnset`). Both delegate to a private `Store(...)` that floors the export level at 0 and **removes** the entry when it is fully default (`!exported && exportLevel == 50 && importLevel == -1`), so the dictionary only ever holds non-default goods — the invariant that keeps a default game byte-stable.
- **State (game):** `AutoExportMode` enum (`PerGood = 0`, `ExportAllOverLevel = 1`); `Game.AutoExportMode` (default `PerGood`) + `SetAutoExportMode`. `ColonyHasExportAbility(colony)` = `colony.Buildings.Any(b => Ruleset.Building(b).GrantsExport)`. `SetColonyExport(colony, goodsId, exported, exportLevel?)` validates the good is storable+tradeable (food allowed — FreeCol-faithful; else `InvalidMoveException`) and folds to `Colony.SetExport` (default level when `exportLevel` is null). `SetColonyImport(colony, goodsId, importLevel)` applies the same storable+tradeable gate and folds to `Colony.SetImport`. `EffectiveImportLevel(colony, goodsId)` (public oracle, FreeCol `getEffectiveImportLevel`) returns the good's set level, or — when unset — `WarehouseCapacity(colony)`.
- **Import cap on auto-delivery (`ServeTradeRouteStop`):** the trade-route stop-service (see [trade-routes](trade-routes.md)) now delivers each non-loaded good only up to `EffectiveImportLevel(colony, goodsId) − colony.StoreOf(goodsId)` (FreeCol `getImportAmount = max(0, level − present)`); any surplus the cap forbids is **left aboard** the carrier (FreeCol leaves un-deliverable cargo on the carrier). A good with an unset level caps at the warehouse capacity, i.e. delivers its whole load as before. Always applied (not gated on `ENHANCED_TRADE_ROUTES`) — the per-good opt-in self-gates it (see Deviations).
- **Export-control UI (ADR-006; `86d3f62q8`):** `ColonyPanel.CustomHouseArea()` renders the per-good controls, and `Rebuild` only adds the section when `_game.ColonyHasCustomHouse(_colony)` (a public oracle wrapping the private `ColonyHasExportAbility`). The section lists `ExportableGoods()` — `Ruleset.GoodsTypes` filtered to storable + tradeable, ordinal-ordered, mirroring the engine's `SetColonyExport` gate — each as a row of: a goods icon, an `Export_{short}` `CheckBox` (`ButtonPressed = ExportOf(goodsId).Exported`), and a `Retain_{short}` `SpinBox` (`Value = ExportOf(goodsId).ExportLevel`, `MaxValue = _game.ColonyWarehouseCapacity(_colony)`, step `CargoLot` 100). Each row also carries a **"max" `SpinBox`** (`Import_{short}`) for the import level: `MaxValue = ColonyWarehouseCapacity`, `Value = Game.EffectiveImportLevel(colony, goodsId)` (so an untouched good reads as the warehouse capacity = "no cap"). The `Toggled`/`ValueChanged` handlers forward to the host's `Action<Colony, string, bool, int> _setExport`, **each preserving the other dimension** — toggling passes the row's current level, changing the level passes the row's current flag — so neither resets the other. `GameController.SetColonyExport(colony, goodsId, exported, retainLevel)` is the thin command (wired via `OpenColonyPanel`'s new delegate arg): it calls `Game.SetColonyExport`, catches `InvalidMoveException` into the status notice (never thrown to the UI), sets a friendly notice, and `RefreshView()`s. The **import** "max" `SpinBox` forwards to a panel-side `SetImportLevel(goodsId, value, capacity)` that calls `Game.SetColonyImport` directly (like the other `_game` reads the panel already makes) and `_onChange()`s; a value **at capacity** is normalised to `ImportLevelUnset` ("at capacity ⇒ no extra cap"), so leaving an untouched control at its max persists nothing. `Game.ColonyWarehouseCapacity(colony)` is a public oracle wrapping the internal `WarehouseCapacity` so both sliders cap where the level stops being meaningful. No engine rule lives in the panel — it reads `ExportOf`/`EffectiveImportLevel` and forwards.
- **Save (v28 export, v67 import; additive):** `SavedColony.Exports` is `IReadOnlyDictionary<string, SavedExport(bool Exported, int Level, int? ImportLevel)>`, **omitted when empty**; the top-level `AutoExportMode?` is **omitted when PerGood**. The per-good **import level** rides as a third `SavedExport` field (v67): `ImportLevel` is `int?` and the save maps `ImportLevelUnset` (−1) → `null` so the serializer's `WhenWritingNull` **omits** it — an export-only row therefore serializes byte-identically to v66, and a good with only an import level is still written (it is non-default, so it's in the `Exports` map). A game with no toggles and the default mode serializes **byte-identically to v66** (the whole `Exports` token is still omitted); pre-v28 saves load with no exports and PerGood; pre-v67 saves (whose rows lacked the field, i.e. `null`) load with every good's import level **unset**. `Restore` folds each saved export via `colony.SetExport(...)` then, when `ImportLevel` is present, `colony.SetImport(...)`.
- **Auto-sell:** `AutoSellExports(owner, colony)` runs at the **end** of `RunColonyTurn` — after production, `SpillWarehouseOverflow`, and the eat/grow step (mirrors FreeCol `ServerColony.csNewTurn`, where the customs sale runs **last**, after the food-consumption/birth step; an earlier placement would let exported food rob this turn's growth). It early-returns unless `ColonyHasExportAbility(colony)`. Then, over `colony.Stores.Keys` in **ordinal id order** (determinism, ADR-009), it skips non-storable / non-market goods, computes eligibility (`ExportAllOverLevel ? !goods.IsFood : ExportOf(goodsId).Exported`), and sells `StoreOf − ExportLevel` (when positive) via the existing `SellColonyGoods(owner, colony, …, ignoreBoycott)` — so it credits the owner's market after tax and moves the price exactly like a manual sale.
- **Boycott gate (`customIgnoreBoycott` OR de Witt):** a **player-level** allow flag is computed **once above the per-good loop**: `bool mayTradeBoycotted = Ruleset.GameOptions.CustomIgnoreBoycott || CustomHouseTradesWithForeignCountries(owner.PlayerId);` (the de Witt oracle, `Game.cs` ~11057, already encodes "ability elected AND at peace/alliance with ≥1 rival European power"). Before selling each eligible good the loop applies FreeCol's `Player.canTrade(type, Market.Access.CUSTOM_HOUSE)`: `if (!mayTradeBoycotted && !owner.Market.CanTrade(goodsId)) continue;` — so when **neither** condition allows it a boycotted good is skipped entirely (the sell path is never entered). When **either** allows it the good falls through and is sold with `SellColonyGoods(owner, colony, goodsId, surplus, ignoreBoycott: mayTradeBoycotted)`. `SellColonyGoods`'s internal overload has a `bool ignoreBoycott = false` parameter that, when true, skips its `!Market.CanTrade` guard (the manual `public` sale path passes the default `false`, keeping its strict boycott refusal). **This also fixed the latent End-Turn crash:** the auto-sell once called `SellColonyGoods` unconditionally on every eligible surplus, so a boycotted custom-house good threw `InvalidMoveException` during `EndTurn`; now the boycott is always handled (smuggle or skip) before the sell, so `EndTurn` never throws on a boycotted good. The option is parsed in `Ruleset.ParseGameOptions` (`model.option.customIgnoreBoycott`, classic `true`) onto `GameOptions.CustomIgnoreBoycott`, with a `Ruleset.WithCustomIgnoreBoycott(bool)` override seam mirroring `WithFogOfWar`. **Session-only — not persisted** (no save bump; a reload re-derives it from the spec default), like fog-of-war and the victory conditions.
- **Coastal-build gate (`customsOnCoast`):** the option is parsed in `Ruleset.ParseGameOptions` (`model.option.customsOnCoast`, classic **false**) onto `GameOptions.CustomsOnCoast`, with a `Ruleset.WithCustomsOnCoast(bool)` override seam mirroring `WithCustomIgnoreBoycott` (session-only — **no save bump**; a reload re-derives it from the spec default). `Game.BuildRefusal` adds a COASTAL check after `RequiredAbilityRefusal`: `if (!target.IsUnit && BuildingRequiresCoast(target.Id) && !IsColonyCoastal(colony)) return "A {shortName} can only be built in a coastal colony.";`. `BuildingRequiresCoast(buildingId)` looks the building up (`Ruleset.FindBuilding`) and returns its **effective** `coastalOnly`: `building.HasAbility(CoastalOnlyAbility)` (variant data set it true) **or** (`Ruleset.GameOptions.CustomsOnCoast && building.Abilities.ContainsKey(CoastalOnlyAbility)`) — i.e. the option flips the classic-declared `coastalOnly=false` on. The custom house's `coastalOnly` ability lands in `BuildingType.Abilities` via `CollectAbilitiesUpChain` (the declaration is kept even at value false, so `ContainsKey` detects it). No record mutation — the gate reads the option live, so `WithCustomsOnCoast(true)` on the just-loaded ruleset takes effect with no rebuild of the building dictionary.
- **Sale notice (transient):** `CustomHouseSaleNotice(int ColonyId, string ColonyName, string GoodsId, int Amount, int Gold)` (a `record struct` in `GameSession`, mirroring `Combat`'s `ColonyLossNotice`/`ColonyRaidNotice`). `AutoSellExports` records one per good sold from a **human-owned** colony (`IsHumanOwned(colony)` gate — foreign powers still sell, silently), capturing the surplus shipped and `SellColonyGoods`'s after-tax return as `Gold`. Held in a private `List<CustomHouseSaleNotice> _customHouseSaleNotices` beside the other transient notice lists, exposed read-only as `Game.CustomHouseSaleNotices`, and `.Clear()`-ed at the top of `EndTurn` alongside the combat/loss/raid/gift lists. **Transient — never serialized** (no save bump; `CurrentVersion` unchanged), like the other notice lists. The presentation layer formats the English message (ADR-006); the notice carries only raw ids/amounts.
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** no RNG anywhere in this feature; the L5 soak is byte-stable because no soak colony has a custom house (it needs Peter Stuyvesant's `buildCustomHouse`, which the AI never gets), so `AutoSellExports` early-returns every turn — and even with one built, the default PerGood mode with no toggles sells nothing. The boycott gate adds no new state and is the classic default (on), so the default game's evolution is unchanged — it only removes a crash path. The **import cap** is a pure `min` against the effective level on the trade-route delivery path, draws no RNG, and is **inert until a player sets a level** (unset ⇒ capped at warehouse capacity ⇒ no change); the soak runs no import-capped trade routes, so it stays byte-stable and twin-deterministic. The save tokens are omitted at defaults; the boycott option is session-only (no save bump). The **coastal-build gate** draws no RNG (a pure ability/coast check in the build path) and is inert in classic (option off + `coastalOnly=false` ⇒ `BuildingRequiresCoast` returns false for every classic building), so the default game's build behaviour and the soak are byte-stable; the option is session-only (no save bump).
