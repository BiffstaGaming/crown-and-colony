## 1. How it works (plain English)

There's one explorer on the map. Click it to select (gold ring), click a neighbouring tile to walk there. Rough ground (forests, hills) uses up the turn faster than open plains; water is off-limits. When the unit can't move any further, end the turn and it's refreshed.

You can also give a unit a **standing order** instead of moving it. **Fortify** a unit and it spends a turn digging in; from then on it defends **half again as hard** (+50%) — invaluable for a soldier guarding a colony or a chokepoint. **Sentry** rests a unit until something happens (so it stops asking for orders). **Clear orders** wakes a fortified or resting unit, and **disband** removes a unit you no longer want for good. You can also **clear a specialist's speciality** — turn an expert (say an expert farmer you've got too many of) back into a plain free colonist, so it can be retrained or put to general work. Moving a fortified unit wakes it — you trade the defensive bonus for the march.

**Go to.** You can point a unit at a far-off tile and tell it to **go there** ("goto"). It finds the cheapest route through ground it has already explored and walks itself toward the destination a bit each turn, all on its own, until it arrives — then the order clears. If something blocks the way (an enemy steps onto the route, or it simply runs out of road it can see) it waits and tries again next turn; moving it by hand cancels the trip. While a unit is travelling on a goto it no longer stops to ask you for orders, so the game **cycles** straight to the next unit that still needs your attention — and tells you when none are left and the turn can end.

You can goto more than just a patch of empty ground. Point a unit at **a settlement** — one of your own colonies, a native village, or an enemy colony — and it walks right up to it: into your own colony, or to a tile **right beside** a village/enemy town it can't simply stroll into (from there you can trade, scout, or attack). Point a **ship** across **open sea** and it sails the water route to that tile. And point a ship at the **edge of the map (the high seas)** with a "go to Europe" order and it sails out to the high seas on its own and then **sets off across the ocean to Europe** — you don't have to nurse it to the edge by hand.

**Wasting away in the wilderness (attrition).** Some units can't survive long out in the open. If you leave one of them standing on an empty patch of map, turn after turn, with no colony or village to shelter in, it slowly **wastes away** — and after too many turns it's simply gone, and you're told it was lost. The clock resets the moment it reaches a settlement (or sails off to Europe), so the rule of thumb is: keep these units inside a colony or moving toward one with a purpose, never parked alone in the bush. In the standard game this only ever affects the **Indian Convert** (the native who joins your colony) — a convert can survive **8 turns** in the open, but a ninth finishes it. Every other unit — colonists, soldiers, ships, wagons — is immune and can sit in the wilderness forever.

**Where a unit comes from, and naming it.** Every colonist, soldier, and other *person* quietly remembers **where it came from** — the nation that first raised it (its "nationality") and the look of the people it was born among (its "ethnicity"). Normally these just match whoever owns it, but they stick to the unit even if it later changes hands: a colonist **captured** in battle keeps the colours of the side that raised it, and a native who joins your colony can keep a native look. Ships and wagons aren't people, so they carry none of this. You can also **christen an individual unit** — give a favourite ship or veteran its own name — and it keeps that name for the rest of the game (clear the name and it goes back to its plain type name). None of this changes how a unit fights or moves; it's flavour and book-keeping that the game remembers for you.

**Worked example:** the unit has 3 movement points. Stepping onto plains costs 3 — one move per turn. Stepping onto a forest costs 6, which the unit doesn't have… but a unit with any movement left may always make one move, so it steps in and is left with 0.

**Stepping into the unknown ends the turn.** A land unit that walks onto a tile it has **never seen before** (still black for its side) uses up the rest of its turn there, however much movement it had left — exploring the wilderness is slow going. A scout with four moves who steps off the edge of the known world stops on that first new tile and waits until next turn. Stepping onto a tile you've *already* explored is normal and costs only the usual terrain move, so a fast unit can keep going across familiar ground. (This is why a "go to" order never plans a route through the unknown — it can't see what's there — so any move into the black is one you make by hand, and that one step ends the turn.) Ships are exempt: scouting the sea doesn't halt a ship.

## 2. Detailed rules

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Target off-map | rejected |
| Target not one of the 8 neighbours | rejected (one step at a time) |
| Land unit → water, or naval unit → land | rejected |
| Target holds an enemy unit (e.g. a native brave) | rejected — *attack* it instead (see [combat](combat.md)) |
| Target holds a native settlement | rejected — attack / trade / speak from beside it |
| Target holds a colony you don't own (a colonial unit with a differing `OwnerId`, or any native brave) | rejected as a *move* — instead assault it from beside it: a colonial land unit can **capture** an ungarrisoned rival colony (`AttackColony`), a brave can **pillage** an undefended one (`PillageColony`) — see [combat](combat.md) |
| 0 movement points left | rejected |
| cost ≤ movement left | allowed; pay the terrain's move cost |
| cost > movement left | allowed **for all remaining points** only if near-full movement (`left+2 ≥ max`, where `max` = the unit's full turn movement *including its role bonus*) or small shortfall (`cost ≤ left+2`) or target is a settlement; otherwise rejected |
| **Land** unit steps onto a tile **unexplored for its owner** | allowed; the move resolves at its normal terrain cost, then **all remaining movement is spent** (`MovementLeft → 0`) — entering the unknown ends the turn (`86d3fpxm5`). A naval unit is exempt; an already-explored target is the normal case above. |

- Unit capabilities come from the ruleset (`UnitType`): free colonist 3 MP land, caravel 12 MP naval, etc.
- **Initial (per-turn) movement = unit-type base + role movement bonus** (FreeCol `Unit.getInitialMovesLeft` folding `model.modifier.movementBonus`): a **mounted** role adds **+9** (one "move" is 3 points, so +3 tiles) — dragoon, scout, cavalry, mounted/native-dragoon braves; the **missionary** role adds **+3**; foot roles (soldier/infantry/pioneer) and the unarmed default add nothing. The bonus is applied at each turn's movement reset (`Game.InitialMovement`); a freshly-equipped mount gets the bonus from its next turn (equipping doesn't refund moves mid-turn). **Ferdinand Magellan** adds **+3** to a naval unit's initial movement (`InitialMovement` folds the owner's Congress `model.modifier.movementBonus`, naval-gated = his scope; among fathers only Magellan carries it). *The per-nation-type naval +3 is a separate scoped modifier, still deferred (no classic power uses the naval nation type).*
- **Ownership & equipment** (Phase 5 slice 5b): a unit carries an `OwnerNationId` (null = the human player; a native nation id = a brave) and a military **role** (`RoleId`/`RoleCount` — unarmed by default; soldier/dragoon when equipped). `Game.PlayerUnits`/`NativeUnits` partition the unit list by owner. A unit cannot move onto a tile held by an enemy — combat is a separate action ([combat](combat.md)). Native units never lift the player's fog and can't be selected.
- **Standing orders** (`86d3c9pfh`, FreeCol `Unit.UnitState`): a unit carries an `Orders` state — **Active** (default), **Fortifying**, **Fortified**, or **Sentry**.

  | Order | Check | Effect |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Fortify** (`CheckFortify`/`Fortify`) | on-map land unit, not already (forti)fied | sets *Fortifying* and spends the turn (0 moves); at the next turn reset it ages to *Fortified*, a **+50% defence** bonus in field combat (FreeCol `model.modifier.fortified` — see [combat](combat.md)). Ships can't fortify (they sentry). |
  | **Sentry** (`CheckSentry`/`Sentry`) | any on-map unit | rests the unit (0 moves); a sentry unit is skipped when cycling for orders, **but auto-wakes when an enemy unit moves adjacent** (`ActivateSentries`, FreeCol `csActivateSentries`) — so a sentry genuinely guards the ground. |
  | **Clear orders** (`ClearOrders`) | any unit | back to *Active* (no movement refund). |
  | **Disband** (`CheckDisband`/`Disband`) | any unit, except a carrier still holding passengers | removes the unit from the game for good (its hold, if any, is lost). |
  | **Clear speciality** (`CheckClearSkill`/`ClearSkill`) | an on-map colonial **specialist** (a unit with a `model.unitChange.clearSkill` change — every expert/master/preacher, not a free colonist/servant/criminal/non-person) | reverts the unit to a **free colonist** (FreeCol `clearSpeciality`), e.g. to turn a surplus expert into a general worker. RNG-free, no save change (a type swap). FreeCol forbids clearing a teacher's skill; our teachers are in-colony building workers (never on-map), so the on-map gate covers that. |

  **Moving wakes a unit** — `MoveUnit` resets a fortified/sentry unit to *Active*, so you trade the dig-in bonus for the move (FreeCol clears the state on a move). It also **wakes any sentried *enemy* unit adjacent to its destination** (`ActivateSentries`, FreeCol `csActivateSentries`, `86d3fpx2h`): the instant a hostile unit steps next to a sentry, that sentry returns to *Active* (so it is no longer skipped and the player is prompted to react). Only enemies trigger it — a player's own/allied units never wake each other's sentries. RNG-free (an order flip only). The order persists across save/load (v23, omitted for an *Active* unit so a no-orders game stays byte-identical).

  **Skip this turn** (`86d3f0vuy`, FreeCol `SkipUnitAction`, **Space**): a *presentation-side* convenience, distinct from the persisted `Orders` above. The selected unit's id is added to a **session-only skip set** (`GameController._skippedThisTurn`, never serialized — ADR-009); the unit keeps its movement and Active order but is **passed over by the W unit-cycle** until the set clears at turn rollover. The cycle oracle `NextUnitToMove(player, skip)` takes the set as an optional argument so the "needs orders" predicate stays authoritative in `GameLogic`. See [hud-input](hud-input.md).

  **Input surfaces for these orders:** the selected-unit panel's Orders row carries a **Skip** button (Space) and a **Disband** button (D), and **D** opens a `ConfirmationDialog` before disbanding (it's irreversible); Disband is gated on `CheckDisband` exactly like the other order buttons. Full keyboard/mouse reference: [hud-input](hud-input.md).

  **Stepping onto a Lost City Rumour explores it** — after a `MoveUnit` *or* `Disembark` of a colonial land unit lands on a rumour tile, `Game` investigates it (see [lost-city-rumours](lost-city-rumours.md)). The outcome can **consume** the unit (an expedition vanishes) or **replace its type** (a learned skill), so a caller must treat the moved unit as possibly gone/transformed after the call returns.

- **Goto / multi-turn move orders** (`86d3c9pfy`, FreeCol `Unit.setDestination` + `Map.searchMap`): a unit carries an optional `Destination` (a map tile). It is set via the oracle/mutator pair `CheckSetDestination`/`SetDestination` (rejected when off-map, the unit's own tile, water-for-a-land-unit/land-for-a-ship *except a settlement target*, an unexplored target, or no route exists) and cleared by `ClearDestination`, by **arrival**, or by **any manual `MoveUnit`** (FreeCol `setDestination(null)`).

  **Destination kinds** (`86d3drn06`, FreeCol `setDestination(Location)` — a destination is a `Tile`, a `Settlement`, the high seas, or `Europe`). Only the destination **tile's X/Y** is ever stored; the *kind* is **derived at runtime** by `Game.DestinationKindOf(unit, goal)` from what occupies the tile, so **no new save state** is added (the save format is unchanged, v48):

  | Kind | When | Routing & arrival |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Tile` | open land for a land unit, **open water for a ship** (a naval/open-sea goto), or an **own colony** | route *onto* the goal (`FindPath`); arrives when standing **on** it |
  | `Settlement` | a settlement the unit **can't enter** — a native settlement, or a colony it doesn't own | route *beside* the goal (`Pathfinder.FindPathAdjacent`); arrives when standing **adjacent** to it (FreeCol "arrive adjacent" — from there the unit can trade/scout/attack) |
  | `Europe` | a **naval** unit aimed at a **high-seas** tile | route *onto* the high-seas tile (`FindPath`); on arrival the unit hands off to the existing **`SailToEurope`** command and the goto clears (`SailedToEurope`) |

  | Goto step | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Each of the player's goto units, on its turn (`ProcessGotos`, before the movement reset) | walks toward its destination as far as this turn's movement allows |
  | Reached the destination (on a tile/open-sea goal, or **beside** a settlement goal) | the goto **clears** (`GotoOutcome.Reached`) |
  | A ship reached its **Europe** destination's high-seas tile | sails for Europe via `SailToEurope`; the goto **clears** (`GotoOutcome.SailedToEurope`) |
  | Ran out of movement mid-route | stops; goto **kept**, resumes next turn (`OutOfMoves`) |
  | No route this turn (blocker/fog) | stops; goto **kept**, retries next turn (`NoPath`) |
  | A step lands on an unresolved Lost City Rumour (a strange-mounds prompt) | stops **on the rumour tile** so the player decides there (FreeCol: exploring a rumour ends the move); goto **kept** (`Interrupted`) |
  | A step consumes/transforms the unit (e.g. an expedition vanishes) | the goto ends (`NotGoing`) |

  A goto routes **around** enemies and settlements (impassable tiles), so it never auto-attacks — if the only route is blocked it stops with `NoPath` and waits. A `Settlement` destination is the one case where the goal tile itself is impassable: the unit stops on a reachable **neighbour** of it instead.

  The route is found by a deterministic, **RNG-free** A* (`Pathfinder`) over the terrain move-cost model, restricted to tiles the owner has **explored** (so a goto never routes through the unknown), recomputed **every step** so it adapts to revealed terrain and moved units. A unit only ever steps where `CheckMove` allows, so the goto obeys every normal movement rule. The A* is element-agnostic — the caller's `passable` predicate (`Game.CanPathEnter`) keeps a **land** unit on land and a **ship** on water, so **the same search routes a ship over open sea** as routes a land unit overland (closing the old "naval pathing" gap). For a **land** unit the A*'s per-step cost folds in the **road/river "follow it" bonus** (the same `ImprovementMovement.MoveCost` `CheckMove` charges), so a goto **prefers a road or river corridor** — a longer route along a road can be cheaper than a short hop over open plains, and the planner picks the cheaper one. **Ships** get no such bonus (rivers and roads are land features), so a naval goto routes on plain terrain cost.

  **Unit cycling** (`NextUnitToMove`/`HasUnitsToMove`): the next of a player's units still needing orders this turn (lowest id first), skipping those with no moves left, those resting (fortifying/fortified/sentry), those **building a tile improvement** (a busy pioneer), those on a goto (they auto-advance), and any off the map. The presentation layer drives selection from this oracle; the goto/cycling **input UI** (shift-click / G-key, the route overlay) is a separate P7 task.

- **Attrition** (`86d3drmzp`, FreeCol `ServerUnit.csNewTurn` + `UnitType.getMaximumAttrition`): a unit carries an `Attrition` counter — the number of turns it has spent standing in the open wilderness.

  | At the world-advance step (once per `EndTurn`, after every player's turn) | Result |
  |---|---|
  | The unit is **in the open** — on a map tile (not sailing/in Europe/aboard), with **no colony and no native settlement** on that tile — and its type has a **finite** maximum attrition | `Attrition` += 1 |
  | …and `Attrition` now **exceeds** the type's maximum (`Attrition > max`, FreeCol's strict `>`) | the unit **wastes away** — removed from the game, an `AttritionNotice` recorded for its owner (FreeCol's `model.unit.attrition` UNIT_LOST message) |
  | The unit is anywhere else — **in a settlement** (colony or native village), sailing, in Europe, or aboard a ship — **or** its type has **no** finite maximum (it is immune) | `Attrition` is **reset to 0** (FreeCol's `else setAttrition(0)`) |

  - The maximum comes from the unit type (FreeCol `UnitType.getMaximumAttrition`; an unset cap is **infinite** = the type never accrues). **In the classic ruleset only `model.unit.indianConvert` has a finite cap (`maximum-attrition="8"`)** — so it is destroyed on its **9th** consecutive open-wilderness turn, and **every other classic unit type is immune** (infinite max → never accrues). The feature is therefore **active but narrowly scoped** in classic, and effectively **dormant** in a typical game (which has no convert wandering the open map), so the **default game stays byte-identical** (the save field omits when 0; Soak green).
  - The step is **RNG-free** (ADR-009) and self-contained — it neither reads nor advances any RNG stream, so it can't perturb the human's stream-0 byte-stability.
  - `AttritionNotice` (owner id, lost unit type id, tile) is **transient per-turn UI scratch** (refreshed each step, never saved); the presentation surfaces it after the turn ("your X wasted away near Y").

- **Identity: nationality + ethnicity** (`86d3drmzz`, FreeCol `Unit.nationality`/`Unit.ethnicity` + `Unit.initialize`): a unit carries an origin **`Nationality`** (its personal allegiance) and **`Ethnicity`** (the look of the people it was born among).

  | Event | Result |
  |---|---|
  | A **person** unit is spawned | both stamped with the **owner's nation** (FreeCol `owner.getNationId()`): a native brave's `OwnerNationId` (the native nation type id), or a colonial player's `NationId` (null for the classic nation-less human → both null) |
  | A **ship/wagon** is spawned (`!Type.IsPerson`) | neither is set — they stay null (FreeCol's `isPerson()` gate) |
  | A unit **changes owner** (capture, promotion, type swap) | origin is **not** re-stamped — a captured colonist keeps the nationality/ethnicity of the side that raised it (FreeCol changes them only on a *willing* switch) |

  - **Save** (`SavedUnit.Nationality`/`Ethnicity`, v52): omitted when the origin still **equals the owner-derived nation** (`Game.OwnerNationOf` — the spawn default for every fresh unit, including a brave whose nation already rides `Owner`), and re-derived from the owner on load; only a **diverged** origin (a captured colonist) writes an explicit token. So the **default game is byte-identical to v51** and pre-v52 saves re-derive each unit's origin from its owner. RNG-free.
  - **Scope note (faithful subset):** a native **convert** that joins a colony is spawned fresh at the colony (no source-brave object in our model), so it currently takes the colonial owner's nation for *both* fields. FreeCol keeps the convert's native **ethnicity** while taking the colony **nationality** (`hasNativeEthnicity`) — a refinement deferred until converts carry their source nation (no classic gameplay depends on it today).

- **Identity: custom name** (`86d3drmzu`, FreeCol `Unit.name` via `Nameable`): a unit carries an optional **`Name`** — a player-given individual name (default null → it shows its type's generic name). `Game.NameUnit(unit, name)` sets it (trimmed) or **clears** it back to the generic name when the name is null/blank (FreeCol treats an empty rename as a reset). RNG-free. **Save** (`SavedUnit.Name`, v52): omitted when un-christened, so an unnamed unit is byte-identical to v51.

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** ✅ **cross-check done (2026-06-13).** The partial-movement rule above is FreeCol's exactly (`Unit.getMoveCost`, Unit.java:2227). The tile-improvement "follow it" cost change (rivers **and** pioneer-built roads — `ImprovementMovement.MoveCost`, applied per step in `CheckMove` for land units) is now wired; see [rivers-tile-improvements](rivers-tile-improvements.md). Not yet implemented from that method: the settlement-target clause. For 3-MP units the rule is equivalent to the old skeleton behaviour; it differs for faster units (pinned by test).

## 3. Technical design

- `Unit`: mutable state (Position, MovementLeft), internal setters — only `Game` mutates it.
- `Game.InitialMovement(unit)` = `unit.Type.Movement + (int)role.MovementBonus` (role resolved null-safely from `Ruleset.Roles`, so minimal rulesets without role data just get the base). Used by the per-turn reset in `EndTurn` and as the "full movement" reference in `CheckMove`'s partial-move rule. `Unit` no longer carries a `ResetMovement` (it can't see the ruleset to resolve the role).
- `Game.CheckMove(unit, target) → MoveCheck{Allowed, Cost, Reason}`: the single legality oracle; UI calls it before attempting. `Game.MoveUnit` throws `InvalidMoveException` if used without checking.
- **Unexplored-ends-turn** (`86d3fpxm5`, FreeCol behaviour: the pathfinder forbids planned routes through the unexplored — `BaseCostDecider` returns `ILLEGAL_MOVE` for a not-yet-explored target — so any entry is a manual single step that spends all moves): `Game.MoveUnit` captures `steppedIntoUnknown = !unit.Type.IsNaval && !WasExploredForOwner(unit, target)` **before** resolving the move (the fog is lifted by `RevealForOwner` mid-method, so the check must precede it). After the move, if `steppedIntoUnknown` and the unit still has movement, `MovementLeft` is set to 0. `WasExploredForOwner(unit, tile)` returns true (no penalty) for a native-owned unit (`OwnerNationId != null`, lifts no fog) or when the unit's colonial owner's `Explored` set already contains the tile; otherwise the tile was black and the rule fires. `CheckMove` is unchanged (the move is still *legal* and costs the normal terrain amount; only the post-move remainder is zeroed), so a goto — which only ever routes over explored ground — is unaffected. RNG-free.
- **Movement scalar constants** (`86d3drpgg`, `Specification.MovementConstants` on `Ruleset.MovementConstants` — the movement analogue of `ColonyConstants`/`GameOptions`): the three FreeCol movement scalars that had no parsed home are now data-overridable, parsed once in `Ruleset.ParseMovementConstants`, classic values **byte-identical** (no save change):
  - **`BaseMoveCost`** (classic **3**, FreeCol `TileType.basicMoveCost` for plains and the `/3` "one whole move" denominator) — sourced from the **plains** terrain's parsed `MoveCost` (the canonical normal-terrain cost), else the classic default. Carried for fidelity/the variant.
  - **`MinMoveCost`** (classic **1**, FreeCol's lowest positive move cost = a river/road `movement-cost`) — sourced from the **cheapest positive `movement-cost`** among the parsed improvement types; read by the `Pathfinder` heuristic's admissible per-step lower bound (replacing the old hardcoded `Pathfinder` constant, now `Pathfinder.DefaultMinEnterCost` used only as the no-ruleset test default).
  - **`PartialMoveThreshold`** (classic **2**, FreeCol `Unit.getMoveCost`'s hardcoded `+2` "make 1/3 and 2/3 count as 3/3" slack) — no spec element, so it takes the classic default and is overridable only by constructing the bundle directly (the variant path); read by `CheckMove`'s partial-move rule (replacing the inline `+2`).
  - The per-**terrain** enter cost (3/6/9) stays on `TerrainType.MoveCost`, a river/road's per-enter cost on `TileImprovementType.MovementCost`, line-of-sight on `UnitType.LineOfSight`, and terrain defence on `TerrainType.DefenceBonus` — all already data-driven; this bundle only covers the remaining un-homed **scalar** movement numbers.
- Selection is presentation state (`GameController._selectedUnit`), not game state.
- `UnitMarker`: `_Draw` disc + selection ring; positioned via `MapView.TileCentre`.
- **Orders** (`Unit.Orders`, a `UnitOrders` enum): `Game.Fortify`/`Sentry`/`ClearOrders`/`Disband` follow the `CheckX`/`X` + `MoveCheck`/`InvalidMoveException` pattern. The per-turn reset in `EndTurn` ages `Fortifying → Fortified` before resetting movement; `MoveUnit` resets the mover's `Orders` to `Active` and calls `ActivateSentries(mover)` — which flips any sentried **enemy** unit on a tile adjacent to the mover's new position back to `Active` (FreeCol `ServerUnit.csActivateSentries`; gated on `AreEnemies`, RNG-free). Combat reads `Unit.IsFortified` into `DefenceContext.Fortified` in the field-`Attack` path (see [combat](combat.md) §3). Save: `SavedUnit.Orders` (v23) is nullable and omitted for an *Active* unit, so an all-active game serializes byte-identically to v22 and a pre-v23 save loads every unit *Active*.
- **Goto** (`Unit.Destination`, a `Position?`; `GameSession/Game.Goto.cs`): `Pathfinder.FindPath(map, start, goal, passable)` is a pure deterministic A* — open set keyed by the total order `(f, g, Y, X)` so the path is byte-stable, enter-cost = `TerrainType.MoveCost`, diagonals free, Chebyshev × `MovementConstants.MinMoveCost` (classic 1) admissible heuristic, **no RNG**. `Game.CanPathEnter` supplies the `passable` predicate (the `CheckMove` blocking rules minus adjacency/movement, plus the owner-`Explored` fog gate). `CheckSetDestination`/`SetDestination`/`ClearDestination` are the ADR-006 set surface; `AdvanceDestination` returns a `GotoAdvance{Outcome, StepsTaken}` and walks the unit via `MoveUnit` (re-asserting the destination after each step, since `MoveUnit` clears it). `ProcessGotos(player)` runs in `RunPlayerTurn` (before the world's movement reset), advancing the player's goto units in id order; it is a no-op when none have a goto, so it draws no randomness and leaves every RNG stream byte-identical (ADR-009). `NextUnitToMove`/`HasUnitsToMove` are the cycling oracles. Save: `SavedUnit.DestX`/`DestY` (v36) are nullable and omitted when no goto, so a goto-free game serializes byte-identically to v35 and a pre-v36 save loads with no destination.
  - **Destination kinds** (`86d3drn06`): `Game.DestinationKindOf(unit, goal)` derives a `DestinationKind` (`Tile` / `Settlement` / `Europe`) **purely from the tile** (no persisted kind — the save still stores only `DestX`/`DestY`, format unchanged at v48). `RouteFor(unit, goal)` picks the router: a `Settlement` (a native settlement or a colony the unit can't enter) routes via the new `Pathfinder.FindPathAdjacent` (a single A* that returns the first least-cost tile **adjacent** to an impassable goal, byte-stable); every other kind routes via `FindPath`. `HasArrivedAt(unit, goal)` is the arrival predicate (on the goal for `Tile`/`Europe`, **adjacent** for `Settlement`). `AdvanceDestination` and `CheckSetDestination` both go through `RouteFor`/`HasArrivedAt`, and the terrain-element gate in `CheckSetDestination` is skipped for a `Settlement` target (the unit arrives on an adjacent tile of its own element, so the settlement's own terrain needn't match). The private `Arrive(unit, goal, steps)` finalises a goto: it clears `Destination`, and for a `Europe` destination whose ship now stands on the high-seas tile it **calls the existing `Game.SailToEurope`** (not reimplemented here) and reports `GotoOutcome.SailedToEurope`; otherwise `Reached`. Naval/open-sea routing needs no special code — the same `Pathfinder` routes a ship over water because `CanPathEnter` gates land-vs-water by `unit.Type.IsNaval`.

- **Attrition** (`Unit.Attrition`, an `int`; `GameSession/Game.cs`): `Game.ProcessAttrition()` runs in `EndTurn`'s world-advance phase (after the per-player turns, alongside the fortify-aging/movement-reset loop) and is the only mutator of `Unit.Attrition`. It iterates a snapshot of `_units` (removal mutates the list), classifies each unit as "in the open" (`unit.IsOnMap && ColonyAt(pos) is null && NativeSettlementAt(pos) is null`), and either accrues +1 (open + finite cap), resets to 0 (sheltered, or immune), or — when attrition exceeds the cap — `_units.Remove`s the unit and records an `AttritionNotice`. The cap is read via `Game.MaxAttritionOf(UnitType)` → `int.MaxValue` (FreeCol `INFINITY`, never accrues) for any type except `IndianConvertUnitTypeId`, which returns the `IndianConvertMaximumAttrition` constant (8 = classic spec). **This constant is a deliberate, documented stopgap:** the faithful source of the cap is the unit type's `maximum-attrition` XML attribute (FreeCol `UnitType.getMaximumAttrition`), which our `UnitType` record does **not** yet parse; promoting it to a real parsed field (the variant-friendly fix — follow-up `86d3drmzp`) is a one-line change at `MaxAttritionOf`. Save: `SavedUnit.Attrition` (v50) is nullable and omitted when 0, so a game where no unit has accrued attrition (every classic default game) serializes byte-identically to v49 and a pre-v50 save loads every unit at 0; the cap is **not persisted** (re-derived from the ruleset on load). RNG-free (ADR-009). `AttritionNotice` (a top-level `readonly record struct` in `Game.cs`) is transient — never saved.
- **Identity** (`Unit.Nationality`/`Unit.Ethnicity`/`Unit.Name`, `string?`; `GameSession/Game.cs`): `Game.InitNationalityAndEthnicity(unit)` mirrors FreeCol `Unit.initialize` — it stamps both from `Game.OwnerNationOf(unit)` (a brave's `OwnerNationId` ?? the colonial player's `NationId`, gated on `Type.IsPerson` = FreeCol `isPerson()`) and is called at **every** spawn site (the central `SpawnUnit`, the human starting roster `SpawnHumanStartingUnits`, the foreign-power landing `LandForeignPower`). `UpgradeUnitType` (capture/promotion/clearSkill/experience swap) copies `Nationality`/`Ethnicity`/`Name` onto the swapped unit, so origin + name survive the type change (a type swap is the same individual). `Game.NameUnit(unit, name)` is the ADR-006 name command (trims, or clears to null on a blank — RNG-free, like `RenameColony`). **Save:** `From` writes `Nationality`/`Ethnicity` only when they differ from `OwnerNationOf(unit)` (omit-when-equals-owner → default byte-identical to v51); `Restore` sets `unit.Nationality = persisted ?? OwnerNationOf(unit)` (a null re-derives the owner's nation, so a brave/pre-v52 unit gets its origin back, while a diverged persisted token is honoured verbatim). `Name` is plain omit-when-null. All RNG-free (ADR-009), so the human's stream-0 byte-stability is untouched.
- **Military classifier** (`86d3c9x15`): `Game.IsMilitaryUnit(unit)` is a pure read for the unit report's Military group (FreeCol `Unit.isOffensiveUnit` / `ReportMilitaryPanel`, faithful subset): `!unit.Type.IsNaval && (unit.Type.Offence > 0 || Ruleset.Role(unit.RoleId).IsOffensive)` — a non-naval unit whose type is inherently offensive (artillery) or whose equipped role is offensive (soldier/dragoon). Faithful-subset deviation: FreeCol additionally counts an *unarmed* veteran via the `expertSoldier` ability, which `UnitType` doesn't model yet, so such a unit currently lists under the report's Labour group. No RNG, no save change.
- **Equip role at the affordable maximum count** (`86d3fpx80`, FreeCol `QuickActionMenu` equip loop → `Settlement.equipForRole(unit, role, count)`): `Game.EquipRole` no longer fixes the count at 1. It calls `MaxAffordableRoleCount(unit, colony, target)` — which steps the count down from the role's `MaximumCount` to 1 and returns the first count the colony's store can fully pay for (`RoleGoodsDelta(unit, target, count)` positive deltas ≤ store), mirroring FreeCol's `for (count = maximum-count; count > 0; count--)`. The **pioneer** (`maximum-count="5"`, 20 tools/count) therefore arms at **5** tool-units (100 tools) from a stocked colony, stepping down through 4/3/2/1 as the stock allows; single-count roles (soldier/dragoon/scout/missionary) resolve to 1 (already validated affordable by `CheckEquipRole`). `RoleGoodsDelta` now takes a `targetCount` (the target side scales by it — `Role.getRequiredGoods(count)`; the current side still refunds the unit's present `RoleCount`), so **disarming** a count-5 pioneer banks all 100 tools back (`ReturnRoleEquipmentToColony`/the *Disarm* path are unchanged callers, defaulting count 1). `CheckEquipRole`'s affordability gate is still count-1 (FreeCol shows the role only when at least one count is affordable). No save change (`RoleCount` already persists). No RNG.
