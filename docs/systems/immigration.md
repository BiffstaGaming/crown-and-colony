## 1. How it works (plain English)

New colonists don't only come from babies born in your colonies — they also **emigrate from Europe** to your docks. Two things bring them:

- **Crosses** (religious enthusiasm). Every colony has a free **chapel** that makes **1 cross per turn**; build a church and it makes more. Crosses are the "religious unrest" that makes people in the old world want to start a new life across the ocean.
- A small **flat bonus** (+2 per turn) just for being a colonial power.

Some nations emigrate faster than others: the **English** are a nation of religious dissenters, so their people are **a third keener to leave** — their immigration target is **−33%**, and immigrants reach their docks sooner all game long. (Other nations have their own advantages elsewhere.)

These add up into an **immigration pool**. When the pool reaches the **target** (it starts at **15**, or **10** for the English), one colonist **emigrates** — it appears waiting on your **dock in Europe**. Each time that happens the target goes **up by 2**, so immigrants come a little slower as the game goes on.

There's a catch: colonists **standing idle in Europe slow immigration down** — each one waiting on the dock costs **−4 per turn** off the pool (so ship them to the New World to keep the flow going). The pool can stall but never goes below zero.

You don't have to wait. Europe always shows **three recruits** on offer; you can **pay gold to recruit one immediately**. The price starts at **200**, drops toward a **floor of 80** as your pool fills (you're nearly getting one for free anyway), and **jumps by 30** every time you pay — so each impatient purchase makes the next one dearer.

**Worked example:**
> Your one colony's chapel makes 1 cross a turn; with the +2 bonus your pool climbs 3 a turn: 3, 6, 9, 12, **15** — and on the fifth turn a colonist emigrates to your Europe dock. The target is now 17. That colonist now sits in Europe; until you sail it home it shaves 4 a turn off the pool, so the next immigrant takes much longer. Impatient, you pay **200** gold to recruit a second from the dock right away — the next recruit will cost **230**.

**A Fountain of Youth** (a rare Lost City Rumour — see [lost-city-rumours.md](lost-city-rumours.md)) sends a sudden **rush of immigrants** (eight, at the standard difficulty) to your Europe dock. If you've elected **William Brewster** — the founding father who lets you *choose* your emigrants — you pick each one of that rush from the recruits on offer, the same way you'd choose a normal emigrant. Without Brewster they simply arrive (chosen for you). And if you've already cut ties with Europe (after declaring independence), there's no dock to receive them, so the fountain does nothing.

**Some recruits step off the boat ready for work.** Most immigrants arrive as plain, unarmed colonists. But a handful of **experts** show up **already equipped in the role they're famous for**: a **veteran soldier** lands already a **soldier** (carrying his muskets), a **hardy pioneer** already a **pioneer** (with a full load of tools), a **seasoned scout** already a **scout** (mounted), and a **jesuit missionary** already a **missionary**. You don't have to arm them yourself — they save you the muskets/tools/horses. (Every other recruit — free colonists, indentured servants, petty criminals, and the other experts — still arrives unarmed, as before.) This only matters once one of those four rare experts actually emigrates, which is uncommon: they're weighted far lower in the recruit pool than ordinary colonists.

**A safety net so you're never fully stuck.** Without churches your pool can't grow, and an empty pool can never reach the target — a power that has lost all its colonies *and* every colonist would otherwise be stranded forever, unable to ever get another settler. So early in the game (before **1600**) any colonial power in exactly that plight — no colony, no colonist anywhere, an empty pool — is handed **one free "survival" colonist** on its Europe dock to keep the nation alive. It's a single top-up, not a stream: once that colonist is waiting in Europe the power has a colonist again, so it won't keep getting more — sail it home and rebuild. After 1600 there's no rescue (you're expected to have a foothold by then). In a normal game your free chapel always makes crosses, so your pool is never permanently empty and you'll never see this — it only catches a power that has truly been wiped out. (In the current build this safety net runs for the **rival AI powers**; the human is never falsely declared beaten while it has *any* unit left, and the pre-1600 free-recruit corner for the human is an explicitly-unmodelled nicety — see [the technical notes](#3-technical-design).)

**What the player sees and does:** the **Europe screen** ([europe.md](europe.md)) shows the recruits, their price, and the immigration pool/target, with a Recruit button per slot. Recruited/emigrated units appear in Europe (`UnitsInEurope`) and can be shipped home ([transport.md](transport.md)).

## 2. Detailed rules

All values are the classic ruleset at the default (**medium**) difficulty, read from `specification.xml`.

| Input / condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Immigration per turn | `crosses produced by all colonies` + Europe contribution |
| Europe contribution | `(persons on the dock × −4) + 2`, but clamped so the **turn's total** immigration is never negative (a person *aboard a ship* in Europe does not count — see [transport.md](transport.md)) |
| Initial target | **15** (`model.option.initialImmigration`), **× the nation's religious-unrest factor** (English `immigration` type ×0.67 → effective 10) |
| Emigrant produced | whenever pool ≥ target: one recruit leaves the dock for Europe; repeat while still ≥ target |
| On each emigration | pool reduced by the target (surplus kept — `saveProductionOverflow=true`); target **+2** (`crossesIncrement`) |
| Dock | **3** slots (`MigrantCount`), each a weighted-random recruitable unit type |
| Recruit weights | spec `recruit-probability`: free colonist / indentured servant / petty criminal **20**, experts **1**, everything else 0 |
| Recruit price | `max(base × max(target − pool, 0) / target, floor)`, base **200**, floor **80** |
| On each **paid** recruit | pay the current price; base **+30** (`recruitPriceIncrease`), floor **+0** (`lowerCapIncrease`); **then** also consume the pool + raise the target exactly as a free emigration does |
| Recruit placement | the unit lands in Europe (`InEurope`); the dock refills with a fresh weighted draw at the bottom slot |

**Deviations from original 1994 / FreeCol behavior:**
- **Recruit selection on free emigration (`86d3c9xft`).** William Brewster's recruit *ban* (no servants/criminals on the dock) is applied — see [founding-fathers.md](founding-fathers.md) — and his `selectRecruit` *choice* is now wired: a **human** who has elected Brewster (`model.ability.selectRecruit`) **pauses** when an emigrant is due (`Game.PendingEmigration`) and picks which dock slot emigrates via `Game.ChooseEmigrant(slot)`, instead of a random one auto-emigrating. A player **without** Brewster (and **every** AI) keeps the historical random auto-emigrate — so the RNG stream + goldens are byte-identical for the common case. The pending choice is **in-memory only** (not persisted this wave); a reloaded game re-pauses on the next turn the player is still due an emigrant.
- **Fountain-of-Youth recruit choice (`86d3c9ujx`).** The FoY immigration burst (a Lost City Rumour — see [lost-city-rumours.md](lost-city-rumours.md)) now routes through the **same** select-recruit seam (FreeCol `MigrationType.FOUNTAIN`): a **Brewster human** picks each of the `dx` (medium 8) free immigrants from the dock — `Game.PendingEmigration` is armed as a Fountain-of-Youth choice (`IsFountainOfYouth`, `Remaining = dx`), and each `Game.ChooseEmigrant` lands one immigrant **without consuming immigration or raising the target**, re-arming until all `dx` are chosen. Everyone else — the AI, foreign powers, and a human **without** Brewster — keeps the historical *direct* generation (the same `dx` weighted draws off the explorer's owner stream), so every non-choosing player is **byte-identical** (ADR-009). A player with **no Europe** (a rebel/independent player whose dock is closed, or a minimal ruleset) hits the FreeCol `noEurope` gate: nothing happens and the human gets a distinct "no Europe" rumour notice instead of the generic success line. In-memory only (no save bump).
- **Religious-unrest modifier** (`86d3c7yca`, FreeCol `RELIGIOUS_UNREST_BONUS`): the immigration target is reduced by the player's **nation-type** `religiousUnrestBonus` — the **English** (`immigration` nation type) carry **−33%**, so they need a third fewer points per emigrant, from the start (15 → effective 10). We store the **raw** target (so the save and the flat `crossesIncrement` growth are unchanged) and apply the factor on use (`EffectiveImmigrationRequired`), which is equivalent to FreeCol's reduced-stored value; the human defaults to **no nation** (×1), so a default game is byte-identical. The **first nation-type advantage modifier** wired (the pattern Dutch trade etc. will follow).
- **Survival auto-recruit (`86d3drn0e`, FreeCol `MigrationType.SURVIVAL`).** When a colonial power has been stripped bare — no colonies (so no cross production, and its pool can never reach the target), no colonist anywhere (map, Europe, or at sea) — and it is **before** the `mandatoryColonyYear` (classic **1600**), it is handed **one free colonist** on its own Europe dock so a dry immigration pipeline can't extinguish it permanently. This is FreeCol's `checkForDeath ⇒ IS_AUTORECRUIT ⇒ csEmigrate(0, SURVIVAL)` rescue. The forced emigrant **does not** consume immigration or raise the target (the SURVIVAL case is not the NORMAL path), and its dock-refill type is drawn on a **dedicated isolated RNG stream** (not the player's main stream), so the rule perturbs no other draw and the default game stays byte-identical (ADR-009). It fires at most once per starvation episode — once the survivor waits in Europe the power "has a colonist" again, so the gate (which mirrors FreeCol's `hasColonist ⇒ IS_ALIVE` short-circuit) won't refire. **Scope:** wired for the **foreign powers only** — see the human carve-out under Technical design; the gate reads only banked state (no turn counter), so **no new persisted field and no save bump**.
- **Recruits arrive equipped in their role (`86d3c7yg8`, FreeCol `equipEuropeanRecruits`).** When the classic `model.option.equipEuropeanRecruits` is on (its default, **true**), a recruit emigrating/recruited from the Europe dock steps ashore **already in its unit type's default role** (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csEmigrate` spawns `new ServerUnit(…, recruit.getRole())`, whose role is `UnitType.getDefaultRole()`), instead of the bare unarmed default role. In the classic ruleset four expert types carry a non-default `<default-role>`, so they alone arrive equipped: **veteran soldier → `model.role.soldier`**, **hardy pioneer → `model.role.pioneer`**, **seasoned scout → `model.role.scout`**, **jesuit missionary → `model.role.missionary`** (every other recruit keeps `model.role.default`). The role count is the role's `maximum-count` — matching FreeCol's `ServerUnit(…, type, role)` ctor, which calls `changeRole(role, role.getMaximumCount())` — so a hardy pioneer arrives at the pioneer role's full **5** tool-loads (100 tools), the other three at count **1**. **Faithful-subset simplification:** FreeCol reads each unit type's parsed `<default-role>` child; our `UnitType` does not yet parse that field (and the ruleset parser was out of scope for this change), so the mapping is the four classic declarations, hardcoded in `Game.Emigration.cs` (`DefaultRecruitRole`) with a guard that falls back to the unarmed default role if the mapped role is absent from a minimal/variant ruleset. We also do **not** model FreeCol's equipment **cost** (FreeCol's `equipForRole` debits the goods from Europe/stock); the recruit simply arrives equipped — a deliberate simplification, since the equip machinery and Europe goods debit for recruits is not present. The `equipEuropeanRecruits` option itself is not yet parsed into `Ruleset` (a small follow-up); we treat it as its classic default (on). **Determinism (ADR-009):** the equip is purely deterministic — it sets the role on an already-spawned unit and draws **no RNG** — so the human's stream 0 and the seeded default game stay byte-identical (the soak proves it); only the role/role-count of the (rare) expert recruit changes, and that already round-trips through `Unit.RoleId` (**no save bump**).
- **Recruits reach the New World by ship.** Boarding a recruit onto a ship and sailing it home is implemented — see [transport.md](transport.md). (Recruits still can't be carried directly into an existing colony's population yet.)
- **Per-player from FP-5**: every colonial player accrues immigration and recruits on its **own** dock, drawing from its **own** RNG stream (`RandomFor(player)`) — the foreign-power AI runs the same accrual + auto-emigration and recruits when affordable (capped Europe pile-up; see [players.md](players.md)). Recruited/emigrated units carry the owner's id (the human is 0), so a foreign power's colonists never land on the human's dock. The **recruit-dock availability filter** is `Game.IsRecruitable` — a unit type is offered only when its `recruit-probability > 0` and no elected father bans it (Brewster's `canRecruitUnit=false`); this is the filter both the initial dock and every refill draw through. (FreeCol's *per-nation* `canRecruitUnit`/availability beyond Brewster is still omitted.)

## 3. Technical design

**Domain model:** immigration state is **per player** (`Player`, ADR-019), alongside the parallel liberty/Founding-Father state; `Game`'s no-argument properties pass through to the human:
- `Immigration` / `ImmigrationRequired` — the pool and its target.
- `RecruitDock` — the three unit-type ids on offer.
- `RecruitPrice` — the computed current price; `BaseRecruitPrice` / `RecruitLowerCap` — the escalating internals (persisted).

**Data sources:** `UnitType` gains two parsed fields — `RecruitProbability` (the spec's direct `recruit-probability` attribute, **not** inherited via `extends`) and `IsPerson` (the `model.ability.person` ability, resolved up the `extends` chain). Crosses are `model.goods.crosses` (`is-farmed=false`, `storable=false`), produced unattended by `model.building.chapel`/`church`/`cathedral`. The base `gameOptions` **game options** are parsed once into the `GameOptions` bundle: `Ruleset.GameOptions` carries the immigration trio `InitialImmigration` (15), `EuropeanUnitImmigrationPenalty` (−4) and `PlayerImmigrationBonus` (+2) (`86d3d335r`) **plus the `gameOptions.years` year/turn-gate cluster** `MandatoryColonyYear` (1600), `LastColonialYear` (1800) and `IndependenceTurn` (468) (`86d3drpgg`) — the base-`gameOptions`-group analogue of `Ruleset.Difficulty` (which holds the per-difficulty-level group). Each is a plain document-wide `<integerOption>` lookup (`value` else `defaultValue`), falling back per-option to `GameOptions.ClassicDefaults` when absent; FreeCol groups these in `common/option/GameOptions.java` (`INITIAL_IMMIGRATION` / `EUROPEAN_UNIT_IMMIGRATION_PENALTY` / `PLAYER_IMMIGRATION_BONUS` / `MANDATORY_COLONY_YEAR` / `LAST_COLONIAL_YEAR` / `INDEPENDENCE_TURN`). The `Game.InitialImmigration`/`EuropeUnitImmigrationPenalty`/`PlayerImmigrationBonus`/`MandatoryColonyYear` members remain only as classic-default aliases of the bundle (static fallbacks — the fresh-`Player` target default, the pre-v12 save fallback, and the survival-gate const — which run in a static context with no `Ruleset` in hand); the **live** Europe contribution and survival gate read `Ruleset.GameOptions`. `Ruleset.LastColonialYear` now delegates to `Ruleset.GameOptions.LastColonialYear`, so the independence and limit-engine consumers are unchanged; `IndependenceTurn` (the Col1 pre-1780 score cutoff, turn 468) is parsed into the bundle so the whole `gameOptions.years` cluster has one home — its scoring consumer is not yet wired (our independence bonus is order-based), so a missing option falls back to the classic 468.

**Algorithms & formulas** (`GameSession/Game.cs`):
- `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate(player)` (called from `RunPlayerTurn` for every colonial player, after liberty): drains each of the player's colonies' crosses store into its pool (mirroring bells→liberty; the player's own fathers fold via `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`), adds the clamped Europe contribution `(ownPersonsInEurope × Ruleset.GameOptions.EuropeanUnitImmigrationPenalty) + Ruleset.GameOptions.PlayerImmigrationBonus` (its own dock persons; the −4/+2 now read from the parsed `GameOptions` bundle — `86d3d335r`), then emigrates while `pool ≥ target`. A **human with William Brewster** (`model.ability.selectRecruit`) instead **pauses** on the first due emigrant — sets `_pendingEmigration` (a `PendingEmigrationChoice`) and returns, no RNG drawn for the choice; everyone else auto-emigrates a random dock slot drawn from `RandomFor(player)` exactly as before. `Game.Emigration.cs` (partial) holds `PendingEmigration`/`ChooseEmigrant(slot)` — the latter runs `Emigrate`+`ReduceImmigration`+target-raise (the same per-emigrant bookkeeping), then re-arms for a backlog or clears. **In-memory only** (not persisted).
- **Fountain-of-Youth routing (`86d3c9ujx`).** `Game.GenerateFountainRecruits(ownerId, random)` (called from the LCR `ResolveOutcome`) now returns a `FountainResult` and dispatches three ways: **no Europe** (empty `RecruitDock` — a closed/rebel dock or a minimal ruleset) → `NoEurope`, no recruits; a **Brewster human** (and no normal emigrant already pending) → arms `_pendingEmigration` as a `PendingEmigrationChoice` with `IsFountainOfYouth: true, Remaining: dx` and returns `QueuedForChoice`, drawing **no RNG off `random`** (each pick's refill draws later on the player's own stream inside `ChooseEmigrant`); **everyone else** → the unchanged `dx` × `CreateEuropeRecruit(owner, DrawRecruitType(owner, random))` direct loop, returning `Generated` (byte-identical). `ChooseEmigrant` branches on `PendingEmigrationChoice.IsFountainOfYouth`: a FoY pick runs `Emigrate` (extract + dock refill) and decrements `Remaining` only — **no `ReduceImmigration`, no target raise** (FreeCol `MigrationType.FOUNTAIN`) — re-arming until the burst is spent. The result drives the human's rumour notice (`ExploreRumour.DescribeFountainOutcome`). **In-memory only** (no save bump).
- **Survival auto-recruit (`86d3drn0e`).** `Game.Emigration.cs` holds `MaybeSurvivalRecruit(player)` (and the gate `IsSurvivalStalled`), invoked by a **single line** at the tail of `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate` (the accrual lives in `Game.cs`; the survival logic is entirely in the partial). The gate is `!IsHuman && PlayerType == Colonial && CurrentYear < Ruleset.GameOptions.MandatoryColonyYear && RecruitDock.Count > 0 && no colonies && no owned person unit anywhere` — the in-boundary, derivable distillation of FreeCol's `checkForDeath ⇒ IS_AUTORECRUIT`. The cutover year now reads the parsed `Ruleset.GameOptions.MandatoryColonyYear` (the classic `model.option.mandatoryColonyYear`, 1600; `86d3drpgg`); `Game.MandatoryColonyYear` survives only as the static classic-default alias (`GameOptions.ClassicDefaults.MandatoryColonyYear`). When it fires, the front dock recruit is extracted, the dock refills via `DrawRecruitType(player, SurvivalRandom(player))`, and a Europe unit is created — **no `ReduceImmigration`, no target raise** (SURVIVAL ≠ NORMAL). `SurvivalRandom(player)` forks a transient `Pcg32Random` from the player's current RNG state (`SaveState().State`, which does **not** advance it) salted by `SurvivalStreamId` (104, well clear of every per-player and reserved stream) and the current `Turn`, so the draw is deterministic per seed and on reload **without** any persisted survival-RNG field, and **never shifts** the player's main stream. **Human carve-out (ADR-009):** the rescue can only fire because a player lost all units, which for the human can only happen via AI actions — and the human's scoped state (stream 0, gold, dock) must stay independent of what the AI does within a turn (the invariant `IsHumanDefeated` documents and the stream-0 byte-stability tests guard). So the gate excludes the human (`!IsHuman`); the human keeps the existing conservative "alive on any surviving unit, no false defeat" stance, with the pre-1600 free-recruit nuance left as an explicitly-unmodelled corner (as `IsHumanDefeated` already notes).
- **Equip-on-spawn (`86d3c7yg8`).** Every emigration/recruitment path — the auto-emigrate loop, the Brewster choice (`ChooseEmigrant`), the paid recruit (`Recruit`), Fountain of Youth (`GenerateFountainRecruits`), and the survival auto-recruit (`MaybeSurvivalRecruit`) — funnels through the single spawn chokepoint `CreateEuropeRecruit(player, unitTypeId)` (in `Game.cs`), mirroring FreeCol's lone `new ServerUnit(…, role)` in `csEmigrate`. That method now calls `EquipEuropeanRecruit(unit)` (in `Game.Emigration.cs`) before adding the unit: if `DefaultRecruitRole(unit.Type.Id)` returns a role (the four classic experts), it sets `unit.RoleId` to that role and `unit.RoleCount` to `Ruleset.Role(roleId).MaximumCount` (5 for the pioneer role, 1 for the others); otherwise the unit keeps the unarmed default. `DefaultRecruitRole` is the classic `<default-role>` map (veteranSoldier→soldier, hardyPioneer→pioneer, seasonedScout→scout, jesuitMissionary→missionary), guarded so an unknown/absent role falls back to default. No RNG is drawn, so the soak stays byte-identical. (The `CreateEuropeRecruit` chokepoint and the equip helper live in `Game.cs`/`Game.Emigration.cs` respectively — a single line in `Game.cs` invokes the partial's helper, keeping all spawn paths consistently equipped from one place.)
- `RecruitPrice` getter = `max(base · max(required−immigration,0) / required, floor)` — FreeCol `Europe.getCurrentRecruitPrice` verbatim.
- `ReligiousUnrestFactor(player)` — the player's nation-type `religiousUnrestBonus` resolved to a multiplier (1.0 with no nation / no modifier; English ×0.67); `EffectiveImmigrationRequired(player) = round(ImmigrationRequired × factor)`. The auto-emigrate loop and the price/recruit checks compare the pool against this **effective** target (the raw `ImmigrationRequired` field still grows by the flat `crossesIncrement` and is what the save stores).
- `ReduceImmigration()` — subtract the **effective** target, keep surplus (`saveProductionOverflow=true`).
- `DrawRecruitType(player)` — seeded weighted pick over `Ruleset.UnitTypes` by `RecruitProbability`, drawing from `RandomFor(player)` (the human's stream 0, a foreign power's own stream); same pattern as Founding-Father offers (ADR-009 RNG, no `System.Random`).
- `Recruit(slot)` / `CheckRecruit(slot)` — the paid path; mirrors `ServerPlayer.csEmigrate`'s RECRUIT case (pay → `increaseRecruitmentDifficulty` → fall through to the NORMAL pool consume + target raise).

**Integration points:** runs in `Game.EndTurn` between `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` and `AdvanceSailing`. The Europe penalty counts only **person** units with `Location == InEurope` that are **not aboard a ship** (a docked trade ship, or a recruit already boarded for home, does not suppress immigration — see [transport.md](transport.md)). `CheckFoundColony` now also rejects off-map units (Europe emigrants can't found colonies).

**Persistence:** save **v12** adds `Immigration`, `ImmigrationRequired`, `BaseRecruitPrice`, `RecruitLowerCap`, and `RecruitDock`. Pre-v12 saves load with the classic defaults (target 15, base 200, floor 80) and a freshly drawn dock.
