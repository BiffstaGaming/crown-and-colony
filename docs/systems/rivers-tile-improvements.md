## 1. How it works (plain English)

A **tile improvement** is something laid on a map tile that changes what it's worth — most importantly a **river**. Rivers do two helpful things:

- **They speed up travel.** Move a unit *along* a river — stepping from one river tile to the next — and that step is cheap: it costs only a third of a normal move, so a unit can cover a lot of ground following a waterway. (The bonus only applies when you go from one river tile to another river tile; stepping off the river, or onto a river from dry land, costs the normal amount.)
- **They make the land richer.** A tile with a river on it produces *more* of several goods when a colonist works it — extra furs and lumber especially, plus a little more grain, sugar, tobacco, cotton, ore and silver. That's why colonies cluster along rivers.

**The rules, in plain words (river, classic ruleset):**
- A river gives **+2 furs, +2 lumber**, and **+1 each** to grain, sugar, tobacco, cotton, ore and silver, on top of whatever the tile already produces.
- Moving from a river tile to an adjacent river tile costs **1 movement point** (a third of a normal 3-point move). Any move that isn't river-to-river costs the normal amount.
- Rivers come in two sizes (small and large) for looks; both give the same bonuses. The size you see is worked out purely from the picture (a long, connected river draws as a large one) — it isn't stored in the save.
- **A river that reaches the sea brings fish to the water beside it.** A sea (or lake) tile next to a *river mouth* — a coastal land tile that has a river on it — gets **+1 fish**, on top of the usual coastal +2.

**Worked example:**
> A free colonist working a grassland tile with a river produces its normal grain plus **1 extra grain**, and if it's a forest tile the river adds **2 lumber** and **2 furs**. A scout following a river south hops tile-to-tile for 1 point each instead of 3, so with a typical move allowance it can travel three river tiles in the space of one normal overland move.

**What the player sees and does:** rivers are placed by the map generator at game start (springs on high inland ground flowing down to the sea), the player doesn't build them (they're natural). Rivers are now **drawn on the map** as connected blue courses over the terrain (fog-gated like the land — visible only on explored tiles, dimmed on remembered ones). Players benefit from founding colonies on river tiles (richer yields, plus the +1 river-mouth fish on the coast beside a river) and from routing units along rivers (cheap movement). *(Large-river section growth/tributary joining is still simplified — see §2/§5; the drawn small/large size is a render-time read of connectivity, not a stored field.)*

**Pioneers build the rest.** Unlike rivers, **roads, plowed fields and cleared forests are man-made** — a colonist carrying tools (a **pioneer**, equipped from a colony's stock of tools; an expert *hardy pioneer* works twice as fast) builds them by standing on a tile and spending several turns at work. Each kind needs the right ground:

- **Road** — buildable on *any* land. Once built, moving from one road tile to an adjacent road tile is cheap (the same 1-point "follow it" cost as a river; roads and rivers connect to each other). A road also slightly enriches a few goods (furs, lumber, ore, silver).
- **Plow** (a plowed field) — buildable only on *cleared* farmland (not forest, not hills/mountains, not arctic, not water). It adds **+1** to each farmed crop the tile grows (grain, sugar, tobacco, cotton).
- **Clear forest** — buildable only on a *forest* tile. It cuts the trees down, turning the tile into its underlying cleared terrain (e.g. mixed forest → plains, conifer forest → grassland) and dumping a **one-off load of lumber** into the nearest colony that works the tile. A river running through the tile survives the clearing.

Each completed improvement uses up one load of the pioneer's tools (20 tools). When the pioneer runs out of tools it becomes a plain colonist again. How long the work takes depends on the ground (tougher terrain takes longer) plus the improvement itself; a hardy pioneer halves the calendar time. A pioneer at work is "busy" — it's skipped when cycling through units that still need orders, and clearing its orders abandons the half-finished job.

**Worked example (pioneer):**
> A free colonist with tools stands on a plains tile and is ordered to build a road. Plains take 3 turns of work and a road adds none, so after **3 turns** the road appears and the colonist — having spent its one load of tools — reverts to an unarmed colonist. A *hardy* pioneer would have finished the same road in **2 turns** (it works 2 turns' worth each turn). Ordered instead to plow, the work takes 3 + 2 = **5 turns**, after which the tile grows +1 grain.

## 2. Detailed rules

Faithful to FreeCol's classic `model.improvement.river`.

**Production bonuses (additive, applied to the tile's worked yield):**

| Goods | River delta |
|---|---|
| `model.goods.grain` | +1 |
| `model.goods.sugar` | +1 |
| `model.goods.tobacco` | +1 |
| `model.goods.cotton` | +1 |
| `model.goods.furs` | +2 |
| `model.goods.lumber` | +2 |
| `model.goods.ore` | +1 |
| `model.goods.silver` | +1 |
| any other goods | 0 |

**Movement:**

| Condition | Cost to enter the destination tile |
|---|---|
| Both the tile left **and** the tile entered have a river | river cost (**1**), if it is strictly cheaper than the terrain's normal cost |
| Either tile lacks a river | the destination terrain's normal cost (no bonus) |
| River cost ≥ terrain cost | the terrain's normal cost (the bonus never makes a move *dearer*) |
| River cost ≤ 0 | the terrain's normal cost (a move is **never free** — FreeCol's explicit guard) |

Costs are in FreeCol movement units (3 = one normal move), the same scale as `TerrainType.MoveCost`.

**River-mouth fish bonus (`fishBonusRiver`, +1 fish):** a non-high-seas water tile adjacent to a **river-mouth land tile** earns +1 fish, on top of the coastal +2. A river-mouth land tile is a land tile that **carries a river and is itself adjacent to water** — i.e. the river reaches the sea there. A river entirely inland (no water neighbour) is not a mouth, so it confers no fish bonus. This is a pure read of the river layer + adjacency (no stored mouth flag), faithful to FreeCol stamping `fishBonusRiver` on the sea tiles a river flows into.

**Magnitude / style:** the river type carries a `Magnitude` band — 1 = small (minor) river, 2 = large (major) river — identical in production and movement at either magnitude (FreeCol styling only). The **drawn** size is decided **at render time** from connectivity, not from the stored magnitude: an explored river tile in a connected component of ≥ 4 tiles draws as a large (thick) course, shorter runs as small ones (a stored magnitude 2 still forces large, for forward compatibility). No drawn-style field is saved (ADR-006, no save bump).

**Pioneer-built improvements (road / plow / clear-forest):**

A unit may build an improvement when it is a colonial land unit, on the map, holding a role that grants `model.ability.improveTerrain` (the pioneer role) with at least one equipment count left, standing on a tile whose terrain the improvement `AppliesTo` (its scopes — see below), where the improvement is not already present (road/plow; clear-forest is gated by the forest scope), and with movement left to start. Ordering the build commits the unit for the turn (its movement drops to 0) and sets a work counter.

| Improvement | Buildable on | Work turns (base + add) | Tools | Effect on completion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Road** (`model.improvement.road`) | any land (scope: not water) | terrain + **0** | 20 (one count) | movement-cost 1 along connected road/river tiles; +2 furs, +2 lumber, +1 ore, +1 silver |
| **Plow** (`model.improvement.plow`) | cleared land — not forest, hills, mountains, arctic, water | terrain + **2** | 20 | +1 grain, +1 sugar, +1 tobacco, +1 cotton |
| **Clear forest** (`model.improvement.clearForest`) | forest only | terrain + **2** | 20 | terrain → its cleared base type (mixed→plains, conifer→grassland, …); a one-off lumber batch (20, scrub 10) to the owning colony |

- **Work each turn:** the unit reduces its work counter by **2** if it is a *hardy pioneer* (the pioneer role's expert unit — FreeCol `Ability.EXPERT_PIONEER`), else **1**. When the counter reaches 0 the improvement lands. Total work to start = the terrain's `basic-work-turns` + the improvement's `add-work-turns` (FreeCol `TileImprovement` ctor).
- **Tools:** completion spends `expended-amount` (1) of the role's equipment count; a count is 20 tools (the pioneer role's `required-goods`). A pioneer out of tools reverts to a plain colonist (FreeCol `changeRoleCount`).
- **Stacking:** a tile may carry several improvements (a river *and* a road, a river *and* a plow). The improvement layer is multi-valued per tile; clearing a forest changes the terrain but leaves any river in place. Only one of each improvement type per tile.
- **Movement (generalised):** the "follow it" reduced cost applies when **both** the tile left and the tile entered carry *some* movement-granting improvement (a river or a road), using the cheapest such cost on the destination — so roads connect to roads, rivers to rivers, and roads to rivers. Plowed fields grant no movement bonus.

**Deviations from original 1994 / FreeCol behavior:**
- **Directional river connectivity / river style not modelled in the rules.** FreeCol stores each river tile's *style* (which of its edges the river runs along) and checks it so the move bonus only applies along a segment that actually connects in the direction of travel. We store river *presence* per tile, not style, so the move bonus applies whenever **both endpoints carry a river** — the faithful-in-spirit rule. The river-mouth fish bonus does **not** need the stored style: a river-bearing coastal land tile is a mouth by adjacency. The *drawn* river style (small vs. large) is derived at render time from connectivity (see Magnitude / style above), not stored.
- **Single-magnitude placement.** Rivers are stamped at the small magnitude (1). FreeCol grows river *section sizes* (small → large → fjord), joins tributaries into one river, and branches deltas at the mouth. Our faithful subset lays a single river presence on each tile a walk crosses (no section growth, joining, or deltas). Large rivers are *drawn* via the render-time connectivity heuristic; the save format already carries per-tile magnitude, so generator-stamped large rivers remain a forward-compatible data change.
- **Rivers are generator-placed; road/plow/clear-forest are pioneer-built.** The river is the only improvement the generator places. Pioneers build road/plow/clear-forest (this slice). The fish-bonus types are parsed-through (their +fish is applied directly by `Game.TileYieldPotential`, not via a placed improvement).
- **Clear-forest resource exposure not modelled.** FreeCol rolls a 5% chance (`expose-resource-percent`) to reveal a bonus resource when a forest is cleared. We parse and carry `ExposeResourcePercent` but do **not** roll it — that would need a world-stream RNG draw inside the per-turn completion; deferred so the completion stays RNG-free and the human's stream 0 stays byte-identical. (Lumber delivery + the terrain change are applied.)
- **One pioneer build per tile-equip, simplified tool count.** `EquipRole` arms a pioneer at count 1 (20 tools) — one improvement before reverting — rather than FreeCol's up-to-5 (`maximum-count`). A pioneer restored from a save with a higher count (or set up so) spends one count per improvement and keeps building until out, faithfully; only the *initial* equip is capped at 1 for now.
- **Owning-colony delivery by footprint.** A cleared forest's lumber goes to the player's colony whose 3×3 footprint covers the tile (nearest, then by id), our equivalent of FreeCol's per-tile `getOwningSettlement` (we have no per-tile colony-ownership layer). No owning colony → the lumber is lost (as in FreeCol when a tile has no owner).

## 3. Technical design

**Domain model** (all in `game/src/GameLogic/World/Improvements/`, engine-free, ADR-006):
- `TileImprovementType` — immutable rule data for an improvement type (id, `Magnitude`, `MovementCost`, `AddWorkTurns`, `Modifiers`). Factory helpers: `FromModifiers(...)` (build from `(goodsId, delta)` pairs, additive by default) and `ClassicRiver(magnitude = 1)` (the canonical classic river instance, values verbatim from the spec). `RiverId` constant, `ShortName`, `GrantsMovementBonus`.
- `ImprovementModifier` — one production bonus (`GoodsId`, `Type`, `Value`), mirroring `ResourceModifier`/`FatherModifier`; reuses the shared `ModifierType` + `ModifierMath.Apply` (`Specification/FoundingFather.cs`). Scalar value-record, so modifier lists compare by sequence equality.
- `ImprovementProduction` — pure `YieldDelta(improvement, goodsId)` (and an `IEnumerable` overload for a tile carrying several improvements). Sums the matching modifiers applied to a zero base: additive modifiers contribute their flat value; a percentage modifier contributes nothing on its own (it scales an existing yield, folded in by the caller during the deferred wiring).
- `ImprovementMovement` — pure movement rules. `ReducedCost(improvementCost, baseCost)` is FreeCol's `TileImprovementType.getMoveCost(originalCost)` verbatim (replace base only when `0 < improvementCost < baseCost`; never returns zero). `RiverMoveCost(from, to, baseCost)` applies the "follow the river" bonus: reduced only when both the from-tile and to-tile carry a river that grants a bonus.

**Data sources:** FreeCol `data/rules/classic/specification.xml` → `<tile-improvement-type>`. The river declares eight additive `<modifier>` children; the pioneer types declare `required-role="model.role.pioneer"`, `expended-amount="1"` (one pioneer count = 20 tools), scopes, and — for clear-forest — `<tile-type-change>` children (forest → cleared base + a `<production>` lumber amount). **`Ruleset.cs` parses `<tile-improvement-types>` in full**: `ParseImprovementModifier` reads each `<modifier>`; `ParseImprovementScope` reads each `<scope>` (a terrain predicate `method-name`/`method-value`, or a `type`/`match-negated` terrain-id match); `ParseTileTypeChange` reads each `<tile-type-change>`'s `to` + `<production>`. The type carries `IsNatural`, `RequiredRoleId`, `ExpendedAmount`, `ExposeResourcePercent`, `Scopes`, and `TileTypeChanges`, with helpers `AppliesTo(terrain)` (every scope satisfied — FreeCol `isTileTypeAllowed`), `ChangeFrom(terrainId)`, `IsRoad`/`IsPlow`/`IsClearForest`, `ChangesTerrain`. Exposed via `Ruleset.ImprovementTypes`, `Ruleset.Improvement(id)`, `Ruleset.RiverType`. The hard-coded `ClassicRiver()` factory remains only as a test convenience — generation and load both go through the parsed type. `RoleType.CanImproveTerrain` (granted ability `model.ability.improveTerrain`) identifies the pioneer role.

**Placement** (`MapGenerator.MakeRivers`, a faithful subset of FreeCol `TerrainGenerator.createRivers` + `River.flowFromSource`/`flow`):
- A **river-allowed** tile is lowland land — not water, not hills/mountains, not arctic (the river type's negated scopes).
- A **spring** ("good river tile", FreeCol `Tile.isGoodRiverTile`) is a river-allowed tile whose entire 8-neighbourhood is land, so a source never starts on the coast.
- From the seed-shuffled springs, each not already a river starts a **walk**: pick a random direction, lay the river on the current tile, then step (50% straight, 25% left, 25% right — FreeCol's `DirectionChange`, nudged every other step) to the next tile; the walk ends at water (the river mouth), an existing river, the map edge, a non-allowed tile, or the per-river length cap (`MaxRiverLength = 16`).
- The pass stops once laid river tiles reach the **budget** = `allowedTileCount · RiverNumber / 100` (`RiverNumber = 15`, FreeCol's classic `model.option.riverNumber` default — hard-coded from the spec for now, like `MountainNumber`).
- Runs after terrain/mountains/high-seas and **before** bonus resources, drawing from the **map-gen RNG (stream 0)** — so it reorders the stream-0 draw sequence (a deliberate map-gen change; the L4 goldens were regenerated). Deterministic per seed (ADR-009).

**Algorithms & formulas:**
- Production delta: `YieldDelta = Σ over modifiers with matching goodsId of ModifierMath.Apply(type, 0, value)` (`ImprovementProduction.YieldDelta`).
- Movement: `MoveCost = (from has a movement-bonus improvement ∧ to has one) ? ReducedCost(min(to's bonus costs), baseCost) : baseCost`, where `ReducedCost` replaces the base only when `0 < improvementCost < baseCost` (`ImprovementMovement.MoveCost`; `RiverMoveCost` is the single-improvement river special case kept for the river L1 tests).
- Pioneer work: `WorkTurnsToComplete = terrain.WorkTurns + improvement.AddWorkTurns`; per-turn decrement = `2` (hardy pioneer) or `1`; completion at `≤ 0` (`Game.ProcessImprovements`/`CompleteImprovement`).

**Integration points (now wired):**
- **Yield** — `Game.TileYieldPotential` adds `ImprovementProduction.YieldDelta(Map.ImprovementsAt(tile), goodsId)` (the multi-improvement overload) **after** the index-10 resource modifiers (improvement `<modifier>`s are additive at index 50), before the coastal-fish bonus. A river's and a plow's flat deltas both land on top of any resource scaling, matching FreeCol's modifier order.
- **River-mouth fish** — after the coastal `+2`, `Game.TileYieldPotential` adds `RiverMouthFishBonus` (+1) for `model.goods.fish` when `IsRiverMouthWater(tile)` — a non-high-seas water tile with at least one adjacent `IsRiverMouthLand` tile (a land tile that has a river *and* a water neighbour). Both bonuses stack (FreeCol applies `fishBonusLand` + `fishBonusRiver`).
- **Movement** — `Game.CheckMove` computes `cost = ImprovementMovement.MoveCost(Map.ImprovementsAt(unit.Position), Map.ImprovementsAt(target), terrain.MoveCost)` for **land units only** (ships never get the bonus), before the partial-movement rule. The bonus applies when both tiles carry a movement-granting improvement (river or road), using the cheapest cost on the destination — so road↔road, river↔river and road↔river steps all pay the reduced cost (1).
- **Rendering** — `RiverOverlay` (a `Node2D` first under `MapView`, so above terrain and below settlements/units/markers) reads `GameMap.HasRiver`/`RiverAt` + the fog sets via `ShowState`. It draws each explored river tile's spokes to the midpoint toward every adjacent explored river tile (a continuous course), with a dark rim under a water fill; an isolated river tile draws a small pool. Style (small/large width) is the render-time connectivity heuristic above. (Roads/plowed fields are not yet drawn — a presentation follow-up.)

**Pioneer build action (`Game`):**
- `CheckBuildImprovement(unit, improvementId)` gates the order (FreeCol `isWorkerAllowed` + `Tile.isImprovementAllowed`): a colonial on-map unit whose `RoleId` grants `CanImproveTerrain` with `RoleCount > 0`, the improvement non-natural, `improvement.AppliesTo(terrain)`, not already present (road/plow), and with movement left. `BuildImprovement` sets `unit.WorkImprovementId`, `unit.WorkTurnsLeft = WorkTurnsToComplete(terrain, improvement)` (= `terrain.WorkTurns + improvement.AddWorkTurns`), and zeroes movement.
- `ProcessImprovements(player)` runs in `RunPlayerTurn` (before the movement reset, alongside `ProcessGotos`): for each of the player's improving units (id order) it subtracts the per-turn work (`2` for a hardy pioneer — the pioneer role's `ExpertUnit` — else `1`) and, at `≤ 0`, calls `CompleteImprovement`. It is **RNG-free and a no-op when nobody is improving**, so the human's stream 0 stays byte-identical (ADR-009).
- `CompleteImprovement(unit)` (FreeCol `csImproveTile`): for a terrain-changing improvement (`ChangeFrom(terrain.Id)` non-null — clear-forest) it `Map.SetTerrain`s the tile to the cleared base and delivers the `<production>` goods (lumber) to `OwningColonyOf(ownerId, tile)` (the player's colony within the tile's 3×3 footprint); otherwise it `Map.AddImprovement`s the road/plow. Either way it spends `ExpendedAmount` of the role count (reverting to the default role at 0) and clears the work-state. `ClearOrders` also abandons an in-progress improvement.

**Persistence (save v48):** `GameMap` carries a sparse `Position → List<TileImprovementType>` layer (multi-valued — a river plus a pioneer road/plow), exposed as `ImprovementsAt`/`RiverAt`/`HasImprovement`/`AllImprovements`, mutated by `AddImprovement`/`RemoveImprovement`/`SetImprovement`/`SetTerrain`. `SaveGame` stores it as `SavedImprovement(index, improvementId, magnitude)` under `Improvements` — **one entry per (tile, improvement)**, ordered by index then id; a tile with a river and a road emits two entries at the same index, grouped back into a list on load. Omitted when the map carries none (byte-identical to v46); a v47 river save (one improvement per tile) round-trips identically. **v48** additionally stores a pioneer's in-progress work as `SavedUnit.WorkImprovement` + `WorkTurnsLeft`, both additive + omitted when the unit is not improving, so a game with no active pioneering stays byte-identical to v47; pre-v48 saves load with no unit improving. On load the ruleset re-supplies each improvement's rule data from the id and re-stamps the saved magnitude.
