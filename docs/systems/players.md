## 1. How it works (plain English)

*Audience: anyone — no jargon, no class names.*

Every side in the game is a **player**. Today there is exactly one — **you**, the human colonial power — but the game is now built so that foreign European powers and the native nations can join as players too, without rewriting the rules. Each player carries its *own* private things: its treasury and tax rate, its *own* European market (prices it sees when it trades), the liberty bells it has banked and the Founding Fathers it has elected, its immigration progress and the colonists waiting on its Europe dock, and the map it has personally explored (its fog of war). The shared world — the map itself, all the units and colonies on it, the native settlements, whose turn it is, and the game's hidden "dice" — stays common to everyone.

**The rules, in plain words:**
- **You pick your nation** when you start a new game: the **Dutch**, **French**, **English** or **Spanish** (or "No nation" to play the plain, advantage-free start). Each power has its own **advantage** — the Dutch are master traders (the prices in their Europe fall more slowly when they sell, so they keep more of their profit), the French get on better with the natives (the natives take less offence at them), the Spanish are fierce against native settlements (they fight harder when assaulting a village), and the English draw immigrants faster (their settlers need fewer crosses to cross the Atlantic). Your chosen nation also names your colonies from its own historical list — a Dutch player's first town is **New Amsterdam**, an English player's is **Jamestown**, and so on. Whichever nation you pick is taken out of the rival pool, so no computer power plays the same one as you. (Leave it on "No nation" and you get exactly the classic, neutral start as before.)
- **You start** the way Colonization does: a **caravel** anchored just off a stretch of coast, carrying a **pioneer** (a colonist with tools, for building roads and clearing land) and a **soldier** (a colonist with muskets). They land on the shore for you to found your first colony and begin exploring.
- The game has several players: **you** (the human), three **foreign powers** (rival European nations), and the **native nations**. The foreign powers now **play for themselves** (a simple computer player): each lands its colonists and ship on the map far away from you — and the rivals now **spread out along the coast** a fair distance apart, rather than all crammed into one corner — founds **up to three colonies**, sends any remaining colonist out to explore — **making for any Lost City Rumour it has spotted** to claim the prize, or, failing that, **walking to the nearest native settlement it has found but not yet visited** to call on its chief — and **runs a small economy** — each colony grows food, puts its spare colonists to work **in its workshops** turning raw goods into more valuable ones (and making hammers to finish whatever it's building), it banks liberty bells toward a Founding Father, its immigration ticks up, it **sells its spare goods in Europe for gold** and **recruits new colonists** when it can afford them; and it now keeps a **soldier or gun standing guard** in its colonies so they aren't easy pickings. If one of its colonies has **no soldier or gun defending it**, the power will **arm an idle colonist standing there into a soldier** — or a **mounted dragoon** if the colony has stocked up both muskets and horses (it prefers the stronger, mounted troop) — spending that colony's own muskets and horses; it keeps those weapons back from the market for this rather than selling them all, and it would rather raise a guard here than send the colonist off to found another undefended colony. A guard that reaches its post then **digs in** (fortifies) for the extra defence a dug-in unit gets, instead of standing in the open. Each colony also **decides what to build next** when its workshop is idle: first a **cannon (artillery)** if the colony has no defender standing on it and can make one, then a **wagon train** if the colony is inland (cut off from the sea) and the power doesn't already own one to haul goods overland, and otherwise the most useful **building** it can afford. If a power ever comes by a **treasure train** — by sacking a native settlement or digging up a Lost City Rumour — it now **walks the treasure home to one of its colonies and banks the gold**, instead of leaving it stranded on the map. A power's **ships now earn their keep**, too: a rival no longer lets the colonists and experts it recruits or trains pile up uselessly on its Europe dock — it **sends a ship across to collect them, sails them to the New World, and drops them straight into the colony that most needs another worker** (the one with the most idle hands and empty workshop seats), where they get to work. As it explores, a power's colonist that can't settle will also **send a missionary into a nearby native village** (if it's carrying one) to set up a mission there, or **speak with a nearby native chief** for a gift — its own first-contact audience, separate from yours (it never uses up your visit). A power also **improves the land around its colonies**: a colonist carrying tools (a **pioneer** — including the one each power starts with, or one it arms at a colony that has stocked up tools) goes out and **builds roads, plows fields, and clears forest** on its colony's tiles, just as you can, spending its tools as it works (it keeps a load of tools back from the market for this rather than selling them all). The **native nations now act too** — their braves roam their lands, and when a tribe is angry enough its braves come and **raid your units** (see [natives](natives.md)).
- A foreign power can now **turn on you by itself**. It used to go to war only if *you* struck first; now, if you keep founding colonies right on its doorstep, its resentment builds up turn after turn, and once that resentment boils over it **declares war on you** — and then its soldiers come for your units and towns (the war behaviour above). Settle a respectful distance away and a power left in peace stays at peace; crowd it and you provoke it. (Its grudge is its own — your own books toward it aren't touched until the war is actually declared, at which point the war is mutual.)
- A foreign power trades and recruits entirely in *its own* Europe: it sells into *its own* market (so its sales never change the prices you see), recruits onto *its own* dock, and spends *its own* gold. It keeps its colony's food (it never sells the food its colonists eat), so it can't starve itself for cash. When a colony has a spare workshop slot it now puts the **best colonist for the job** in it — if it has an expert that matches the workshop (say a master distiller for a rum still) that expert takes the slot, otherwise a plain colonist does. And when a power has gold to spare it now **spends in Europe**: it **trains an expert** of a kind its colonies can use, and **buys a ship** (so it can eventually carry colonists across) or a **cannon** for defence.
- A turn goes around the players in a ring: you take your turn, then each foreign power takes its (computer-played) turn, then each native nation takes its (raid/wander) turn, then the world ticks over (alarm cools, the turn counter advances) and it's your turn again.
- The foreign powers play in their own corner of the map under their own fog — you won't see them or their colonies until your own scouts find them. Their decisions use their own private dice, so they never disturb your game: the same starting seed always plays out identically.
- There is one human player; you reach "your" gold, market, Congress, fog, etc. *through that player*.
- Each player's money, market, fathers, immigration and explored map are its own — they do not leak between players.
- Units and colonies are a single shared list; each one remembers which player owns it (an owner id — the human is 0). Whether two units are friends or enemies, and whose fog a unit lifts, is now decided by *who owns them*, not by "is it native" — so when foreign powers arrive their units are correctly treated as rivals with no extra rules.
- Saving the game writes each player's private state; loading an old save (from before players existed) treats all of it as belonging to the single human.

**Worked example:**
> You sell 100 sugar in Europe. The sugar price you get, and the gold you receive, come from *your* market and *your* treasury. When a rival power arrives later, it will have sold into *its own* market at *its own* price — your prices are unaffected.

**What the player sees and does:** the map now draws **every unit you can see**, not just your first one — all your own on-map units, plus any foreign-power unit or native brave that comes into your line of sight. A unit that isn't yours wears a coloured ground-ring (a rival power's nation colour, or a tribe colour) so you can tell friend from foe at a glance; your own units have none (slice 1c-1). The Europe/colony panels and the HUD still read the same treasury, market, dock, immigration and fog from the human player.

## 2. Detailed rules

*Audience: designers/testers — exact, but still readable.*

- A `Player` owns **only** player-scoped state: identity (`PlayerId`, `NationId`, `IsHuman`, `PlayerType`), `Gold`, `TaxRate`, its own `Market`, `Liberty`/`Congress`/`CurrentFather`/`OfferedFathers`, `Immigration`/`ImmigrationRequired`/`BaseRecruitPrice`/`RecruitLowerCap`/`RecruitDock`, and `Explored`.
- The world stays on `Game`: the map, ruleset, turn counter, the main RNG, units, colonies, native settlements, and the global id counters.
- **Ownership (FP-2):** `Unit.OwnerId` and `Colony.OwnerId` are the authoritative colonial-owner ids (the human is 0; foreign powers have their own ids). Native units carry `OwnerNationId` (the native nation id). The rules resolve ownership through `Game` helpers: `IsHumanOwned`, `IsOwnedBy(unit, player)`, `SameOwner(a, b)`, and the enemy test `AreEnemies(a, b)` = different owner (the **stance hook**). Diplomacy **stance/tension is now recorded** per colonial pair (FP-6a — see [diplomacy](diplomacy.md)), but `AreEnemies` is **still pure owner-inequality**: making it stance-aware (no attacking at peace) would change current behaviour and lands in FP-6b. Founding-father abilities resolve to the unit's owning player (`AbilityForUnit`), so a foreign power never uses the human's fathers.
- **Human start (FreeCol `model.nationType.default` roster):** `Game.New` lands the human's classic starting roster at a **coastal** start tile (settleable land with both a water and a land neighbour, nearest the equator) — a **pioneer** (free colonist + pioneer role), a **soldier** (free colonist + soldier role) and a **caravel** on adjacent water (`SpawnHumanStartingUnits`/`HumanStartingRoster`). Placement is deterministic (no RNG → stream 0 byte-stable); each unit lifts the human's fog. A ruleset variant missing a type, or a landlocked start with no water for the caravel, simply skips that unit. (This replaced the old single-free-colonist start; the foreign powers already used the same per-nation roster via `LandForeignPower`.) The starting **roster does not depend on the chosen nation** — every human starts with the classic caravel + pioneer + soldier; only the advantage + colony names follow the nation (below).
- **Human nation pick (`86d3drn5x`, FreeCol's New-Game nation selection):** `Game.New(humanNationId: …)` seeds the human `Player.NationId` with the chosen European nation (forwarded from the new-game dialog via `GameController.PendingNation`, ADR-006). Because every national advantage is already **nation-id-driven and player-scoped** — `NationTypeModifiers(player, …)` folds the nation type's `<modifier>`s for the Dutch trade bonus (`MarketVolumeFactor`), the French native-alarm reduction (`AccrueAmbientAlarm`), the Spanish offence-vs-natives (`OffenceAgainstNativeFactor`) and the English religious-unrest immigration cut (`ReligiousUnrestFactor`); and `ColonyNamesFor(ownerId)` already reads `player.NationId` for the colony-name list — **seeding the human's `NationId` is the complete wiring**: no per-advantage code changed, the human now folds exactly the modifiers the foreign powers already fold and founds by its nation's names. The pick is **validated** (`ruleset.EuropeanNations.Any(n => n.Id == id && n.Selectable && !n.IsRef)`): an unknown/REF/native id, or null, falls back to the **nation-less** human (no advantage), so the default new game is unchanged. The chosen nation is **excluded from the foreign-power roster** (`SpawnRivalsAndNatives(…, humanNationId)`) so no rival duplicates the human's nation (FreeCol removes the human's nation from the AI pool). **No new RNG draw, no roster change → stream 0 byte-identical (ADR-009)**; the default (no pick) game is byte-identical to before.
- **Players (FP-3b/FP-4):** `Game.New` creates the human (id 0), `ForeignPowerCount` (3) foreign colonial powers (the first selectable non-REF European nations), and one `PlayerType.Native` player per distinct native nation present. Ids are dense (0, then natives, then foreign powers). `Game.Players` is the uniform list; `Game.CurrentPlayer` is the ring pointer (the human between turns). The foreign powers **land on the map** (FP-4) far from the human (`LandForeignPower`: colonists on settleable land, ship on adjacent water, ≥ `ForeignLandingMinDistance` away, deterministic — no RNG); they fall back to docking in Europe only if no on-map tile is free. **Coast spread (`86d3c9w5n`, FreeCol `EuropeanStartingPositionsGenerator`):** each power's anchor is now the farthest free coastal tile from the human that is also ≥ `MinDistanceBetweenPowers` (8 Chebyshev — FreeCol's per-player spacing, scaled to our 36×24 world) from every already-placed power's anchor, so the rivals **spread along the coast** instead of all clustering at one corner; the spacing relaxes automatically (best available tile, then Europe dock) on a crowded/small map so placement never fails. `SpawnRivalsAndNatives` threads the placed anchors and `LandForeignPower` returns its chosen anchor. Still RNG-free → stream 0 byte-stable. Natives stay inert.
- **Foreign-power AI (FP-4 / 1c-2 / 1c-3a′ / 1c-3f / besiege):** `RunForeignPowerTurn` drives each power's units in stable by-id order. **A loaded treasure train acts first** (`86d3c9vq9`, AI logistics, FreeCol `CashInTreasureTrainMission`): a unit that `CarryTreasure` with gold aboard cashes in where it stands if at an owned colony (`CheckCashInTreasureTrain`/`CashInTreasureTrain` — banks the net gold to the power, the train leaves the game), else steps toward the nearest owned colony (`NearestColonyOf(power, …)` → `StepToward`). The cash-in is RNG-free; the step draws the power's own stream. Guarded on owning a loaded treasure train, so a power without one is unaffected (a treasure train was previously a non-founder with no offence → it fell through to "wait" forever). **At war with the human** (`StanceBetween(power, human) == War` — war only starts when the human attacks it), an **armed** unit goes on the offensive: a **land** unit beside an **undefended** human colony captures it (`AdjacentCapturableHumanColony` → `CapturePlayerColony`, 1c-3f — the decisive move, taking priority over the unit-hunt; a garrisoned colony's defender is fought through the unit-hunt first), otherwise it hunts the human's nearest **same-domain** unit (land soldiers hunt land units; **warships hunt the human's ships**, `NearestHumanUnit` domain-matched), attacking when adjacent (`AttackHumanUnit`, 1c-2/1c-3a′) else stepping toward it. **With no field unit to chase, a land unit instead marches on the nearest human colony** (`NearestHumanColony` → `StepToward`, the besiege fallback `86d3bx03d`) — so it closes on a colony to capture rather than wandering off to explore (a garrisoned target's garrison becomes the next field unit; an undefended one is captured on arrival). Otherwise an **armed land unit garrisons the power's colonies** (`86d3c9vxj`, FreeCol `DefendSettlementMission`): founding a new colony keeps first priority, but a unit that won't found marches to the nearest **undefended own colony** — one with no armed land unit on its tile (`NearestUndefendedOwnColony`/`ColonyHasArmedDefender`) — and stands guard there (a unit already in an own colony stays put, no thrash), so the power's colonies aren't left open to capture/pillage. **A guard that reaches/holds its post digs in** (`86d3e49cm`, FreeCol's `DefendSettlementMission` ending in `Unit.fortify`): `GarrisonFortify(unit)` calls `Fortify` when `CheckFortify(unit).Allowed`, both when the unit is already standing on its own colony tile and when its march this turn lands it there — so it ages `Fortifying → Fortified` at the next turn reset for the +50% dig-in (`DefenceContext.Fortified`) instead of standing un-entrenched (a no-op when already fortified, so it isn't re-issued each turn). Only then (at peace) does a colonist found a colony where it stands while the power has fewer than `MaxAiColonies`; failing that, a **missionary-role colonist establishes a mission** in an adjacent native settlement the power does not already hold a mission in (`86d3c9vta` missionary half, FreeCol `MissionaryMission`: `AdjacentUnmissionedSettlement` → `EstablishMission(power, …)` — converting the tribe over time + easing its alarm; the whole mission mechanic is RNG-free, so it never touches the human's stream 0; an Angry/Hateful tribe kills the missionary, a faithful risk the AI accepts exactly as a human one does), then it **speaks with the chief** of an adjacent native settlement it hasn't visited (`86d3dk5mm`, AI scout-chief: `AdjacentUnvisitedSettlement` → `Visit(power, …, RandomFor(power))` for a gift/tales on the power's own stream — **per-player first contact** (`HasBeenVisitedBy`), so it never consumes the human's audience), then **seeks out a known Lost City Rumour** (`86d3c9vta`/`86d3dfj5x`) — `StepToward` the nearest rumour tile in its fog (`NearestKnownRumour`, sparse `GameMap.Rumours`), which auto-resolves on arrival via the existing `TryExploreRumour` (AI-correct: rolls on the power's own stream, auto-investigates strange mounds inline, no human prompt) — then, if it knows of no rumour, **heads for the nearest known unvisited native settlement** (`86d3c9vta` scout facet: `NearestUnvisitedSettlement` → `StepToward` — the nearest native settlement in the power's fog whose chief it `!HasBeenVisitedBy`, so it makes for a concrete chief-audience target rather than wandering, and the adjacent-chief `Visit` branch fires on arrival; fog-gated so it never beelines for an unseen settlement), and only if it knows of neither does it step toward the nearest unexplored tile; ships and unarmed/idle units wait. It is a flat priority switch, **not** a planner. All choices — including the combat/capture — draw from the player's own RNG stream (`RandomFor(player)`; combat via the internal `Attack`/`AttackColony(…, RandomFor(power))` overloads), so it is replay-stable and never disturbs the human's stream 0. A captured human colony is reported via a transient `ColonyLossNotice` (see [combat](combat.md)).
- **AI logistics — transport missions (`86d3c9vq9`, FreeCol `TransportMission`/`WishRealizationMission`/`Wish`):** before the per-unit loop, `RunForeignPowerTurn` calls `RunForeignPowerTransport(power, atWarWithHuman)`, which drives every owned **carrier ship** (`IsNaval && IsCarrier`, not under repair) in stable by-id order — so a carrier on a transport job spends its movement here and **holds position** in the per-unit loop (it has no legal move left) rather than being marched off by the explore fallback. An **armed** carrier at war with the human is skipped (it hunts in the per-unit loop — FreeCol: a transport with no cargo may turn pirate). A one-leg-at-a-time FSM, faithful to FreeCol's collect-then-deliver: **in Europe with passengers** → `SailToNewWorld` (deliver the load); **in Europe, empty** → board own dock colonists, **experts first** (an expert satisfies a stronger worker wish, FreeCol `WorkerWish` prefers the wanted expert; ordered `ExpertProduction is not null` desc then by id), up to the hold (`CargoSlotsFree` → `AiTransportLoad`), then `SailToNewWorld` if it loaded anyone (it waits on the dock with no one to carry / no own colony to deliver to); **on the map carrying passengers** → make for the colony that most wants a worker (`BestWorkerWishColony` — the highest `WorkerWishValue` = idle colonists + empty building work-slots, the lightweight "wish"; ties by id) and, on arrival beside it, put each passenger ashore onto the colony tile (`Disembark`) and `JoinColony` it (the **wish realisation** — the colonist enters the population and is put to work next economy pass); **on the map, empty** → if colonists wait in its Europe, make for the high seas (`NearestHighSeasTile` → `StepToward`) and `SailToEurope` to fetch them, else it is left for the explore fallback. Every move draws the power's **own** stream (`StepToward`); boarding, sailing, disembarking and joining are **RNG-free** — so the human's stream 0 is byte-identical (ADR-009; the soak holds). **No save change** (units ride the existing `_units` list + carrier/passenger ids). This closes the FP-6 gap the economy left open: a recruited/trained/bought colonist no longer rots on the Europe dock. **Deviations (faithful subset):** no multi-stop cargo planner (FreeCol `Cargo`'s 4-waypoint LOAD/PICKUP/UNLOAD/DROPOFF lifecycle, `DESTINATION_UPPER_BOUND=4` route optimisation, the 3-strike DUMP) — each carrier serves one leg; a "wish" is the per-turn `WorkerWishValue` need-score, **not** a persisted posted/assigned `Wish` object; and **goods are not hauled** — our AI economy already auto-sells a colony's surplus directly to its market each turn (`RunForeignPowerEconomy`), so there is no leftover stack to ship and shipping it would double-sell (FreeCol ships goods because its colonies don't auto-sell). So this slice covers the **worker** leg (the real gap) plus the prior **treasure** cash-in; goods stay with the direct-sell economy.
- **AI pioneering (`86d3c9vta`, FreeCol `PioneeringMission`/`TileImprovementPlan`/`pioneersNeeded`):** woven into the peaceful path of `RunForeignPowerTurn`. A unit mid-build is skipped (`unit.IsImproving` — its work is advanced by `ProcessImprovements` at the start of the power's turn, see [rivers-tile-improvements](rivers-tile-improvements.md)). A **tooled pioneer** (`IsPioneer` — a role granting `improveTerrain` with equipment left) is handled **before founding** (a pioneer improves, not founds): it builds the best plan on its own tile (`BuildablePlanOnTile` → `BuildImprovement`) when that tile is one its colonies work, else marches to the nearest planned footprint tile (`NearestImprovementPlanTile` → `StepToward`). Plans are ranked by `AiImprovementPriority` (plow 3 > clear-forest 2 > road 1) over `ColonyFootprintTiles` (each colony tile + its eight neighbours), each gated by `IsWorthwhilePlan` (applicable, not already present, no own pioneer already on it, and a plow only on a tile that farms grain). After founding, an **idle colonist standing in an own colony that stocks tools** is armed as a pioneer (`EquipRole(power, …)`) when the power wants another (`PowerWantsAnotherPioneer`: below its cap — one per colony, capped at `MaxAiPioneers` 2 — and with plannable work); and, rather than explore, an idle colonist marches to the nearest tool-stocked colony to be armed (`NearestEquippableColony`). To make that viable the **economy keeps one pioneer's tools** (`PioneerToolCost` 20) in store instead of selling them all (`RunForeignPowerEconomy`'s sell loop reserves tools when `PowerWantsAnotherPioneer`). The plan ranking, `BuildImprovement` and `EquipRole` are RNG-free; the march draws the power's own stream — so the human's stream 0 stays byte-identical (the soak proves it). A pioneer out of tools reverts to a colonist (and may re-tool), exactly like a human one.
- **AI defence — arming a colonist (`86d3e49cm`, FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.giveNormalMissions` arming + `buyDragoon`'s ARMED+MOUNTED preference + `DefendSettlementMission`):** woven into the peaceful path of `RunForeignPowerTurn`, **before the found-colony branch** (so a defenceless colony raises a guard rather than marching the colonist off to found another undefended colony — expansion yields to defence, mirroring FreeCol computing `badlyDefended` early). An **idle plain-role colonist** (`HasDefaultRole`, `OffenceBase ≤ 0`) standing on its **own** colony tile, when that colony **lacks a military land defender** (`ColonyHasMilitaryDefender`/`IsMilitaryUnit` — the same under-defended test the build planner's artillery trigger uses), is armed to the **strongest affordable** military role: `BestAffordableMilitaryRole` prefers `model.role.dragoon` (50 muskets + 50 horses, the role `buyDragoon` favours for its ARMED+MOUNTED abilities) over `model.role.soldier` (50 muskets), falling back to soldier when horses are short and to no-arm when even muskets are short — via the existing `CheckEquipRole(…).Allowed` then `EquipRole(power, …)`, which **consumes the colony's own muskets/horses** (RNG-free). Because the colony is gated on being under-defended, a defended colony never strips a worker. To make this viable the **economy keeps the equipment back** (`RunForeignPowerEconomy`'s sell loop, `MilitaryReserveFor`): when an under-defended own colony holds an arm-able idle colonist, it reserves the strongest affordable role's muskets (50) — plus horses (50) when a dragoon is affordable — from the surplus sell, the equipment-reserve counterpart of the pioneer-tools reserve, so the economy doesn't cash out the very weapons the arm step spends a turn later (a defended colony, or one with no arm-able colonist, reserves nothing and sells its surplus as usual). The armed colonist **digs in** via the FP-5 garrison branch on its following turn (`Fortify`). The role pick is by ordinal preference + colony stock and `EquipRole`/`Fortify` are RNG-free — so the human's stream 0 stays byte-identical (the soak proves it). **Faithful SUBSET (documented deviation):** a single under-defended check arming one colonist to one role, **not** FreeCol's full `badlyDefended` + `ColonyPlan` military scheduler (no defence-strength target, no multi-unit garrison plan, no Europe dragoon *purchase* — `RunForeignPowerEuropeSpend` still only buys artillery/a ship).
- **Breaking the peace from territorial tension (`86d3c9udb`, FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.determineStances`):** before the unit loop, `RunForeignPowerTurn` calls `AccrueTerritorialTension(power)` — for a power at `Peace`/`CeaseFire` with the human (a no-op when `Uncontacted` or already `War`), it adds `TensionLandTaken` (200, FreeCol `Tension.TENSION_ADD_LAND_TAKEN`) to the power's tension toward the human **per human colony** within `ColonialTensionRadius` (3 Chebyshev tiles, the colony's 3×3 work footprint + a 2-tile buffer; a colony carries no settlement type, so this is a flat constant) of any of the power's own colonies. The accrual is **directional** (`ChangeTension(power, human, …, symmetric: false)` — only the power's view rises, mirroring FreeCol where the territory owner gains the tension). The turn then re-derives the stance via `StanceFromTension(currentStance, tensionTowardHuman)`; on a Peace/CeaseFire→War flip it **declares war exactly as an attack does** — `SetStance(power, human, War)` + symmetric `ChangeTension(power, human, TensionWar)` — so the end-of-turn `UpdateColonialStances` (which re-derives the pair's stance from the *lower-id* player's tension) keeps it at War instead of immediately de-escalating from the human's still-zero directional tension. This makes the `StanceFromTension` Peace→War branch (>`TensionWar`+DELTA = 1010) **reachable in play**: before it, colonial tension only ever rose on an attack, which already set War directly, so a power never declared war on its own. The accrual + declaration are entirely **RNG-free** (no `RandomFor` draw, no stream-0 draw), so a default game (no human colony crowding a power) stays byte-identical (ADR-009). Per-turn `DecayColonialTension` still cools tension, so a war that ends (decays to ≤590 → CeaseFire) can re-flare if the human keeps crowding — a faithful oscillation. `AccrueTerritorialTension` is `internal` (ADR-006) so the RNG-free accrual is asserted directly against `RandomState`. See [diplomacy](diplomacy.md).
- **Foreign-power economy (FP-5):** every colonial player (the human and the foreign powers) now runs the same economy each turn — `RunColonyTurn` for each owned colony, then `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` + `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate`. A foreign power then runs `RunForeignPowerEconomy`: (1) **pursue a Founding Father** — if it has none chosen and any is offered, pick one from its offered set by its own RNG (so banked liberty is eventually elected, then `RefreshDockForRecruitability` runs for its own dock); (1b) **plan each colony's workers** (`86d3c9vmr`, see the AI-colony-economy note in §3) — `PlanColonyTileWork` staffs cash crops + food onto the best tiles (food-first), then `PlanColonyBuildingWork` sends the remaining idle colonists to the best production buildings (refineries/carpenter/town hall), so worked tiles **and** staffed workshops (not just the unattended centre) feed the economy before selling; (2) **sell surplus** — for each owned colony, in stable id order, sell every *tradeable* good's stock above its reserve to the power's **own** market via `SellColonyGoods(power, …)`, **never `Colony.FoodId`** (food is a tradeable market good in the classic spec, so it is excluded explicitly to avoid starving the colony for gold). The reserve is `AiTradeReserve` (0) for most goods, but **tools** are reserved (`PioneerToolCost` 20) when the power wants another pioneer (see AI pioneering), and **muskets/horses** are reserved (`MilitaryReserveFor`) when an under-defended own colony holds an arm-able idle colonist (see AI defence — arming) — so the economy doesn't cash out the equipment the pioneer/arm steps spend later; (3) **recruit** — while it has fewer than `AiMaxEuropeRecruits` (2) of its own colonists waiting in Europe and can afford `CheckRecruit(power, 0)`, recruit from its own dock. The cap bounds the per-turn Europe recruiting (the AI transport mission `86d3c9vq9` now **ships** the dock colonists over — see the AI-logistics bullet above); (3b) **Europe spend** (`86d3c9vmr` inc 6, `RunForeignPowerEuropeSpend`, run **before** the recruit loop so a deliberate expert is preferred over a random recruit and isn't crowded out of the person cap) — when its treasury is above `AiEuropeSpendFloor` (1500), it invests the surplus, at most one unit of each kind per turn: **train** the cheapest affordable specialist (`CheckTrain`/`TrainUnit`) whose `ExpertProduction` matches an **exact** good one of its colonies is producing (or the one-step refined good of it) — `WantedColonyGoods` — while under the Europe person cap; then **buy** the cheapest affordable carrier ship (`CheckBuyUnit`/`BuyUnit`) if it owns no naval carrier (`OwnsNavalCarrier`), else **buy artillery** for defence. The spend is by gold/ordinal id — **no RNG draw**. All RNG draws use `RandomFor(power)` (the father pick, the recruit/emigrate dock draws, the offer weights); all trade is on `power.Market`; founding-father production modifiers fold the power's **own** Congress (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`, `HasAbilityFor(player, …)`) — so a foreign economy is fully isolated from the human's stream 0 and market. Foreign powers can't yet **buy goods** in Europe (that needs a docked ship — FP-6); a bought ship and a trained/recruited expert are now **shipped to the New World** by the AI transport mission (`86d3c9vq9`, see the AI-logistics bullet above) rather than idling on the dock.
- **Turn ring:** `EndTurn` walks `_players` from `_currentPlayerIndex` (via `NextPlayerIndex`), calling `RunPlayerTurn` on each — a colonial player's colonies produce and it accrues liberty/immigration, then a foreign power additionally runs its economy + unit AI; a **native nation runs `RunNativeTurn`** (its braves raid the human when alarmed, else wander — slice 1b, see [natives](natives.md)) — then runs the world steps (sailing, native alarm decay, movement reset, `Turn++`) once and returns to the human. `RunPlayerTurn` dispatches by `PlayerType`: a native runs its raid/wander AI and returns; the human and the foreign powers share the produce/accrue path (the human's path is byte-identical to before — it folds its own Congress and draws from stream 0), and only foreign powers additionally run the economy/unit AI.
- **Per-player RNG (ADR-009):** each player has a deterministic PCG stream — the human is stream 0 (`Game._random`); each non-human player gets its own `Player.Rng` = `new Pcg32Random(seed, RngStreamId)` (id `PlayerId + 1`, avoiding the native-placement stream 1), created at `New` and saved/restored with its state (so resume is exact). The AI reads it via `RandomFor`. Because each stream is independent, foreign-power AI draws never shift stream 0.
- **Fog per owner (FP-4):** `RevealForOwner(unit)` lifts the **owning** colonial player's fog (the human's for a human unit, a foreign power's for its own); native units lift none. So a foreign power explores under its own fog, and the human never sees a foreign colony until a human unit discovers its tile (the presentation also gates colony markers on the human's explored set).
- The human is **player id 0**, `IsHuman = true`, `PlayerType = Colonial`, and `NationId` = the nation chosen at New Game (`86d3drn5x`) or **null** for the classic nation-less default (no advantage; the byte-identical baseline). It is found via `Game.HumanPlayer` / `IsHuman`, **never by list index** (turn order is spawn/variant-driven).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** for FP-1 the human draws from the game's single main RNG stream (stream 0); the refactor does not add, remove, or reorder any RNG draw, so seeded games, goldens and save-resume stay byte-identical. Per-player RNG streams arrive with the foreign powers.

|---|---|
| Read `game.Gold` / `Market` / `Congress` / `Explored` / `RecruitPrice` … | Pass-through to `game.HumanPlayer.X` (human-only) |
| Sell/Buy/Recruit/Visit/trade (a mutating action) | Public method delegates to an internal `Player`-taking overload, which mutates that player's state |

**Deviations from FreeCol:** FreeCol keeps each player's `units`/`settlements` as live lists on the player; we keep units and colonies as **flat global lists referenced by an owner id** (FreeCol itself treats those lists as derived, not the source of truth). Founding-father *production* modifiers fold **per player** from FP-5 (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`, `HasAbilityFor(player, …)`; the public no-player overloads delegate to the human for presentation/tests, behaviourally identical to before); combat abilities already resolved per-owner (`AbilityForUnit`, FP-2). Live visibility (`CurrentlyVisible`/`IsVisible`) is filtered to the **human's own** units + colonies (FP-2); it's still a single human fog (one viewer) — true per-player fog is unneeded while only the human has a screen. Natives are `Player` rows; a native unit's owner is its `OwnerNationId` (its `OwnerId` stays unused, 0), and the native AI resolves a unit's owning player by matching `Player.NationId == OwnerNationId` (no separate owner-id wiring needed — slice 1b). The foreign-power AI is a deliberate **flat per-unit priority switch** (FP-4) plus a growing economy: it wages war (1c-2/1c-3a′/1c-3f/besiege) and, from `86d3c9vmr`, **staffs each colony's tiles** with cash crops + food (`PlanColonyTileWork`, the tile subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan` — see §3). From `86d3c9vmr` it also **chooses what to build** (`RunForeignColonyBuildPlan`, the building subset of FreeCol's build planner — see §3) and, from increment 4b, **buildable units** before buildings: artillery for an under-defended colony, then a wagon train for an inland one. From `86d3c9vmr` (increment 5) it also **staffs each colony's buildings** (`PlanColonyBuildingWork`, the building subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan.assignWorkers`): after the food-first tile plan, the colonists still idle are sent to the highest-value production buildings (refineries, the carpenter's house, the town hall). With the colony economy proven, **`MaxAiColonies` was lifted from 1 to 3** (a power now founds up to three colonies; the soak confirms multiple AI colonies stay fed + solvent). From `86d3c9vmr` increment 6 it also **picks the best worker for each workshop** (`PlanColonyBuildingWork` now assigns the idle colonist that adds the most value — its matching expert if one is idle, else a free colonist; FreeCol `getBestWorker`) and, when flush, **trains an expert + buys a ship/artillery in its own Europe** (`RunForeignPowerEuropeSpend`). From `86d3drn5d` it also **ranks colony SITES by its national advantage** (`ScoreColonySite` — a trade power values goods by sale price + prizes coastal sites, a production power values raw yield volume; see §3); when founding an **expansion** colony it hill-climbs one step toward a strictly-better adjacent site before settling (its first colony still settles its landing immediately). It still does **not**: apply the ×1.2 national-advantage ranking to the *building/worker* planner (only site selection) or run FreeCol's full expert-swap (it places idle experts marginally, but does not evict a free colonist from a slot to seat a freshly-arrived expert); it cannot buy goods in Europe (no docked ship hauls them yet, FP-6). From `86d3c9vq9` it **does** now ship its Europe colonists/experts to the New World — the AI transport mission (`RunForeignPowerTransport`) ferries them over and lands them into the colony that most wants a worker (a faithful subset of FreeCol `TransportMission`/`WishRealizationMission`: one leg at a time, a per-turn worker-wish need-score not a persisted `Wish`, and no goods haul since the economy auto-sells surplus — see §2). It founds with no minimum-distance-between-colonies rule beyond the universal colony-spacing check, and its first colonists land directly on the map rather than sailing from Europe.

## 3. Technical design

*Audience: developers / future Claude sessions.*

**Domain model:**
- `Player` (`GameSession/Player.cs`) — `sealed class` holding player-scoped state. Public read API (identity + scalars with `internal set`; `IReadOnlyList`/`IReadOnlySet` views over private collections), with `internal` mutable accessors (`CongressList`, `OfferedFathersList`, `RecruitDockList`, `ExploredSet`) used by the rules on `Game`. `RecruitPrice` is computed here. `PlayerType` enum = `{ Colonial, Native }`.
- `Game` holds `IReadOnlyList<Player> Players`, a cached `HumanPlayer` (the human established at creation by its `IsHuman` role), and exposes the former player fields as thin pass-through properties to `HumanPlayer`. Mutating seams have an internal `(Player player, …)` overload; the public method delegates to `HumanPlayer`.
- `RestoredPlayer` (`GameSession/Player.cs`) — a domain DTO (positions, dictionaries) handed to `Game.Restore`, keeping `Game` free of persistence-format types.

**Algorithms & formulas:** the turn/economy formulas are unchanged — the same methods now read/write a `Player` and draw from `RandomFor(player)`: `RunColonyTurn(owner, colony)`, `TileYield(player, …)`, `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`/`HasAbilityFor(player, …)`, `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers(player)`, `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate(player)`, `GenerateOffers(player)`, the `Init/Draw/Emigrate` recruit-dock helpers, and `Reveal/RevealAround(player, …)`. `CreateEuropeRecruit(player, …)` and `BuyUnit(player, …)` stamp `OwnerId`. The FP-5 economic policy lives in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (constants `AiTradeReserve` = 0, `AiMaxEuropeRecruits` = 2) + `OwnPersonsInEurope`. Its first step — banking toward a father — picks the offered father by **value, not at random** (`SelectFoundingFatherFor`, `86d3e49ej`): a custom-house grantor (Stuyvesant) outright, else the highest `WeightForAge(CurrentAge)`, ties by ordinal id — an RNG-free SELECT faithful to FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.selectFoundingFather` (the offer SET is still seeded by `GenerateOffers`). See [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md).

**AI colony economy — owner-scoped assignment seams (`86d3c9vmr` increment 1).** The full-fidelity AI colony planner (worker-assignment → build-queue → Europe spend, a faithful port of FreeCol `ColonyPlan`) is being built in CI+soak-green increments. Increment 1 lays the foundation: player-scoped overloads `CheckAssignWork(player, …)`, `AssignWork(player, …)` and `TileWorkOptions(player, …)` that rank/gate by *that player's* tile yields (`TileYield(player, …)`, folding its own founding fathers) instead of the human's — so a foreign power's planner will rank tiles by its own economy. `AutoAssignIdleToFood` is now owner-aware (the public form routes to `PlayerById(colony.OwnerId)`), fixing a latent bug where AI colonies ranked food tiles by the *human's* fathers; behaviour-identical in the classic ruleset (no father affects grain), so the soak stays byte-stable. **Increment 3 — the worker planner (`PlanColonyTileWork`).** Called per foreign-power colony in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (after that turn's `RunColonyTurn`, before the sell loop), it ports the tile subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan`: (a) **rank** the tradeable, tile-farmed, non-food raws — value = Σ neighbour `TileYield(owner,…)` × the raw's market sale price — top two kept (ties by ordinal id); (b) **greedily fill** the worker slots (`Population − building workers`) — while the plan's **projected net food** is positive, work the top cash raws round-robin on their best tiles; otherwise work the best **food** tile across every farmed food good (grain on land, **fish** on ocean), so the colony never plans itself into starvation, landing the matching expert via `PickIdleWorkerFor`; (c) **diff-apply** the plan — a tile already worked with the planned good is left untouched, so a colonist keeps accruing on-the-job experience (`86d3c9pgj`) on a stable tile rather than being churned each turn. The projected net food uses the same survival formula as the live `ColonyNetFood` query (increment 2), computed over the plan-in-progress (with type-blind free-colonist yields — an expert that lands later only raises real output, so the food guard is conservative); `ColonyNetFood` itself is the live-colony query, exercised by tests and consumed by the later Europe-spend increment. All per-tile yields are computed once into a small matrix (the modifier fold is the cost) so re-planning every turn stays within the <2 ms EndTurn budget. **One-turn lag:** because the planner runs *after* this turn's `RunColonyTurn`, a freshly-staffed tile first produces **next** turn — this turn's sell loop sells what the *previous* plan (+ centre) produced. This is coherent in steady state and keeps the pass deterministic. **RNG-free** (pure ordinal/yield ranking → human stream 0 untouched); the SoL `ProductionBonus` is invariant to assignment in our model (keys off population+liberty, not worked-unit count), so FreeCol's production-bonus guard does not apply, but a tile's **base** yield must be >0 before the bonus is added (a positive bonus must never make a barren tile look workable). **No save change.** These behaviours are tested in `AiColonyEconomyTests`.

**Increment 5 — the building-worker planner (`PlanColonyBuildingWork`) + the `MaxAiColonies` lift.** Called per foreign-power colony in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` immediately **after** `PlanColonyTileWork` (so the food-first tile plan runs first and the building fill only ever sees colonists left idle *after* it — it can never pull a food worker, keeping the colony's survival margin a soak invariant). It ports the building subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan.assignWorkers`: it builds an **available-goods set** = the colony's stored goods + its centre-tile unattended outputs + its worked-tile outputs, then values each building by the per-turn **market value of the output its next free-colonist worker would add** — for each non-unattended, non-breeding production entry whose every input is in that available set, Σ (per-worker `ApplyWorkerProductionModifiers(freeColonist, …)` output × the good's market `BidPrice` for a tradeable refined good, or a fixed `NonTradeableOutputValue` for hammers/tools/bells/crosses). **Construction materials are valued high (16) while a build is queued** (FreeCol's HAMMERS priority — so the carpenter's house out-ranks a refinery and construction actually finishes) and modestly (8) otherwise; bells (6) then crosses (4) below. It greedily staffs the single highest-valued building with a free workplace (ties by ordinal building id), folds that building's own output into the available set (so a downstream building can unlock), and repeats until no idle colonist or no fundable building remains. A building with no fed entry scores 0 and is skipped (a worker there would produce nothing). **RNG-free** (pure value/ordinal ranking → human stream 0 untouched). With this proven, **`MaxAiColonies` was lifted 1 → 3** (`internal const`, so AI tests drive a power to its cap without hard-coding the number); the soak (25 seeds × 200 turns) stays green — no starvation, no negative treasury, byte-identical save round-trip, within the 2 ms budget — confirming multiple AI colonies stay fed and solvent. **Deviations (since superseded by increment 6 for the worker choice):** not FreeCol's input-exhaustion output-scaling. **No save change.** Tested in `AiColonyEconomyTests` (refinery fed from store and from centre production; unfed refinery skipped; carpenter staffed when a build is queued vs refinery preferred when idle; never exceeds idle colonists; food tile workers untouched; RNG-free; deterministic; the end-to-end `ForeignPowerEconomy_StaffsAForeignColonysBuildings`).

**Increment 6 — best-worker building fill + Europe train/buy (`86d3c9vmr`, FreeCol `getBestWorker` + `trainAIUnitInEurope`).** Two refinements complete the worker/spend half of the planner. (a) **Best-worker fill:** `PlanColonyBuildingWork` no longer values every building by a free colonist and assigns one — for each candidate building it now picks the **best idle worker** (`BestIdleWorkerFor`): it tries a free colonist (idle whenever `IdleColonists` exceeds the specialist overlay) plus each distinct idle specialist type (ordinal order), values the building with that worker's own index-30 modifiers folded in (`BuildingWorkerValue(b, workerType)`), and keeps the worker with the highest value — so a building's **matching expert** (master distiller ×2 rum) tops it and wins the slot, while a free colonist is preferred on a tie (an unrelated expert is left idle for a slot it actually boosts). The chosen worker is seated via the new `internal AssignBuildingWork(colony, buildingId, type)` overload (a specialist must be in the idle overlay; otherwise it falls back to a free colonist). Faithful to FreeCol's "the unit that most improves production wins the slot, favouring the expert"; **RNG-free** (pure value/ordinal ranking). (b) **Europe spend:** `RunForeignPowerEuropeSpend` (called in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` **before** the recruit loop, so a deliberate wanted expert is preferred over a random recruit and isn't crowded out of the person cap) — when `power.Gold ≥ AiEuropeSpendFloor` (1500), it trains the cheapest affordable specialist whose **exact** `ExpertProduction` is in `WantedColonyGoods(power)` while under `AiMaxEuropeRecruits`, then buys the cheapest affordable carrier ship if `!OwnsNavalCarrier(power)` (else artillery), at most one big-ticket unit per turn. `WantedColonyGoods` is the **exact** goods the colonies produce (each worked-tile good + centre unattended output, kept distinct so grain≠fish even though both store as food) plus the one-step refined goods makeable from those raws (cloth-from-cotton, rum-from-sugar…) — transient warehouse stock is excluded (it conflates storage ids and the sell loop drains it first). The seams: `internal CheckTrain`/`TrainUnit(player, unitTypeId)` (price via `EuropeUnitPrice`, flat for classic specialists — no per-type ratchet; the unit docks as a person via `CreateEuropeRecruit`, owner-stamped) and the existing owner-scoped `BuyUnit(player, …)`. Every choice is by gold/ordinal id — **no RNG draw**, all spend the power's **own** gold → human stream 0 byte-identical (ADR-009). **No save change** (the trained/bought unit rides the existing `_units` list + owner id). **Deviations:** it places idle experts marginally but does **not** run FreeCol's full expert-swap (evicting a seated free colonist to seat a freshly-arrived expert), apply the national-advantage ranking, or **ship** the Europe units over (FP-6 transport AI). Tested in `AiColonyEconomyTests` (best-worker: matching expert seated when it's the sole idle colonist, the expert routed to its matching building and never an unrelated one, RNG-free; Europe spend: `CheckTrain` gates on type+gold, `TrainUnit` docks an owned specialist spending its own gold, the spend trains a wanted specialist + buys a ship when flush, buys nothing when poor, and leaves the human's stream 0 byte-identical under a heavy-spend perturbation).

**National-advantage colony-site ranking (`86d3drn5d`, FreeCol `Player.getColonyValue` × `AIPlayer.getAIAdvantage`).** A foreign power now weights candidate colony sites by its **national advantage** — the analogue, for *where to settle*, of the ×1.2 advantage tilt FreeCol's `ColonyPlan` applies to *what to build*. `ScoreColonySite(power, tile)` sums, over the centre tile and its eight **land** neighbours, the potential output of every good the tile could yield (via `TileYieldPotential`); **what that output is worth depends on the power's advantage** (`AdvantageOf(power)` = `NationType.ShortName`, the analogue of FreeCol `getAIAdvantage` — `trade`/`conquest`/`cooperation`/`immigration`, or `""` for a power with no nation):
- **No nation / neutral advantage** (`cooperation`/`immigration`/none) — each good is valued at its **European sale price** (`GoodsUnitValue` = the good's own market price, or its `StoredAs` good's price for food-class goods grain/fish which have no market of their own, food = 1). This is the un-tilted **base**; the human, who plans no sites here, is never scored, so a default game is byte-identical.
- **`trade`** (Dutch) — the same price-weighted base, but each tile's tradeable value is taken at **×1.2** (`ColonySiteAdvantageFactor`) and a **coastal** site (any water neighbour — a port a ship can reach) earns another **×1.2**. A trade power therefore prizes coastal, high-value cash sites.
- **`conquest`** (Spanish — the production archetype) — value is the **raw yield amount** itself, *ignoring* market price, with a tile's contribution taken at ×1.2 when its best single-good yield reaches the `GoodProductionYield` band (FreeCol `GOOD_PRODUCTION` = 4). A production power therefore prizes high-output land (ore/grain volume) even when it sells cheaply. So the **same two tiles are ranked in opposite order** by a trade vs a production power — the test asserts exactly this. (Scores are **not comparable across advantages** — each scores on its own scale; only same-advantage comparisons rank sites.)

The score is wired into the founding branch of `RunForeignPowerTurn`: when a non-pioneer founder can found and the power **already owns a colony** (an *expansion* colony), `BetterAdjacentColonySite` compares the unit's current tile against each legal adjacent site it can step to (`IsLegalColonySite` + `CheckMove`) and, if a neighbour scores **strictly** higher, the founder hill-climbs **one step** toward it (`StepToward`) to found from the better tile next turn. Because terrain is fixed and only a *strictly*-better neighbour lures it, the walk reaches a local maximum in a few steps and then founds (no thrash, guaranteed termination). The power's **first** colony is never deferred — it settles its landing immediately, so a power always gets a turn-one foothold (this is what keeps `MultiPlayerTests`/`DiplomacyTests`, which assert a rival colony exists after one `EndTurn`, green). `ScoreColonySite` is a **pure read** of terrain + market seed (no RNG, no mutation) and the step draws the power's **own** stream, so the human's stream 0 stays byte-identical (ADR-009); **no save change** (no new persisted state). **Deviations (documented):** the site score is the *advantage-ranking subset* of FreeCol's `getColonyValue` — it omits the high-seas-distance, settlement-spacing, food-floor and resource-penalty categories (legality + spacing are still enforced by `CheckFoundColony`/`IsLegalColonySite`), and the advantage tilt is applied to **site selection only**, not yet to the building/worker planner.

**Difficulty-scoped AI tuning (`86d3drn56`).** Three foreign-power AI constants moved off their `Game.cs` consts onto `Ruleset.Difficulty.Ai` (an `AiTuning` value record — see [difficulty](difficulty.md)): the **colony cap** `MaxColonies` (the founding checks read `Ruleset.Difficulty.Ai.MaxColonies`; `Game.MaxAiColonies` is now an `internal static` alias over the classic value, preserving the test API), the **Europe spend floor** `EuropeSpendFloor` (`AiEuropeSpendFloor` is now an instance property over `…Ai.EuropeSpendFloor`), and the offensive **seek/travel range ladder** `SeekRangeLadder` (the seek-and-destroy loops read `…Ai.SeekRangeLadder`). This is a **faithful subset**: FreeCol hardcodes all three in `EuropeanAIPlayer` (`nColonies < 3`, the `getSeekAndDestroyMission` 8/12/16 ladder; the spend floor is our own AI-budget concept) and exposes **no** `model.difficulty.*` option for any of them, so they are held at the classic-medium values (3 / 1500 / 8-12-16) across every level — but are now **data-overridable** so a difficulty level or an Australia-style variant could make the rival AI tougher without a code change. Value-preserving (default game byte-identical; no save change).

**Increment 4 — the build-queue (`RunForeignColonyBuildPlan`).** Called per foreign-power colony in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (after the sell loop); when the colony has **nothing queued**, it picks one buildable. **Buildable UNITS take precedence over buildings** (increment 4b — FreeCol `StandardAIPlayer`/`ColonyPlan` defence + transport priorities), in a fixed deterministic order, then the building plan falls through:
1. **Artillery when under-defended** — if **no** `IsMilitaryUnit` land unit owned by the colony's owner stands on the colony tile (`ColonyHasMilitaryDefender`), **and** `model.unit.artillery` is in `BuildableUnits(colony)` (which gates on the armory build-ability + hammers/tools availability), queue artillery. FreeCol prioritises defence for an undefended colony.
2. **Wagon train for inland transport** — else, if the colony is **landlocked** (`!IsColonyCoastal` — no adjacent water, so no ship can serve it) **and** the owner owns no `model.unit.wagonTrain` anywhere yet (`OwnerOwnsWagonTrain`), **and** the wagon is in `BuildableUnits(colony)` (the free carpenter's house enables it; the one-per-colony `units lt settlements` limit is enforced inside `CheckSetBuild`), queue a wagon train.
3. **Otherwise the building plan** — builds the highest-value building (FreeCol `ColonyPlan.updateBuildableTypes`, building subset). Value = `BuildingBuildWeight ÷ difficulty`: the **weight** is the max over the classes the building belongs to — production/building 0.9, storage 0.85, liberty 0.75, export 0.6, military 0.4, fortify 0.3, refined-production 0.25, teach 0.2, breeding/repair 0.1 (classified from `BuildingType`'s `DefenceBonus`/`GrantsExport`/`BellBonus`/`WarehouseStorage`/`Teaches`/`RepairsNavalUnits`/`BreedingDivisor`/its produced goods), each × its support (1.0, except liberty 0.01 at full SoL); **difficulty** = `max(1, sqrt(Σ shortfall of required goods × (the good's input farmable here ? 1 : 5)))`. Buildings above the colony's size-profile level (`≤2→1, ≤4→2, ≤8→3, else 4`) are skipped, **except** defence/export (always considered).

Buildability is always discovered through `BuildableUnits`/`Buildables` (= `CheckSetBuild(...).Allowed`), never re-derived. An in-progress build is left untouched; `RunConstruction` already completes it over turns and a lone queued unit repeats until capped (`REMOVE_EXCEPT_LAST`). **RNG-free** — the unit triggers and the building ranking are pure ordinal/weight tests, drawing **no** RNG at all (it runs on a foreign power's own turn, so even were it to draw it would not touch human stream 0; it draws nothing). **No save change** (a unit id rides the existing build-queue strings). **Deviations:** this is **not** FreeCol's full value-ranking of units against buildings or its transport-route/wish planning — just the two unit triggers (defence, then inland transport) plus the building fallback (simplest faithful subset). The per-AI-advantage ×1.1/1.2 nudges are not applied; supports are mostly FreeCol's 1.0/FIXME constants; **docks**/`PRODUCE_IN_WATER` (FreeCol's FISH class) are not modelled, so docks aren't built — immaterial, since our worker planner already works ocean fish tiles without them (the drydock/shipyard chain is still classified via `RepairsNavalUnits`); and the **PRODUCTION** class fires for any refinery whose raw is farmable on a neighbour tile rather than FreeCol's top-2 produce-set (same-class candidates tie at 0.25, so difficulty breaks the tie — small impact).

**Integration points:** `Game.New` constructs the human `Player` (with its `Market`), then the foreign powers (each with its own `Rng` and a recruit dock drawn from its own stream); `Game.Restore` builds each player from a `RestoredPlayer` and tops up every colonial player's dock (a no-op for a full FP-5 dock, a fresh draw for an empty pre-FP-5 one). `EndTurn` → `RunPlayerTurn` runs the produce/accrue path for every colonial player and the AI for the foreign powers.

**Persistence:** see [save-load](save-load.md). Save **v20** introduced a `Players[]` array (`SavedPlayer` record: identity + gold/tax/market/liberty/Congress/immigration/dock + explored as compact row-major indexes). Load path is keyed on `Version >= 20`; ≤v19 saves fold the flat fields into one human player (`SaveGame.FoldFlatFieldsToHumanPlayer`). A v20 save still writes the flat fields for now (every pre-v20 load path stays exercised); they are dropped at FP-7.
