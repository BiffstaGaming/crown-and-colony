## 1. How it works (plain English)

*Audience: anyone — no jargon, no class names.*

Every side in the game is a **player**. Today there is exactly one — **you**, the human colonial power — but the game is now built so that foreign European powers and the native nations can join as players too, without rewriting the rules. Each player carries its *own* private things: its treasury and tax rate, its *own* European market (prices it sees when it trades), the liberty bells it has banked and the Founding Fathers it has elected, its immigration progress and the colonists waiting on its Europe dock, and the map it has personally explored (its fog of war). The shared world — the map itself, all the units and colonies on it, the native settlements, whose turn it is, and the game's hidden "dice" — stays common to everyone.

**The rules, in plain words:**
- The game has several players: **you** (the human), three **foreign powers** (rival European nations), and the **native nations**. The foreign powers now **play for themselves** (a simple computer player): each lands its colonists and ship on the map far away from you, founds a colony, sends its remaining colonist out to explore, and **runs a small economy** — its colony grows food and produces goods, it banks liberty bells toward a Founding Father, its immigration ticks up, it **sells its spare goods in Europe for gold** and **recruits new colonists** when it can afford them; and it now keeps a **soldier or gun standing guard** in its colonies so they aren't easy pickings. The **native nations now act too** — their braves roam their lands, and when a tribe is angry enough its braves come and **raid your units** (see [natives](natives.md)).
- A foreign power trades and recruits entirely in *its own* Europe: it sells into *its own* market (so its sales never change the prices you see), recruits onto *its own* dock, and spends *its own* gold. It keeps its colony's food (it never sells the food its colonists eat), so it can't starve itself for cash.
- A turn goes around the players in a ring: you take your turn, then each foreign power takes its (computer-played) turn, then each native nation takes its (raid/wander) turn, then the world ticks over (alarm cools, the turn counter advances) and it's your turn again.
- The foreign powers play in their own corner of the map under their own fog — you won't see them or their colonies until your own scouts find them. Their decisions use their own private dice, so they never disturb your game: the same starting seed always plays out identically.
- There is one human player; you reach "your" gold, market, Congress, fog, etc. *through that player*.
- Each player's money, market, fathers, immigration and explored map are its own — they do not leak between players.
- Units and colonies are a single shared list; each one remembers which player owns it (an owner id — the human is 0). Whether two units are friends or enemies, and whose fog a unit lifts, is now decided by *who owns them*, not by "is it native" — so when foreign powers arrive their units are correctly treated as rivals with no extra rules.
- Saving the game writes each player's private state; loading an old save (from before players existed) treats all of it as belonging to the single human.

**Worked example:**
> You sell 100 sugar in Europe. The sugar price you get, and the gold you receive, come from *your* market and *your* treasury. When a rival power arrives later, it will have sold into *its own* market at *its own* price — your prices are unaffected.

**What the player sees and does:** the map now draws **every unit you can see**, not just your first one — all your own on-map units, plus any foreign-power unit or native brave that comes into your line of sight. A unit that isn't yours wears a coloured ground-ring (a rival power's nation colour, or a tribe colour) so you can tell friend from foe at a glance; your own units have none (slice 1c-1). The Europe/colony panels and the HUD still read the same treasury, market, dock, immigration and fog from the human player.

## 2. Detailed rules

*Audience: designers/testers — exact, but still readable.*

- A `Player` owns **only** player-scoped state: identity (`PlayerId`, `NationId`, `IsHuman`, `PlayerType`), `Gold`, `TaxRate`, its own `Market`, `Liberty`/`Congress`/`CurrentFather`/`OfferedFathers`, `Immigration`/`ImmigrationRequired`/`BaseRecruitPrice`/`RecruitLowerCap`/`RecruitDock`, and `Explored`.
- The world stays on `Game`: the map, ruleset, turn counter, the main RNG, units, colonies, native settlements, and the global id counters.
- **Ownership (FP-2):** `Unit.OwnerId` and `Colony.OwnerId` are the authoritative colonial-owner ids (the human is 0; foreign powers have their own ids). Native units carry `OwnerNationId` (the native nation id). The rules resolve ownership through `Game` helpers: `IsHumanOwned`, `IsOwnedBy(unit, player)`, `SameOwner(a, b)`, and the enemy test `AreEnemies(a, b)` = different owner (the **stance hook**). Diplomacy **stance/tension is now recorded** per colonial pair (FP-6a — see [diplomacy](diplomacy.md)), but `AreEnemies` is **still pure owner-inequality**: making it stance-aware (no attacking at peace) would change current behaviour and lands in FP-6b. Founding-father abilities resolve to the unit's owning player (`AbilityForUnit`), so a foreign power never uses the human's fathers.
- **Players (FP-3b/FP-4):** `Game.New` creates the human (id 0), `ForeignPowerCount` (3) foreign colonial powers (the first selectable non-REF European nations), and one `PlayerType.Native` player per distinct native nation present. Ids are dense (0, then natives, then foreign powers). `Game.Players` is the uniform list; `Game.CurrentPlayer` is the ring pointer (the human between turns). The foreign powers **land on the map** (FP-4) far from the human (`LandForeignPower`: colonists on settleable land, ship on adjacent water, ≥ `ForeignLandingMinDistance` away, deterministic — no RNG); they fall back to docking in Europe only if no on-map tile is free. Natives stay inert.
- **Foreign-power AI (FP-4 / 1c-2 / 1c-3a′ / 1c-3f / besiege):** `RunForeignPowerTurn` drives each power's units in stable by-id order. **At war with the human** (`StanceBetween(power, human) == War` — war only starts when the human attacks it), an **armed** unit goes on the offensive: a **land** unit beside an **undefended** human colony captures it (`AdjacentCapturableHumanColony` → `CapturePlayerColony`, 1c-3f — the decisive move, taking priority over the unit-hunt; a garrisoned colony's defender is fought through the unit-hunt first), otherwise it hunts the human's nearest **same-domain** unit (land soldiers hunt land units; **warships hunt the human's ships**, `NearestHumanUnit` domain-matched), attacking when adjacent (`AttackHumanUnit`, 1c-2/1c-3a′) else stepping toward it. **With no field unit to chase, a land unit instead marches on the nearest human colony** (`NearestHumanColony` → `StepToward`, the besiege fallback `86d3bx03d`) — so it closes on a colony to capture rather than wandering off to explore (a garrisoned target's garrison becomes the next field unit; an undefended one is captured on arrival). Otherwise an **armed land unit garrisons the power's colonies** (`86d3c9vxj`, FreeCol `DefendSettlementMission`): founding a new colony keeps first priority, but a unit that won't found marches to the nearest **undefended own colony** — one with no armed land unit on its tile (`NearestUndefendedOwnColony`/`ColonyHasArmedDefender`) — and stands guard there (a unit already in an own colony stays put, no thrash), so the power's colonies aren't left open to capture/pillage. Only then (at peace) does a colonist found a colony where it stands while the power has fewer than `MaxAiColonies`, else step toward the nearest unexplored tile; ships and unarmed/idle units wait. It is a flat priority switch, **not** a planner. All choices — including the combat/capture — draw from the player's own RNG stream (`RandomFor(player)`; combat via the internal `Attack`/`AttackColony(…, RandomFor(power))` overloads), so it is replay-stable and never disturbs the human's stream 0. A captured human colony is reported via a transient `ColonyLossNotice` (see [combat](combat.md)).
- **Foreign-power economy (FP-5):** every colonial player (the human and the foreign powers) now runs the same economy each turn — `RunColonyTurn` for each owned colony, then `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` + `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate`. A foreign power then runs `RunForeignPowerEconomy`: (1) **pursue a Founding Father** — if it has none chosen and any is offered, pick one from its offered set by its own RNG (so banked liberty is eventually elected, then `RefreshDockForRecruitability` runs for its own dock); (1b) **plan each colony's tile workers** (`86d3c9vmr`, see the AI-colony-economy note in §3) — staff cash crops + food onto the best tiles before selling, so worked tiles (not just the unattended centre) feed the economy; (2) **sell surplus** — for each owned colony, in stable id order, sell every *tradeable* good's stock above `AiTradeReserve` (0) to the power's **own** market via `SellColonyGoods(power, …)`, **never `Colony.FoodId`** (food is a tradeable market good in the classic spec, so it is excluded explicitly to avoid starving the colony for gold); (3) **recruit** — while it has fewer than `AiMaxEuropeRecruits` (2) of its own colonists waiting in Europe and can afford `CheckRecruit(power, 0)`, recruit from its own dock. The cap bounds the Europe pile-up (no AI shipping until FP-6). All RNG draws use `RandomFor(power)` (the father pick, the recruit/emigrate dock draws, the offer weights); all trade is on `power.Market`; founding-father production modifiers fold the power's **own** Congress (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`, `HasAbilityFor(player, …)`) — so a foreign economy is fully isolated from the human's stream 0 and market. Foreign powers can't yet **buy goods** in Europe (that needs a docked ship — FP-6).
- **Turn ring:** `EndTurn` walks `_players` from `_currentPlayerIndex` (via `NextPlayerIndex`), calling `RunPlayerTurn` on each — a colonial player's colonies produce and it accrues liberty/immigration, then a foreign power additionally runs its economy + unit AI; a **native nation runs `RunNativeTurn`** (its braves raid the human when alarmed, else wander — slice 1b, see [natives](natives.md)) — then runs the world steps (sailing, native alarm decay, movement reset, `Turn++`) once and returns to the human. `RunPlayerTurn` dispatches by `PlayerType`: a native runs its raid/wander AI and returns; the human and the foreign powers share the produce/accrue path (the human's path is byte-identical to before — it folds its own Congress and draws from stream 0), and only foreign powers additionally run the economy/unit AI.
- **Per-player RNG (ADR-009):** each player has a deterministic PCG stream — the human is stream 0 (`Game._random`); each non-human player gets its own `Player.Rng` = `new Pcg32Random(seed, RngStreamId)` (id `PlayerId + 1`, avoiding the native-placement stream 1), created at `New` and saved/restored with its state (so resume is exact). The AI reads it via `RandomFor`. Because each stream is independent, foreign-power AI draws never shift stream 0.
- **Fog per owner (FP-4):** `RevealForOwner(unit)` lifts the **owning** colonial player's fog (the human's for a human unit, a foreign power's for its own); native units lift none. So a foreign power explores under its own fog, and the human never sees a foreign colony until a human unit discovers its tile (the presentation also gates colony markers on the human's explored set).
- The human is **player id 0**, `IsHuman = true`, `PlayerType = Colonial`, `NationId = null` (the classic human has no European nation type until FP-3). It is found via `Game.HumanPlayer` / `IsHuman`, **never by list index** (turn order is spawn/variant-driven).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** for FP-1 the human draws from the game's single main RNG stream (stream 0); the refactor does not add, remove, or reorder any RNG draw, so seeded games, goldens and save-resume stay byte-identical. Per-player RNG streams arrive with the foreign powers.

| Input / condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Read `game.Gold` / `Market` / `Congress` / `Explored` / `RecruitPrice` … | Pass-through to `game.HumanPlayer.X` (human-only) |
| Sell/Buy/Recruit/Visit/trade (a mutating action) | Public method delegates to an internal `Player`-taking overload, which mutates that player's state |
| `EndTurn` accrues liberty + immigration | Credited to the human player (it owns every colony in FP-1); FP-4/5 loop all players |
| Load a v20+ save | Player state read from the save's `Players[]` |
| Load a v19-and-earlier save | The flat top-level fields fold into one human player (`Players[]` absent) |

**Deviations from FreeCol:** FreeCol keeps each player's `units`/`settlements` as live lists on the player; we keep units and colonies as **flat global lists referenced by an owner id** (FreeCol itself treats those lists as derived, not the source of truth). Founding-father *production* modifiers fold **per player** from FP-5 (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`, `HasAbilityFor(player, …)`; the public no-player overloads delegate to the human for presentation/tests, behaviourally identical to before); combat abilities already resolved per-owner (`AbilityForUnit`, FP-2). Live visibility (`CurrentlyVisible`/`IsVisible`) is filtered to the **human's own** units + colonies (FP-2); it's still a single human fog (one viewer) — true per-player fog is unneeded while only the human has a screen. Natives are `Player` rows; a native unit's owner is its `OwnerNationId` (its `OwnerId` stays unused, 0), and the native AI resolves a unit's owning player by matching `Player.NationId == OwnerNationId` (no separate owner-id wiring needed — slice 1b). The foreign-power AI is a deliberate **flat per-unit priority switch** (FP-4) plus a growing economy: it wages war (1c-2/1c-3a′/1c-3f/besiege) and, from `86d3c9vmr`, **staffs each colony's tiles** with cash crops + food (`PlanColonyTileWork`, the tile subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan` — see §3). From `86d3c9vmr` it also **chooses what to build** (`RunForeignColonyBuildPlan`, the building subset of FreeCol's build planner — see §3). It does **not yet**: assign **building** workers or refine goods, run FreeCol's marginal `getBestWorker`/expert-swap, apply the ×1.2 national-advantage ranking, build **units** (artillery/wagons), or **buy/train in Europe** (those are the remaining `86d3c9vmr` increments); it cannot buy goods in Europe (no docked ship yet, FP-6), founds with no minimum-distance-between-colonies rule, caps at one colony (`MaxAiColonies = 1`, lifted once the planner is proven), and its colonists land directly on the map rather than sailing from Europe.

## 3. Technical design

*Audience: developers / future Claude sessions.*

**Domain model:**
- `Player` (`GameSession/Player.cs`) — `sealed class` holding player-scoped state. Public read API (identity + scalars with `internal set`; `IReadOnlyList`/`IReadOnlySet` views over private collections), with `internal` mutable accessors (`CongressList`, `OfferedFathersList`, `RecruitDockList`, `ExploredSet`) used by the rules on `Game`. `RecruitPrice` is computed here. `PlayerType` enum = `{ Colonial, Native }`.
- `Game` holds `IReadOnlyList<Player> Players`, a cached `HumanPlayer` (the human established at creation by its `IsHuman` role), and exposes the former player fields as thin pass-through properties to `HumanPlayer`. Mutating seams have an internal `(Player player, …)` overload; the public method delegates to `HumanPlayer`.
- `RestoredPlayer` (`GameSession/Player.cs`) — a domain DTO (positions, dictionaries) handed to `Game.Restore`, keeping `Game` free of persistence-format types.

**Algorithms & formulas:** the turn/economy formulas are unchanged — the same methods now read/write a `Player` and draw from `RandomFor(player)`: `RunColonyTurn(owner, colony)`, `TileYield(player, …)`, `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …)`/`HasAbilityFor(player, …)`, `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers(player)`, `AccumulateImmigrationAndEmigrate(player)`, `GenerateOffers(player)`, the `Init/Draw/Emigrate` recruit-dock helpers, and `Reveal/RevealAround(player, …)`. `CreateEuropeRecruit(player, …)` and `BuyUnit(player, …)` stamp `OwnerId`. The FP-5 economic policy lives in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (constants `AiTradeReserve` = 0, `AiMaxEuropeRecruits` = 2) + `OwnPersonsInEurope`.

**AI colony economy — owner-scoped assignment seams (`86d3c9vmr` increment 1).** The full-fidelity AI colony planner (worker-assignment → build-queue → Europe spend, a faithful port of FreeCol `ColonyPlan`) is being built in CI+soak-green increments. Increment 1 lays the foundation: player-scoped overloads `CheckAssignWork(player, …)`, `AssignWork(player, …)` and `TileWorkOptions(player, …)` that rank/gate by *that player's* tile yields (`TileYield(player, …)`, folding its own founding fathers) instead of the human's — so a foreign power's planner will rank tiles by its own economy. `AutoAssignIdleToFood` is now owner-aware (the public form routes to `PlayerById(colony.OwnerId)`), fixing a latent bug where AI colonies ranked food tiles by the *human's* fathers; behaviour-identical in the classic ruleset (no father affects grain), so the soak stays byte-stable. **Increment 3 — the worker planner (`PlanColonyTileWork`).** Called per foreign-power colony in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (after that turn's `RunColonyTurn`, before the sell loop), it ports the tile subset of FreeCol `ColonyPlan`: (a) **rank** the tradeable, tile-farmed, non-food raws — value = Σ neighbour `TileYield(owner,…)` × the raw's market sale price — top two kept (ties by ordinal id); (b) **greedily fill** the worker slots (`Population − building workers`) — while the plan's **projected net food** is positive, work the top cash raws round-robin on their best tiles; otherwise work the best **food** tile across every farmed food good (grain on land, **fish** on ocean), so the colony never plans itself into starvation, landing the matching expert via `PickIdleWorkerFor`; (c) **diff-apply** the plan — a tile already worked with the planned good is left untouched, so a colonist keeps accruing on-the-job experience (`86d3c9pgj`) on a stable tile rather than being churned each turn. The projected net food uses the same survival formula as the live `ColonyNetFood` query (increment 2), computed over the plan-in-progress (with type-blind free-colonist yields — an expert that lands later only raises real output, so the food guard is conservative); `ColonyNetFood` itself is the live-colony query, exercised by tests and consumed by the later Europe-spend increment. All per-tile yields are computed once into a small matrix (the modifier fold is the cost) so re-planning every turn stays within the <2 ms EndTurn budget. **One-turn lag:** because the planner runs *after* this turn's `RunColonyTurn`, a freshly-staffed tile first produces **next** turn — this turn's sell loop sells what the *previous* plan (+ centre) produced. This is coherent in steady state and keeps the pass deterministic. **RNG-free** (pure ordinal/yield ranking → human stream 0 untouched); the SoL `ProductionBonus` is invariant to assignment in our model (keys off population+liberty, not worked-unit count), so FreeCol's production-bonus guard does not apply, but a tile's **base** yield must be >0 before the bonus is added (a positive bonus must never make a barren tile look workable). **Deviations:** the ×1.2 national-advantage ranking multiplier, the refined-price averaging, building-worker assignment, the raw-input-exhaustion guard, and FreeCol's marginal `getBestWorker`/expert-swap are later increments. **No save change.** These behaviours are tested in `AiColonyEconomyTests`.

**Increment 4 — the build-queue (`RunForeignColonyBuildPlan`).** Called per foreign-power colony in `RunForeignPowerEconomy` (after the sell loop); when the colony has **nothing queued**, it builds the highest-value building (FreeCol `ColonyPlan.updateBuildableTypes`, building subset). Value = `BuildingBuildWeight ÷ difficulty`: the **weight** is the max over the classes the building belongs to — production/building 0.9, storage 0.85, liberty 0.75, export 0.6, military 0.4, fortify 0.3, refined-production 0.25, teach 0.2, breeding/repair 0.1 (classified from `BuildingType`'s `DefenceBonus`/`GrantsExport`/`BellBonus`/`WarehouseStorage`/`Teaches`/`RepairsNavalUnits`/`BreedingDivisor`/its produced goods), each × its support (1.0, except liberty 0.01 at full SoL); **difficulty** = `max(1, sqrt(Σ shortfall of required goods × (the good's input farmable here ? 1 : 5)))`. Buildings above the colony's size-profile level (`≤2→1, ≤4→2, ≤8→3, else 4`) are skipped, **except** defence/export (always considered). An in-progress build is left untouched; `RunConstruction` already completes it over turns. **RNG-free** (pure weight/difficulty ranking → human stream 0 untouched). **Deviations:** buildable **units** (artillery if under-defended, wagons if inland) are increment 4b; the per-AI-advantage ×1.1/1.2 nudges are not applied; supports are mostly FreeCol's 1.0/FIXME constants; **docks**/`PRODUCE_IN_WATER` (FreeCol's FISH class) are not modelled, so docks aren't built — immaterial, since our worker planner already works ocean fish tiles without them (the drydock/shipyard chain is still classified via `RepairsNavalUnits`); and the **PRODUCTION** class fires for any refinery whose raw is farmable on a neighbour tile rather than FreeCol's top-2 produce-set (same-class candidates tie at 0.25, so difficulty breaks the tie — small impact).

**Integration points:** `Game.New` constructs the human `Player` (with its `Market`), then the foreign powers (each with its own `Rng` and a recruit dock drawn from its own stream); `Game.Restore` builds each player from a `RestoredPlayer` and tops up every colonial player's dock (a no-op for a full FP-5 dock, a fresh draw for an empty pre-FP-5 one). `EndTurn` → `RunPlayerTurn` runs the produce/accrue path for every colonial player and the AI for the foreign powers.

**Persistence:** see [save-load](save-load.md). Save **v20** introduced a `Players[]` array (`SavedPlayer` record: identity + gold/tax/market/liberty/Congress/immigration/dock + explored as compact row-major indexes). Load path is keyed on `Version >= 20`; ≤v19 saves fold the flat fields into one human player (`SaveGame.FoldFlatFieldsToHumanPlayer`). A v20 save still writes the flat fields for now (every pre-v20 load path stays exercised); they are dropped at FP-7.
