## 1. How it works (plain English)

Scattered across the new world are **mysterious ruins** — Lost City Rumours. At the start of every game a handful of land tiles are quietly seeded with one (you won't see them through the fog until you get close). They sit out in the wilderness, never on a settlement, never on top of a unit, never right on your doorstep, and never up in the frozen polar fringe.

**Walking a land unit onto a rumour tile investigates it** — and *that's* when the dice are rolled. Right now the possible results are:

- **Nothing** — just an empty ruin.
- **A tribal chief's gift** — a handful of gold (40–119) lands in your treasury.
- **A learned skill** — a plain colonist (or an indentured servant / petty criminal) emerges a **seasoned scout**.
- **New colonists** — a free colonist joins you, standing on the tile.
- **A Fountain of Youth** — a rush of immigrants (eight, at the standard difficulty) crowds onto your dock in Europe, ready to be shipped over. (Rare.)
- **Ancient ruins** — a modest find: a little gold for a small ruin, or, for a richer one, a **treasure train** you escort home.
- **A city of gold (Cibola)** — the dream: a **big treasure train** (thousands of gold) appears on the tile to escort home. (The rarest find.) There are only **seven** cities of gold in the whole game — the legendary "Seven Cities of Gold". Once all seven have been discovered, a later Cibola rumour finds only ordinary ruins instead (a small gold find, or at most a modest treasure train).
- **The expedition vanishes** — the exploring unit is lost for good. (This is the rare bad outcome.) A **seasoned scout never vanishes**, and with **Hernando de Soto** in your Congress *every* rumour is good — so a scout, especially under de Soto, explores ruins almost fearlessly.

On land the natives own, two more results are possible:

- **Strange mounds** — instead of resolving at once, you're asked **"investigate these strange mounds?"** Leave them be and you simply walk away (the rumour is gone, no gain, no harm). Dig in and you take your chances: the mounds turn out to be a chief's gift, ancient ruins, a vanished expedition, an empty dig — or a **desecrated burial ground**.
- **A native burial ground** — you've defiled the dead. The owning nation turns **hateful** (their settlements' alarm jumps to the maximum). No loot, just a furious neighbour.

Whatever the result, the rumour is a one-shot: investigating it clears it from the map for good (declining strange mounds clears it too — you don't get a second look). Only your own colonists explore — a native brave walking over a rumour leaves it untouched.

**You're told what you found.** When one of your units explores a rumour, a one-line message appears in the status bar — "Tribal chiefs share their treasure with you!", "You uncover ancient ruins — treasure!", "You find nothing of note.", and so on — so the result is never silent. (Strange mounds are the exception: those ask you the investigate/decline question first, and tell you the outcome when you dig.)

The more land a map has, the more rumours appear (roughly one per 35 land tiles).

## 2. Detailed rules

- **How many:** target = `width × height × 45% / 35` (FreeCol's "a rumour every `rumourNumber`=35 land tiles", using our generator's ~45%-land estimate in place of FreeCol's `landMass` option). On the default 36×24 map that's **11**. The actual number can be fewer if eligible tiles run out (never more).
- **Where a rumour may sit:** dry **land**; not on a tile that already has a rumour; not on a **settlement** (colony or native); not on a tile holding a **unit**; not in the **polar rows** (FreeCol `Map.isPolar`: the top and bottom three rows); not in the player's **3×3 start area** (FreeCol removes rumours around a starting colony).
- **Placement is once, at game start**, and is **deterministic for a seed** — the same seed always produces the same rumour tiles.
- **Exploring** a rumour removes it (one-shot); a consumed rumour never comes back and is absent from later saves.

### Exploring a rumour (the outcome roll)

- **Trigger:** a **colonial (non-native) land unit** that *moves onto* (or *disembarks onto*) a tile holding a rumour investigates it immediately (FreeCol fires on any move where `newTile.hasLostCityRumour() && owner.isEuropean()`). A native brave never explores; a ship never can (rumours are on land). The roll uses the **owner's** RNG stream (the human's, or an AI power's own) so one player exploring never disturbs another's economy.
- **The weighted table** (FreeCol `LostCityRumour.chooseType`, classic **medium** difficulty — *good 48% / bad 23% / neutral 29%*):
  - **Good** (weight ×48): **Fountain of Youth 2** (always available to a colonial explorer); then a unit that *can learn* splits **Learn 30 / Tribal-chief 30 / Colonist 20**, a unit that cannot (an expert, a soldier, artillery, …) splits **Tribal-chief 50 / Colonist 30**; then, for any explorer, the treasure finds **Ruins 6 / Cibola 4**, and **strange Mounds 8** (added unconditionally, as FreeCol does — off native-owned land a Mounds roll just degrades to **Nothing** at resolve, so its weight stays in the table).
  - **Bad** (a sub-list normalised to a total weight of 100): off native land, **Expedition vanishes** takes the whole 100. **On native-owned land** it splits **Burial ground 25 / Expedition vanishes 75** (FreeCol's `RandomChoice.normalize` of `25·bad / 75·bad`). Either way the bad side occupies the same 100-weight footprint, so only the good side's mounds shift the table on native land.
  - **Neutral** (weight ×29): **Nothing**.
  - **Native-owned gate (at resolve):** an actual **Mounds** or **burial-ground** *outcome* needs a tile the natives own (`GameMap.IsNativeOwned`). Both sit on the weighted table regardless (FreeCol adds Mounds unconditionally; the burial ground is in the bad sub-list only on native land), but off native-owned land a Mounds/burial roll **degrades to Nothing at resolve** with no extra RNG draw (`Game.ExploreRumour`) — matching FreeCol's `ServerUnit` remap and keeping the full FreeCol distribution (the 8·good weight stays in the denominator, rather than inflating the other outcomes).
  - **The good/bad percentages are tilted by three explorer modifiers** (`Game.ChooseRumourType`, FreeCol `chooseType`), applied in this order:
    - **Expert scout never vanishes** (`UnitType.ExpertScout` from `model.ability.expertScout`; the **seasoned scout**): Expedition-vanishes is dropped from the bad sub-list. If that leaves the bad side empty (a scout off native land, where there's no burial either), the **bad chance falls to 0** (a degenerate case FreeCol handles first) — note the scout's +% bonus is then *not* applied, exactly as FreeCol. On native land the scout's only remaining bad outcome is the burial ground (normalised to 100).
    - **Hernando de Soto** (`rumoursAlwaysPositive`, via the owner's Congress): forces **bad 0 / good 100** — every rumour is good.
    - Otherwise, the unit's own **`exploreLostCityRumour`** percentage (`UnitType.ExploreLostCityRumourBonus`; seasoned scout **+10**) scales **good ×(1+bonus/100)** and **bad ÷(1+bonus/100)**, rounded as Java's `Math.round` (`floor(x+0.5)`) — e.g. +10% turns 48/23 into 53/21.
    - A plain colonist (no scout, no de Soto, bonus 0 → ×1.0) is unaffected by these three modifiers (the table is computed with the base 48 / 23 percentages).
- **The outcomes:**
  - **Nothing** — no effect.
  - **Tribal chief** — gold = `random(0…dx·10) + dx·5` with `dx = 10 − rumourDifficulty`; medium `dx = 8`, so **40–119 gold** to the unit's owner.
  - **Learn** — the unit's `model.unitChange.lostCity` change applies (free colonist / indentured servant / petty criminal → **seasoned scout**), keeping its id and remaining movement.
  - **Colonist** — a **free colonist** (the only classic unit with `model.ability.foundInLostCity`) musters on the tile under the explorer's owner.
  - **Fountain of Youth** — `dx` (medium **8**) fresh immigrants are generated onto the owner's Europe dock, each a weighted recruit draw (FreeCol `ServerEurope.generateFountainRecruits`). They arrive as units *in Europe* (not as the three recruit candidates), so the owner still ships them across.
  - **Ruins** — `random(0…dx·2)·300 + 50` gold (medium 50–4550): a small find (**< 500**) is paid straight to the owner's treasury; a larger one spawns a **treasure train** on the tile (see [treasure-train](treasure-train.md)).
  - **Cibola** — a city of gold, **while any of the finite Seven Cities remain**: `random(0…dx·600) + dx·300` (medium **2400–7199**) as a **treasure train** on the tile, decrementing the per-game `CitiesOfCibolaRemaining` counter (starts at `Game.CibolaCityCount` = 7, FreeCol's `NameCache` Cibola list). Once the counter hits **0** (all seven found), a Cibola roll **degrades to the Ruins case** (a `goto case Ruins` — drawing the ruins amount, NOT a cibola treasure draw, exactly matching FreeCol's `getNextCityOfCibola() == null` fall-through to `case RUINS`), so the eighth-and-later Cibola pays a small gold find (or a modest ruins-sized treasure train). The counter persists (save **v63**, omit-when-default).
  - **Expedition vanishes** — the exploring unit is removed from the game.
  - **Burial ground** (native land only) — the natives who own the tile turn **hateful**: every settlement of that nation jumps to maximum alarm. No gold, no unit change. (FreeCol also forces a formal state of *war*; we have no native-vs-colonial war stance, so the nation-wide max-alarm is the hostility analogue — see Deviations.)
  - **Strange mounds** (native land only) — does **not** resolve at once. The explorer is offered **investigate** or **decline**:
    - **Decline** — the rumour is removed with no effect and **no dice rolled** (FreeCol `declineMounds`).
    - **Investigate** — runs FreeCol's *degradation loop*: the table is re-rolled until it lands an outcome mounds can yield — **tribal chief**, **expedition vanishes**, **ruins**, **burial ground**, or **nothing** (a first *nothing* is re-rolled; only a *second* nothing sticks). A re-rolled **ruins** result also draws one extra "ruins+burial" value that FreeCol consumes but which never changes the outcome — it stays *ruins* (a faithful quirk). Fountain of Youth / learn / colonist / mounds / cibola are never accepted here — they re-roll.
- A learnable unit can only *learn* if it has a `lostCity` change; an expert has none, so its good rolls fall to Fountain-of-Youth / tribal-chief / colonist (FreeCol's `allowLearn` gate).
- **The investigate/decline choice** is the human's: stepping onto a strange-mounds tile raises a transient `PendingMounds` prompt (the rumour waits on the tile). The presentation surfaces it as the **strange-mounds decision panel** (`86d3cqqu5`, `MoundsDecisionPanel`) — opened from `GameController.RefreshView` whenever `Game.PendingMounds` is set — whose **Investigate** / **Leave them be** buttons call the public `Game.ResolvePendingMounds(bool)` oracle (a thin wrapper over `InvestigatePendingMounds`/`DeclinePendingMounds` that returns a one-line, player-facing outcome description, keeping the internal `LostCityRumourType` out of the UI). An **AI / foreign power** has no UI, so it **auto-investigates** immediately on its own stream.

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** the **count estimate** uses our ~45%-land fraction rather than FreeCol's `landMass`=25% option (we have no such option; 45% matches the land our continents actually grow, so the count matches what the player sees — faithful to FreeCol's *intent*, a rumour per 35 land tiles, not to the 25 constant). We skip FreeCol's `SLOSH` edge-inset sampler — our maps already keep a watery margin, so uniform sampling over land tiles gives the same inset effect. **Difficulty constants:** the rumour tuning now all comes from the difficulty level — `rumourDifficulty` (`Ruleset.Difficulty.RumourDifficulty`, medium 2 → `dx = 10 − 2 = 8`) and the good/bad outcome **percentages** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.RumourGoodPercent`/`RumourBadPercent`, medium 48/23, parsed as `percentageOption`s). See [difficulty](difficulty.md). We do **not** replicate Java's RNG bit-sequence — faithfulness is to the *rules and weights*, drawn through our seeded RNG (ADR-009). **Fountain of Youth** now matches FreeCol's *human* path: a player who has elected **William Brewster** (`model.ability.selectRecruit`) hand-picks each of the `dx` immigrants through the shipped select-recruit prompt (`MigrationType.FOUNTAIN` → `PendingEmigration`/`EmigrationChoicePanel`), with the `noEurope` gate surfaced; the AI, foreign powers, and a human *without* Brewster keep the direct generation (byte-identical) — see [immigration](immigration.md). **Cibola** is now finite, matching FreeCol: a per-game `CitiesOfCibolaRemaining` counter (starts at 7, FreeCol's `NameCache` Cibola-name list) spawns the big treasure train while any city remains and degrades to a Ruins-style find once exhausted (we count the cities rather than naming them — there is no `NameCache`, but the gameplay limit and the fall-through are faithful; the amounts match). **Strange mounds — prompt trigger:** FreeCol offers the investigate/decline prompt only for rumours its **map generator pre-stamped** as `MOUNDS` (a persisted per-tile type). Our rumours carry no per-tile type, so we offer the prompt for **any** `MOUNDS` rolled at explore time on native-owned land. This is the slice's one deliberate divergence: it keeps the save at v28 (no per-tile-type field, no v29) and the *outcome machinery* is identical — it's only more generous about *when* the choice appears, never less faithful to what investigating does. The generation-time pre-stamp is a possible later v29 refinement. **Burial-ground effect:** FreeCol raises the nation's tension to *hateful* **and forces a state of war**; we have no native-vs-colonial war stance (natives aren't colonial players), so we port it to **nation-wide maximum alarm** only — the same hostility analogue the other land grievances use (`ClaimLandByStealing`). **Deferred refinement:** the **seasoned-scout exploration bonus** and **Hernando de Soto's** always-positive ability (good-outcome bias) still need unit-type ability/modifier parsing.

## 3. Technical design

- **Placement** lives in `Game.New` (not `MapGenerator.Generate`): it runs **after** the map, the starting unit, native settlements and foreign powers are placed, so it can exclude every occupied tile and the start area. `LostCityRumourGenerator.Place(map, excluded, random)` returns the chosen positions (eligible land tiles, shuffled by the seeded RNG, take `target`); `Game.New` folds them in via `map.AddRumour`.
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** placement draws from a **dedicated stream** — `new Pcg32Random(seed, LcrStreamId)` with `LcrStreamId = 100`, a reserved id **above every per-player stream** (`Player.RngStreamId = playerId + 1`, so foreign powers occupy 2,3,4…). Because the scatter never touches the human's stream 0 (`_random`), every economy/combat/immigration draw — and the L5 soak's byte-stability — is unchanged. The stream is **gen-time only**: it is never saved or resumed (like map gen and native placement); a loaded game rebuilds rumours from the saved tile list, not by re-scattering.
- **Tile model:** `GameMap` holds a sparse `HashSet<Position> _rumours`, parallel to `_resources` — `HasRumour(p)`/`Rumours` (read) and `AddRumour`/`RemoveRumour` (internal: place at gen, consume on explore). A rumour is **not** stored on `TerrainType` (immutable rule-data).
- **Save (v25, additive):** `SaveGame.Rumours` is a row-major `int[]` of rumour tile indexes, **omitted when empty** so a rumour-free game stays byte-identical to v24 and pre-v25 saves load with none. `From` writes `game.Map.Rumours.Select(p => p.Y·W + p.X)`; `Restore` decodes them into the `GameMap` ctor's `rumours` param (no value needed — presence only). **No save bump for the outcome slice:** exploring only mutates already-saved state — units (spawned/removed/upgraded), player gold, and the v25 rumour set (a consumed tile drops out) — so a save round-trips unchanged. (The treasure finds will add a per-unit treasure amount → save **v26**, a later slice.)

- **Outcome resolution** lives in `Game.cs` (it touches units, gold and the map, like combat):
  - **Trigger.** `MoveUnit` and `Disembark` call `TryExploreRumour(unit, target)` *after* the move/landing completes. It gates on a colonial (`!IsNative`) **land** (`!IsNaval`) unit on a `Map.HasRumour(target)` tile, then resolves via `ExploreRumour(unit, target, RandomFor(owner))`. Mirroring FreeCol's `csMove`, the explore runs once the unit has arrived and its fog has lifted.
  - **Per-owner RNG (ADR-009).** The reward draws from `RandomFor(owner)` — the human's stream 0, a foreign power's own stream — exactly as combat threads `IGameRandom` through its internal `Attack*` overloads. The human path consumes stream 0 (its *own* exploration), AI paths their own streams, so an AI exploring never shifts the human's economy and vice-versa. The L5 soak (whose autoplay now steps on rumours) is twin-deterministic and round-trip-stable (it asserts those, not a frozen baseline, so a faithful weighting change like the unconditional Mounds add does not break it).
  - **Choosing + resolving.** `ChooseRumourType` builds the weighted `(type, weight)` list (good/bad/neutral as above; Fountain of Youth is listed first in the good branch, mirroring FreeCol) and `WeightedPick` selects it (FreeCol `getWeightedRandom`: a single entry returns with no draw, otherwise `random.Next(total)` walks the cumulative weights). `ExploreRumour` then applies the effect (`_units.Remove` / `Player.Gold +=` / `UpgradeUnitType` / `SpawnUnit(type, target, ownerId)` / `GenerateFountainRecruits`) and finally `Map.RemoveRumour(target)`. `UpgradeUnitType` replaces the unit object (same id), so callers must treat the passed reference as spent — both hooks call the explore last and touch the unit no further.
  - **Player-facing outcome notice (`86d3c9umy`).** A human-driven explore resolves *inside the move* with no return value the UI reads (unlike a player-initiated attack, which returns a `CombatResult`), so — exactly as the AI-phase combat/raid/gift notices do — `ExploreRumour` records a transient `RumourNotice(Message, Position)` for every **non-mounds** outcome **owned by the human** (an AI/foreign explorer has no UI, so its outcomes are never recorded). The notice carries a *pre-formatted* one-line description (`DescribeMoundsOutcome(outcome)`, the same helper the mounds prompt uses) rather than the internal `LostCityRumourType`, keeping the enum out of the presentation assembly (ADR-006). The list (`_rumourNotices`, exposed read-only as `RumourNotices`; drained by `TakeRumourNotices`) is transient per-turn scratch — never serialized; cleared at the top of `EndTurn` belt-and-braces (the UI normally drains it mid-turn). The strange-mounds path returns *before* this point, so it records no immediate notice — its description comes through `ResolvePendingMounds`. `GameController.RefreshView` drains `TakeRumourNotices` into the one-line status `_notice` (joining multiple), so both an interactive move and a standing-goto walked during `EndTurn` surface their outcome.
  - **Fountain of Youth.** `GenerateFountainRecruits(ownerId, random)` returns a `FountainResult` and dispatches by player (`86d3c9ujx`): **no Europe** (closed/empty `RecruitDock`, or a minimal ruleset) → `NoEurope`, nothing happens (FreeCol `europe == null` → `noEurope` gate); a **select-recruit human** (Brewster, and no normal emigrant already pending) → arms `Game.PendingEmigration` as a Fountain-of-Youth choice (`IsFountainOfYouth`, `Remaining = dx`) and returns `QueuedForChoice`, drawing **no RNG off `random`**; **everyone else** → `dx` × `CreateEuropeRecruit(owner, DrawRecruitType(owner, random))` directly (FreeCol `ServerEurope.generateFountainRecruits`, the AI path) → `Generated`. The injected `random` is threaded through the `DrawRecruitType(Player, IGameRandom)` overload (the legacy `DrawRecruitType(Player)` delegates with `RandomFor(player)`), so the direct path's FoY type roll and `dx` recruit draws run sequentially on **one** owner stream — and the choice path draws nothing on it (each pick's refill draws later on the player's own stream inside `ChooseEmigrant`), so **every non-choosing player is byte-identical** (ADR-009). The result feeds the human's context-aware notice (`DescribeFountainOutcome`). The recruits are real units `InEurope` (already saved), so **no save bump**. See [immigration](immigration.md).
  - **Difficulty constants.** All three come from the difficulty level: `RumourDifficultyDx` is the computed `10 − Ruleset.Difficulty.RumourDifficulty` (raw 2 → dx 8; transform in code), and the base percentages are `Ruleset.Difficulty.RumourBadPercent` (23) / `RumourGoodPercent` (48), parsed from the `badRumour`/`goodRumour` `percentageOption`s. `FoundInLostCityUnitTypeId="model.unit.freeColonist"`; the learn change is `UnitChangeTypeIds.LostCity` (`model.unitChange.lostCity`, already parsed by `Ruleset`). See [difficulty](difficulty.md).
  - **Strange mounds + burial ground.** `ChooseRumourType` now takes the `target` tile and reads `Map.IsNativeOwned(target)`: it **conditional-adds** `Mounds` (weight 8·good) to the good list and replaces the flat `ExpeditionVanishes 100` with `NormalizeTo100([(BurialGround, 25·bad), (ExpeditionVanishes, 75·bad)])` **only on native land** — so a wilderness tile's `(type, weight)` list, and hence `WeightedPick`'s `random.Next(total)`, is byte-identical to before (the determinism guard). `ExploreRumour` peeks the type, applies the **resolve-time gate** (a `Mounds`/`BurialGround` on a non-native tile → `Nothing`, belt-and-braces for a future gen-stamp), and if the peek is `Mounds` **returns immediately without resolving or removing** — the caller decides. The shared `ResolveOutcome(unit, target, outcome, random)` (extracted from the old switch, now also handling `BurialGround` → `ApplyBurialGround`) is used by both the direct `ExploreRumour` and `InvestigateMounds`.
  - **The degradation loop.** `InvestigateMounds` runs `DegradeMounds` then resolves + removes. `DegradeMounds` mirrors FreeCol `ServerUnit` verbatim: re-roll `ChooseRumourType` (one draw per iteration); a **sticky** `sawNothing` flag accepts `Nothing` only the second time; `ExpeditionVanishes`/`TribalChief` accept at once; `Ruins` accepts and **draws one extra `random.Next(100)`** (consumed for byte-fidelity — the outcome stays `Ruins` regardless, the FreeCol fall-through quirk); `BurialGround` accepts on native land; everything else re-rolls. **Uncapped**, as FreeCol (it terminates fast). The exact draw order — `ChooseRumourType` first, the ruins+burial draw only on a `Ruins` iteration — is pinned by the scripted-RNG L1 tests (a wrong count empties the queue and throws).
  - **Burial-ground effect.** `ApplyBurialGround(target)` resolves `Map.NativeOwnerOf(target)` and raises every settlement of that nation via `ChangeNativeAlarm(s, NativeSettlement.MaxAlarm)` (clamped to max = hateful). No gold/unit change, no RNG.
  - **The investigate/decline choice.** A transient `PendingMoundsDecision(UnitId, Tile)` (mirroring `_pendingDemand`, **never serialized**) is set by `TryExploreRumour` when a **human** explore peeks `Mounds`; an AI/foreign power instead calls `InvestigateMounds` inline on its own stream (headless — keeps the soak driveable). The presentation reads `PendingMounds` and calls `InvestigatePendingMounds` (resolve on stream 0) / `DeclinePendingMounds` (remove, no draw). A save mid-prompt simply reloads with the tile still an un-explored rumour (no per-tile state, so **no save bump**). *(The Godot decision panel itself is a deferred presentation slice — the engine API + AI auto-investigate are complete and L1-covered.)*
  - **Pending-decision guards** (single outstanding decision, no re-roll/double-resolve): (1) `TryExploreRumour` no-ops a **human** explore while `_pendingMounds` is set — so a human can't roll a second rumour, or re-roll the same one by stepping off and back, before answering (the AI never sets it, so AI/soak exploration is unaffected). (2) `EndTurn` **auto-declines** an unanswered prompt (mirrors the tribute-demand auto-refuse) — clearing it before the AI turns so it can't strand a rumour or block exploration across the round. (3) `InvestigateMounds` re-asserts `Map.HasRumour(target)` and no-ops (no reward, no draw) if the rumour was already consumed (e.g. by another unit racing the human's decision). Together these keep one physical rumour to one resolution.
