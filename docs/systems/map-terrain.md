## 1. How it works (plain English)

The world is a rectangular grid of square tiles, each with one terrain type from the rules data. For now the map is a placeholder: a 2-tile ocean border around one landmass whose terrain is randomly scattered (but always the same for the same game seed). On screen each terrain is a flat colour — ocean blue, plains tan, forests green, mountains grey.

**What the player sees and does:** the coloured map; pan with middle/right-mouse drag, zoom with the wheel.

**The high seas.** The deep water along the far east and west of the map is the **high seas** — the open ocean a ship sails into to reach Europe. It isn't a fixed strip of columns: the game looks at how far each edge-water tile is from the nearest land and marks the genuinely open water (a few tiles clear of any coast) as high seas, so the high-seas line hugs the shape of the coastline. There's always at least one high-seas tile on each side so a ship can always find a way out.

**Mountains & hills look like ranges.** Elevated terrain isn't sprinkled tile-by-tile any more. Instead the game grows **ranges**, just like the original: it picks a few starting spots on the land, then walks in a straight-ish direction laying down a chain of mountains, dropping hills (and the odd extra mountain) on the tiles right beside the chain. The result reads as ridgelines snaking across a continent, not random grey dots. After the ranges are laid, a handful of stray hills and mountains are still scattered here and there for variety. A bigger map (or more land) gets proportionally more elevation, so a small world isn't crammed with mountains and a huge one isn't bare. As always, the same seed makes the same ranges every time.

**Choosing the world (new game).** When you start a new game the menu first asks two things: the **world size** (Small 30×20, Standard 36×24, Large 56×38, Huge 80×52) and how much of it is **land** (Sparse 30%, Normal 45%, Dense 50%). A bigger map gives more room to expand and explore; more land means larger continents and less sea. Leave them on the defaults (Standard / Normal) and you get exactly the world the game has always generated. The same seed plus the same two choices always makes the same world.

**Playing on the real America.** As well as a freshly-generated random world, you can choose to play on the **fixed map of the Americas** — the same hand-drawn New World map that ships with FreeCol (a tall north-to-south strip of the two American continents, 40 tiles wide by 180 tall). Its coastlines and mountains are exactly where the map's author drew them, so the same continents appear every time. We take only the *land shape* from that map; the game still lays its own rivers, fish, mineral deposits, native villages and your starting position on top, so each America game still differs by seed (a different river here, a different silver lode there) while the continents stay put. On the random-world option the world-size and land-mass choices apply as before; on the America option they're ignored (the map is a fixed size).

**Fishing the coast.** Open ocean yields a flat 2 fish, but water hugging the shore yields more — a **coastal** water tile (one that borders three or more land tiles) gives **4 fish** instead of 2. This is the classic "+2 fish on the coast" rule that makes a colony founded on the shoreline worth building: its sea tiles feed the town. Only fish gets the bonus, only genuinely coastal water earns it (a single far-out land tile two squares away isn't enough), and the open-sea **high seas** never get it. Worked example: a colony with one ocean tile that touches three of its land tiles can put a fisherman there for 4 food a turn instead of 2. And where a **river meets the sea**, the water beside that river mouth gets a further **+1 fish** — see [rivers-tile-improvements](rivers-tile-improvements.md).

**Regions.** Behind the scenes the map is also carved into named *regions*, exactly as the original game does. The frozen top and bottom rows are the **arctic** and **antarctic**; the western sea is the **Pacific** and the eastern sea the **Atlantic**, each split into a north and south half; every separate continent is its own **land region** (a very big one is chopped into ~75-tile pieces so no single region swallows a whole continent); runs of hills and mountains become **mountain regions**; and any pond or inland sea with no water route out to the open ocean is recognised as a **lake** rather than ocean. Every tile belongs to exactly one region. Each region carries a *score* — the points a player will earn for discovering it once exploration is added (a faraway sea like the Pacific is worth more; bigger land regions are worth more). Regions are computed purely from the finished terrain, so the same map always produces the same regions, on every machine.

## 2. Detailed rules

- Coordinates: (0,0) top-left; X east, Y south. Diagonals count as adjacent (8 neighbours), as in the original game.
- Same seed → identical map, every time, every machine (ADR-009).
- **Climate-band generation (Phase 2a):** a continent grown from seeded blobs (a configurable land fraction — default 45% — with watery margins); per-tile climate triple — temperature from latitude (40 °C equator → −20 °C poles, jittered), humidity from smoothed noise (0–100), and a **lowland** altitude (1–3) — hills and mountains are no longer scattered per-tile here (see the range pass below); terrain picked among ruleset types whose `<gen>` envelope contains the triple (forest-vs-clear is a separate ~45% roll; off-envelope triples take the climate-nearest type). The open-sea **route to Europe (high seas)** is a band hugging the east/west edges: a near-edge ocean tile becomes high seas when there is no land within a few tiles of it, so the high-seas line follows the coastline's distance rather than sitting on fixed columns.
- Result: hot wet equator → savannah/tropical forest, dry bands → desert/scrub, poles → arctic/tundra/boreal.

**Mountain & hill ranges (`86d3c9w71`).** After the climate terrain is laid, `MapGenerator.MakeMountains` grows elevation as **ranges** by a directional walk, faithful to FreeCol `TerrainGenerator.createMountains`. Two passes over the land: (1) **ranges** — until a tile budget of `½ · landCount / mountainNumber` (FreeCol `randomHillsRatio = 0.5`, `mountainNumber = 10` → ≈5% of land) is met, take the next shuffled non-elevation land tile, pick a random walk direction and a length `maxLength − rand(maxLength/2)` (`maxLength = max(w,h)/10`), and step that many land tiles laying a mountain at each step plus, on each step tile's 8 land neighbours, a mountain (2/8), a hill (5/8) or nothing (1/8); the walk stops at water or the map edge. (2) **sprinkle** — the other half of the budget (≈5% of land) is scattered as 25% mountains / 75% hills ("random hills here and there"). So elevation reads as ridgelines, and the total elevation share (~10% of land) scales with both map size and land fraction. **Bonus resources are placed last** (after the range pass *and* the high-seas reclassification), faithful to FreeCol's "add bonuses only after the map is completed, otherwise we risk creating resources on fields where they do not belong (like tobacco on hills)" — so a tile retyped to mountains carries mountain resources (ore/silver), never the lowland resource it might otherwise have rolled.

**World-size & land-mass options (`86d3c9w9c`).** Map width/height and the land fraction are **new-game options** (FreeCol's `model.option.mapWidth`/`mapHeight`/`landMass`). `MapGenerator.Generate` takes a `landMassFraction` (the share of tiles grown into land); `Game.New` takes `mapWidth`/`mapHeight`/`landMassFraction`. The offered presets live in `WorldSizeOptions` ("data not code", like the game variants): sizes Small 30×20 / Standard 36×24 / Large 56×38 / Huge 80×52, land Sparse 30% / Normal 45% / Dense 50% — bounds mirror FreeCol (width 30–200, height 20–200, land 15–50%). **The defaults equal the shipped world (36×24, 45%)**, and the default-valued call uses the identical arithmetic as before, so a default new game is **byte-identical** (ADR-009) — the visual goldens and soak baseline are untouched; only a *non-default* choice changes the map. The land fraction only sets how many tiles `GrowContinent` grows (the per-tile RNG draw order is unchanged), so a non-default world is still fully determined by `(seed, width, height, landMass)`.

**High-seas band (86d3c9w8b).** Faithful to FreeCol `Map.resetHighSeas`: after terrain is laid, each row's contiguous ocean strip up to 10 columns in from the east/west edge is examined, and an ocean tile with no land within 4 tiles (8-direction Chebyshev) is reclassified as high seas; each side keeps its furthest-from-land tile as a guaranteed exit. Replaces the former rule that fixed high seas to the two outermost columns. RNG-free, no save bump (see §3).

**Coastal fish bonus (86d3c9we8).** Faithful to FreeCol's classic `model.improvement.fishBonusLand` (an index-50 **additive +2** modifier on `model.goods.fish`, scoped `isWater && !highSeas`). A water tile's fish *potential* gains a flat **+2** when it has **more than two** adjacent land tiles — the Col1 "≥3 land neighbours ⇒ 4 fish, else 2 fish" rule, applied by FreeCol's map generator `TerrainGenerator.perhapsAddBonus` (`adjacentLand > 2`). We compute this from terrain + 8-direction adjacency at read time (no stored improvement), so it stays a pure function of the map: **high-seas** tiles are excluded (the improvement's `match-negated` scope); **lake** tiles (non-high-seas water) qualify. The bonus is added in `TileYieldPotential` after the bonus-resource modifiers, so founding-father and expert modifiers stack on top of it exactly as they do for any tile. The river-mouth `fishBonusRiver` (+1) is **now implemented** (a non-high-seas water tile adjacent to a river-mouth land tile gets a further +1 fish, stacking on the coastal +2) — see [rivers-tile-improvements](rivers-tile-improvements.md) for its rule. RNG-free, no save bump (yields are derived from terrain + the river layer + adjacency, never stored).

**Bonus resource placement (`86d3c9wbp`).** Resources are scattered in the final pass once terrain is settled (after the mountain ranges *and* the high-seas band), faithful to FreeCol `perhapsAddBonus`. A **land** tile with a resource table gets one at the **bonus-number** chance (**10%**, FreeCol `BONUS_NUMBER`). A **water** tile gets one only where it borders **more than one** land tile, at FreeCol's adjacency-scaled odds `1 / (10 − adjacentLand)` — so open ocean stays bare while a tightly-enclosed bay is richest; *which* resource is the terrain's weighted pick.

**Per-resource quantity (`86d3c9wbp`, persistence facet — v46).** Some resources are *finite*: FreeCol's `minerals`/`ore`/`silver` carry a `minimum-value`..`maximum-value` range (e.g. minerals 40–400), and a placed deposit gets a random amount in that range that depletes as it's worked. We now model and persist that quantity: each `ResourceType` parses its min/max, a placed finite deposit is rolled to a `MinValue..MaxValue` amount (a limitless resource — most of them — carries none), the `GameMap` holds a sparse `tile → quantity` layer, and the save stores it (`ResourceQuantities`, v46, omitted when none). **The roll is now run at game start** (`Game.New` calls `Game.RollResourceQuantities` right after map generation), so every placed finite deposit carries a quantity from turn 1. It rolls on a *reserved* RNG stream (102), so it shifts no stream-0 draw — the human's economy/turn sequence is byte-identical; what it *does* change is that a default save now writes a `ResourceQuantities` token, so the map golden and soak baseline were regenerated this wave. *Deviations / deferred:* **depletion-when-worked** is out of scope (we persist the rolled amount but don't yet deplete it). FreeCol's high-seas-**connectivity** gate on water resources is still approximated by the land-neighbour rule (an enclosed lake can still draw a fish).

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** uses FreeCol's climate *data* and option *bounds* but not its exact climate algorithm; the **mountain/hill range walk now follows FreeCol `createMountains` directly** (directions, length, fringe odds, and the half-ranges/half-sprinkle split). Because the classic spec's hill/mountain `<gen>` envelopes span all latitudes, a single hill type and a single mountain type suffice at every latitude — we don't need FreeCol's per-latitude mountain/hill tile-type lists. FreeCol's `LAND_GENERATOR_TYPE` (classic/continent/archipelago/islands) and rivers are still future work. Square grid topology as in the 1994 original.

### Regions (86d3c9w12)

After terrain is laid down, every tile is assigned to exactly one region, in this order (earlier passes win a tile; later passes skip already-claimed tiles):

1. **Polar bands** — land in rows `[0, POLAR_HEIGHT)` is **arctic**; land in rows `[Height − POLAR_HEIGHT − 1, Height)` is **antarctic** (`POLAR_HEIGHT = 2`, so 2 top rows / 3 bottom rows — the same asymmetry FreeCol's loops produce). Polar water is left for the ocean pass. Score 0.
2. **Oceans** — a **Pacific** (west) and **Atlantic** (east), each with a **north** and **south** quadrant. Each quadrant is flood-filled (8-direction) from the first water tile down its outer column, in three escalating bounds — own quadrant, own horizontal half, the whole map — so a region can overflow into its opposite quadrant when the geography demands. Tiles live in the four leaf quadrants; the parent ocean carries the discovery score (**Pacific = 100**, Atlantic = 0, `PACIFIC_SCORE_VALUE`).
3. **Lakes** — any water the directional ocean fill cannot reach (an enclosed body with no sea route out) becomes its own score-0 **lake** region (`RegionType.Lake`), so no water tile is left unassigned. This is exactly FreeCol's `TerrainGenerator.createLakeRegions` set ("`!isLand && getRegion()==null`" after the oceans are assigned). Those tiles are then **retyped to lake *terrain*** (`model.tile.lake`, FreeCol `makeLakes` `t.setType(lakeType)`) in `MapGenerator.Generate` after region assignment — so a lake carries the lake terrain's gen-data (fish, no settle, move cost) rather than ocean's. (Rivers, which are RNG-bearing, remain a separate slice.)
4. **Mountain regions** — each contiguous block of hill/mountain (`IsElevation`) land not already in a polar band becomes one mountain region, scored `2 × tile-count`.
5. **Land regions** — each remaining contiguous landmass (8-direction) becomes one land region. A landmass larger than `LAND_REGION_MAX_SIZE = 75` is split into ~75-tile chunks (target 75, or half the remainder when it is below 150). Score = `max((int)(regionSize / totalLandTiles × 1000), 5)` (`LAND_REGIONS_SCORE_VALUE`, `LAND_REGION_MIN_SCORE`); the denominator is *all* land tiles, polar and mountain included.

**Determinism:** region assignment draws **no randomness** (no RNG parameter at all) — it is a pure function of the finished terrain, mirroring [native land claims](natives.md). It therefore perturbs no RNG stream (ADR-009) and recomputes byte-identically at game start and on load.

**Faithful subset / deviations:** the mountain regions are derived from the finished hill/mountain terrain (a contiguous-block scan, RNG-free), whereas FreeCol builds a `ServerRegion` *during* its `createMountains` walk; both yield the same region type and `2 × size` score, so the only difference is that our region pass runs after terrain rather than alongside the walk. (The walk itself — `MapGenerator.MakeMountains` — now matches FreeCol's range generation; see §2.) FreeCol's nine "geographic thirds" virtual bounding boxes (used only to seed native settlement placement) and the RIVER / COAST / DESERT region types are **deferred** until rivers and that placement hook exist. The LAKE region type **is** now produced, and its tiles carry **lake terrain** (`model.tile.lake`, see step 3); only rivers and the COAST/DESERT region types remain deferred. Region **discovery** mechanics (discoverable/prediscovered flags, per-player discovery, naming, the explorer's-map UI and the score award) are **P6** — regions carry a `ScoreValue` now but no discovery state.

**Save impact (no version bump).** `RegionType` serialises as its enum ordinal inside the v35 region layer, so a default map that contains an enclosed lake now writes `Type = 4` where it previously wrote `0` (Ocean). This is an existing-field **value** change, not a format/schema change (no new field), so the save version stays **45** — consistent with v35's stated design that the region layer is persisted "only so a loaded game keeps the exact ids it was played with **even if this algorithm later changes**". Pre-existing saves load their persisted region types verbatim (an old lake stays tagged Ocean); only newly-generated maps classify lakes. No loader logic branches on lake-vs-ocean, so no migration is needed.

## 3. Technical design

- `Position` (record struct): adjacency + neighbour enumeration.
- `GameMap`: immutable terrain grid (row-major array), bounds checks throw off-map.
- `MapGenerator.Generate(ruleset, w, h, IGameRandom, landMassFraction = 0.45)`: pure function of its inputs; `landMassFraction` (default `MapGenerator.DefaultLandMassFraction`) is the `targetLand` ceiling in `GrowContinent` (replaces the former `0.45` literal). `Game.New(ruleset, seed, mapWidth = 36, mapHeight = 24, …, landMassFraction = 0.45)` forwards all three world-shape params. **Pipeline order:** `GrowContinent` → humidity noise → climate-terrain loop (lowland only, no per-tile elevation, no resources) → `MakeMountains` (range walk) → `ResetHighSeas`, then the **shared decorate tail** `BuildDecoratedMap`: `MakeRivers` → **resource pass** → `RegionGenerator.Assign`/`GameMap.SetRegions` (no RNG) → lake retype → final `GameMap`. The tail is factored out of `Generate` so the fixed-map path reuses it verbatim (see `DecorateFixedMap` below) — the random default game's draw order is unchanged, so it stays byte-identical.
- **Fixed scenario maps (America), slice A+B:** `FixedMap` (engine-free, ADR-006) loads a **terrain-only** `GameMap` from an embedded grid — FreeCol's hand-made `M_America_Mazim.fsm` (GPL v2), extracted to `game/data/maps/america.txt` (header `WIDTH HEIGHT`, then row-major terrain short-names; 40×180; provenance in `game/data/maps/PROVENANCE.md`, embedded via `GameLogic.csproj`). `MapSource` enum (`Random`/`America`); `FixedMap.TryLoad(source, ruleset)` returns the loaded terrain map for `America` and `null` for `Random` (the caller generates that). `MapGenerator.DecorateFixedMap(terrainMap, ruleset, random)` runs the **same `BuildDecoratedMap` tail** (rivers + resources + regions + lake) on the loaded terrain, so the fixed map gains every gameplay layer a scenario file doesn't carry. `Game.New` gains `MapSource mapSource = MapSource.Random`: it calls `FixedMap.TryLoad`, and on a hit decorates that terrain (its 40×180 dimensions override the world-size args, which apply only to the random branch — the start latitude uses `map.Height`); on a miss it generates as before. **Determinism (ADR-009):** the fixed map draws rivers/resources from the same map stream, so an America game is fully determined by its seed; the default (`Random`) branch is untouched, so the soak/goldens are unchanged. *Terrain only is taken from the `.fsm`* — rivers/resources/natives/start are ours. **Presentation (slice C):** `NewGameDialog` gains a **Map** dropdown (Random / America) above the size/land/difficulty rows; picking America **disables** the size & land dropdowns (a fixed map sets its own dimensions). The choice rides `GameController.PendingMapSource` (a static surviving the menu→game scene change, like `PendingWorldSize`) into `GameController.StartNewGame(seed, size, land, difficulty, mapSource)` → `Game.New`. The parameterless `StartNewGame(seed)` (tests/goldens) passes `MapSource.Random`, so it is unchanged.
- **Mountain & hill ranges:** `MapGenerator.MakeMountains(ruleset, terrain, land, w, h, IGameRandom)` ports FreeCol `TerrainGenerator.createMountains`. It collects the land tiles, deterministically Fisher–Yates-shuffles them (private `Shuffle`, FreeCol `RandomUtils.randomShuffle`), and runs the two passes described in §2 (walked ranges to a `½·landCount/MountainNumber` budget with `maxLength = max(w,h)/10`; then a 25/75 mountain/hill sprinkle for the other half). Constants `MountainNumber = 10` (FreeCol `model.option.mountainNumber` classic default) and `RandomHillsRatio = 0.5`. The old `RollAltitude` is now `RollLowlandAltitude` (always 1–3). **This pass draws RNG**, so it reorders the stream-0 draw sequence — a deliberate map-gen change for this item (ADR-009 allows it; the goldens + downstream seed-pinned tests were regenerated/retargeted accordingly). Resources move to a dedicated post-terrain pass (FreeCol "add bonuses last") so a retyped mountain tile gets mountain resources, not a stale lowland one.
- **High-seas band:** `MapGenerator.ResetHighSeas` (pure, RNG-free), a port of FreeCol `Map.resetHighSeas(distToLandFromHighSeas, maxDistanceToEdge)` with the classic spec values `DistanceToHighSea = 4` / `MaxDistanceToEdge = 10`: it resets every high-seas tile to ocean, then for each row walks the contiguous ocean strip inward from each east/west edge (up to `MaxDistanceToEdge` columns, stopping at the first land) and promotes an ocean tile to high seas when no land lies within `DistanceToHighSea` (8-dir Chebyshev), with a furthest-from-land fallback so each side always has at least one exit. **It runs after `MakeMountains` and before the resource pass, and consumes no RNG**, so it never needs to drop a resource (none exist yet) — a high-seas tile simply never receives one (the resource pass skips water, which has no resource table). **No save bump:** high seas is a terrain `Type`, not a save-format field — a value change inside the existing terrain layer, like lake tagging; old saves keep their terrain, new games get the band. Region assignment is unaffected (it doesn't distinguish ocean subtypes).
- **World-size options:** `WorldSizeOptions` (static, GameLogic) holds the immutable `WorldSize(Name, Width, Height)` and `LandMass(Name, Fraction)` preset lists + the default indices; no save change (map dimensions persist since save v2). Presentation: `NewGameDialog` (programmatic overlay, ADR-006) lets the player pick before starting and forwards the choice via `GameController.PendingWorldSize`/`PendingLandMass` (statics that survive the menu→game scene change, like `PendingLoadPath`) into `GameController.StartNewGame(seed, size, landMass)` → `Game.New`. The parameterless `StartNewGame(seed)` (tests/goldens) uses the shipped defaults, so it is unchanged.
- **Bonus resource placement (`86d3c9wbp`):** the resource pass (after `ResetHighSeas`) rolls per tile from its final terrain's weighted table (`PickWeightedResource`) — **land** at `LandBonusChance = 0.10` (FreeCol `BONUS_NUMBER`), **water** only when `LandNeighbours(tile) > WaterResourceMinLandNeighbours (=1)` at `1 / (WaterResourceOddsBase (=10) − adjacentLand)` odds (FreeCol `perhapsAddBonus`). A tile whose terrain has no resource table draws nothing; open ocean (≤1 land neighbour) draws nothing. **Draws RNG → a deliberate stream-0 reorder** for this item (goldens regenerated; one downstream seed-pinned monarch idle-test retargeted). `GameMap.Resources` stays `Position → id`; the constants are hardcoded from the spec for now (read `bonusNumber` from the map-gen options later, with `mountainNumber`/high-seas).
- **Per-resource quantity (`86d3c9wbp`, v46):** `ResourceType` parses `minimum-value`/`maximum-value` (→ `MinValue`/`MaxValue`; `HasQuantityRange` ⇔ `MaxValue > 0`, the inverse of FreeCol's `limitless`); `ResourceType.RollQuantity(IGameRandom)` returns `MinValue + rng.Next(MaxValue − MinValue + 1)` (FreeCol `RandomRange`), 0 for a limitless type. `GameMap` carries a sparse `Position → int` quantity layer (`ResourceQuantityAt`/`ResourceQuantities`/`SetResourceQuantity`, ctor-restored like the resource/rumour layers). `Game.RollResourceQuantities(seed)` walks the placed resources in row-major order and stamps a rolled amount on each finite one, drawing from a reserved stream (`ResourceQuantityStreamId = 102`) so stream 0 is untouched. Save field `SavedResourceQuantity` (`ResourceQuantities`, v46, omitted when none). **`Game.New` now calls `RollResourceQuantities(seed)` right after `MapGenerator.Generate`**, so a placed finite deposit carries a quantity from turn 1. The reserved stream means no stream-0 reorder (the human's draw sequence is byte-stable; soak unaffected), but a default save now emits a `ResourceQuantities` token, so the L4 map golden + soak baseline were regenerated this wave.
- **Coastal fish bonus:** `Game.TileYieldPotential` (in the tile-yield cluster of `Game.cs`) adds `CoastalFishBonus = 2` to a fish potential when `IsCoastalWater(tile)` — a private helper that returns true for water that isn't `model.tile.highSeas` and has `> CoastalLandNeighboursRequired (=2)` land neighbours (`tile.Neighbours().Count(n => InBounds(n) && !TerrainAt(n).IsWater)`). Pure (terrain + adjacency), RNG-free, no stored state; ports FreeCol `TerrainGenerator.perhapsAddBonus` / `fishBonusLand`. The **river-mouth +1** (`RiverMouthFishBonus`, `IsRiverMouthWater`) stacks on top of this — full detail in [rivers-tile-improvements](rivers-tile-improvements.md).
- **Regions:** `RegionType` (enum `Ocean=0`/`Land=1`/`Mountain=2`/`River=3`/`Lake=4`, serialized by ordinal — `River` reserved, `Lake` produced for enclosed water), `Region` (record: `Id`, `Type`, `ScoreValue`, `Key?`, `ParentId?` — `Id` equals the index into `GameMap.Regions`; fixed regions carry a `model.region.*` key, dynamic land/mountain regions a null key; ocean quadrants link to their parent ocean via `ParentId`). `GameMap` holds a dense row-major `int[]` region layer (`RegionIdAt` → id or `GameMap.NoRegion`; `RegionOf` → `Region?`; `Regions` table) installed via the internal `SetRegions`; the ctor takes optional `(regionIds, regions)` for save restore, following the resource/rumour sparse-layer convention. `RegionGenerator.Assign(GameMap) → (int[], IReadOnlyList<Region>)` is `static`, RNG-free, deterministic (fixed y-then-x seed scan + `Direction.values()` neighbour order); the eight fixed regions always exist (ids 0 arctic, 1 antarctic, 2 pacific +3/4, 5 atlantic +6/7) for stable ids, then lake (enclosed water), mountain, and land regions follow. Flood fill mirrors FreeCol `Map.floodFillBool` (including the bounded carve used to split oversized landmasses).
- Rendering (`MapView`, ADR-014): **isometric diamonds with FreeCol art** — the unchanged square grid projects 45° (`screen = ((x−y)·64, (x+y)·32)`, tiles 128×64, `TileW`/`TileH`). Base diamond per terrain (2 variants picked by position hash — no RNG draws); forest/hills/mountains render as base + overlay (base mapping mirrors the climate pairs); fog uses FreeCol's `unexplored` art; unmapped terrain renders a magenta diamond. Conversions in `TileCentre`/`TileAt` (exact diamond picking via rounding in grid space). Art provenance: `game/assets/freecol/PROVENANCE.md`. Not yet adopted: beach/river transitions, 16-variant forest connection bitmasks, hi-res `.size9` art.
