## 1. In plain English

Put an **expert** colonist (an expert ore miner, a master distiller, an elder statesman, …) into a **school** and they teach the colony's other colonists, lifting them up the skill ladder one step at a time:

> **petty criminal → indentured servant → free colonist → the teacher's own expertise.**

- The teacher always works on the colony's **least-skilled** colonist first — a petty criminal is taught before a plain free colonist.
- Each step takes a few turns: the criminal→servant and servant→free steps take **4** turns; learning an actual expertise takes **4, 6, or 8** turns depending on how hard it is (an expert ore miner 4, a master distiller 6, an elder statesman 8). A colony under **good government** (high Sons of Liberty) learns faster — the production bonus shaves turns off, down to a minimum of 1.
- There are three school tiers, each able to teach a higher class of expert **and** holding more teachers at once: the **schoolhouse** teaches one simple expert (skill 1) to one student; the **college** seats **two** masters (skill 2) who teach two students in parallel; the **university** seats **three** of the most learned (skill 3+, e.g. the elder statesman) teaching three students at once. An expert too advanced for the school it sits in simply can't teach there. **Each teacher works on its own student** — a college with two teachers graduates two colonists in the same turns, not one after the other.
- A **colonial regular** is special: in a school it teaches the **veteran soldier** skill, not "colonial regular".

**Assigning a teacher.** You put a teacher into a school yourself: open the colony, and each school building shows a **"Teach: <expert>"** button for every idle expert that's *qualified to teach there*. Click it and that expert takes a teaching slot. The game won't let you place a colonist who can't teach — a plain free colonist (nothing to teach yet) or an expert too advanced for the building (an elder statesman in a schoolhouse) simply isn't offered. This mirrors the original, where you drag a unit into the schoolhouse; the difference from the generic "add a worker" (＋) is that ＋ adds a plain free colonist, who can neither teach nor be taught in a school.

**Worked example.** An expert ore miner sits in a schoolhouse; the colony also has a petty criminal. After 4 turns the criminal becomes an indentured servant; 4 more, a free colonist; 4 more, an expert ore miner — 12 turns to turn a convict into a skilled miner. Under 100% Sons of Liberty (production bonus +2) each step needs only 2 turns. Put a *second* expert into a college alongside the first and a second colonist is climbing the ladder at the same time.

*(Classic Colonization picks the *student* automatically — there is no "choose who learns" prompt; we follow that. You do choose who *teaches*.)*

**Learning a trade by working it (no school needed).** Schools aren't the only road to expertise. A **free colonist** left working the same job long enough **teaches itself** and upgrades to the matching expert — a farmer becomes an expert farmer, and (the original game's building version, `86d3kgbpd`) a colonist working a distillery becomes a **master distiller**, one in the town hall an **elder statesman**, a carpenter a **master carpenter**, and so on. It's the slowest road — a small chance each turn that grows with the work done, peaking at ~4% once the colonist is fully experienced — and only **free colonists** learn this way (a servant or criminal must first climb to free colonist, and an existing expert has nothing to learn). Tile work has always self-taught; **building work now does too**, matching the original 1994 game (FreeCol teaches building experts only in schools).

**Clearing a speciality.** The reverse of schooling: an **expert** standing on a colony tile can be turned **back** into a plain free colonist with the **Clear speciality** button on the colony screen — handy when you'd rather have a flexible worker than a specialist. The button appears only for a unit that actually has a learned speciality (a free colonist, servant or criminal has nothing to clear).

## 2. Rules we implement

- **The schools** (`BuildingType`): schoolhouse (1 workplace, teaches skill ≤ **1**), college (2 workplaces, ≤ **2**), university (3 workplaces, ≤ **4**). All three carry the **teach** ability (the schoolhouse declares it; college/university inherit it up the `extends` chain). The **workplace count is the teacher count** — a college holds 2 teachers, a university 3.
- **Who teaches:** **every** expert occupying a school building whose skill is within the building's `[MinimumSkill, MaximumSkill]` window (the floor is ≥ 1 for every classic school). A school with more than one eligible teacher runs them **all** this turn, one student each — parallel teaching (`86d3fpyc0`/`86d3fpye6`). An over-skilled expert (elder statesman, skill 3, in a schoolhouse) cannot teach there.
- **Who learns:** each teacher takes the colony's **least-skilled** teachable colonist it can raise — anywhere in the colony (a worked tile, a building, or idle) — but **no two teachers take the same colonist in one turn** (FreeCol `findStudent`'s `u.getTeacher()==null`). Only petty criminals, indentured servants and free colonists are teachable (only they have an education ladder); an existing expert is never a student, nor is a colonist sitting in a school (it's staff). The teacher itself is never its own student.
- **What they become:** one rung per completed cycle, toward the teacher's taught skill — criminal→servant→free→the-teacher's-expertise. A colonial regular teaches the veteran-soldier skill (`skill-taught` indirection).
- **How long:** the spec base turns (criminal/servant rungs 4; an expertise 4/6/8 by expert), **reduced by the colony's Sons-of-Liberty production bonus**, floored at 1. **Each teacher carries its own progress counter** (one per teacher slot); a counter **resets** when its student graduates or that teacher has no eligible student that turn.
- **Assigning a teacher (`86d3fpxd6`):** the player explicitly designates a teacher with `Game.AssignTeacher(colony, school, expertType)`, gated by `CheckAssignTeacher` — the school must teach and have a free workplace, the named colonist must be idle, and its skill must fall inside the school's min/max window. A non-expert (below the floor) or an over-skilled expert is **refused up front** rather than seated as dead weight (FreeCol `WorkLocation.getNoAddReason` MINIMUM/MAXIMUM_SKILL). `AssignableTeachers(colony, school)` lists the eligible idle experts for the colony screen; unlike the generic `AssignBuildingWork` (which seats a free colonist), this places the **named expert**.
- **On-the-job learning (`86d3c9pgj` tiles / `86d3kgbpd` buildings):** a **free colonist** working a tile OR a building accrues that turn's output as experience and rolls a per-turn chance — `experience / (100·maxExp/4)`, peaking at **4%** once experience caps (the free colonist's `maximum-experience` is 200) — to upgrade **in place** to that work's expert (the tile good's expert, or the building's expert). Only free colonists are eligible; the count model is untouched (one occupant swaps type). **Buildings are a Col1 addition** — FreeCol's classic experience table lists only the 9 outdoor experts and teaches building experts through schools; we keep our "classic" spec a byte-faithful FreeCol mirror and supply the building rate in code. Distinct from schooling: no teacher needed, and only free colonists (not servants/criminals) learn.
- **Deviations / not yet modelled:**
  - **Progress is per-teacher-slot, not bound to one specific student across turns** (FreeCol binds a teacher to a specific student until it graduates). Within one turn every teacher sees the same colony state and claims a distinct student; across turns, if the colony's least-skilled colonist *changes* mid-cycle, a teacher's accrued turns carry to the new least-skilled student it picks rather than the original — a faithfulness edge with small gameplay impact (a late-arriving criminal can graduate a rung a turn early). Flagged as a follow-up.
  - **Tie-break among equally-least-skilled students** (`86d3c9p7f` follow-up, FreeCol `findStudent`): a student already **working the teacher's expert good** wins the tie (`getWorkType() == expertise` — e.g. an expert ore miner teaches the colonist already mining ore before an equally-skilled farmer). Remaining ties (no trade match, or several) fall back to the stable enumeration order (tiles row-major → non-school buildings → idle). Deterministic, no RNG.
  - Building **upkeep** (gold) is unmodelled project-wide. Student selection is automatic (classic `allowStudentSelection=false`); only teacher placement is the player's.

**Clearing a speciality (`86d3fpxge`).** `Game.CheckClearSkill(unit)` / `Game.ClearSkill(unit)` revert an expert to a plain free colonist (FreeCol `InGameController.clearSpeciality`): allowed for an **on-map** colonial specialist that has a `clearSkill` unit-change (every expert/master/preacher), refused for a free colonist / servant / criminal / non-person / native / off-map unit. FreeCol forbids clearing an **active teacher's** speciality; in our model teachers are **in-colony building workers**, never on-map units, so the on-map gate already covers that case (no separate teacher check needed). The colony screen surfaces this as a **Clear speciality** button per on-map expert standing on/beside the colony, gated on `CheckClearSkill` (ADR-006); see [colonies.md](colonies.md) §2.

## 3. Technical design

*Audience: developers / future sessions.*

- **Data (`86d3c9p7f` slice 1):** `BuildingType.MaximumSkill`/`MinimumSkill` (spec `maximum-skill`/`minimum-skill`, the latter inherited down the `extends` chain — the school window is 1..1/1..2/1..4) + `BuildingType.Teaches` (`model.ability.teach`, inherited). `UnitType.SkillTaught` (spec `skill-taught`, **own attribute, non-inherited**, defaults to the type itself) + `SkillTaughtOrSelf`. The `model.unitChange.education` table is parsed by `Ruleset.ParseEducationTurns` into `from → (to → turns)` — recovering every row past the by-`from` collapse the generic `<unit-type-change>` parse causes (the same fix as the experience table).
- **`Ruleset.GetTeachingType(teacher, student)`** (port of `UnitType.getTeachingType`): `taught = Unit(teacher).SkillTaughtOrSelf`, `taughtLevel = Unit(taught).Skill`; null if the student is already at/above that skill or has no education rows; if the student's rows reach `taught` directly, that; else it climbs **one** rung (a `to` with `Skill < taughtLevel`, recursing) — yielding a single step per cycle. **`NeededTurnsOfTraining`** returns the base turns of the rung `GetTeachingType` picks.
- **Colony state (`86d3fpyc0`):** `_schoolTrainingTurns` is now `building id → List<int>` — **one accrued-turn counter per teacher slot** (the overlay analogue of FreeCol's per-`Unit` `turnsOfTraining`; we have no per-colonist objects). `AddSchoolTrainingTurn(buildingId, slot)` grows the list as needed; `ResetSchoolTraining(buildingId, slot)` zeroes one slot (dropping the entry when all slots are 0); `ResetAllSchoolTraining(buildingId)` drops the building; `SchoolTrainingTurnsAt(buildingId)` reads slot 0 (the single-teacher reading kept for the schoolhouse/old callers) and `SchoolTrainingTurnsAt(buildingId, slot)` reads any slot; `RestoreSchoolTraining` has a list overload (v61) + a legacy int overload (pre-v61 → slot 0). Students upgrade **in place** without touching the worker count: `UpgradeTileWorker` (sets/clears the tile overlay type + clears XP), `UpgradeBuildingWorker` (swap one occupant's overlay type — add for a free→non-free promotion, remove for a non-free→free), `UpgradeIdleWorker` (same for the idle pool, free idle being implicit). `ReconcileWorkerTypes` sweeps training for a building no longer present.
- **The teach step (`Game.RunSchoolTeaching`):** runs in `RunColonyTurn` **after growth** (population + bonus settled, FreeCol's reason for deferring `csCheckTeach`), **before** the custom-house sale. For each `Teaches` building: `EligibleTeachers` lists every occupant in the school's `[MinimumSkill, MaximumSkill]` window, Ordinal-ordered (the index = its **slot**). Then, **in two passes** so every teacher's selection sees the same colony state: **pass 1** — each teacher (slot) runs `FindLeastSkilledStudent(colony, teacher, claimed)` (least-skill-first across tiles row-major → **non-school** buildings → idle; on a skill tie a student **producing the teacher's `ExpertProduction` good** wins; skips any student already in the `claimed` set — FreeCol `findStudent`'s `u.getTeacher()==null`; **no RNG**), claims that student (a per-turn `StudentRef` keyed by tile / building+occurrence-index / idle+occurrence-index), accrues a turn on its slot, and queues a graduation when `SchoolTrainingTurnsAt(slot) >= needed = max(1, NeededTurnsOfTraining − ProductionBonus)`; **pass 2** — apply the queued graduations (`UpgradeStudent` → `GetTeachingType(teacher, student).Id`). Folding graduations into pass 1 would mutate the worker overlay mid-loop and shift a later teacher's claim indices. A teacher with no eligible student resets its slot (progress lapses); a school with no teacher resets all slots.
- **Assign-teacher command (`86d3fpxd6`):** `Game.CheckAssignTeacher(colony, buildingId, teacherType)` (the gate: school teaches + free workplace + that type idle + `MinimumSkill ≤ Skill ≤ MaximumSkill`) → `Game.AssignTeacher` (draws the named idle expert into the school via `Colony.AssignBuildingWorker`). `Game.AssignableTeachers(colony, buildingId)` returns the distinct idle expert types that pass the gate, Ordinal-ordered, for the colony panel. The panel's `ColonyPanel.BuildingCell` renders one **`AssignTeacher_{school}_{expert}`** button per assignable teacher (ADR-006: the panel reads the oracle, forwards the command — no `GameController` edit; the click calls `_game.AssignTeacher` directly, mirroring the existing `_game.AssignBuildingWork` building-staff pattern).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** the teach step draws **no** RNG and iterates in a fixed order (Ordinal teacher order, row-major student search), so it is twin-deterministic and the human's stream 0 is untouched. The L5 soak's AI colonies are all free colonists and never staff an expert into a school, so `RunSchoolTeaching` is a pure no-op there → byte-stable.
- **Persistence:** save **v61**, additive — `SavedColony.SchoolTrainingSlots` (`building id → per-teacher-slot turns`, omitted when no school is mid-training, so a non-teaching game is byte-identical to v60). The legacy single-int `SchoolTraining` field (v32..v60) is still **read** for older saves — restored as a building's slot-0 turns — but no longer written. Student *type* changes ride the existing worker-overlay save; only the per-teacher accrued turns are new.
- **Building on-the-job learning (`86d3kgbpd`):** `Game.AccrueAndRollBuildingExperience(colony, buildingId, expertType, produced, rng)` mirrors the tile `AccrueAndRollExperience`. `RunBuildingProduction` finds the building's expert (`BuildingExpertType` → `Ruleset.ExpertForProducing(output good)`) and tallies that good's output this turn as the experience gain, then calls it. It accrues into `Colony._buildingWorkerExperience` — a **per-building shared pool** (building occupants of a type are fungible, so there is no per-colonist counter as for tiles) — and, on a successful roll, `UpgradeBuildingWorker(buildingId, freeColonist, expert)` graduates **one** free occupant and clears the pool. Draws **no** RNG when no free colonist staffs the building (clearing any stale pool), so it never perturbs the stream where nothing can be learned. The probability falls back to `Game.ClassicBuildingExperienceProbability = 4` because FreeCol's spec carries no building experience row (a variant may add one; then that value wins). Uses the owner's stream (`RandomFor(owner)`, stream 0 for the human), like the tile path — deterministic per seed. **Save v70** (additive): `SavedColony.BuildingWorkerExperience` (per-building pool, **only positive values written** so a 0 pool is byte-identical to v69 — matching the per-tile filter).
