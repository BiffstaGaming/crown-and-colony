## 1. How it works (plain English)

*Audience: anyone — no jargon.*

Every European power keeps track of how it feels about every **other** European power: a **stance** (are we *uncontacted*, at *peace*, or at *war*?) and a hidden **tension** meter (a grudge level that climbs when you're wronged and slowly cools over time).

- You begin **uncontacted** with each rival — you simply haven't met. The first time one side actually *sees* the other (a unit or colony comes into view), you become **at peace** with each other. Meeting carries no grudge.
- **Stance decides whether you can fight at all.** Once you have *met* a rival and are at **peace** (or a **cease-fire** truce, or an **alliance**), you simply **cannot attack** them — the order is refused — and your soldiers can't even step onto a tile their unit is standing on; you must be **at war** first. But while you are still **uncontacted** (you've never met), an attack *is* allowed, and throwing that first punch is what **declares the war**. So in plain terms: a power you're at peace with is safe from you, and you from them, until someone formally goes to war; an unmet stranger you can strike on sight (and instantly be at war with). **Exception — privateers:** a ship with the `piracy` ability can raid rival shipping (or be raided) **without** provoking war, flying no flag (see [combat](combat.md)).
- **Going to war** (by attacking an unmet rival, by the King ordering it, or by a rival breaking the peace) makes you **at war** — both ways, immediately — and spikes the grudge to the top.
- Each turn, tension **cools** a little on its own (the same slow fade the native nations' anger uses) — and the relationship **follows the grudge meter**: a war that has cooled enough becomes a **cease-fire** (an uneasy truce), and a cease-fire that keeps cooling drifts back to **peace**. A power can also **break the peace** on its own when you crowd it with colonies (its grudge climbs past the war line).

**Important, this slice:** the computer players **act on War**: a rival at war comes after your units (slice 1c-2), **marches on your colonies** when there's no field unit to chase (besiege), and **captures an undefended colony** outright (1c-3f) — see [combat](combat.md). And now that **stance gates combat**, a power you are at **peace** with no longer attacks you, and you cannot attack it either, until war is declared (`86d3drn45`). The computer powers also **haggle treaties amongst themselves** during their turn: two rivals at war with each other trade peace offers back and forth — pruning the clauses one side won't stomach, dropping its asking price — until they settle a truce or the talks break down (`86d3drn4h`). **The human now has a negotiation screen of their own** (`86d3c9xpt`/`86d3c9ubw`) — see *The human negotiation screen* below. What's still missing: gold/goods/colony/unit clauses on a *human-built* offer (the human proposes a bare change-of-stance for now) and the multi-round haggle from the human's side, plus the Founding Fathers that care about diplomacy (de Witt, Franklin).

**The native nations are not part of this yet.** Your relationship with each native settlement is still its own separate "alarm" meter ([natives](natives.md)); folding the two systems together is future work.

**Worked example:**
> A rival builds a colony six tiles away. You can't see it, so you stay *uncontacted*. Your scout walks over a hill and the colony comes into view — now you're both *at peace*, tension 0. A few turns later you attack one of their wagon trains: instantly you're both *at war*, tension jumps to maximum, and from then on it ticks down a little each turn (but stays *war* until a future peace deal).

**What the player sees and does:** there is now a **Diplomacy screen** (see *The human negotiation screen* below) — a button on the HUD, plus a mission menu when a scout walks up to a rival's colony. The underlying stance/tension state still lives in the save and is queryable by the rules/tests.

### The human negotiation screen (`86d3c9xpt` / `86d3c9ubw`)

You finally have a seat at the table. There are two ways in:

- The **Diplomacy** button on the HUD opens the negotiation screen at any time.
- A **scout walks up to a rival's colony** and is offered a short mission menu — *Spy on the colony* (sneak a one-off look inside, exactly as before) or *Open negotiations* with that colony's owner. (A non-scout soldier that walks up to the same colony still just assaults it.)

On the screen you can do two things:

1. **Answer offers a rival has made you.** When a computer power has proposed a treaty to you this turn (an alliance, a cease-fire, a peace deal — the proactive offers described above), it's waiting here. You **Accept** it (it takes effect immediately — the relationship flips, and any grudge change applies) or **Decline** it (it's simply dropped; nothing happens). The screen even pops up on its own at the start of your turn if a rival has made you an offer.
2. **Build and send a treaty of your own.** Pick a rival you've **met** and assemble a treaty out of any combination of clauses — exactly the deal-pieces the computer powers trade amongst themselves (`86d3e4bbv`):
   - **Pay gold** to them, or **demand gold** from them (dial the amount up and down in 100-gold steps).
   - **Give goods** from one of your colonies' warehouses.
   - **Cede one of your colonies**, or **demand one of theirs**.
   - **Hand over one of your units**, or **demand one of theirs**.
   - **Incite** them to go to war with a third power you've met.
   - A **change of relations** suited to where you stand (the same de-escalation ladder: at war → peace or cease-fire; at peace → alliance; under a cease-fire → peace or alliance).

   Each clause you add shows up in a running list (with a Remove button); when you're happy, **Submit** it. The rival weighs the whole package with the very same judgement it uses for any deal. If it's a fair deal for them, they **accept** and every clause settles at once. If it isn't, they don't just walk away — they **counter**: they throw out the parts they'd never agree to, and where the only sticking point is gold *they'd* pay they offer to **pay half as much**, then hand the trimmed deal back. You then **Accept** their counter (it settles) or **Reject** it (nothing happens).

> **Worked example:** The Spanish, losing their war with you, offer a cease-fire at the start of your turn — the Diplomacy screen pops up showing their offer. You'd rather have a full peace *and* be paid for it, so you Decline, then open your own negotiation with Spain: you add a *peace* clause and a *demand 2000 gold* clause and Submit. Spain can't stomach paying that much, so they counter — keeping the peace but offering to pay only ~1000. You Accept, and the war ends with gold in your pocket.

This is now the **full negotiation table** from your side: you assemble an arbitrary multi-clause treaty and get one accept/counter/reject answer back. The one thing still simpler than the original is the *back-and-forth*: the rival answers your offer once (accept, a single counter, or reject) rather than haggling over many rounds the way two computer powers do. Everything the screen does runs through the existing, tested treaty machinery — the screen itself invents no rules.

### Demanding tribute under threat (`86d3drn3b`)

An **armed unit** standing next to a **native settlement** can **demand tribute** instead of attacking — shaking the village down for gold under the threat of force. The settlement decides whether to pay based on how it already feels about you:

- A settlement that is still **calm** (Happy or Content) hands over a modest purse of gold.
- One that is **wary** (Displeased) pays roughly **half** as much.
- An **angry or hateful** one **refuses outright** and you get nothing.

Either way the demand is an **insult**, so the settlement gets **more alarmed** with you whether or not it paid — keep shaking down the same village and it will eventually turn hostile. And a village can only be squeezed **once every few turns**: demand again too soon and it simply refuses (it has nothing more to give right now). Making the demand uses up the unit's turn, exactly like an attack would.

This is the aggressive cousin of a scout's friendly visit: a scout *asks* the chief for a gift, a soldier *demands* one. (The settlement evaluation is its own thing here; folding it into the full European treaty system is future work — the treaty machinery already reserves a "tribute" context for it.)

### Diplomatic trade (backend)

Two European powers can strike a **treaty** — a deal where several things change hands all at once. A treaty has a **proposer** (the side offering it) and a **recipient** (the side it's offered to), and a list of **clauses**. Each clause is one thing being traded:

- **Gold** — "I'll pay you 200 gold" (the gold leaves one treasury and lands in the other).
- **Goods** — "…in exchange for 100 tobacco from your colony" (the goods move from one colony's warehouse to another's).
- **A change of relations** — "…and we agree to peace" (the two powers' stance flips to whatever the clause says — peace, a cease-fire truce, or an outright alliance). A change of relations also moves the hidden **grudge meter**: making peace or allying *cools* it, declaring war *spikes* it (so a peace deal isn't undone the very next turn, and a fresh war starts genuinely hostile).
- **A colony** — "…and you can have my colony" (a whole colony — its people, buildings and stores — changes owner, intact; nobody is plundered as it would be in a wartime capture).
- **A unit** — "…and take this colonist too" (a single unit changes allegiance and stays where it is).
- **An incitement** — "…go to war with the Spanish for me" (the power you're dealing with is set at war with a *third* power; the gold you pay for the favour is just another gold clause in the same treaty).

A treaty only does something when it is **accepted and settled**: at that moment every clause is carried out together. Until then it's just a proposal sitting in memory. A treaty with no clauses isn't a real offer, so settling an empty one does nothing.

**This is the plumbing, not the screen.** There's no negotiation table to click on yet. The computer players **weigh up** an offer with a self-contained calculator that scores a proposed treaty from a rival's point of view and answers "would they accept this?" (gold they'd gain is good, gold they'd pay is bad; making peace is attractive when you're weak, war when you're strong; a colony or unit they'd have to give up from a thin holding is a deal-breaker). And the rivals now **use** that judgement during their turn: a power that is **losing a war** with you — it is militarily weak — will **sue for peace on its own**, ending the war without you lifting a finger; a strong power presses on. (The same calculator is the seam a future negotiation screen will use to let a power answer a deal *you* offer.)

**The computer players don't only beg for peace — they now make offers off their own back.** Two new proactive moves on a rival's turn:

- **An alliance.** A power that is **militarily strong** and **on friendly terms** (at peace) with another power, where the two of them **share a common enemy** (both are at war with some third power), will **offer that friend an alliance** — the classic "the enemy of my enemy is my friend." If the offer goes to another computer player, it's settled there and then; if it's aimed at *you*, it's set aside for the (not-yet-built) negotiation screen — never forced on you.
- **A cease-fire.** When a war has ground to a **stalemate** — the two sides are evenly matched, so neither can land a knockout — a power will **offer a cease-fire truce** rather than keep bleeding. (A side that is clearly *winning* presses on; a side that is clearly *losing* sues for outright peace, as before.)

**How "the offer reaches you" works.** When a computer power's proactive offer is aimed at the human, it isn't applied — it's **queued up** in a pending-offers list for the negotiation screen to show you later (you decide at the table). Until that screen exists, the offer simply waits there and expires; nothing changes behind your back. (A computer-to-computer offer skips the queue and is negotiated immediately.) Because none of this is saved and the only dice-roll — the haggle's "have I lost patience?" — is rolled on each computer player's *own* private dice, never yours, an un-provoked game still plays exactly the same.

**A treaty also remembers the situation it arose in** (its *context*): a peace deal struck at the very moment two powers **first meet** is **free** — they're just formalising "hello, let's not fight" — so it's always accepted no matter who's stronger. A peace deal at any other time is weighed the normal way (a strong power may refuse to stop a war it's winning). The default for any treaty is the ordinary, weighed kind, so nothing about existing deals changes.

A rival that **can't accept an offer as-is doesn't just walk away — it haggles back.** It makes a **counter-offer**: it throws out the clauses it hates (the parts it would refuse at any price), and where the only sticking point is gold *it* would pay, it offers to **pay half as much** rather than abandon the whole deal — then hands the trimmed offer back. If after all that there's still nothing it would agree to (every clause was a deal-breaker, or what's left still costs it more than it's worth), it gives up and rejects. And even a salvageable haggle doesn't go on forever: each round there's a growing chance the rival simply **loses patience** and stops countering — so a back-and-forth between two computer players always ends, either in a deal both sides accept or in a collapse. (Today this haggling is exercised computer-to-computer; the human will drive it from the negotiation screen later.) What's still ahead: gold-for-peace deals proposed proactively (the computer powers now propose **alliances and cease-fires** on their own — see below — but not yet a gold sweetener), and the negotiation screen itself. (Because nothing is saved here and the only dice-roll — "have I lost patience?" — is rolled on each computer player's *own* private dice, never the human's, an un-provoked game still plays exactly the same.)

> **Worked example (a counter-offer):** A rival is offered a colony it values at ~300 gold, but the offer asks it to pay 380 gold for it — a net loss as it stands, so it can't accept. Instead of rejecting, it counters: it keeps the colony clause but offers to pay only ~190 gold, which turns the deal net-positive for it, and proposes *that* trimmed version back to the other side.

> **Worked example:** You're at war with the Dutch and want it to end. You build a treaty you propose to them with two clauses: *you pay 300 gold*, and *both sides go to peace*. When it's accepted, 300 gold leaves your treasury for theirs and the war between you becomes peace — in one settlement.

## 2. Detailed rules

*Audience: designers/testers.*

- **Scope: colonial ↔ colonial only.** Stance/tension are tracked between `PlayerType.Colonial` players (the human + the foreign powers). Native players are **ignored** by every helper — they keep `NativeSettlement.Alarm`/`AlarmLevel` ([natives](natives.md)). This mirrors FreeCol's own split (Europeans flip stance instantly on an attack; natives accumulate settlement alarm and self-declare later) and avoids re-baselining the native system. Unifying them is deferred.
- **`Stance` values** (`Stance.cs`): `Uncontacted` (0, the default for any unrecorded pair) · `Peace` (1) · `War` (2) · `CeaseFire` (3) · `Alliance` (4). A faithful subset of FreeCol's `Uncontacted/Alliance/Peace/CeaseFire/War`. Values are explicit and **appended** (not in FreeCol's declaration order) so earlier saved ordinals stay stable — `Alliance` was appended as 4 (`86d3c9u3z`), leaving 0–3 untouched. `Alliance` is **only** set by a diplomacy *action* (a `StanceTradeItem`); the tension→stance machine never produces it (it stays within Uncontacted/Peace/War/CeaseFire).
- **Directional storage.** Each `Player` holds its **own** view of every other player it has met: `Stances` (other `PlayerId` → `Stance`) and `Tensions` (other `PlayerId` → 0..`MaxTension`). An absent entry reads as `Uncontacted`/`0`. (FreeCol likewise stores per-player maps.)
- **Stance gates combat & movement legality (`86d3drn45`).** `AreEnemies(a, b)` — the single predicate behind `DefenderAt` and so behind `CheckAttack`/`CheckMove`/fog — is now stance-aware for colonial pairs: a shared owner is never an enemy; if **either** unit is native, owner-inequality decides (natives are on alarm, not stance — so native raids and colonist-vs-brave combat are **completely unaffected**); if **both** are colonial, the pair is hostile **only at `War`, or while `Uncontacted`** (the FreeCol edge case where the two have never met — the human starts Uncontacted with every rival, and an attack-while-uncontacted is what declares the war). At `Peace`/`CeaseFire`/`Alliance` they are **not** enemies: `CheckAttack` returns "no enemy to attack there", and `CheckMove` rejects stepping onto a tile a non-enemy foreign unit holds (a new guard: any `!SameOwner` unit on the target tile that `DefenderAt` did *not* flag as an enemy blocks the move — you can neither share nor attack a peaceful neighbour's tile). Entering a **rival colony** is already blocked at every stance by the existing owner-check in `CheckMove`. This is faithful-with-a-twist to FreeCol: vanilla lets a field-unit attack *declare* war on contact (owner-inequality), which we keep only for the Uncontacted edge; once you have *met* and are at peace, we require a war declaration first (the intended behaviour change — the AI no longer attacks the human at peace, and vice-versa). `CheckAttackColony` (colony capture) keeps its owner-inequality test: the AI capture path is already war-gated upstream, and a human colony assault while Uncontacted stays legal exactly like a unit attack.
- **Transitions** (all deterministic, no RNG; the transitions themselves don't gate legality — the gate lives in `AreEnemies` above):

| Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|
| First contact: a colonial player's explored fog first covers a tile holding another colonial player's on-map unit or colony (`DetectColonialContacts`, run once per `EndTurn`) | both directions `Uncontacted` → `Peace`, tension unchanged (FreeCol `CONTACT_MODIFIER` = 0). Already-met pairs (Peace/War) are skipped |
| Attack: a colonial unit attacks another colonial player's unit (`Attack`) | both directions → `War`, tension += `TensionWar` (1000, clamped to `MaxTension` 1100). Recorded **before** combat resolves, so win/loss is irrelevant. The attack *records* the war but the **legality** of the attack is now decided up front by `AreEnemies` (see *Stance gates combat & movement legality* below): an attack at `Peace`/`CeaseFire`/`Alliance` is refused (no war is recorded — you never reach this hook), an attack while `Uncontacted` or `War` proceeds and records War here. **Skipped entirely when the attacker or defender has `model.ability.piracy`** (a privateer raids without war — 1c-3d-i; the stance is left as-is, not reset) |
| Each turn (`DecayColonialTension`, run in `EndTurn`) | every recorded colonial-pair tension cools by `value/100 + 4` (floored at 0) — the same formula as native alarm decay |
| Each turn after decay (`UpdateColonialStances` → `StanceFromTension`, FP-6b) | each met pair's stance re-derives from its (cooled) tension (FreeCol `Stance.getStanceFromTension`, DELTA=10 hysteresis): **War → CeaseFire** at tension ≤ 590 (`CONTENT.limit−DELTA`); **CeaseFire → Peace** at ≤ 90 (`HAPPY.limit−DELTA`); **Peace/CeaseFire → War** above 1010 (`HATEFUL.limit+DELTA`); `Uncontacted` unchanged. Symmetric, ordered by id |

- **Numeric scale.** Tension is 0..`MaxTension` (1100 = FreeCol `Tension.Level.HATEFUL.limit` + 100); `TensionWar` (1000) is the FreeCol WAR modifier; the stance thresholds use `CONTENT.limit` 600, `HAPPY.limit` 100, `DELTA` 10. These mirror the native-alarm constants but are declared in `GameSession` to avoid a dependency on `GameLogic.Natives`.
- **Peace→War from tension alone is currently unreachable in play:** the only thing that raises colonial tension is an attack, which already sets War directly (and decay only lowers tension). The `StanceFromTension` Peace→War branch is implemented faithfully for when a non-attack tension source lands (e.g. land-taking), and is covered by a direct unit test.
- **Colonial-colony assault → war (1c-3e):** assaulting a rival **colony** (`AttackColony`) now also records mutual War + a tension spike (both ways, before the colony changes hands), the same hook as a unit attack — closing the FP-6a gap. (Capturing your own colony back is impossible; the trigger only fires between distinct colonial players.)
- **Foreign-AI colony capture uses War as its gate (1c-3f):** a foreign power only seizes the human's undefended colony when it is already at `Stance.War` with the human (the same gate as the 1c-2 unit-hunt; war still starts only from the human's own attack). The AI capture reuses `AttackColony`, so its war/tension hook fires again, harmlessly (the pair is already at war). The AI does not yet *declare* war on its own — see the deviations below. Capture mechanics live in [combat](combat.md).
- **The King can impose war or peace (`86d3c9r7j`):** the home Monarch's `DECLARE_WAR`/`DECLARE_PEACE` actions now call `SetStance` to drag the human into (or out of) a war with a King-chosen European rival — a stance-change source *other* than the human's own attack. Stance-only (no tension delta), drawn on the monarch's own RNG. This is the first non-attack stance transition that actually fires in play. Rules live in [monarchy](monarchy.md).

**Deviations from FreeCol:** colonial tension **decay** is a deliberate symmetry with native alarm — FreeCol has no European tension decay; the slice scope asks for it. Decay feeds the stance machine, which can only *de-escalate* from it (war→cease-fire→peace), never escalate (decay lowers tension; escalation needs >1010, which decay can't produce) — so it cannot surprise the player. **Combat legality now follows stance** (`86d3drn45`) — a deliberate twist on FreeCol, which lets a field-unit attack *declare* war on any rival on owner-inequality alone (we keep that only for the `Uncontacted` edge case; at `Peace`/`CeaseFire`/`Alliance` we require a war declaration first, so a met-and-peaceful neighbour is safe from attack both ways). The diplomacy actions themselves — `Alliance` + the trade clauses that set stance/incite, and their tension modifiers (alliance −500, peace-treaty −250, cease-fire −250, resume-war +750, war-inciter +250) — are **now implemented** as the diplomatic-trade backend (`86d3c9u3z`/`86d3c9u94`); the AI can **score** an offer (`EvaluateTrade`, `86d3c9uar`), **counter-offer** when it can't accept as-is (`CounterOffer`/`NegotiateTrade`, `86d3c9uar` — prune the clauses it values negatively, halve gold it pays, give up via a seeded patience roll on its own stream), and now **acts on its turn** — a power losing a war **sues for peace** (`RunForeignPowerDiplomacy`/`RespondToTrade`, `86d3c9uar` turn-wiring), and a power now **proactively offers** an **alliance** (when strong + friendly + sharing an enemy) or a **cease-fire** (when a war is stalemated) (`ProposeProactiveTreaties`, `86d3drn4f`); a treaty also carries a **`TradeContext`** that frees a peace clause on first contact (`86d3drn2g`); see *Diplomatic trade (backend)*. **The human's negotiation UI is now built** (`86d3c9xpt`/`86d3c9ubw`) — the `NegotiationPanel` consumes `PendingHumanProposals` (accept/decline) and lets the human open a single-clause stance proposal answered by `EvaluateTrade`, with a scout-at-a-rival-colony entry (spy / negotiate). What remains out: gold-for-peace clauses proactively proposed (the AI proposes bare stance clauses today), and gold/goods/colony/unit clauses on a *human-built* offer + a human-side multi-round haggle.

## 3. Technical design

*Audience: developers / future sessions.*

- **`Stance` enum** (`GameSession/Stance.cs`) — `Uncontacted=0/Peace=1/War=2/CeaseFire=3/Alliance=4`; zero-value default means an absent map entry / pre-FP-6a save reads as `Uncontacted` for free. Values are **appended** (`CeaseFire` then `Alliance`) so every earlier ordinal is byte-stable — adding `Alliance` needs **no save-version bump**. Serialized as its integer ordinal (no `JsonStringEnumConverter` configured), matching how `PlayerType` is stored.
- **`Player`** holds `_stance`/`_tension` (`Dictionary<int,…>`) with public read views `Stances`/`Tensions` and `internal` mutable views `StanceMap`/`TensionMap` (the established field + read-view + internal-mutable-view pattern).
- **`Game`** API: `StanceBetween(a,b)`/`TensionBetween(a,b)` (public reads; default Uncontacted/0); `SetStance(a,b,stance,symmetric=true)`/`ChangeTension(a,b,delta,symmetric=true)` (`internal`; both clamp/guard so they are a **no-op unless `a` and `b` are distinct colonial players** — the single place the colonial-only rule is enforced, so every call site is naturally native-safe). Constants `MaxTension`/`TensionWar`.
- **Transition sites:** `DetectColonialContacts` → `DecayColonialTension` → `UpdateColonialStances` run in `EndTurn`'s world-step block (beside `DecayNativeAlarm`); the attack→war record sits in `Attack` right after the defender is resolved, guarded by `defenderNation is null` (a colonial defender). All are append-only side-effects — they never read or modify `AreEnemies`/`CheckMove`/`CheckAttack`/fog, so legality is unchanged and the human's RNG stream 0 is untouched (no draws).
- **`StanceFromTension(current, tension)`** (`internal static`) is the pure FreeCol `Stance.getStanceFromTension` port (a `switch` expression; directly unit-tested). `UpdateColonialStances` applies it after decay, over met pairs (those with a stance entry; `Uncontacted` skipped) in id order, setting the new stance symmetrically.
- **Offensive targeting (FP-6a, FreeCol `UnitSeekAndDestroyMission`):** when a power is at `War`, `RunForeignPowerTurn` picks each armed unit's target via `PickAttackTarget` — a scored seek-and-destroy over human unit-tiles (same naval/land domain) **and**, for a land unit, human colonies, within an **escalating Chebyshev range** (`SeekRangeLadder` 8/12/16; the first gate yielding any target wins). The score is `1020 − 100·d` plus a combat term: a unit-tile adds the **truncated** `(int)(100·(off − def))` (vs the tile's strongest `DefenderAt`, `off = OffenceBase`, `def = DefencePowerOf`), `+1000` per treasure train on the tile, `+500` for a naval defender caught on land; a colony adds the **rounded** `round(50·off) + Population − 200·stockadeLevel` (loot drawn, fortifications avoided; stockade 1 / fort 2 / fortress 3 → −200/−400/−600, matching FreeCol `calculateSettlementValue` — note this is the settlement-*value* penalty, **distinct** from the combat defence-bonus %). The round-vs-truncate asymmetry is FreeCol's. A **garrisoned** colony is excluded from the capture candidates — its garrison is scored as a unit-tile and fought first (else the colony would outscore its own garrison, the capture be refused, and the unit wander off). Pure (no RNG): ties resolve by a stable candidate order (units by id, then colonies by position) with strict-max, so the human's stream 0 is untouched; combat/movement still draw `RandomFor(power)`. The preserved adjacent-undefended-colony capture, an **out-of-range pursuit** (close on the nearest human unit at any distance when nothing scores within 16, so a warship / non-founder land unit never idles), and the besiege fallback wrap it. **Approximations vs FreeCol** (documented): Chebyshev tiles stand in for FreeCol's turn-cost path length (no AI pathfinder); `OffenceBase` is **pre-situational** so it omits FreeCol's +50% global `attackBonus` (and other situational offence) that `getOffencePower` adds — the unit term `100·(off−def)` and colony term `round(50·off)` are understated by ~`100·0.5·base` / ~`round(50·0.5·base)`, while `def` *is* fully situational (`DefencePowerOf`); the `+100` unarmed-expert-soldier bonus is omitted (no unit-type ability bag yet); and the `8/12/16` ladder is a **unified** range gate where FreeCol splits it per role (naval 8, ordinary land 8→16, REF-only 12). We deliberately **return** the unit-tile score that vanilla FreeCol's `scorePath` discards (a known upstream bug) so units remain targetable. The targeting is still **human-target only** (the `NearestHumanUnit`/`IsHumanOwned` filters), but `AreEnemies` is **now stance-aware** (`86d3drn45`): combat legality respects War/Uncontacted-vs-Peace/CeaseFire/Alliance, so the engine refuses an attack on a power you are at peace with — closing the FP-6 gap (b). Broadening the AI's *targeting* to any at-war rival (not just the human) remains future work.
- **Determinism:** contact iterates colonial players in `PlayerId` order over the already-deterministic `Explored` fog; decay + stance-update iterate keys/pairs in id order; the offensive scorer iterates candidates in a stable id/position order with no RNG. No RNG anywhere in the stance/targeting logic.

### Diplomatic trade (backend) — `86d3c9u2f`+

- **Model** (`GameSession/Diplomacy/`, pure value objects in the `…GameSession.Diplomacy` namespace):
  - **`DiplomaticTrade`** — a treaty with `ProposerId`/`RecipientId` (the two colonial parties, by `PlayerId`) and an ordered `Items` list (`IReadOnlyList<TradeItem>`); `Add(item)` appends (chainable), `IsValid(game)` is "≥1 clause and every clause valid", `Apply(game)` settles each still-valid clause in order (an item that became invalid since the offer is **skipped, not thrown** — the rest still settle). FreeCol `common/model/DiplomaticTrade`.
  - **`TradeItem`** — abstract clause base; each carries `Source`/`Destination` (`PlayerId`s, so a single treaty can move value both ways — FreeCol `TradeItem.getSource`/`getDestination`), with `IsValid(game)`/`Apply(game)`. FreeCol `common/model/TradeItem`.
- **Concrete clauses** (`86d3c9u94`):
  - **`GoldTradeItem(source, destination, amount)`** — pays `amount` gold from `source`'s treasury to `destination`'s. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferGold` (distinct colonial powers, `amount > 0`, payer can afford it — FreeCol `GoldTradeItem.isValid`: `0 ≤ gold ≤ source.getGold()`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferGold` (debit source, credit destination).
  - **`GoodsTradeItem(source, destination, sourceColonyId, destinationColonyId, goodsId, amount)`** — moves `amount` of `goodsId` from one colony's warehouse to another's. **Faithful-subset shape:** FreeCol's `Goods` is bound to a *source settlement*; colonial players here hold goods only in colonies (no player-level pool), so the clause names the specific **source and destination colonies**, with `Source`/`Destination` the colonies' owning players. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferColonyGoods` (positive amount; real ruleset goods id; distinct colonial powers each still owning their named colony; source holds ≥ `amount`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferColonyGoods` (debit/credit via `Colony.AddGoods`). A clause whose colony was lost or drained since the offer becomes invalid and is **skipped** by `DiplomaticTrade.Apply`.
  - **`StanceTradeItem(source, destination, stance)`** (`86d3c9u3z`) — sets the mutual stance between the two parties (in practice `Peace`/`CeaseFire`/`Alliance` — end a war, agree a truce, or ally). `IsValid` = `Game.CanSetStance` (distinct colonial powers — the only pairs whose stance is tracked, and exactly the pairs for which the stance change is not a no-op); `Apply` = `Game.ApplyStanceWithTension(source, destination, stance)` (symmetric), which sets the stance **and** applies the stance-change tension modifier — see *Tension on a stance change* below.
  - **`ColonyTradeItem(source, destination, colonyId)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — hands a whole colony from `source` to `destination`, **intact**. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferColony` (distinct colonial powers; the colony still exists and is owned by `source` — FreeCol `ColonyTradeItem.isValid`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferColony`, which **reuses the wartime `CaptureColony` path** (so the former owner's ships caught in the colony's port are damaged/sunk exactly as in a seizure) but **rolls no plunder** — a negotiated handover transfers the colony whole, so the apply path stays RNG-free. A colony lost since the offer is **skipped**.
  - **`UnitTradeItem(source, destination, unitId)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — hands one unit from `source` to `destination`. **Faithful-subset shape:** FreeCol's `isValid` is "the unit is the source's *and* its type is available to the destination"; colonial players share the one classic ruleset, so we model the ownership half — `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferUnit` (distinct colonial powers; the unit still exists and is a **non-native** unit owned by `source`). `Apply` = `Game.TransferUnit`, a pure reparent of `Unit.OwnerId` (FreeCol `unit.changeOwner`); the unit's position/location are untouched. A unit lost or reassigned since the offer is **skipped**.
  - **`InciteTradeItem(source, destination, victim)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — incites the **`source`** power (the clause's source — the party that *agrees to fight*; in a normal offer this is the recipient who accepts the deal) to war against a *third* power, `victim`, for the `destination`'s benefit. `IsValid` = `Game.CanIncite` (victim ≠ `source` ≠ `destination`, all three distinct colonial powers — FreeCol `InciteTradeItem.isValid`); `Apply` = `Game.Incite`, faithful to FreeCol `ServerGame.csAcceptTrade` (`source.csChangeStance(WAR, victim)`): it sets `source`↔`victim` to `War` **with the war stance-change tension modifier** (via `ApplyStanceWithTension`), then adds the **war-inciter spike** (`TensionWarInciter` = 250, FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_WAR_INCITER`) to the victim's tension toward the `destination` (the instigator). The gold the destination pays travels as a separate `GoldTradeItem` clause in the same treaty.
- **`Game` seam** — `Game.Diplomacy.cs` (a thin partial; `Game.cs` untouched) exposes `public void SettleTrade(DiplomaticTrade trade) => trade.Apply(this)`, plus the `internal` mutators the clauses call: `CanTransferGold`/`TransferGold`, `CanTransferColonyGoods`/`TransferColonyGoods` (colony lookup reuses the existing `ColonyById`), `CanSetStance`, `ApplyStanceWithTension` (the stance clause + incite route through this), `CanTransferColony`/`TransferColony` (reuses the private `CaptureColony`), `CanTransferUnit`/`TransferUnit` (reuses the private `UnitById`), and `CanIncite`/`Incite`. Each `Transfer*`/`Incite` re-checks its `Can*` guard, so an invalid call is a no-op (never drives a treasury negative / never throws on a stale colony or unit).
- **Tension on a stance change** (`86d3c9u3z`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csChangeStance` + `Stance.getTensionModifier`): a treaty's stance change carries the FreeCol tension consequence. `StanceTensionModifier(oldStance, newStance)` (`internal static`, pure) ports the realistic transitions of FreeCol's table — **calming:** peace→alliance −500, cease-fire→alliance −750, war→alliance −1000, cease-fire→peace −250, war→peace −500, war→cease-fire −250; **escalating:** peace/alliance→war +1000 (= `TensionWar`), cease-fire→war +750 (`RESUME_WAR`); a no-change transition (same stance, incl. war→war) is 0. `ApplyStanceWithTension(a, b, newStance)` reads the current stance, calls `SetStance` then `ChangeTension` by the modifier (symmetric, mirroring `csChangeStance`'s per-direction modifier — in our always-symmetric model both directions share the same `old`, so they apply the same delta). **The diplomacy apply path is the only caller** — the generic `SetStance` used by first contact, the tension→stance machine, and monarch-imposed war/peace stays deliberately tension-free, so those paths are unchanged. Incite additionally adds the war-inciter +250 (`TensionWarInciter`, FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_WAR_INCITER`) to the victim's view of the destination (directional). All deterministic — no RNG.
- **AI evaluation (`86d3c9uar`)** — `public TradeEvaluation EvaluateTrade(powerId, trade)` (+ `bool ShouldAcceptTrade` convenience) scores a proposed treaty from a colonial power's point of view, a faithful subset of FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.acceptDiplomaticTrade`: each clause is valued by `internal int EvaluateTradeItem(powerId, item)` and the power **accepts** exactly when *no* clause is unacceptable (returns the `InvalidTradeItem` = `int.MinValue` sentinel) **and** the summed value is ≥ 0. Per-clause values (FreeCol the matching `TradeItem.evaluateFor`): **gold** ±amount (received +, paid −); **goods** at the power's market — giver −`AskPrice·amount`, receiver +`round(BidPrice·amount·(1−tax/100))`; **stance/incite** by the FreeCol military-strength ratio `StrengthRatio = ours/(ours+theirs)` over `LandPowerOf` (war wanted when strong / refused when ratio < 0.33; peace wanted when weak, +1000 at ratio < 0.33 / refused at ratio > 0.66; incite −`round(50/ratio)`, 0 if already at war, invalid if allied with the victim); **colony**/**unit** by a documented proxy (population × 300 / flat 200) with FreeCol's AI "don't trade away below `TRADE_MARGIN` 5 colonies / 10 units" guards. **`TradeEvaluation`** (`Diplomacy/`) is the `(Accept, Value, UnacceptableItems)` result. **Pure** — reads the game, changes nothing, draws no RNG (ADR-009). **Approximations vs FreeCol** (documented): the colony/unit values are flat proxies for FreeCol's full `Colony.evaluateFor`/`Unit.evaluateFor`; the multi-round haggling and patience cut-off of the full method are out of scope (single-shot accept/reject core). **Founding-father diplomacy is now folded in:** Franklin's `alwaysOfferedPeace` forces a peace clause from a Franklin party to score 0 (the `franklin` branch — see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)); his `peaceTreaty +50%` peace-hold lives at its own faithful home (the Peace→War re-declaration, `PeaceTreatyHolds`), not in this evaluator.
- **AI turn-wiring (`86d3c9uar` + `86d3drn4h`)** — `RunForeignPowerTurn` now *uses* the evaluation. `internal bool RespondToTrade(power, trade)` scores an offered treaty via `EvaluateTrade` and `SettleTrade`s it iff the power accepts (the AI-consumption seam; both the self-proposed peace and a future negotiation UI route through it). `RunForeignPowerDiplomacy(power, alreadyAtWar)` is called **unconditionally** each foreign turn (the call site passes `alreadyAtWar` — the human-war flag captured *before* this turn's territorial-tension accrual, so a war just declared this turn is pressed, not undone). Its **human sue-for-peace** half (gated internally on `alreadyAtWar` + still-at-war-with-human) builds a single-clause peace `DiplomaticTrade` (`StanceTradeItem` to `Peace`) and `RespondToTrade`s it, so a militarily weak power (low `StrengthRatio` → peace scores positive) sues for peace and the war ends; a strong power's evaluation rejects it and it fights on (bounded: one peace offer/turn, no haggling, RNG-free). Its **foreign-foreign** half (`86d3drn4h`) drives the multi-round `NegotiateTrade` over each foreign-foreign war — see *AI turn-wiring of `NegotiateTrade`* below. **Deterministic** — the human path draws no RNG; the foreign-foreign haggle draws only the AI powers' own streams; human stream 0 byte-identical (ADR-009); no save change.
- **AI counter-offers + give-up loop (`86d3c9uar`)** — `public DiplomaticTrade? CounterOffer(int powerId, DiplomaticTrade received, int round = 0)` (`Game.Diplomacy.cs`) is the faithful subset of FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.acceptDiplomaticTrade`'s **prune + counter + give-up** branch, for a power that cannot accept an offer as-is. Steps, in FreeCol order:
  1. **Score** every clause via the pure `EvaluateTradeItem`, counting the `InvalidTradeItem` (= `int.MinValue`) sentinels.
  2. **Reject** (return `null`) when too large a fraction is unacceptable: `unacceptable / (unacceptable + items.Count) > 0.5 − 0.5·round` (FreeCol's `version` is our `round`; the tolerance shrinks as the haggle drags on).
  3. **Prune** the invalid clauses; if nothing remains → `null`.
  4. **Give-up roll** (the *only* RNG here): `RandomFor(power).Next(1 + round) > CounterPatienceThreshold` (= 5), FreeCol's `randomInt("Enough diplomacy?", 1 + version) > 5`. At `round = 0` the bound is 1 (draw always 0 → never gives up); the bound grows each round so a long haggle eventually exceeds 5 and the power walks away. Drawn from the **power's own stream** (`RandomFor`) — never the human's stream 0.
  5. **Prune negatives** ascending by score: for each negative clause, if removing it leaves the running sum `≥ GoldCounterFloor` (= 50) **and** it is a `GoldTradeItem` the power *pays* (`Source == powerId`), **halve** it (`reduced = amount − value/2`, `value /= 2`, FreeCol's exact magic-number); otherwise **drop** it. Stop once the sum is non-negative (all remaining, non-worse clauses are kept). Positives are always kept.
  6. **Propose or reject**: a counter is returned only when the final value `≥ 0` and at least one clause remains; else `null`. The counter is a fresh `DiplomaticTrade(received.ProposerId, received.RecipientId)` carrying the surviving (and reduced-gold) clauses.
  The **human never auto-counters** (`powerId == _human.PlayerId` → `null`): the human negotiates at the (future) table, so `CounterOffer` is AI-only and stream 0 stays untouched. `internal bool NegotiateTrade(Player proposer, Player recipient, DiplomaticTrade trade)` (`Game.cs`) drives a **bounded AI-to-AI** back-and-forth: the answerer either accepts (`RespondToTrade` settles) or `CounterOffer`s a trimmed deal that the other side then weighs, swapping sides each round, until one accepts, a side gives up (`CounterOffer` → `null`), or the `MaxNegotiationRounds` (= 8) cap is hit. Both parties must be non-human (a human negotiation is the UI's job) → it never draws stream 0. **Determinism:** the give-up roll is each power's own stream; valuation and trade-building are pure; the human's seeded game is byte-identical (ADR-009). **Now wired into the foreign turn (`86d3drn4h`)** — see *AI turn-wiring of `NegotiateTrade`* below. **Approximations vs FreeCol:** the Franklin "always offered peace" special-case and the CONTACT-context peace exemption are omitted (no founding-father diplomacy / contact negotiation yet); the per-round `round`/`version` is a caller argument rather than carried on the (un-persisted) trade.
- **AI turn-wiring of `NegotiateTrade` (`86d3drn4h`)** — `RunForeignPowerDiplomacy(power, alreadyAtWar)` (`Game.cs`) now runs **two** diplomacy passes on a foreign power's turn (called unconditionally; the call site in `RunForeignPowerTurn` passes `alreadyAtWar`, the human-war flag captured before this turn's territorial-tension accrual):
  1. **Sue for peace with the human** — unchanged from `86d3c9uar`, but gated on `alreadyAtWar` *inside* the method now (a single-shot `RespondToTrade` on a `Peace` `StanceTradeItem`; RNG-free).
  2. **Haggle foreign-foreign wars** — for each *other* foreign power this one is at `War` with, where `power.PlayerId < other.PlayerId` (so each war pair is driven by the **lower-id** party exactly once per round — the higher-id power's own turn finds the pair already at peace or still-collapsed and skips it), it proposes a single-clause `Peace` treaty and calls the bounded multi-round `NegotiateTrade(power, other, peace)`. The answerer accepts / prunes-and-counters / gives up until a truce settles or the talks collapse. The other powers are iterated in stable `PlayerId` order and snapshotted to a list (a settled treaty mutates stance mid-iteration). `NegotiateTrade` is AI-to-AI only and the give-up roll draws **each power's own** stream — never the human's stream 0 — so a human game is byte-identical (ADR-009). Bounded by `MaxNegotiationRounds` (= 8). No save change. (Foreign-foreign wars are rare in current play — they arise from an `InciteTradeItem` or a `SetStance` — but when they exist they now resolve through diplomacy instead of standing forever.)
- **Trade context (`86d3drn2g`, FreeCol `DiplomaticTrade.TradeContext`)** — `DiplomaticTrade` now carries a `TradeContext` (`Contact`/`Diplomatic`/`Trade`/`Tribute`, `GameSession/Diplomacy/TradeContext.cs`), set by a third constructor argument that **defaults to `Diplomatic`** so every existing call site (and so the default game) is byte-identical. It is an **evaluation-only** property — `DiplomaticTrade.Apply` ignores it entirely; it changes only how `EvaluateTrade` weighs a **stance clause**. `EvaluateTrade` threads `trade.Context` into `EvaluateTradeItem(powerId, item, context)` → `EvaluateStance(powerId, otherId, stance, context)`: at `Contact` a peace/cease-fire/alliance clause is forced **free** (scored `0` — never a cost, never `InvalidTradeItem`), exactly as FreeCol's `CONTACT` branch of `acceptDiplomaticTrade` forces a peace/uncontacted clause to 0; at every other context the established **strength-ratio gate** applies (a strong power still refuses peace, a weak one still leaps at it +1000). `CounterOffer` likewise evaluates with `received.Context` and stamps the same context on the counter. `Trade` is modelled for faithfulness but weighed like `Diplomatic` here (no carrier-trade flow yet); `Tribute` now has its standalone demand flow against native settlements (the bullet below), though it is not yet a clause in the treaty container. The context is **not persisted** (the trade is in-memory; no save change).
- **Tribute demands by offensive units (`86d3drn3b`, FreeCol `InGameController.demandTribute` + `DemandTributeMessage`)** — a colonial unit adjacent to a **native settlement** can demand gold under threat instead of attacking (the `TradeContext.Tribute` negotiation). The command lives in `Game.cs` (it leans on the native alarm/RNG infrastructure):
  - **`CheckDemandTribute(unit, target)`** — a read oracle (ADR-006), mirroring `DemandTributeMessage` validation: the unit must be on the map, **colonial** (not native — braves use the native-demand path), with **offensive strength** (`OffenceBase(unit) > 0`, the same gate `CheckAttackSettlement` uses), **movement left**, standing **on or next to** a native settlement on `target`. *Faithful subset:* FreeCol allows "armed **or** has `model.ability.demandTribute`" (an unarmed scout can demand); we gate on offence only, since that ability isn't modelled yet.
  - **`EvaluateTributeDemand(settlement, rng)`** — the accept/refuse rule, a pure function of the settlement's `AlarmLevel`, its `<gifts>` range, the cooldown, and **one** RNG draw: **Happy/Content → `GiftsAmount(type, rng) / 10`**, **Displeased → `/ 20`**, both capped at **100** (`TributeGoldCap`); **Angry/Hateful → 0** (refuses, **no RNG drawn**). `GiftsAmount` is the same continuous `<gifts>` roll the scout-beads path uses (FreeCol `gifts.getAmount("Tribute", random, true)`). Returns 0 (a refusal, no RNG) when on **cooldown**: `LastTribute > 0 && LastTribute + TributeCooldownTurns(=5) >= Turn` — a never-demanded settlement (`LastTribute == 0`) is always demandable (we track 1-based turns, where FreeCol's `lastTribute + 5 < year` always passes for a fresh settlement).
  - **`DemandTribute(unit, target)`** (+ an explicit-RNG overload for tests) — resolves it: if `EvaluateTributeDemand > 0`, the gold is **minted to the demander** (we model no native treasury, exactly as scout-beads/plunder do). **Either way** (paid or refused) the demand is an insult: `ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, +TensionAddNormal=200)`, `settlement.LastTribute = Turn` (the cooldown stamp), and `unit.MovementLeft = 0`. Returns a `TributeResult(Paid, Gold)`. The amount draws from the **demander's own stream** (`RandomFor(owner)`), so a foreign power demanding never perturbs the human's stream 0 (ADR-009); the explicit-RNG overload draws nothing from the game's saved stream.
  - **`NativeSettlement.LastTribute`** is a **transient** (not serialized) per-settlement turn stamp, default 0 — consistent with the no-native-treasury / transient-military-stock abstractions, so every save round-trips byte-identically. *Faithful subset:* FreeCol lowers the demanded gold from the native player's treasury and routes it through a model message; we mint to the demander and surface the result directly (no native treasury, no message channel). The demand is currently a **command on an existing offensive unit** (a human action / available to an AI); a dedicated AI "demand instead of attack" mission is future work.
- **AI proactive proposals (`86d3drn4f`, FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.determineStances` + the proposal side of `acceptDiplomaticTrade`)** — `public void ProposeProactiveTreaties(Player power)` (`Game.Diplomacy.cs`) is a power's **own-turn proposal pass**, beyond suing for peace when losing. Two moves, in order:
  1. **Cease-fire on a stalemated war** — for each colonial power `power` is at `War` with (in stable id order, snapshotted), if the war is a **stalemate** — `|StrengthRatio − 0.5| ≤ CeaseFireStalemateBand` (= 0.15, i.e. ratio 0.35..0.65, neither side decisive) — it offers a single-clause `CeaseFire` `StanceTradeItem`. Outside the band a winner presses on and a loser sues for outright peace via the existing path.
  2. **Alliance with a strong, friendly, shared-enemy power** — for each colonial power `power` is at `Peace` with (stable id order, snapshotted), if `power` is **strong enough** (`StrengthRatio ≥ AllianceStrengthThreshold` = 0.5) **and** the pair **shares an enemy** (some *third* colonial power both are at `War` with — `SharesAnEnemy`), it offers an `Alliance` `StanceTradeItem` ("enemy of my enemy").
  Each offer is built at `TradeContext.Diplomatic` and routed by `OfferStance`: an offer to **another AI** goes straight into the existing **`NegotiateTrade`** backend (settled now on the **proposer's own** RNG stream, or the talks collapse); an offer to the **human** is appended to **`PendingHumanProposals`** (a transient list, **not saved**) for the backlogged negotiation UI and is **never auto-applied** (the human decides at the table). `PendingHumanProposals`/`TakePendingHumanProposals()` (drain-and-clear) / `ClearPendingHumanProposals()` are the seam. **Determinism (ADR-009):** the only randomness is the AI-to-AI `NegotiateTrade` give-up roll on each AI's **own** stream; queuing a human offer draws nothing; so the human's stream 0 is byte-identical and the default game (no war / no alliance opportunity) is unchanged. **Faithful subset:** FreeCol weighs candidates via the full nation-summary machinery and may sweeten with gold; we offer a bare stance clause and lean on the existing strength-ratio evaluation to accept/decline. **Turn-wiring note:** `ProposeProactiveTreaties` is a self-contained, directly-tested seam; its single call site inside `RunForeignPowerDiplomacy`/the round-reset clear in `EndTurn` lives in `Game.cs` (a separate lane) — see *Open issues*.
- **Benjamin Franklin's `peaceTreaty` peace-hold (`86d3c7xu7`, FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.peaceHolds`)** — the one place a power **breaks the peace on its own** is the territorial-tension **Peace→War re-declaration** in `RunForeignPowerTurn` (a power crowded by human colonies whose `StanceFromTension` flips Peace/CeaseFire→War). That flip is gated by `internal bool PeaceTreatyHolds(Player power, Player otherParty)` (`Game.cs`): when the *other party* (the human) holds Franklin, the power that would declare war first rolls a **per-turn reprieve** and, on success, lets the peace stand this turn. The probability is `internal double PeaceTreatyHoldProbability(Player power, Player otherParty)` — the **faithful FreeCol formula**: `prob = (peaceProbability/100)^n`, then `prob = p.apply(prob, turn, PEACE_TREATY)` (Franklin's +50% → ×1.5, via `PeaceTreatyModifierFactor`), clamped to [0,1]. `n = turn − peaceTurn` is the turns since the treaty took force, read from the per-pair **peace-turn stamp** (`Player.PeaceTurns`, set on a Peace/Alliance transition in `SetStance`/`StampPeaceTurn`, cleared on War, persisted at save **v53**). The base `peaceProbability` is the classic `model.option.peaceProbability` game option (a `percentageOption`, default **90** → 0.90 multiplier; `GameOptions.PeaceProbabilityMultiplier`). So the hold-probability **decays** with the age of the peace (1.0 at `n=0` thanks to the ×1.5, falling as `n` grows). The roll mirrors FreeCol's `randomInt(100) < (int)(100·prob)` and is drawn from the **power's own** RNG stream (`RandomFor(power)`) — never the human's stream 0. **Default game byte-identical (a deliberate divergence from raw FreeCol):** a non-Franklin other party (or a pair with no recorded peace) yields probability **0**, so the gate short-circuits to "no hold" **without drawing** — a non-Franklin game declares war identically and draws nothing new (the human's seeded game is byte-identical, ADR-009). Raw FreeCol applies the 0.90 base even without Franklin; we gate the whole peace-hold on the modifier's presence because the default game must replay byte-for-byte. The detailed father write-up is in [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md).
- **Trade container in-memory, no RNG:** the trade container is **not** persisted (held in memory; settling is a deterministic sequence of transfers, drawing nothing from any RNG stream, ADR-009 — see [save-load](save-load.md)). The AI *evaluation* is a pure verdict; the *turn-wiring* (`86d3c9uar`) that calls it — `RespondToTrade`/`RunForeignPowerDiplomacy`, a power suing for peace on its turn — and the *proactive proposals* (`86d3drn4f`) are deterministic on each AI's own stream (a human-bound proactive offer is queued, not applied). The Franklin peace-hold (`86d3c7xu7`) draws only the rolling power's own stream, and only when the human holds Franklin. **Save:** the only diplomacy state persisted is the per-player stance/tension maps (FP-6a) and — from the decaying peace-hold (`86d3c7xu7` follow-up) — the per-pair `peaceTurn` stamps (`Player.PeaceTurns`, save **v53**, omit-when-empty so a no-established-peace game is byte-identical). Still out: gold-for-peace clauses proposed proactively.
- **Human negotiation UI (`86d3c9xpt` / `86d3c9ubw` / `86d3e4bbv`, presentation — ADR-006)** — `game/presentation/NegotiationPanel.cs` is the human's seat at the table, built entirely over the shipped backend (no rules, no save change, no RNG of its own). Four modes, all on one `PanelContainer` (`UI/NegotiationPanel` in `main.tscn`):
  - **Answer AI offers.** On open it drains `Game.TakePendingHumanProposals()` (the queue `ProposeProactiveTreaties` fills) and shows an **Accept** / **Decline** row per offer. Accept calls `Game.SettleTrade(offer)` (the same apply path the AI-to-AI deals use — the mutual stance flips and the tension modifier applies); Decline drops the offer (nothing applied). `GameController.RefreshView` **auto-opens** the panel at the start of the human's turn when `Game.PendingHumanProposals.Count > 0` (a non-draining peek, so the check is side-effect-free) and the panel isn't already up — the same one-shot idiom as the monarch/mounds/emigration prompts.
  - **Target chooser.** The "Open a negotiation" list shows the human's **contacted** colonial rivals (`Game.ForeignNationStances(humanId)` filtered to a non-`Uncontacted` stance; the scout variant pins the list to the colony's owner). Each `Negotiate_{id}` button opens the **offer builder** against that rival (`OpenBuilder`).
  - **Offer builder + haggle (the full negotiation table, `86d3e4bbv`).** Against a chosen rival the panel keeps an ordered `_clauses` list and renders one **add-clause control per FreeCol clause kind** (FreeCol's `NegotiationDialog` offer/demand panels): **gold** give/demand with a ±100 spinner (`_goldAmount`), **goods** (the first non-empty stockpile in one of the human's colonies → the rival's first colony), **colony** give one of ours / demand one of theirs, **unit** give/demand (non-native units only), **incite** the rival against a third contacted colonial power, and **stance** from the de-escalation ladder (`OfferableStances`, one stance clause at a time — adding one replaces any prior). Each "Add" reads a `Game` oracle (`Game.Colonies`/`Game.Units`/`Game.Ruleset.GoodsTypes`/`Game.ForeignNationStances`/`Player.Gold`/`Colony.StoreOf`) for the first eligible item and appends the matching `TradeItem` — the panel **builds clause objects only**; every validity/valuation rule stays in the backend. A clause kind with nothing eligible simply renders no button. **Submit** (`Submit`) assembles a `DiplomaticTrade(human, rival, Diplomatic)` and routes it through the rival's own `Game.EvaluateTrade(rivalId, trade)`: **accepted** → `Game.SettleTrade` applies all clauses and the builder closes; **not accepted** → `Game.CounterOffer(rivalId, trade)` (the rival's prune + halve-gold + give-up loop, on the rival's own RNG stream) is asked for a trimmed deal — a returned counter is held in `_counter` and surfaced with **Accept counter** (`Game.SettleTrade(counter)`) / **Reject counter** (drop); a flat rejection (no counter) closes the builder with a notice. **Faithful-subset note (documented):** the AI answers a human offer with a *single* accept/counter/reject round (the human accepts or rejects what comes back) — the panel does not re-loop the human's own counter, so FreeCol's many-round human-side haggle is one shot here. The only RNG is inside `Game.CounterOffer` (the AI's give-up roll on its **own** stream — never the human's stream 0, ADR-009).
  - **Scout mission menu** (`OpenScoutMissions`, FreeCol `getScoutForeignColonyChoice` / `moveScoutColony`). `GameController.TryOpenScoutColonyMissions` intercepts a map click on a rival colony **when the selected unit passes `Game.CheckSpyOnColony`** (it carries `model.ability.spyOnColony` — the scout role — has moves, and is adjacent): instead of the click-to-capture assault, the panel shows **Spy** (runs `Game.SpyOnColony` and surfaces the `ColonyInteriorSnapshot` glimpse — the spy always succeeds, FreeCol-exact) and **Negotiate** (re-opens the panel pinned to the colony's owner). A non-scout unit clicking the same colony still falls through to `AttackColonyAt`. The HUD also carries a standalone **Diplomacy** button (`UI/DiplomacyButton` → `OpenNegotiationPanel`) for the general view. Signal-safe rebuilds use the `RemoveChild`+`QueueFree` idiom (a child button's handler drives the rebuild).

**Persistence:** see [save-load](save-load.md). Save **v20, additive** (the wave convention): `SavedPlayer.Stances`/`SavedPlayer.Tensions` (`Dictionary<int,…>`, omitted via `WhenWritingNull` when empty) — so a never-contacted game (all current goldens / the human-only baseline) writes **no new bytes** and is byte-identical; older saves load `Uncontacted`/`0`. `ToSavedPlayer` writes them, `BuildPlayer` restores them via `RestoredPlayer`.
