## 1. How it works (plain English)

*Audience: anyone — no jargon.*

Every European power keeps track of how it feels about every **other** European power: a **stance** (are we *uncontacted*, at *peace*, or at *war*?) and a hidden **tension** meter (a grudge level that climbs when you're wronged and slowly cools over time).

- You begin **uncontacted** with each rival — you simply haven't met. The first time one side actually *sees* the other (a unit or colony comes into view), you become **at peace** with each other. Meeting carries no grudge.
- **Attacking** a rival's unit makes you **at war** — both ways, immediately — and spikes the grudge to the top. (You don't have to be at war to attack; attacking is what *causes* the war.) **Exception — privateers:** a ship with the `piracy` ability can raid rival shipping (or be raided) **without** provoking war, flying no flag (see [combat](combat.md)).
- Each turn, tension **cools** a little on its own (the same slow fade the native nations' anger uses) — and the relationship **follows the grudge meter**: a war that has cooled enough becomes a **cease-fire** (an uneasy truce), and a cease-fire that keeps cooling drifts back to **peace**. (A flare-up the other way — peace straight to war from tension alone — needs a tension source we don't have yet, so in practice war only starts from an actual attack.)

**Important, this slice:** the relationship is tracked, evolves on its own, and the computer players now **act on War**: if you attack a rival European power (which puts you both at war), its armed units will come after your units (slice 1c-2), **march on your colonies** when there's no field unit to chase (besiege), and **capture an undefended colony** outright (1c-3f) — see [combat](combat.md). What's still missing: a computer player **declaring** war on its own (war only ever starts from *your* attack today — they don't yet pick fights or break the peace), the diplomacy actions (offer peace/alliance), and the Founding Fathers that care about diplomacy (de Witt, Franklin).

**The native nations are not part of this yet.** Your relationship with each native settlement is still its own separate "alarm" meter ([natives](natives.md)); folding the two systems together is future work.

**Worked example:**
> A rival builds a colony six tiles away. You can't see it, so you stay *uncontacted*. Your scout walks over a hill and the colony comes into view — now you're both *at peace*, tension 0. A few turns later you attack one of their wagon trains: instantly you're both *at war*, tension jumps to maximum, and from then on it ticks down a little each turn (but stays *war* until a future peace deal).

**What the player sees and does:** nothing new on screen yet — there's no diplomacy UI and the rivals remain off-screen under your fog. The state exists in the save and is queryable by the rules/tests.

### Diplomatic trade (backend)

Two European powers can strike a **treaty** — a deal where several things change hands all at once. A treaty has a **proposer** (the side offering it) and a **recipient** (the side it's offered to), and a list of **clauses**. Each clause is one thing being traded:

- **Gold** — "I'll pay you 200 gold" (the gold leaves one treasury and lands in the other).
- **Goods** — "…in exchange for 100 tobacco from your colony" (the goods move from one colony's warehouse to another's).
- **A change of relations** — "…and we agree to peace" (the two powers' stance flips to whatever the clause says — peace, a cease-fire truce, or an outright alliance). A change of relations also moves the hidden **grudge meter**: making peace or allying *cools* it, declaring war *spikes* it (so a peace deal isn't undone the very next turn, and a fresh war starts genuinely hostile).
- **A colony** — "…and you can have my colony" (a whole colony — its people, buildings and stores — changes owner, intact; nobody is plundered as it would be in a wartime capture).
- **A unit** — "…and take this colonist too" (a single unit changes allegiance and stays where it is).
- **An incitement** — "…go to war with the Spanish for me" (the power you're dealing with is set at war with a *third* power; the gold you pay for the favour is just another gold clause in the same treaty).

A treaty only does something when it is **accepted and settled**: at that moment every clause is carried out together. Until then it's just a proposal sitting in memory. A treaty with no clauses isn't a real offer, so settling an empty one does nothing.

**This is the plumbing, not the screen.** There's no negotiation table to click on yet. The computer players **weigh up** an offer with a self-contained calculator that scores a proposed treaty from a rival's point of view and answers "would they accept this?" (gold they'd gain is good, gold they'd pay is bad; making peace is attractive when you're weak, war when you're strong; a colony or unit they'd have to give up from a thin holding is a deal-breaker). And the rivals now **use** that judgement during their turn: a power that is **losing a war** with you — it is militarily weak — will **sue for peace on its own**, ending the war without you lifting a finger; a strong power presses on. (The same calculator is the seam a future negotiation screen will use to let a power answer a deal *you* offer.)

A rival that **can't accept an offer as-is doesn't just walk away — it haggles back.** It makes a **counter-offer**: it throws out the clauses it hates (the parts it would refuse at any price), and where the only sticking point is gold *it* would pay, it offers to **pay half as much** rather than abandon the whole deal — then hands the trimmed offer back. If after all that there's still nothing it would agree to (every clause was a deal-breaker, or what's left still costs it more than it's worth), it gives up and rejects. And even a salvageable haggle doesn't go on forever: each round there's a growing chance the rival simply **loses patience** and stops countering — so a back-and-forth between two computer players always ends, either in a deal both sides accept or in a collapse. (Today this haggling is exercised computer-to-computer; the human will drive it from the negotiation screen later.) What's still ahead: offering alliances/cease-fires and gold-for-peace deals proactively, and the negotiation screen itself. (Because nothing is saved here and the only dice-roll — "have I lost patience?" — is rolled on each computer player's *own* private dice, never the human's, an un-provoked game still plays exactly the same.)

> **Worked example (a counter-offer):** A rival is offered a colony it values at ~300 gold, but the offer asks it to pay 380 gold for it — a net loss as it stands, so it can't accept. Instead of rejecting, it counters: it keeps the colony clause but offers to pay only ~190 gold, which turns the deal net-positive for it, and proposes *that* trimmed version back to the other side.

> **Worked example:** You're at war with the Dutch and want it to end. You build a treaty you propose to them with two clauses: *you pay 300 gold*, and *both sides go to peace*. When it's accepted, 300 gold leaves your treasury for theirs and the war between you becomes peace — in one settlement.

## 2. Detailed rules

*Audience: designers/testers.*

- **Scope: colonial ↔ colonial only.** Stance/tension are tracked between `PlayerType.Colonial` players (the human + the foreign powers). Native players are **ignored** by every helper — they keep `NativeSettlement.Alarm`/`AlarmLevel` ([natives](natives.md)). This mirrors FreeCol's own split (Europeans flip stance instantly on an attack; natives accumulate settlement alarm and self-declare later) and avoids re-baselining the native system. Unifying them is deferred.
- **`Stance` values** (`Stance.cs`): `Uncontacted` (0, the default for any unrecorded pair) · `Peace` (1) · `War` (2) · `CeaseFire` (3) · `Alliance` (4). A faithful subset of FreeCol's `Uncontacted/Alliance/Peace/CeaseFire/War`. Values are explicit and **appended** (not in FreeCol's declaration order) so earlier saved ordinals stay stable — `Alliance` was appended as 4 (`86d3c9u3z`), leaving 0–3 untouched. `Alliance` is **only** set by a diplomacy *action* (a `StanceTradeItem`); the tension→stance machine never produces it (it stays within Uncontacted/Peace/War/CeaseFire).
- **Directional storage.** Each `Player` holds its **own** view of every other player it has met: `Stances` (other `PlayerId` → `Stance`) and `Tensions` (other `PlayerId` → 0..`MaxTension`). An absent entry reads as `Uncontacted`/`0`. (FreeCol likewise stores per-player maps.)
- **Transitions** (all deterministic, no RNG, none gate any legality):

| Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|
| First contact: a colonial player's explored fog first covers a tile holding another colonial player's on-map unit or colony (`DetectColonialContacts`, run once per `EndTurn`) | both directions `Uncontacted` → `Peace`, tension unchanged (FreeCol `CONTACT_MODIFIER` = 0). Already-met pairs (Peace/War) are skipped |
| Attack: a colonial unit attacks another colonial player's unit (`Attack`) | both directions → `War`, tension += `TensionWar` (1000, clamped to `MaxTension` 1100). Recorded **before** combat resolves, so win/loss is irrelevant. Does **not** gate the attack. **Skipped entirely when the attacker or defender has `model.ability.piracy`** (a privateer raids without war — 1c-3d-i; the stance is left as-is, not reset) |
| Each turn (`DecayColonialTension`, run in `EndTurn`) | every recorded colonial-pair tension cools by `value/100 + 4` (floored at 0) — the same formula as native alarm decay |
| Each turn after decay (`UpdateColonialStances` → `StanceFromTension`, FP-6b) | each met pair's stance re-derives from its (cooled) tension (FreeCol `Stance.getStanceFromTension`, DELTA=10 hysteresis): **War → CeaseFire** at tension ≤ 590 (`CONTENT.limit−DELTA`); **CeaseFire → Peace** at ≤ 90 (`HAPPY.limit−DELTA`); **Peace/CeaseFire → War** above 1010 (`HATEFUL.limit+DELTA`); `Uncontacted` unchanged. Symmetric, ordered by id |

- **Numeric scale.** Tension is 0..`MaxTension` (1100 = FreeCol `Tension.Level.HATEFUL.limit` + 100); `TensionWar` (1000) is the FreeCol WAR modifier; the stance thresholds use `CONTENT.limit` 600, `HAPPY.limit` 100, `DELTA` 10. These mirror the native-alarm constants but are declared in `GameSession` to avoid a dependency on `GameLogic.Natives`.
- **Peace→War from tension alone is currently unreachable in play:** the only thing that raises colonial tension is an attack, which already sets War directly (and decay only lowers tension). The `StanceFromTension` Peace→War branch is implemented faithfully for when a non-attack tension source lands (e.g. land-taking), and is covered by a direct unit test.
- **Colonial-colony assault → war (1c-3e):** assaulting a rival **colony** (`AttackColony`) now also records mutual War + a tension spike (both ways, before the colony changes hands), the same hook as a unit attack — closing the FP-6a gap. (Capturing your own colony back is impossible; the trigger only fires between distinct colonial players.)
- **Foreign-AI colony capture uses War as its gate (1c-3f):** a foreign power only seizes the human's undefended colony when it is already at `Stance.War` with the human (the same gate as the 1c-2 unit-hunt; war still starts only from the human's own attack). The AI capture reuses `AttackColony`, so its war/tension hook fires again, harmlessly (the pair is already at war). The AI does not yet *declare* war on its own — see the deviations below. Capture mechanics live in [combat](combat.md).
- **The King can impose war or peace (`86d3c9r7j`):** the home Monarch's `DECLARE_WAR`/`DECLARE_PEACE` actions now call `SetStance` to drag the human into (or out of) a war with a King-chosen European rival — a stance-change source *other* than the human's own attack. Stance-only (no tension delta), drawn on the monarch's own RNG. This is the first non-attack stance transition that actually fires in play. Rules live in [monarchy](monarchy.md).

**Deviations from FreeCol:** colonial tension **decay** is a deliberate symmetry with native alarm — FreeCol has no European tension decay; the slice scope asks for it. Decay feeds the stance machine, which can only *de-escalate* from it (war→cease-fire→peace), never escalate (decay lowers tension; escalation needs >1010, which decay can't produce) — so it cannot surprise the player. The big FreeCol pieces intentionally **not** here (the rest of FP-6b): the AI *acting* on stance (choosing to declare/wage war, native raids), and gating the human's attacks behind a war declaration (FreeCol lets the attack itself declare war — which is what we do). The diplomacy actions themselves — `Alliance` + the trade clauses that set stance/incite, and their tension modifiers (alliance −500, peace-treaty −250, cease-fire −250, resume-war +750, war-inciter +250) — are **now implemented** as the diplomatic-trade backend (`86d3c9u3z`/`86d3c9u94`); the AI can **score** an offer (`EvaluateTrade`, `86d3c9uar`), **counter-offer** when it can't accept as-is (`CounterOffer`/`NegotiateTrade`, `86d3c9uar` — prune the clauses it values negatively, halve gold it pays, give up via a seeded patience roll on its own stream), and now **acts on its turn** — a power losing a war **sues for peace** (`RunForeignPowerDiplomacy`/`RespondToTrade`, `86d3c9uar` turn-wiring); see *Diplomatic trade (backend)*. What remains out: alliance/cease-fire offers, gold-for-peace clauses proactively proposed, and the negotiation UI.

## 3. Technical design

*Audience: developers / future sessions.*

- **`Stance` enum** (`GameSession/Stance.cs`) — `Uncontacted=0/Peace=1/War=2/CeaseFire=3/Alliance=4`; zero-value default means an absent map entry / pre-FP-6a save reads as `Uncontacted` for free. Values are **appended** (`CeaseFire` then `Alliance`) so every earlier ordinal is byte-stable — adding `Alliance` needs **no save-version bump**. Serialized as its integer ordinal (no `JsonStringEnumConverter` configured), matching how `PlayerType` is stored.
- **`Player`** holds `_stance`/`_tension` (`Dictionary<int,…>`) with public read views `Stances`/`Tensions` and `internal` mutable views `StanceMap`/`TensionMap` (the established field + read-view + internal-mutable-view pattern).
- **`Game`** API: `StanceBetween(a,b)`/`TensionBetween(a,b)` (public reads; default Uncontacted/0); `SetStance(a,b,stance,symmetric=true)`/`ChangeTension(a,b,delta,symmetric=true)` (`internal`; both clamp/guard so they are a **no-op unless `a` and `b` are distinct colonial players** — the single place the colonial-only rule is enforced, so every call site is naturally native-safe). Constants `MaxTension`/`TensionWar`.
- **Transition sites:** `DetectColonialContacts` → `DecayColonialTension` → `UpdateColonialStances` run in `EndTurn`'s world-step block (beside `DecayNativeAlarm`); the attack→war record sits in `Attack` right after the defender is resolved, guarded by `defenderNation is null` (a colonial defender). All are append-only side-effects — they never read or modify `AreEnemies`/`CheckMove`/`CheckAttack`/fog, so legality is unchanged and the human's RNG stream 0 is untouched (no draws).
- **`StanceFromTension(current, tension)`** (`internal static`) is the pure FreeCol `Stance.getStanceFromTension` port (a `switch` expression; directly unit-tested). `UpdateColonialStances` applies it after decay, over met pairs (those with a stance entry; `Uncontacted` skipped) in id order, setting the new stance symmetrically.
- **Offensive targeting (FP-6a, FreeCol `UnitSeekAndDestroyMission`):** when a power is at `War`, `RunForeignPowerTurn` picks each armed unit's target via `PickAttackTarget` — a scored seek-and-destroy over human unit-tiles (same naval/land domain) **and**, for a land unit, human colonies, within an **escalating Chebyshev range** (`SeekRangeLadder` 8/12/16; the first gate yielding any target wins). The score is `1020 − 100·d` plus a combat term: a unit-tile adds the **truncated** `(int)(100·(off − def))` (vs the tile's strongest `DefenderAt`, `off = OffenceBase`, `def = DefencePowerOf`), `+1000` per treasure train on the tile, `+500` for a naval defender caught on land; a colony adds the **rounded** `round(50·off) + Population − 200·stockadeLevel` (loot drawn, fortifications avoided; stockade 1 / fort 2 / fortress 3 → −200/−400/−600, matching FreeCol `calculateSettlementValue` — note this is the settlement-*value* penalty, **distinct** from the combat defence-bonus %). The round-vs-truncate asymmetry is FreeCol's. A **garrisoned** colony is excluded from the capture candidates — its garrison is scored as a unit-tile and fought first (else the colony would outscore its own garrison, the capture be refused, and the unit wander off). Pure (no RNG): ties resolve by a stable candidate order (units by id, then colonies by position) with strict-max, so the human's stream 0 is untouched; combat/movement still draw `RandomFor(power)`. The preserved adjacent-undefended-colony capture, an **out-of-range pursuit** (close on the nearest human unit at any distance when nothing scores within 16, so a warship / non-founder land unit never idles), and the besiege fallback wrap it. **Approximations vs FreeCol** (documented): Chebyshev tiles stand in for FreeCol's turn-cost path length (no AI pathfinder); `OffenceBase` is **pre-situational** so it omits FreeCol's +50% global `attackBonus` (and other situational offence) that `getOffencePower` adds — the unit term `100·(off−def)` and colony term `round(50·off)` are understated by ~`100·0.5·base` / ~`round(50·0.5·base)`, while `def` *is* fully situational (`DefencePowerOf`); the `+100` unarmed-expert-soldier bonus is omitted (no unit-type ability bag yet); and the `8/12/16` ladder is a **unified** range gate where FreeCol splits it per role (naval 8, ordinary land 8→16, REF-only 12). We deliberately **return** the unit-tile score that vanilla FreeCol's `scorePath` discards (a known upstream bug) so units remain targetable. Still **human-target only** — making `AreEnemies` stance-aware (attacking any at-war power, not just the human) is the open FP-6 gap (b).
- **Determinism:** contact iterates colonial players in `PlayerId` order over the already-deterministic `Explored` fog; decay + stance-update iterate keys/pairs in id order; the offensive scorer iterates candidates in a stable id/position order with no RNG. No RNG anywhere in the stance/targeting logic.

### Diplomatic trade (backend) — `86d3c9u2f`+

- **Model** (`GameSession/Diplomacy/`, pure value objects in the `…GameSession.Diplomacy` namespace):
  - **`DiplomaticTrade`** — a treaty with `ProposerId`/`RecipientId` (the two colonial parties, by `PlayerId`) and an ordered `Items` list (`IReadOnlyList<TradeItem>`); `Add(item)` appends (chainable), `IsValid(game)` is "≥1 clause and every clause valid", `Apply(game)` settles each still-valid clause in order (an item that became invalid since the offer is **skipped, not thrown** — the rest still settle). FreeCol `common/model/DiplomaticTrade`.
  - **`TradeItem`** — abstract clause base; each carries `Source`/`Destination` (`PlayerId`s, so a single treaty can move value both ways — FreeCol `TradeItem.getSource`/`getDestination`), with `IsValid(game)`/`Apply(game)`. FreeCol `common/model/TradeItem`.
- **Concrete clauses** (`86d3c9u94`):
  - **`GoldTradeItem(source, destination, amount)`** — pays `amount` gold from `source`'s treasury to `destination`'s. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferGold` (distinct colonial powers, `amount > 0`, payer can afford it — FreeCol `GoldTradeItem.isValid`: `0 ≤ gold ≤ source.getGold()`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferGold` (debit source, credit destination).
  - **`GoodsTradeItem(source, destination, sourceColonyId, destinationColonyId, goodsId, amount)`** — moves `amount` of `goodsId` from one colony's warehouse to another's. **Faithful-subset shape:** FreeCol's `Goods` is bound to a *source settlement*; colonial players here hold goods only in colonies (no player-level pool), so the clause names the specific **source and destination colonies**, with `Source`/`Destination` the colonies' owning players. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferColonyGoods` (positive amount; real ruleset goods id; distinct colonial powers each still owning their named colony; source holds ≥ `amount`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferColonyGoods` (debit/credit via `Colony.AddGoods`). A clause whose colony was lost or drained since the offer becomes invalid and is **skipped** by `DiplomaticTrade.Apply`.
  - **`StanceTradeItem(source, destination, stance)`** (`86d3c9u3z`) — sets the mutual stance between the two parties (in practice `Peace`/`CeaseFire`/`Alliance` — end a war, agree a truce, or ally). `IsValid` = `Game.CanSetStance` (distinct colonial powers — the only pairs whose stance is tracked, and exactly the pairs for which the stance change is not a no-op); `Apply` = `Game.ApplyStanceWithTension(source, destination, stance)` (symmetric), which sets the stance **and** applies the stance-change tension modifier — see *Tension on a stance change* below.
  - **`ColonyTradeItem(source, destination, colonyId)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — hands a whole colony from `source` to `destination`, **intact**. `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferColony` (distinct colonial powers; the colony still exists and is owned by `source` — FreeCol `ColonyTradeItem.isValid`); `Apply` = `Game.TransferColony`, which **reuses the wartime `CaptureColony` path** (so the former owner's ships caught in the colony's port are damaged/sunk exactly as in a seizure) but **rolls no plunder** — a negotiated handover transfers the colony whole, so the apply path stays RNG-free. A colony lost since the offer is **skipped**.
  - **`UnitTradeItem(source, destination, unitId)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — hands one unit from `source` to `destination`. **Faithful-subset shape:** FreeCol's `isValid` is "the unit is the source's *and* its type is available to the destination"; colonial players share the one classic ruleset, so we model the ownership half — `IsValid` = `Game.CanTransferUnit` (distinct colonial powers; the unit still exists and is a **non-native** unit owned by `source`). `Apply` = `Game.TransferUnit`, a pure reparent of `Unit.OwnerId` (FreeCol `unit.changeOwner`); the unit's position/location are untouched. A unit lost or reassigned since the offer is **skipped**.
  - **`InciteTradeItem(source, destination, victim)`** (`86d3c9u94`) — incites the **`source`** power (the clause's source — the party that *agrees to fight*; in a normal offer this is the recipient who accepts the deal) to war against a *third* power, `victim`, for the `destination`'s benefit. `IsValid` = `Game.CanIncite` (victim ≠ `source` ≠ `destination`, all three distinct colonial powers — FreeCol `InciteTradeItem.isValid`); `Apply` = `Game.Incite`, faithful to FreeCol `ServerGame.csAcceptTrade` (`source.csChangeStance(WAR, victim)`): it sets `source`↔`victim` to `War` **with the war stance-change tension modifier** (via `ApplyStanceWithTension`), then adds the **war-inciter spike** (`TensionWarInciter` = 250, FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_WAR_INCITER`) to the victim's tension toward the `destination` (the instigator). The gold the destination pays travels as a separate `GoldTradeItem` clause in the same treaty.
- **`Game` seam** — `Game.Diplomacy.cs` (a thin partial; `Game.cs` untouched) exposes `public void SettleTrade(DiplomaticTrade trade) => trade.Apply(this)`, plus the `internal` mutators the clauses call: `CanTransferGold`/`TransferGold`, `CanTransferColonyGoods`/`TransferColonyGoods` (colony lookup reuses the existing `ColonyById`), `CanSetStance`, `ApplyStanceWithTension` (the stance clause + incite route through this), `CanTransferColony`/`TransferColony` (reuses the private `CaptureColony`), `CanTransferUnit`/`TransferUnit` (reuses the private `UnitById`), and `CanIncite`/`Incite`. Each `Transfer*`/`Incite` re-checks its `Can*` guard, so an invalid call is a no-op (never drives a treasury negative / never throws on a stale colony or unit).
- **Tension on a stance change** (`86d3c9u3z`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csChangeStance` + `Stance.getTensionModifier`): a treaty's stance change carries the FreeCol tension consequence. `StanceTensionModifier(oldStance, newStance)` (`internal static`, pure) ports the realistic transitions of FreeCol's table — **calming:** peace→alliance −500, cease-fire→alliance −750, war→alliance −1000, cease-fire→peace −250, war→peace −500, war→cease-fire −250; **escalating:** peace/alliance→war +1000 (= `TensionWar`), cease-fire→war +750 (`RESUME_WAR`); a no-change transition (same stance, incl. war→war) is 0. `ApplyStanceWithTension(a, b, newStance)` reads the current stance, calls `SetStance` then `ChangeTension` by the modifier (symmetric, mirroring `csChangeStance`'s per-direction modifier — in our always-symmetric model both directions share the same `old`, so they apply the same delta). **The diplomacy apply path is the only caller** — the generic `SetStance` used by first contact, the tension→stance machine, and monarch-imposed war/peace stays deliberately tension-free, so those paths are unchanged. Incite additionally adds the war-inciter +250 (`TensionWarInciter`, FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_WAR_INCITER`) to the victim's view of the destination (directional). All deterministic — no RNG.
- **AI evaluation (`86d3c9uar`)** — `public TradeEvaluation EvaluateTrade(powerId, trade)` (+ `bool ShouldAcceptTrade` convenience) scores a proposed treaty from a colonial power's point of view, a faithful subset of FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.acceptDiplomaticTrade`: each clause is valued by `internal int EvaluateTradeItem(powerId, item)` and the power **accepts** exactly when *no* clause is unacceptable (returns the `InvalidTradeItem` = `int.MinValue` sentinel) **and** the summed value is ≥ 0. Per-clause values (FreeCol the matching `TradeItem.evaluateFor`): **gold** ±amount (received +, paid −); **goods** at the power's market — giver −`AskPrice·amount`, receiver +`round(BidPrice·amount·(1−tax/100))`; **stance/incite** by the FreeCol military-strength ratio `StrengthRatio = ours/(ours+theirs)` over `LandPowerOf` (war wanted when strong / refused when ratio < 0.33; peace wanted when weak, +1000 at ratio < 0.33 / refused at ratio > 0.66; incite −`round(50/ratio)`, 0 if already at war, invalid if allied with the victim); **colony**/**unit** by a documented proxy (population × 300 / flat 200) with FreeCol's AI "don't trade away below `TRADE_MARGIN` 5 colonies / 10 units" guards. **`TradeEvaluation`** (`Diplomacy/`) is the `(Accept, Value, UnacceptableItems)` result. **Pure** — reads the game, changes nothing, draws no RNG (ADR-009). **Approximations vs FreeCol** (documented): the colony/unit values are flat proxies for FreeCol's full `Colony.evaluateFor`/`Unit.evaluateFor`; the multi-round haggling, Franklin "always offered peace", and patience cut-off of the full method are out of scope (single-shot accept/reject core).
- **AI turn-wiring (`86d3c9uar`)** — `RunForeignPowerTurn` now *uses* the evaluation. `internal bool RespondToTrade(power, trade)` scores an offered treaty via `EvaluateTrade` and `SettleTrade`s it iff the power accepts (the AI-consumption seam; both the self-proposed peace and a future negotiation UI route through it). `RunForeignPowerDiplomacy(power)` — called only when the power was **already at war** with the human (captured as `alreadyAtWar` *before* the territorial-tension accrual, so a war just declared this turn is pressed, not undone) — builds a single-clause peace `DiplomaticTrade` (`StanceTradeItem` to `Peace`) and `RespondToTrade`s it, so a militarily weak power (low `StrengthRatio` → peace scores positive) sues for peace and the war ends; a strong power's evaluation rejects it and it fights on. Bounded: one peace offer per turn, no haggling, no clause but the stance change. **Deterministic** (`EvaluateTrade` pure + `SettleTrade` a deterministic transfer) — no RNG draw, human stream 0 byte-identical (ADR-009); no save change.
- **AI counter-offers + give-up loop (`86d3c9uar`)** — `public DiplomaticTrade? CounterOffer(int powerId, DiplomaticTrade received, int round = 0)` (`Game.Diplomacy.cs`) is the faithful subset of FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.acceptDiplomaticTrade`'s **prune + counter + give-up** branch, for a power that cannot accept an offer as-is. Steps, in FreeCol order:
  1. **Score** every clause via the pure `EvaluateTradeItem`, counting the `InvalidTradeItem` (= `int.MinValue`) sentinels.
  2. **Reject** (return `null`) when too large a fraction is unacceptable: `unacceptable / (unacceptable + items.Count) > 0.5 − 0.5·round` (FreeCol's `version` is our `round`; the tolerance shrinks as the haggle drags on).
  3. **Prune** the invalid clauses; if nothing remains → `null`.
  4. **Give-up roll** (the *only* RNG here): `RandomFor(power).Next(1 + round) > CounterPatienceThreshold` (= 5), FreeCol's `randomInt("Enough diplomacy?", 1 + version) > 5`. At `round = 0` the bound is 1 (draw always 0 → never gives up); the bound grows each round so a long haggle eventually exceeds 5 and the power walks away. Drawn from the **power's own stream** (`RandomFor`) — never the human's stream 0.
  5. **Prune negatives** ascending by score: for each negative clause, if removing it leaves the running sum `≥ GoldCounterFloor` (= 50) **and** it is a `GoldTradeItem` the power *pays* (`Source == powerId`), **halve** it (`reduced = amount − value/2`, `value /= 2`, FreeCol's exact magic-number); otherwise **drop** it. Stop once the sum is non-negative (all remaining, non-worse clauses are kept). Positives are always kept.
  6. **Propose or reject**: a counter is returned only when the final value `≥ 0` and at least one clause remains; else `null`. The counter is a fresh `DiplomaticTrade(received.ProposerId, received.RecipientId)` carrying the surviving (and reduced-gold) clauses.
  The **human never auto-counters** (`powerId == _human.PlayerId` → `null`): the human negotiates at the (future) table, so `CounterOffer` is AI-only and stream 0 stays untouched. `internal bool NegotiateTrade(Player proposer, Player recipient, DiplomaticTrade trade)` (`Game.cs`) drives a **bounded AI-to-AI** back-and-forth: the answerer either accepts (`RespondToTrade` settles) or `CounterOffer`s a trimmed deal that the other side then weighs, swapping sides each round, until one accepts, a side gives up (`CounterOffer` → `null`), or the `MaxNegotiationRounds` (= 8) cap is hit. Both parties must be non-human (a human negotiation is the UI's job) → it never draws stream 0. **Determinism:** the give-up roll is each power's own stream; valuation and trade-building are pure; the human's seeded game is byte-identical (ADR-009). **Not yet wired into `RunForeignPowerTurn`** — the sue-for-peace path stays a single-shot `RespondToTrade`; `NegotiateTrade`/`CounterOffer` are exercised AI-to-AI and via the response oracle until the negotiation UI / proactive proposals land. **Approximations vs FreeCol:** the Franklin "always offered peace" special-case and the CONTACT-context peace exemption are omitted (no founding-father diplomacy / contact negotiation yet); the per-round `round`/`version` is a caller argument rather than carried on the (un-persisted) trade.
- **In-memory only, no save change, no RNG:** the trade container is **not** persisted (held in memory; no save-version bump — see [save-load](save-load.md)); settling is a deterministic sequence of transfers, drawing nothing from any RNG stream (ADR-009). The AI *evaluation* is a pure verdict, and the *turn-wiring* (`86d3c9uar`) that calls it — `RespondToTrade`/`RunForeignPowerDiplomacy`, a power suing for peace on its turn — is likewise deterministic and RNG-free. Still out: alliance/cease-fire offers, gold-for-peace clauses, and the negotiation UI.

**Persistence:** see [save-load](save-load.md). Save **v20, additive** (the wave convention): `SavedPlayer.Stances`/`SavedPlayer.Tensions` (`Dictionary<int,…>`, omitted via `WhenWritingNull` when empty) — so a never-contacted game (all current goldens / the human-only baseline) writes **no new bytes** and is byte-identical; older saves load `Uncontacted`/`0`. `ToSavedPlayer` writes them, `BuildPlayer` restores them via `RestoredPlayer`.
