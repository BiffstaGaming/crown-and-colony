## 1. How it works (plain English)

Your colonies' town halls ring **liberty bells**; every bell becomes a **liberty point** for your nation. Spend enough liberty and a **Founding Father** joins your Continental Congress — a famous figure who (eventually) grants a lasting bonus. Each round you're **offered one candidate per category** (trade, exploration, military, political, religious); you pick which to recruit, and they're elected the turn your banked liberty reaches their cost. The more fathers you already have, the more liberty the next one costs.

**Worked example:** a new colony's town hall makes 1 bell a turn, so you bank 1 liberty a turn. The first father costs 40 — so after 40 turns, the candidate you chose joins Congress, your liberty resets, and a fresh slate of candidates is offered. (Staff the town hall with statesmen for several bells a turn and elect fathers far quicker.)

**Once you declare independence, the Continental Congress closes its doors:** you elect **no new founding fathers** after the Declaration (the classic rule). The fathers you already have **keep helping you** through the War of Independence — Washington still promotes your winning soldiers, Jefferson still boosts your bells, and so on — but no fresh candidate is ever recruited. Your colonies' bells still ring (they keep your colonies' Sons of Liberty up, which matters for the war), they just no longer add up toward a new father. (A variant can switch this off via a rule option, letting recruitment continue after independence; the classic game leaves it closed.) See [independence](independence.md).

**Portraits:** each candidate is shown with a **portrait** of the historical figure beside their name in the Continental Congress dialog, and the same portrait appears beside every father in the in-game reference (Colopedia → Founding Fathers tab), so you recognise the faces rather than reading only names. The portraits are FreeCol's painted founding-father art.

## 2. Detailed rules

- **Liberty** = total bells produced (bells are consumed into liberty each turn, not stockpiled as goods).
- **Cost of the next father** (FreeCol `getTotalFoundingFatherCost`, verified vs source): with `factor` = **40** (classic **medium**, the value the base game is balanced around),
  - first father (none elected yet): `factor` → **40**
  - thereafter: `2 × (elected + 1) × factor + 1` → **161, 241, 321, 401, …**
- **Offers:** one father per category that still has an un-elected candidate with a non-zero weight for the current age; the candidate is drawn by **seeded weight** (each father has age-1/2/3 weights). Already-elected fathers are never re-offered.
- **Election:** when your chosen father's cost is met, they join Congress, that cost is subtracted from liberty, and a new offer set is generated.
- **No new recruitment after the Declaration of Independence (`continueFoundingFatherRecruitment`, classic default off):** a player who has declared independence (a **Rebel**/**Independent** player type) elects **no new fathers and is offered none** — the Continental Congress closes at the Declaration (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.canRecruitFoundingFather`: a `COLONIAL` player recruits freely, a `REBEL`/`INDEPENDENT` only under `model.option.continueFoundingFatherRecruitment`, classic `false`). The **bells → liberty bake still runs** for a rebel (its colonies' Sons of Liberty keep updating), and **already-elected fathers keep their effects** (their bonuses derive from the player's Congress, with no player-type gate); only **new** recruitment stops. With the option on, post-declaration recruitment continues exactly as for a colonial. See [independence](independence.md).
- **Who the AI banks toward:** a rival European power doesn't choose at random — it picks the offered father most worth having for the current age (the highest age weight), with one override: if a custom house (Peter Stuyvesant) is on offer it grabs that first, because an early custom house makes its colonies much easier to run. Equal-weight ties (or two custom-house offers) are settled by a fixed alphabetical order, so the same situation always yields the same pick. *(You, the human, are never auto-picked for — you always choose your own father.)*

**Pocahontas (native peacemaker)** — the political father who calms the natives. Two effects, both faithful to FreeCol on our per-settlement alarm model:
- **On election:** every native settlement's alarm toward you drops to **0 (Happy)** — the `model.event.resetNativeAlarm` event (`ServerPlayer.java`). All grievances forgotten; braves fall below the raid threshold and stop attacking. *(Deviations: FreeCol also sets a native-nation `Stance.PEACE` object and only resets settlements you've **contacted** — we have neither a native-nation stance type nor fine contact-tracking, so we reset **all** settlements; the alarm-to-0 yields the same observable "no raids".)*
- **Permanently:** the per-turn **ambient** native-alarm **net** is **halved** — the `model.modifier.nativeAlarmModifier` (−50%, read from the spec), folded by `ScaleNativeAlarmGain` into `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` (the proximity-pressure pass). FreeCol accumulates the proximity pressure **and** any resident-mission relief into the same per-settlement total, then scales that **net** once (`ServerPlayer.java:1895-1914`) — so the −50% damps **both** the anger from your footprint **and** a mission's calming inside it (an ordinary mission's relief becomes −5, an expert's −10). The standalone whole-tribe **decay** and **combat tension** are separate paths and stay unscaled (combat tension is applied raw). *(Corrected `86d3h9nha`: we previously scaled only the pressure and applied the mission relief raw as a second call, so a Pocahontas mission calmed a tribe 2× too fast — see [natives](natives.md).)* This is the modifier's **faithful home**: FreeCol damps only the ambient alarm, not combat — so electing Pocahontas roughly halves how fast tribes anger at your expanding colonies. Both effects are human-gated (alarm is toward the human; a foreign power electing Pocahontas is a native-alarm no-op) and ride on the persisted Congress (no save change, no RNG). Identified by father id rather than parsing `<event>` (single-event minimalism).

**Francis Drake (privateer captain)** — the military father who makes privateers fearsome: **+50% offence and +50% defence**, scoped to `model.unit.privateer` (read from the spec). It's the **first scoped father combat modifier**: `Game.OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase` multiply by `FatherCombatFactor`, which folds the unit *owner's* elected fathers' `model.modifier.offence`/`defence` modifiers whose `<scope>` matches the unit's type. Per-owner (a foreign power's Drake boosts *its* privateers, not yours), dormant unless elected, riding the persisted Congress (no save change, no RNG). Drake is the only father with these modifiers, so only privateers are ever affected. *(Implementation note: because every `CombatModel` situational factor is multiplicative, folding Drake's ×1.5 into the base power is mathematically identical to applying it at FreeCol's index 50 — multiplication commutes — so the fold lives in `OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase`.)*

**La Salle (the free-stockade explorer)** — the exploration father who fortifies your colonies: every colony that reaches **population 3** is given a **free stockade** (FreeCol `model.event.freeBuilding` = `model.building.stockade`), and that stockade carries the colony fortification defence bonus (+100% — see [combat](combat.md)), so a La Salle empire defends its settlements without spending hammers on walls. `Game.ApplyFreeBuildings(player)` runs each turn after election and colony growth: for every free building any elected father grants, it adds it to each owned colony that meets the building's required population and lacks it (idempotent, RNG-free, stable colony-id order). It fires both the turn La Salle is elected (existing pop-3 colonies) and later as colonies grow to 3. Per-owner (a foreign power's La Salle fortifies *its* colonies). The granted stockade persists in the colony's building list — no save-format change. *(Deferred: La Salle's `buildingPriceBonus −100%` for a stockade — a free **manual** rebuild, redundant once the event grants one outright — and the stockade's `minimumColonySize` modifier.)*

**John Paul Jones (the free frigate)** — the military father who hands you a warship outright: the moment he joins the Congress, a brand-new **frigate** appears on your **Europe dock**, ready to sail to the New World (FreeCol models this as a founding-father `<unit id="model.unit.frigate"/>` child — a one-time grant on election, not a standing modifier). `Game.AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` spawns each of an elected father's `FreeUnits` via `SpawnInEurope` (the same Europe-dock spawn the monarch's mercenaries/support use), owned by the electing player — so a foreign power's John Paul Jones arms *its* Europe. The grant fires exactly once (it rides the election event, never re-run on load), draws no RNG (so a human election stays byte-stable on stream 0), and the frigate persists as an ordinary Europe unit — no save-format change. The mechanism is generic: any father (or mod) with a `<unit>` child grants it the same way.

**Jacob Fugger (the boycott-breaker)** — the trade father who wipes the slate clean: the turn he joins the Congress, **every boycott on your market ends at once** (FreeCol `model.event.boycottsLifted`). Boycotts come from a Boston Tea Party — dumping a taxed good overboard bans its sale until you pay the back-tax arrears (see [monarchy](monarchy.md)); Fugger clears all of those arrears in one stroke, for free, so the goods trade freely again. `FoundingFather.LiftsBoycotts` parses the event, and on election `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` calls `player.Market.LiftAllBoycotts()` (which just empties the arrears — unlike a new nation's `Reinitialise`, prices and inventory are **not** reset). One-time, per-owner (a foreign power's Fugger clears *its* boycotts), RNG-free, and no save change (arrears already persist). For most games this is a no-op (you've held no tea party), so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Francisco de Coronado (the cartographer)** — the exploration father who shows you the whole map's settlements: the turn he joins the Congress, **every colony on the map is revealed** to you — your own and every rival's — so you instantly know where everyone has settled (FreeCol `model.event.seeAllColonies`). `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` reveals each colony's tile and its ring (`RevealAround(player, colony.Position, 1)`) into the electing player's fog. One-time on election (rides the event), RNG-free (no stream-0 perturbation), per-owner (a foreign power's Coronado reveals colonies to *its* fog), and it rides the existing explored-set save (no format change). *(Deferred: Coronado's continuous `exposedTilesRadius +3` — a permanent wider sight ring around your own colonies — which needs a standing per-colony fog modifier rather than a one-time reveal.)*

**Bartolomé de las Casas (the convert-emancipator)** — the religious father who turns the converted: the moment he joins the Congress, **every native convert you hold becomes a full free colonist** (FreeCol `model.ability.upgradeConvert`). Native converts (`model.unit.indianConvert`, harvested by missions — see [natives](natives.md)) are weaker workers; de las Casas promotes them all in one stroke. `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` checks the elected father's parsed `model.ability.upgradeConvert` ability and, when present, `UpgradeUnitType`s each of the electing player's converts to `model.unit.freeColonist`. One-time on election, RNG-free (no stream-0 perturbation), owner-generic (a foreign power emancipates *its* converts), and no save change (the upgraded units are ordinary colonists). For most games a no-op (no converts yet), so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Father Jean de Brébeuf (the master missionary)** — normally only a **jesuit missionary** converts natives at the expert rate; an ordinary free colonist sent to preach works far more slowly. While Brébeuf sits in your Congress, **every one of your missions converts as if staffed by a jesuit**, even the ones you established with an ordinary colonist (FreeCol `model.ability.expertMissionary`). In our convert maths (see [natives](natives.md)) that means each of your missions gains the jesuit skill term (an extra **+3** convert-progress per turn) regardless of who actually staffs it. This is a *continuous* ability (it applies every turn while he's in the Congress, not a one-time election event), it's owner-scoped (a foreign power with Brébeuf boosts *its* missions, not yours), and it's RNG-free — a player with no missions sees no effect, so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Juan de Sepúlveda (the converting conquistador)** — when you **win a battle at a native settlement where you keep a mission**, there's a chance one of its braves defects to you as a convert. De Sepúlveda makes that **20% more likely** (FreeCol `model.modifier.nativeConvertBonus`). Note this is a *combat* bonus, not a peaceful-mission one: in the original game `nativeConvertBonus` only ever affects the capture-a-convert roll when you storm a settlement (it does **not** speed up ordinary missionary conversions — that's Brébeuf's job above). The Spanish **conquest** national character carries a much larger **+200%** of the same modifier, so a Spanish conquistador who has also elected de Sepúlveda is almost certain to harvest a convert from every mission settlement they raze. Owner-scoped (each player folds its own Congress + nation type); see [combat](combat.md) for the full capture-convert rule and the worked probability.

**Adam Smith (the industrialist) & Peter Stuyvesant (the merchant)** — the two trade fathers that **unlock buildings** rather than tweaking numbers. Each grants an **ability** (Smith → `model.ability.buildFactory`, Stuyvesant → `model.ability.buildCustomHouse`) that satisfies a building's `required-ability` gate, so the colony build menu offers the gated buildings only once that father sits in the Congress: Smith opens the whole **factory tier** (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, rum factory, fur factory) and the **arsenal**; Stuyvesant opens the **custom house**. The wiring is generic and lives on the colony side — `Game.HasAbilityFor(player, …)` reads the elected father's parsed abilities, `ColonyHasAbility`/`RequiredAbilityRefusal` resolve the build gate against them ([colonies](colonies.md) — build-ability gate) — so no per-father code is needed here; electing the father simply makes the gate pass. The factory tier's extra +50% manufacturing is **not** a Smith modifier: it's each factory building's own `rebel-factor 1.5`, which scales the per-worker Sons-of-Liberty production bonus (see [colonies](colonies.md) — building production); a high-liberty colony with a factory therefore out-produces the same workers in the plain shop. Per-owner (a foreign power's Smith opens *its* factories), RNG-free, riding the persisted Congress (no save change).

**Benjamin Franklin (the diplomat) — all three effects now applied (`ignoreEuropeanWars` + `alwaysOfferedPeace` + `peaceTreaty`).** Despite the original task's "natives offer peace" framing, FreeCol's Benjamin Franklin is purely European, with three effects, all now **wired**:
- **`ignoreEuropeanWars`:** while Franklin sits in your Congress, `Game.MonarchPotentialEnemies()` is empty (it reads `HasAbilityFor(_human, model.ability.ignoreEuropeanWars)`), so the King **cannot declare war on your behalf** — but he can still make peace (Franklin stops wars, not peace, faithful to FreeCol's `collectPotentialEnemies` vs `collectPotentialFriends`). Human-gated, rides the persisted Congress.
- **`alwaysOfferedPeace`:** a peace offer made by a Franklin holder is **always taken** by the AI it is offered to. In `Game.EvaluateStance`, when the *other* party to the treaty holds `alwaysOfferedPeace` (`OtherPartyAlwaysOffersPeace`), a peace/cease-fire/alliance stance clause is forced **neutral (scored 0)** — never a cost and never a refusal — exactly FreeCol's `franklin` branch in `acceptDiplomaticTrade`. So even a power that would normally spurn peace (because it dominates you militarily) accepts a Franklin player's peace. Owner-scoped (it's *their* Franklin that disarms *your* refusal), and war clauses are untouched (Franklin makes peace, not war).
- **`peaceTreaty +50%` (the peace-hold, `86d3c7xu7`):** Franklin doesn't only make the *enemy* accept peace — he makes a power that is *already at peace* with you **less willing to break it**, and (now with the full FreeCol formula) **most protective of a fresh treaty, gradually less so as the years pass**. In FreeCol, when an AI's grievance machine (`determineStances`) would flip a recent peace back to war, it first calls `peaceHolds(p)`: it rolls against a **decaying** probability `prob = (peaceProbability/100)^n`, where `n` is the number of turns since the peace took force (so the longer the peace has held, the more likely it eventually breaks — the classic base is **0.90**, i.e. ~90% per turn early on). Benjamin Franklin's `peaceTreaty +50%` modifier on `p` (you, the other party) then **scales that hold-probability up** (`prob = p.apply(prob, turn, Modifier.PEACE_TREATY)` → ×1.5) so the treaty is more likely to stand. We model that at its faithful home — the one place a power re-declares war on its own: the **territorial-tension Peace→War flip** in `RunForeignPowerTurn` (a power you crowd with colonies eventually breaks the peace). That flip is gated by `PeaceTreatyHolds(power, human)`: when you hold Franklin, the power that *would* declare war instead rolls a **per-turn reprieve** at probability `0.90^n × 1.5` (clamped to ≤1), and on success lets the peace stand this turn. We track `n` with a small per-pair **peace-turn stamp** (`Player.PeaceTurns`) set the turn a Peace/Alliance takes force and cleared the turn war is declared — persisted at **save v53** (omitted when empty, so a default game is byte-identical). The roll is drawn from the **power's own** RNG stream (never your stream 0), so a Franklin human's seeded game stays byte-identical (ADR-009). *(One deliberate divergence from raw FreeCol, to keep the default byte-identical: a **non-Franklin** human yields probability **0**, so the gate **short-circuits with no roll at all** — the default game declares war exactly as before. Raw FreeCol applies the 0.90 base even without Franklin; we gate the whole peace-hold on Franklin's modifier being present, since the default game must replay byte-for-byte.)*

All three effects ride the persisted Congress. The first two draw **no RNG** (`MonarchPotentialEnemies` and `EvaluateStance` are pure) and add **no save state**. The third draws only the *rolling power's* own stream — and only when you actually hold Franklin (else no draw) — and persists only the peace-turn stamps (omit-when-empty), so the default game is byte-identical. A "natives never raid" Franklin rule would be a fabrication that duplicates Pocahontas.

**Jan de Witt (the foreign-trade diplomat)** — the trade father who opens dealings with rival nations. FreeCol's de Witt carries three abilities, all now exposed as GameLogic oracles (dormant until he sits in your Congress, so the default game is unchanged):
- **`tradeWithForeignColonies`** (`CanTradeWithForeignColonies`): you may **trade with rival nations' colonies** — the gate a future "sell to a foreign colony" trade UI checks.
- **`customHouseTradesWithForeignCountries`** (`CustomHouseTradesWithForeignCountries`): your **custom houses may sell to foreign markets** even when a good is boycotted at home — but only while you're at **peace (or allied)** with at least one other European power (faithful to FreeCol `Player.canTrade(CUSTOM_HOUSE)`). *(Note: our custom-house auto-export does not yet have a boycott gate to relax — see [colonies](colonies.md) — so this oracle is wired and ready for when that gate lands; today it changes nothing on its own.)*
- **`betterForeignAffairsReport`** (`HasBetterForeignAffairsReport` + `ForeignNationStances` + `ForeignReportRows`): your foreign-affairs report **reveals more about every rival nation** — its diplomatic stance (`ForeignNationStances`) **and** (`86d3ha4cv`) its **SoL% / elected-father count / tax%** (`ForeignReportRows` in `Game.Reports.cs` — each live rival colonial power's `NationalSonsOfLiberty` / `Player.Congress.Count` / `Player.TaxRate`, in id order). The flag unlocks the report *presentation*; the data is always computed, so the report screen (`ColonyReportPanel.BuildForeign`) just reads it and appends the columns when you hold de Witt (FreeCol's `NationSummary` sets those figures to −1, hidden, without the ability). **Now shipped** — the Foreign report tab shows the extra columns.

Each is owner-scoped (a foreign power's de Witt opens *its* dealings, not yours), RNG-free, and rides the persisted Congress (no save change).

**William Brewster (the recruiter)** — fully wired. Three pieces, all faithful to FreeCol: (1) the **recruit-pool ban** — once Brewster is in Congress, indentured servants and petty criminals never appear on the Europe dock (`canRecruitUnit=false`, scoped to those two types; `IsRecruitBlocked` / `IsRecruitable`); (2) the **`newRecruits` event** — on election, `RefreshDockForRecruitability` re-rolls any dock slot now holding a banned type (FreeCol `ServerEurope.replaceRecruits`), so the scum already waiting is swept out; (3) **`selectRecruit`** — a Brewster human, when an emigrant is due, **pauses and chooses** which of the three dock recruits steps ashore rather than getting a random one (the `PendingEmigration` / `ChooseEmigrant` seam, see [immigration](immigration.md); the choice's screen is a UI hook). The re-roll draws on the player's own immigration-type stream — a **default (no-Brewster) game never re-rolls**, so it stays byte-identical.

**Deviations / simplifications:** modifier **scopes** (e.g. `person`/non-person) are not yet evaluated — a father's goods modifier applies to the colony's whole production of that goods, which matches the player-visible result for the applied fathers (each of these goods has a single production source). Production modifiers are applied at the **drain** point (bells→liberty, crosses→immigration), which equals the per-colony total (one truncation, as FreeCol). **Age** (which weights apply) now keys off the calendar **year** against the spec `model.option.ages` thresholds (classic 1600/1700) — `Ruleset.AgeForYear(Game.CurrentYear)`: age 1 to 1599, age 2 from 1600, age 3 from 1700 (turns 1–108 / 109–308 / 309+), faithful to FreeCol `Specification.getAge`. (This replaced the old turn-band approximation 1–99 / 100–199 / 200+.) The cost **`factor`** now comes from the **difficulty level** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.FoundingFatherFactor`; classic veryEasy→veryHard = 24/32/40/48/56), defaulting to **medium 40** — the value the game is balanced around. See [difficulty](difficulty.md).

## 3. Technical design

- `FoundingFather` (record): `Id`, `Type` (`FatherType` enum), age weights `Weight1/2/3`, `WeightForAge(age)`. Parsed from `<founding-father>` in the ruleset.
- `Game`: `Liberty`, `Congress`, `CurrentFather`, `OfferedFathers`, `CurrentAge`; `ChooseFather(id)`; static `FoundingFatherCost(electedCount, factor)` and instance `TotalFoundingFatherCost()` — the `factor` is `Ruleset.Difficulty.FoundingFatherFactor` (difficulty-driven, default medium 40; see [difficulty](difficulty.md)).
- **Age (`86d3c9xy3`):** `CurrentAge => Ruleset.AgeForYear(CurrentYear)`. `Ruleset.FatherAgeYears` (the ascending year thresholds parsed from `model.option.ages`, classic `[1600, 1700]`, with FreeCol's "badAges" fallback to `[1600,1700]` on a missing/malformed option — not exactly two integers, or any year before the starting year, FreeCol's `turn ≥ 1` clamp) and `AgeForYear(year) = 1 + count(year ≥ threshold)` → 1-based age (1/2/3) to feed `FoundingFather.WeightForAge`. Equivalent to FreeCol's turn-threshold `getAge` (`ages=[1, yearToTurn(1600)=109, yearToTurn(1700)=309]`) since `YearForTurn` is monotonic — verified boundary-equal at turns 108/109/308/309. Derived from `Turn` → no save change.
- **Modifier/ability system:** `FatherModifier(TargetId, Type, Value, Index, ScopeUnitTypes?)` with `ApplyTo` + `AppliesTo(unitTypeId)` (unscoped = applies to all; `<scope type=…>` restricts — Francis Drake's combat modifiers scope to the privateer); `FatherAbility(Id, Value, ScopeTypes)`. Combat fathers fold through `Game.FatherCombatFactor(unit, "model.modifier.offence"/"defence")` into `OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase` (per-owner, scoped). From **FP-5** the production fold is **per player**: `Game.ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, goodsId, base)` folds *that player's* elected fathers' modifiers for a goods (ascending index, truncated to int — FreeCol `FeatureContainer.applyModifiers`) and adds that player's Paine tax-rate bell bonus; `HasAbilityFor(player, id)` queries its abilities. The public no-player overloads (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(goodsId, base)`, `HasAbility(id)`) delegate to the human for presentation/tests (behaviourally identical to before). The bell and cross drains run each player's totals through its own fold; the recruit pool filters out that player's father-banned types and its dock refreshes on its own election. So a foreign power's economy uses **its own** Congress, never the human's.
- Turn step `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers(player)` runs in `RunPlayerTurn` for every colonial/rebel/independent player after its colonies: drains each of its colonies' bells into its liberty (modified by its own fathers), then — **unless the player has declared independence** — elects its chosen father if affordable and refreshes its offers when nothing is pending. The bells → liberty bake is **always** run (a rebel's Sons of Liberty must keep updating). The election + offer-refresh half is split into `ElectAndRefreshFounders(player)` and gated by `if (player.PlayerType is not (PlayerType.Rebel or PlayerType.Independent) || Ruleset.GameOptions.ContinueFoundingFatherRecruitment) { … }` (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.canRecruitFoundingFather`; `model.option.continueFoundingFatherRecruitment`, classic default false). `GenerateOffers(player)` does the seeded weighted draw from `RandomFor(player)` (the human's stream 0, a foreign power's own stream) and is also called in `Game.New` for the human so choices exist from turn 1; a foreign power generates its offers lazily on its first turn and picks one via its own AI ([players.md](players.md)). **Determinism (ADR-009):** gating-out the election+offers for a rebel removes that turn's `RandomFor(player)` offers draw — but **only on the Rebel/Independent path**; the **COLONIAL** path's draw sequence (the only one the default soak exercises — it never declares) is byte-identical, and the soak stays byte-stable + twin-deterministic. The option is parsed in `Ruleset.ParseGameOptions` (`model.option.continueFoundingFatherRecruitment`, classic `false`) onto `GameOptions.ContinueFoundingFatherRecruitment`, with a `Ruleset.WithContinueFoundingFatherRecruitment(bool)` override seam mirroring `WithFogOfWar`; **session-only, no save bump**.
- **AI father pick (`86d3e49ej`):** in `RunForeignPowerEconomy`, when a foreign power has offers but no current target, `SelectFoundingFatherFor(power)` chooses it — a **value-weighted, RNG-free** select faithful to FreeCol `EuropeanAIPlayer.selectFoundingFather`: (1) **custom-house override** — if any offered father carries `model.ability.buildCustomHouse` (only Peter Stuyvesant), it is chosen outright ahead of any weight (FreeCol grabs the custom house early to ease its transport burden); else (2) the offered father with the greatest `FoundingFather.WeightForAge(CurrentAge)`, ties broken by **ordinal father-id** (`StringComparer.Ordinal`). This replaced the old uniform `RandomFor(power).Next(...)` draw — the offer **SET** is still seeded by `GenerateOffers`, but the **SELECT** no longer touches the power's RNG stream (ADR-009; the power's own stream advances differently, never stream 0). Human-only `ChooseFather(id)` is unchanged — the human is never auto-picked for.
- **European-diplomacy fathers (`86d3c7xu6`/`86d3c7xbk`):** all read the elected father's parsed `<ability>` records (the generic `el.Elements("ability")` parse already captures them — no Ruleset change). **Franklin** — `Game.OtherPartyAlwaysOffersPeace(otherId)` (`HasAbilityFor(other, model.ability.alwaysOfferedPeace)`) is read by `Game.EvaluateStance`: when the treaty's *other* party holds it, a peace/cease-fire/alliance clause scores **0** (FreeCol's `franklin` branch). Pure, no RNG, not turn-loop-wired → the default game is byte-identical. **Jan de Witt** — three public oracles on `Game`: `CanTradeWithForeignColonies(id)` (`tradeWithForeignColonies`); `CustomHouseTradesWithForeignCountries(id)` (`customHouseTradesWithForeignCountries` **and** at peace/allied with ≥1 other European, per `Player.canTrade(CUSTOM_HOUSE)`); `HasBetterForeignAffairsReport(id)` (`betterForeignAffairsReport`) + `ForeignNationStances(id)` (each rival colonial power's id + the player's stance toward it, in id order — the report data). Each colonial-gated + owner-scoped; the human's default Congress holds none, so all return false/empty until elected. **Brewster** — `RefreshDockForRecruitability` is the `model.event.newRecruits` event (re-roll banned dock slots); `selectRecruit` is the `PendingEmigration`/`ChooseEmigrant` seam (see [immigration](immigration.md)).
- **Persistence:** save v10 stores liberty, Congress, the current target, and the offered set (so a reload restores the same choice); pre-v10 loads empty. Father effects need no extra save state — they ride on the persisted Congress (modifiers/abilities are ruleset-derived). The new Franklin/de Witt effects add **no** save state (no version bump).
- **Portraits (presentation-only, ADR-006):** `ColonyArt.FatherPortrait(shortName)` loads `res://assets/freecol/fathers/<shortName>.jpg` (FreeCol's 200×237 painted portraits, GPL v2 — all 25 classic fathers covered; see [PROVENANCE](../../game/assets/freecol/PROVENANCE.md)), or null when no art exists. Files are named for the father's `ShortName` so the loader is a direct lookup like `GoodsIcon`/`UnitIcon` (the four fathers FreeCol stored under a shorter file name were copied to their short name). `FoundingFatherPanel` renders each offered father as an `Offer_<shortName>` row holding a `Portrait_<shortName>` `TextureRect` (thumbnail, `ExpandMode.IgnoreSize` + `KeepAspectCentered` so it stays a small un-stretched face) beside the existing choose button; `ColopediaPanel.BuildFathers` renders each father as a `Fathers_<shortName>` row holding the portrait beside a `FatherLabel` (the existing name/category/effect text moved onto that child label). The portrait is purely additive — no game state read, no rule logic — and both panels degrade to text-only if a father lacks art.
