## 1. How it works (plain English)

Your colonies' town halls ring **liberty bells**; every bell becomes a **liberty point** for your nation. Spend enough liberty and a **Founding Father** joins your Continental Congress — a famous figure who (eventually) grants a lasting bonus. Each round you're **offered one candidate per category** (trade, exploration, military, political, religious); you pick which to recruit, and they're elected the turn your banked liberty reaches their cost. The more fathers you already have, the more liberty the next one costs.

**Worked example:** a new colony's town hall makes 1 bell a turn, so you bank 1 liberty a turn. The first father costs 40 — so after 40 turns, the candidate you chose joins Congress, your liberty resets, and a fresh slate of candidates is offered. (Staff the town hall with statesmen for several bells a turn and elect fathers far quicker.)

## 2. Detailed rules

- **Liberty** = total bells produced (bells are consumed into liberty each turn, not stockpiled as goods).
- **Cost of the next father** (FreeCol `getTotalFoundingFatherCost`, verified vs source): with `factor` = **40** (classic **medium**, the value the base game is balanced around),
  - first father (none elected yet): `factor` → **40**
  - thereafter: `2 × (elected + 1) × factor + 1` → **161, 241, 321, 401, …**
- **Offers:** one father per category that still has an un-elected candidate with a non-zero weight for the current age; the candidate is drawn by **seeded weight** (each father has age-1/2/3 weights). Already-elected fathers are never re-offered.
- **Election:** when your chosen father's cost is met, they join Congress, that cost is subtracted from liberty, and a new offer set is generated.

**Pocahontas (native peacemaker)** — the political father who calms the natives. Two effects, both faithful to FreeCol on our per-settlement alarm model:
- **On election:** every native settlement's alarm toward you drops to **0 (Happy)** — the `model.event.resetNativeAlarm` event (`ServerPlayer.java`). All grievances forgotten; braves fall below the raid threshold and stop attacking. *(Deviations: FreeCol also sets a native-nation `Stance.PEACE` object and only resets settlements you've **contacted** — we have neither a native-nation stance type nor fine contact-tracking, so we reset **all** settlements; the alarm-to-0 yields the same observable "no raids".)*
- **Permanently:** the per-turn **ambient** native-alarm gain is **halved** — the `model.modifier.nativeAlarmModifier` (−50%, read from the spec), folded by `ScaleNativeAlarmGain` into `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` (the proximity-pressure pass); **gains only** — goodwill and decay (negative deltas) are unaffected, so recovery isn't slowed. This is the modifier's **faithful home**: FreeCol damps only the ambient proximity alarm (`ServerPlayer.csNewTurn`), not combat (combat tension is applied raw) — so electing Pocahontas roughly halves how fast tribes anger at your expanding colonies. Both effects are human-gated (alarm is toward the human; a foreign power electing Pocahontas is a native-alarm no-op) and ride on the persisted Congress (no save change, no RNG). Identified by father id rather than parsing `<event>` (single-event minimalism).

**Francis Drake (privateer captain)** — the military father who makes privateers fearsome: **+50% offence and +50% defence**, scoped to `model.unit.privateer` (read from the spec). It's the **first scoped father combat modifier**: `Game.OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase` multiply by `FatherCombatFactor`, which folds the unit *owner's* elected fathers' `model.modifier.offence`/`defence` modifiers whose `<scope>` matches the unit's type. Per-owner (a foreign power's Drake boosts *its* privateers, not yours), dormant unless elected, riding the persisted Congress (no save change, no RNG). Drake is the only father with these modifiers, so only privateers are ever affected. *(Implementation note: because every `CombatModel` situational factor is multiplicative, folding Drake's ×1.5 into the base power is mathematically identical to applying it at FreeCol's index 50 — multiplication commutes — so the fold lives in `OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase`.)*

**La Salle (the free-stockade explorer)** — the exploration father who fortifies your colonies: every colony that reaches **population 3** is given a **free stockade** (FreeCol `model.event.freeBuilding` = `model.building.stockade`), and that stockade carries the colony fortification defence bonus (+100% — see [combat](combat.md)), so a La Salle empire defends its settlements without spending hammers on walls. `Game.ApplyFreeBuildings(player)` runs each turn after election and colony growth: for every free building any elected father grants, it adds it to each owned colony that meets the building's required population and lacks it (idempotent, RNG-free, stable colony-id order). It fires both the turn La Salle is elected (existing pop-3 colonies) and later as colonies grow to 3. Per-owner (a foreign power's La Salle fortifies *its* colonies). The granted stockade persists in the colony's building list — no save-format change. *(Deferred: La Salle's `buildingPriceBonus −100%` for a stockade — a free **manual** rebuild, redundant once the event grants one outright — and the stockade's `minimumColonySize` modifier.)*

**John Paul Jones (the free frigate)** — the military father who hands you a warship outright: the moment he joins the Congress, a brand-new **frigate** appears on your **Europe dock**, ready to sail to the New World (FreeCol models this as a founding-father `<unit id="model.unit.frigate"/>` child — a one-time grant on election, not a standing modifier). `Game.AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` spawns each of an elected father's `FreeUnits` via `SpawnInEurope` (the same Europe-dock spawn the monarch's mercenaries/support use), owned by the electing player — so a foreign power's John Paul Jones arms *its* Europe. The grant fires exactly once (it rides the election event, never re-run on load), draws no RNG (so a human election stays byte-stable on stream 0), and the frigate persists as an ordinary Europe unit — no save-format change. The mechanism is generic: any father (or mod) with a `<unit>` child grants it the same way.

**Jacob Fugger (the boycott-breaker)** — the trade father who wipes the slate clean: the turn he joins the Congress, **every boycott on your market ends at once** (FreeCol `model.event.boycottsLifted`). Boycotts come from a Boston Tea Party — dumping a taxed good overboard bans its sale until you pay the back-tax arrears (see [monarchy](monarchy.md)); Fugger clears all of those arrears in one stroke, for free, so the goods trade freely again. `FoundingFather.LiftsBoycotts` parses the event, and on election `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` calls `player.Market.LiftAllBoycotts()` (which just empties the arrears — unlike a new nation's `Reinitialise`, prices and inventory are **not** reset). One-time, per-owner (a foreign power's Fugger clears *its* boycotts), RNG-free, and no save change (arrears already persist). For most games this is a no-op (you've held no tea party), so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Francisco de Coronado (the cartographer)** — the exploration father who shows you the whole map's settlements: the turn he joins the Congress, **every colony on the map is revealed** to you — your own and every rival's — so you instantly know where everyone has settled (FreeCol `model.event.seeAllColonies`). `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` reveals each colony's tile and its ring (`RevealAround(player, colony.Position, 1)`) into the electing player's fog. One-time on election (rides the event), RNG-free (no stream-0 perturbation), per-owner (a foreign power's Coronado reveals colonies to *its* fog), and it rides the existing explored-set save (no format change). *(Deferred: Coronado's continuous `exposedTilesRadius +3` — a permanent wider sight ring around your own colonies — which needs a standing per-colony fog modifier rather than a one-time reveal.)*

**Bartolomé de las Casas (the convert-emancipator)** — the religious father who turns the converted: the moment he joins the Congress, **every native convert you hold becomes a full free colonist** (FreeCol `model.ability.upgradeConvert`). Native converts (`model.unit.indianConvert`, harvested by missions — see [natives](natives.md)) are weaker workers; de las Casas promotes them all in one stroke. `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers` checks the elected father's parsed `model.ability.upgradeConvert` ability and, when present, `UpgradeUnitType`s each of the electing player's converts to `model.unit.freeColonist`. One-time on election, RNG-free (no stream-0 perturbation), owner-generic (a foreign power emancipates *its* converts), and no save change (the upgraded units are ordinary colonists). For most games a no-op (no converts yet), so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Father Jean de Brébeuf (the master missionary)** — normally only a **jesuit missionary** converts natives at the expert rate; an ordinary free colonist sent to preach works far more slowly. While Brébeuf sits in your Congress, **every one of your missions converts as if staffed by a jesuit**, even the ones you established with an ordinary colonist (FreeCol `model.ability.expertMissionary`). In our convert maths (see [natives](natives.md)) that means each of your missions gains the jesuit skill term (an extra **+3** convert-progress per turn) regardless of who actually staffs it. This is a *continuous* ability (it applies every turn while he's in the Congress, not a one-time election event), it's owner-scoped (a foreign power with Brébeuf boosts *its* missions, not yours), and it's RNG-free — a player with no missions sees no effect, so it never perturbs an existing save or test.

**Juan de Sepúlveda (the converting conquistador)** — when you **win a battle at a native settlement where you keep a mission**, there's a chance one of its braves defects to you as a convert. De Sepúlveda makes that **20% more likely** (FreeCol `model.modifier.nativeConvertBonus`). Note this is a *combat* bonus, not a peaceful-mission one: in the original game `nativeConvertBonus` only ever affects the capture-a-convert roll when you storm a settlement (it does **not** speed up ordinary missionary conversions — that's Brébeuf's job above). The Spanish **conquest** national character carries a much larger **+200%** of the same modifier, so a Spanish conquistador who has also elected de Sepúlveda is almost certain to harvest a convert from every mission settlement they raze. Owner-scoped (each player folds its own Congress + nation type); see [combat](combat.md) for the full capture-convert rule and the worked probability.

**Adam Smith (the industrialist) & Peter Stuyvesant (the merchant)** — the two trade fathers that **unlock buildings** rather than tweaking numbers. Each grants an **ability** (Smith → `model.ability.buildFactory`, Stuyvesant → `model.ability.buildCustomHouse`) that satisfies a building's `required-ability` gate, so the colony build menu offers the gated buildings only once that father sits in the Congress: Smith opens the whole **factory tier** (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, rum factory, fur factory) and the **arsenal**; Stuyvesant opens the **custom house**. The wiring is generic and lives on the colony side — `Game.HasAbilityFor(player, …)` reads the elected father's parsed abilities, `ColonyHasAbility`/`RequiredAbilityRefusal` resolve the build gate against them ([colonies](colonies.md) — build-ability gate) — so no per-father code is needed here; electing the father simply makes the gate pass. The factory tier's extra +50% manufacturing is **not** a Smith modifier: it's each factory building's own `rebel-factor 1.5`, which scales the per-worker Sons-of-Liberty production bonus (see [colonies](colonies.md) — building production); a high-liberty colony with a factory therefore out-produces the same workers in the plain shop. Per-owner (a foreign power's Smith opens *its* factories), RNG-free, riding the persisted Congress (no save change).

**Benjamin Franklin (the diplomat) — `ignoreEuropeanWars` now applied; the rest still deferred.** Despite the original task's "natives offer peace" framing, FreeCol's Benjamin Franklin is purely European, with three effects: `ignoreEuropeanWars` (the monarch can't drag you into European wars), `alwaysOfferedPeace` (the European AI always accepts your peace offer), and `peaceTreaty +50%` (offered peace holds). Now that the **monarch declare-war** system exists (see [monarchy](monarchy.md)), the first one is **wired**: while Franklin sits in your Congress, `Game.MonarchPotentialEnemies()` is empty (it reads `HasAbilityFor(_human, model.ability.ignoreEuropeanWars)`), so the King **cannot declare war on your behalf** — but he can still make peace (Franklin stops wars, not peace, faithful to FreeCol's `collectPotentialEnemies` vs `collectPotentialFriends`). It's human-gated, rides the persisted Congress (no save/RNG change). The other two effects (`alwaysOfferedPeace`, `peaceTreaty +50%`) still need an **AI diplomatic-trade flow** that doesn't exist yet, so they stay deferred (with de Witt's foreign-trade effect). A "natives never raid" Franklin rule would be a fabrication that duplicates Pocahontas.

**Deviations / simplifications:** modifier **scopes** (e.g. `person`/non-person) are not yet evaluated — a father's goods modifier applies to the colony's whole production of that goods, which matches the player-visible result for the applied fathers (each of these goods has a single production source). Production modifiers are applied at the **drain** point (bells→liberty, crosses→immigration), which equals the per-colony total (one truncation, as FreeCol). **Age** (which weights apply) now keys off the calendar **year** against the spec `model.option.ages` thresholds (classic 1600/1700) — `Ruleset.AgeForYear(Game.CurrentYear)`: age 1 to 1599, age 2 from 1600, age 3 from 1700 (turns 1–108 / 109–308 / 309+), faithful to FreeCol `Specification.getAge`. (This replaced the old turn-band approximation 1–99 / 100–199 / 200+.) The cost **`factor`** now comes from the **difficulty level** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.FoundingFatherFactor`; classic veryEasy→veryHard = 24/32/40/48/56), defaulting to **medium 40** — the value the game is balanced around. See [difficulty](difficulty.md).

## 3. Technical design

- `FoundingFather` (record): `Id`, `Type` (`FatherType` enum), age weights `Weight1/2/3`, `WeightForAge(age)`. Parsed from `<founding-father>` in the ruleset.
- `Game`: `Liberty`, `Congress`, `CurrentFather`, `OfferedFathers`, `CurrentAge`; `ChooseFather(id)`; static `FoundingFatherCost(electedCount, factor)` and instance `TotalFoundingFatherCost()` — the `factor` is `Ruleset.Difficulty.FoundingFatherFactor` (difficulty-driven, default medium 40; see [difficulty](difficulty.md)).
- **Age (`86d3c9xy3`):** `CurrentAge => Ruleset.AgeForYear(CurrentYear)`. `Ruleset.FatherAgeYears` (the ascending year thresholds parsed from `model.option.ages`, classic `[1600, 1700]`, with FreeCol's "badAges" fallback to `[1600,1700]` on a missing/malformed option — not exactly two integers, or any year before the starting year, FreeCol's `turn ≥ 1` clamp) and `AgeForYear(year) = 1 + count(year ≥ threshold)` → 1-based age (1/2/3) to feed `FoundingFather.WeightForAge`. Equivalent to FreeCol's turn-threshold `getAge` (`ages=[1, yearToTurn(1600)=109, yearToTurn(1700)=309]`) since `YearForTurn` is monotonic — verified boundary-equal at turns 108/109/308/309. Derived from `Turn` → no save change.
- **Modifier/ability system:** `FatherModifier(TargetId, Type, Value, Index, ScopeUnitTypes?)` with `ApplyTo` + `AppliesTo(unitTypeId)` (unscoped = applies to all; `<scope type=…>` restricts — Francis Drake's combat modifiers scope to the privateer); `FatherAbility(Id, Value, ScopeTypes)`. Combat fathers fold through `Game.FatherCombatFactor(unit, "model.modifier.offence"/"defence")` into `OffenceBase`/`DefenceBase` (per-owner, scoped). From **FP-5** the production fold is **per player**: `Game.ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, goodsId, base)` folds *that player's* elected fathers' modifiers for a goods (ascending index, truncated to int — FreeCol `FeatureContainer.applyModifiers`) and adds that player's Paine tax-rate bell bonus; `HasAbilityFor(player, id)` queries its abilities. The public no-player overloads (`ApplyGoodsModifiers(goodsId, base)`, `HasAbility(id)`) delegate to the human for presentation/tests (behaviourally identical to before). The bell and cross drains run each player's totals through its own fold; the recruit pool filters out that player's father-banned types and its dock refreshes on its own election. So a foreign power's economy uses **its own** Congress, never the human's.
- Turn step `AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers(player)` runs in `RunPlayerTurn` for every colonial player after its colonies: drains each of its colonies' bells into its liberty (modified by its own fathers), elects its chosen father if affordable, then refreshes its offers when nothing is pending. `GenerateOffers(player)` does the seeded weighted draw from `RandomFor(player)` (the human's stream 0, a foreign power's own stream) and is also called in `Game.New` for the human so choices exist from turn 1; a foreign power generates its offers lazily on its first turn and picks one via its own AI ([players.md](players.md)).
- **Persistence:** save v10 stores liberty, Congress, the current target, and the offered set (so a reload restores the same choice); pre-v10 loads empty. Father effects need no extra save state — they ride on the persisted Congress (modifiers/abilities are ruleset-derived).
