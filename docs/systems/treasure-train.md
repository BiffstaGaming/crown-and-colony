## 1. How it works (plain English)

When you **sack a native settlement** and there's treasure to be had, you don't pocket the gold on the spot any more. Instead a **treasure train** — a slow, defenceless wagon piled with gold — appears where the settlement stood. It's yours, but the gold isn't *banked* yet: you have to **escort the train back to one of your colonies** and **cash it in** there. Until then it just sits on the map, and because it can't fight (and can be **captured**), an enemy who beats it on its tile takes the whole haul. So a rich sack is now a prize you have to *get home*, not an instant windfall.

**Cashing in.** You have two ways to bank the gold — and this works the way the **original 1994 Colonization** did, not the way FreeCol does it:

**Doing it in the game.** On the map, select the treasure train (click it on a colony tile). The selected-unit Orders panel shows a **"Cash in treasure"** button whenever the train can be cashed in where it is; press it and the game shows you **exactly how much gold you'll bank** — and whether the King is taking his transport cut (at a colony) or it's fee-free (you carried it home) — then asks you to confirm. Confirm and the gold lands in your treasury and the train is gone. If you try it somewhere the train can't be cashed in, the status bar tells you why (bring it to a colony, or aboard a ship to Europe) instead of doing anything.

Once you've **carried the treasure home to Europe**, you cash it in on the **Europe screen** instead: a treasure train aboard a galleon docked there shows a **"Cash in treasure (Ng)"** button right next to it in the ship's hold (N is the fee-free amount you'll bank), and if you've unloaded it onto the dock it appears under a **"Treasure"** heading with the same button. Press it and the gold lands in your treasury and the train is gone — no confirmation needed, since carrying it home yourself is always fee-free. *(Previously a treasure train carried home this way had nowhere to be cashed in — it just vanished from the Europe screen and the haul was lost. Now it always has a cash-in button.)*
- **Carry it home yourself.** A treasure train fills a whole galleon's hold, so **load it onto a galleon** (it boards like any passenger), sail to Europe, and cash it in there — and you **keep every last coin**. No transport cut, and no tax either. This is the best deal, but it ties up a galleon for a round trip and risks the treasure to pirates on the way.
- **Let the King ship it from a colony.** Bring the train to one of your colonies and the **King offers to carry the treasure to Europe for you — for a cut equal to your current tax rate**. If your tax is 25%, the King takes 25% and you keep 75%; if it's 50%, you split it down the middle. It's instant and safe, but the higher your taxes have climbed, the more the King skims. (There's no separate fee on top — the cut *is* the tax rate.)

And if **Hernán Cortés** sits in your Congress, the King ships it for **nothing** — full value even from a colony, whatever your tax rate.

So the trade-off is convenience versus cost: hand it to the King for a quick, safe cash-in that costs you your tax rate, or run it home yourself to keep the whole haul.

**Doing it in the game.** Select the treasure train (click it on a colony tile, or — once it's aboard a galleon docked in Europe — pick it from the ship). The selected-unit Orders panel shows a **"Cash in treasure"** button whenever the train can be cashed in where it is; press it and the game shows you the King's offer: at a colony it tells you the King will carry the gold and **take your tax-rate cut**, how much that is, and how much you'd keep — reminding you that sailing it home yourself keeps it all; in Europe it confirms you keep the **full amount**. Confirm and the gold lands in your treasury and the train is gone. If you try it somewhere the train can't be cashed in, the status bar tells you why (bring it to a colony, or aboard a ship to Europe) instead of doing anything.

The cities of gold (Cibola) and rich ruins you'll find in Lost City Rumours hand you treasure trains the same way (see [lost-city-rumours](lost-city-rumours.md)).

## 2. Detailed rules

- The **treasure train** (`model.unit.treasureTrain`) is a land unit with **0 offence / 0 defence**, movement 3, that **carries gold** (`model.ability.carryTreasure`) and **can be captured** (`model.ability.canBeCaptured`). It fills a whole galleon hold (`spaceTaken` 6).
- **Spawn on sack:** when an attacker destroys a native settlement and the sack yields **plunder > 0**, a treasure train carrying that amount musters **on the razed settlement tile**, owned by the attacker (FreeCol `csDestroySettlement`). This **replaces** the old instant-gold plunder — the gold is no longer credited directly. A sack that rolls **no** plunder spawns nothing.
- The plunder amount is unchanged from before (the same settlement-`<plunder>` range + Cortés's richer range); it just becomes the train's cargo instead of gold.
- **Capture:** a treasure train has no defence, so any unit that attacks its tile and wins **captures it** if the winner can capture units (FreeCol generic `CanBeCaptured`; the captor must be armed — `captureUnits` is carried by artillery and the regulars, not a bare colonist). The carried gold travels with the captured train to its new owner. *(A winner that cannot capture destroys the train and its gold — the same fate as any uncaptured capturable unit.)*
- **Cash in** (**Col1 fee model**, `86d3fb5mj` — a deliberate divergence from FreeCol; see below): a treasure train standing at **a colony its owner holds**, docked **in Europe**, **or loaded as cargo on a ship docked in Europe**, can be cashed in. Two cases:
  - **At a colony (the King transports it):** the owner banks `amount × (100 − taxRate) / 100` — i.e. the **King's cut = the current tax rate**. There is no separate transport fee and **no extra tax on top** (the cut *is* the tax). **Hernán Cortés**'s `treasureTransportFee −100%` waives the King's cut entirely → the full amount even at high tax.
  - **In Europe (you carried it home yourself):** the owner banks the **full `amount`** — **no fee and no tax**. A train reaches Europe either docked itself *or* carried there on a galleon (`TreasureIsInEurope`, FreeCol `Unit.isInEurope` follows the carrier).

  The train is consumed on cash-in (freeing the carrier's hold). **Net formula at a glance:** colony → `amount × (100 − taxRate)/100`; Europe → `amount`; Cortés at a colony → `amount`.
- **Carry it home on a galleon:** a treasure train fills a whole galleon hold (`spaceTaken` 6), so only a galleon (or man-o-war) can carry one — it **boards** the ship like any passenger (`Board`), travels with it across the Atlantic, and arrives docked in Europe, where it cashes in **fee-free**. A train that is aboard a ship **still in the New World** (or mid-crossing) is *not* cashable — it must actually reach Europe first.

- **Cashing in from the UI** (`86d3f62q1`): the selected-unit **Orders panel** carries a **"Cash in treasure"** action, shown only for treasure-carrying units and enabled only when `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` allows it where the train stands (at an owned colony, or aboard a galleon docked in Europe). Pressing it raises a confirmation that names the **net gold** you'd bank (`CashInValue`) and whether the King's cut applies or it's fee-free; confirm → the gold is banked and the train leaves the game (clearing the selection). A guard failure (not at a colony / not in Europe) is shown in the **status bar**, never thrown.
- **Cashing in from the Europe screen:** the on-map Orders button only applies to the selected on-map unit, so a treasure train that reached Europe (docked itself or aboard a galleon) had **no reachable cash-in** — the fee-free "carry it home yourself" play dead-ended. `EuropePanel` now offers the same cash-in for a treasure train **in Europe**: a train aboard a galleon docked there gets a **"Cash in treasure (Ng)"** button beside it in the ship's hold list, and a train put on the dock (which is neither a carrier nor a person, so it fell through every existing section) gets its own **"Treasure"** section with the same button. Both are gated on `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` and forward to the existing `CashInTreasureTrain` command; pressing one banks the fee-free net and consumes the train (freeing the carrier's hold), then refreshes the panel. No confirmation dialog (Europe cash-in is unconditionally fee-free).
- **Cashing in from the UI** (`86d3f62q1`, reframed `86d3fb5mj`): the selected-unit **Orders panel** carries a **"Cash in treasure"** action, shown only for treasure-carrying units and enabled only when `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` allows it where the train stands (at an owned colony, or aboard a galleon docked in Europe). Pressing it raises a confirmation framed as the **King's offer**: at a colony it reads "The King will carry your treasure to Europe and take {taxRate}% ({cut}g); you keep {net}g. (Carry it home on a galleon yourself to keep all {amount}g.)"; in Europe it confirms you keep the full fee-free, tax-free amount. Confirm → the gold is banked and the train leaves the game (clearing the selection). A guard failure (not at a colony / not in Europe) is shown in the **status bar**, never thrown.

**Deliberate Col1 divergence (`86d3fb5mj`).** FreeCol charges a **flat `TreasureTransportFee` (medium 60%)** for the King's transport and then taxes the remainder. We instead follow the **original Colonization (1994)**: treasure you carry home yourself is **fee-free *and* tax-free** (you keep it all), and the King's at-colony cut is **your current tax rate** (no separate 60% cut). This is a deliberate balance/fidelity choice (Chris's decision), parallel to the [tax-cadence fix](monarchy.md). The difficulty option `model.option.treasureTransportFee` (60) is still **parsed** (the spec is unchanged) but is **no longer used for the cut** — the cut tracks the tax rate. See also [monarchy](monarchy.md).

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** the cash-in **location** is simplified — FreeCol requires a port *connected to Europe* and only lets the King ship it when you have no suitable carrier; we have no Europe-connectivity graph, so **any owned colony** qualifies (the King ships it for his tax-rate cut), and the full-value path is the in-Europe cash-in (reached by carrying the train there on a galleon, or — once independent — staying in the New World). **No native treasury** is involved (the King/tax economics only). **Deferred:** the **independent/rebel** cash-in (no Europe → full value, no fee) once independence exists; the AI cashing in / valuing in-transit treasure (with FP-6 settlement-sacking AI); the map **treasure-train marker** (presentation — the cash-in *action* is now reachable).

## 3. Technical design

- **Parse:** `UnitType.CarryTreasure` (`model.ability.carryTreasure`, parsed in `Ruleset` next to `CaptureGoods`/`Piracy`). `CanBeCaptured` and `SpaceTaken` (6) already parsed for the treasure train down its `extends wagon` chain.
- **State:** `Unit.TreasureAmount` (read-only) + internal `SetTreasureAmount` (floors negatives, like `AddCargo`). 0 for every non-treasure unit. `UpgradeUnitType` carries the amount across a type swap (defensive — treasure trains have no type change today).
- **Spawn-on-sack:** in `Game.AttackSettlement`'s win branch the instant `_human.Gold += ComputePlunder(...)` is replaced by `int plunder = ComputePlunder(type, hasPlunderAbility, random); if (plunder > 0) SpawnUnit(TreasureTrainUnitTypeId, target, attacker.OwnerId).SetTreasureAmount(plunder);`. **Determinism (ADR-009):** `ComputePlunder` is still called exactly once, in the same position (after the promotion draw), so the human's stream-0 RNG sequence is byte-identical to the old path; `SpawnUnit` draws no RNG. The L5 soak stays byte-stable (and the AI never sacks settlements, so no train spawns there).
- **Capture:** reuses the existing `ResolveLoserOutcome` → `CaptureUnit` path. A defence-0 train loses any fight; with no `model.unitChange.capture` row for the treasure train, `CaptureUnit` flips the **same** `Unit` object's owner, so `TreasureAmount` rides along automatically — no new code.
- **Cash in (Col1 model, `86d3fb5mj`):** `TransportFee(owner, train)` is now the **King's at-colony cut** = `ApplyGoodsModifiers(owner, "model.modifier.treasureTransportFee", owner.TaxRate × amount / 100)` — the tax rate, not the flat `TreasureTransportFee(60)`, folded with the same Congress modifier as before so Cortés's −100% zeroes the cut. `CashInValue(owner, train)` branches on location: `TreasureIsInEurope(train)` → `amount` (full, **no fee and no tax** — you carried it home); else → `amount − TransportFee(owner, train)` (at a colony, the King's cut). **No second tax pass:** the at-colony tax is *expressed as* the King's cut, so the formula is just `amount − cut`. **`TreasureIsInEurope`** (`86d3e4bcp`, GAP A) is the FreeCol `Unit.isInEurope`-follows-the-carrier rule: the train's own `Location == InEurope` **or** it is aboard (`IsAboard`) a ship whose `Location == InEurope`. `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` gates on a `CarryTreasure` unit with `TreasureAmount > 0` standing at an owner-held colony **or** `TreasureIsInEurope` (its `MoveCheck.Yes` cost carries the net for the UI). `CashInTreasureTrain` banks the net to `PlayerById(train.OwnerId)` and removes the train (which also frees the carrier's hold — `Passengers`/`CargoSlotsUsed` read live `_units`). The public `Game.TreasureKingsCut(Unit)` oracle exposes the at-colony cut (`= TransportFee`, 0 in Europe / under Cortés / when not cashable) for the UI's King's-offer wording. No RNG, no save field — pure arithmetic over already-saved state; the difficulty option `TreasureTransportFee` is parsed but unused for the cut.
- **Carry-onto-a-galleon** needs **no new code on the treasure side**: a treasure train's `CarrySlots` is `max(spaceTaken 6, space 0 + 1) = 6`, so the existing `CheckBoard`/`Board` (hold-space gate) already lets it board only a galleon-class hold; `SyncPassengers` propagates the ship's `Location` to the aboard train as the ship sails (`SailToEurope` → `AdvanceSailing` → `InEurope`), and `TreasureIsInEurope` then waives the fee. The whole GAP-A change is the `TreasureIsInEurope` helper + routing the two cash-in gates through it.
- **UI seam** (`86d3f62q1`): `Game.TreasureCashInIsFeeFree(Unit)` is a thin **public** read-only oracle exposing the in-Europe/at-colony branch of `CashInValue` (`=> TreasureIsInEurope(train)`) so the presentation can phrase the confirmation without re-deriving the fee. `GameController.CashInSelectedTreasureTrain` (wired to the Orders `CashInTreasureButton.Pressed`, mirroring the disband handler) re-checks `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` and on a refusal writes the reason to `_notice` (the status bar) instead of throwing; on success it builds a `ConfirmationDialog` showing `CashInValue` (and the fee-free vs King's-cut line), and on confirm re-checks the oracle, calls `Game.CashInTreasureTrain`, nulls `_selectedUnit` (the train is gone) and refreshes. `RefreshView` gates the button: `Visible = sel.Type.CarryTreasure`, `Disabled = !CheckCashInTreasureTrain(sel).Allowed` — exactly the `_sailToEuropeButton` pattern. No GameLogic behaviour change beyond the additive oracle; no save change.
- **Europe-screen seam:** `EuropePanel.CashInButton(Unit train)` builds a `CashIn_{id}` button labelled `Cash in treasure ({CashInValue}g)`; pressing it re-checks `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` and calls `CashInTreasureTrain` (then `Changed()` → refresh). It's added in two places in `BuildDynamicSections`: (1) inside the existing passenger loop, when a passenger `passenger.Type.CarryTreasure && CheckCashInTreasureTrain(passenger).Allowed` (a train aboard a galleon docked in Europe — `TreasureIsInEurope` follows the carrier), placed before the "Put on dock" button; and (2) a new **"Treasure"** section iterating `UnitsInEurope.Where(u => u.Type.CarryTreasure && !u.IsAboard)` (a train **unloaded onto the dock** — `Location == InEurope`, not a carrier, not a person, so it matched neither the `ships` nor the `onDock` filter and previously rendered nowhere). The section is omitted entirely when there are no dock trains. The aboard case is excluded from `treasureOnDock` (`!u.IsAboard`), so the two entries never double-up. Pure presentation reading the same oracles (ADR-006); no GameLogic change, no save change.
- **UI seam** (`86d3f62q1`, King's-offer reframe `86d3fb5mj`): `Game.TreasureCashInIsFeeFree(Unit)` (`=> TreasureIsInEurope(train)`) and `Game.TreasureKingsCut(Unit)` (the at-colony cut, 0 in Europe / under Cortés) are thin **public** read-only oracles the presentation uses to phrase the confirmation without re-deriving the arithmetic. `GameController.CashInSelectedTreasureTrain` (wired to the Orders `CashInTreasureButton.Pressed`, mirroring the disband handler) re-checks `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` and on a refusal writes the reason to `_notice` (the status bar) instead of throwing; on success it builds a `ConfirmationDialog` whose text is **framed as the King's offer** at a colony ("The King will carry … and take {`Game.TaxRate`}% ({`TreasureKingsCut`}g); you keep {`CashInValue`}g. (Carry it home yourself to keep all {amount}g.)") or the full fee-free, tax-free amount in Europe, and on confirm re-checks the oracle, calls `Game.CashInTreasureTrain`, nulls `_selectedUnit` (the train is gone) and refreshes. `RefreshView` gates the button: `Visible = sel.Type.CarryTreasure`, `Disabled = !CheckCashInTreasureTrain(sel).Allowed` — exactly the `_sailToEuropeButton` pattern. No GameLogic behaviour change beyond the additive oracle; no save change.
- **Save (v27, additive):** `SavedUnit.TreasureAmount` is **omitted when 0**, so every non-treasure unit (and a treasure-free game) serializes byte-identically to v26; pre-v27 saves load with 0. `Restore` applies it via `SetTreasureAmount` after the object initializer (it's an internal method, like `AddCargo`).
