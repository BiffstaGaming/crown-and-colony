## 1. How it works (plain English)

The New World is already inhabited. When a game begins, the indigenous nations — the Apache, Sioux, Tupi, Arawak, Cherokee, Iroquois, Inca and Aztec — already have settlements dotted across the map. You discover them as you explore; a settlement you have never seen stays hidden under the fog.

**The rules, in plain words:**
- There are **eight** native nations. Three live in **camps** (Apache, Sioux, Tupi), three in **villages** (Arawak, Cherokee, Iroquois), and two in great **cities** (Inca, Aztec).
- Each nation has **one capital** (bigger, better defended) plus a few ordinary settlements. Some nations spread more widely than others.
- Settlements sit on land, never crowd each other, and never spawn right on top of your landing site — you always get room to settle.
- Each settlement quietly knows a **skill** it could teach a visiting colonist (e.g. expert farmer) — visiting, trading and learning come in later slices.

**Worked example:**
> You sail in and found your first colony. A few turns later your scout walks north-east and the fog peels back to reveal an Iroquois village — a cluster of longhouses with "Iroquois" on its name plate. Press on and you might find their capital, marked with a ★.

**Interacting with a settlement.** Move a colonist next to a settlement and you can **speak with its chief**: the first time, they tell tales of nearby lands (revealing the map around the settlement) and usually hand over a small gold gift. A plain colonist (a free colonist or indentured servant) can also **learn the settlement's skill** — it is taught the settlement's expert profession (e.g. an expert farmer), a free shortcut to a specialist. An ordinary settlement teaches its one skill just once; a nation's **capital** teaches forever.

Each settlement also has an **alarm** level toward you — Happy, Content, Displeased, Angry, or Hateful. It starts peaceful and cools over time, but hostile acts raise it: **attacking one of its braves** adds a lot of alarm, and **killing the brave** adds a lot more. An **angry** settlement won't teach you; a **hateful** one gives no gifts and is dangerous to be near.

**Braves.** Every settlement is guarded by a **brave** — a native warrior standing on the land next to it. Braves are the natives' fighting units (and your targets): you can attack one with an armed unit, and combat plays out by the rules in [combat](combat.md).

**Braves now act on their own.** A calm tribe's braves simply **roam** their lands a tile at a time. But anger a tribe — by attacking its people — and once a settlement turns **Displeased** (or worse) its braves leave camp to **hunt your nearest colonist or soldier**: they march toward it and **attack** when they reach it. So mistreating the natives now has teeth: their warriors come for you. A beaten unarmed colonist is killed, an armed one is knocked back (and the brave may grab his muskets). When a brave attacks you, the game tells you in the status bar ("The Apache raided your Free Colonist!" / "Your Soldier fought off an Apache raid."). The braves of each tribe act using their own hidden dice, entirely separate from yours, so your game still plays out identically for a given starting seed.

**Hostile braves now raid your colonies too.** A brave standing next to one of your colonies that has **no defender garrisoned in it** will **storm it and carry off goods** — it grabs up to half of one warehouse stack (at most 50) and runs. Unlike a European rival, a tribe never *takes* your colony or burns it down (in this slice) — it raids and leaves; the colony, its people and its buildings stay yours. You're told what was taken ("The Apache raided Jamestown and carried off 50 tobacco!"). The fix is the same as for unit raids: keep a soldier garrisoned in any colony near an angry tribe — a defended colony can't be pillaged (the brave must beat the garrison in the field first). Even an *un*garrisoned colony resists far better behind walls: a **stockade** (or fort/fortress) boosts the defending colonist, so a fortified colony often beats the raid off. A brave that loses its raid is killed.

**Angry braves can also demand tribute.** A brave from a tribe that's Displeased (or worse) standing beside one of your colonies it *can't* simply storm — a colony with a soldier garrisoned, or one whose warehouse holds nothing it could carry off but you have gold or food — will instead **demand tribute**: it names some of your goods (or a sum of gold) and waits for your answer. You choose: **pay** (the goods or gold leave that colony, and the tribe is noticeably calmer for it) or **refuse** (you keep everything, but the warriors are still at your gate — a refused brave may simply raid you next turn). If you ignore the demand and end your turn, it counts as a refusal. What they ask for depends on how angry they are and what your colony holds — an angry tribe eyes your most valuable warehouse stack, your muskets, your tools; a colony with nothing worth taking is shaken down for gold. Paying buys you breathing room; refusing keeps your goods but courts a raid.

**Trading.** Sail a ship loaded with goods next to a coastal settlement and you can **sell your cargo** to it for gold — no European tax. Each settlement most wants three particular goods and pays a premium for them (half as much again for its favourite). Bring what they crave and you'll do far better than at the European docks. Trading is friendly: each sale lowers their alarm a little. (Buying *from* settlements, and reaching inland ones with wagon trains, come later.)

**What the player sees and does:** native settlements draw on the map (camp / village / Inca-city / Aztec-city art) with the nation's name beneath, capitals starred. **Clicking a discovered settlement opens an interaction panel** — with a colonist standing beside it you can *speak with the chief* (first contact: reveals nearby lands + a small gold gift), *learn its skill* (a free colonist trains into the settlement's expert), or *attack it*. Each option appears only when it's allowed; if you have no unit beside the settlement, the panel just shows the settlement's mood and what it teaches.

## 2. Detailed rules

**The eight nations** (settlement family · spread · temperament):

| Nation | Settles as | Number of settlements | Aggression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apache | camp | average | high |
| Sioux | camp | average | average |
| Tupi | camp | high | low |
| Arawak | village | low | high |
| Cherokee | village | average | low |
| Iroquois | village | average | average |
| Inca | city | low | low |
| Aztec | city | high* | high |

\* Aztec `number-of-settlements` is `low` in the classic spec (inherited from the abstract `city` type); only the city *settlement* art/size differs from the Inca. (Aztec are simply the aggressive city nation.)

**Settlement types** (pinned from the spec):

| Type | Size (min–max) | Capital size | Claimable radius | Defence bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camp | 4–6 | 6–8 | 1 (+2) | +50% (capital +100%) |
| Village | 6–8 | 8–10 | 1 (+2) | +50% (capital +100%) |
| City (Inca/Aztec) | 8–12 | 10–12 | 2/3 (+2/3) | +100% (capital +200%) |

- Every placed nation gets exactly **one capital**; the rest are non-capital settlements.
- **Per-nation count** (a tuned simplification — see deviations): low band → 1 settlement, average → 2, high → 3. Capitals are placed first (all nations) so every nation appears even when space is tight.
- A settlement tile must be **settleable land** (not water, not mountains/arctic) with **at least half its neighbours land** (no settlements on thin spits/islets).
- Settlements are at least **3 tiles apart** (Chebyshev) and at least 3 tiles from the player's landing tile and its neighbours (so the nearest a settlement can be to the start is 4 tiles — outside the starting colonist's sight).
- Each settlement's **size** is a random value in its type's range; its **taught skill** is a weighted random pick from the nation's skill list (or none if the nation lists no skills).

**Alarm bands** (FreeCol `Tension.Level`, `Tension.java`) — alarm is 0–1000:

| Band | Alarm | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Happy | 0–100 | friendly |
| Content | 101–600 | tolerant |
| Displeased | 601–700 | wary |
| Angry | 701–800 | won't teach its skill |
| Hateful | 801–1000 | no gifts; dangerous |

- Settlements start at alarm **0** (Happy). Each turn alarm cools toward 0 by `value/100 + 4` (FreeCol `ServerPlayer` decay). `Game.ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, delta)` (clamped 0–1000) is the public alarm mutator. Combat now calls it: attacking a brave adds **+200** (`TensionAddNormal`) to its nearest settlement, and **+400** more (`TensionAddUnitDestroyed`) if the brave is killed — see [combat](combat.md). (Nation-level tension and land-taking deltas are later slices; the `TensionAdd*` constants live on `NativeSettlement`.)
- **Ambient alarm — natives resent encroachment.** Each turn (just before the decay), `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` raises a settlement's alarm by the human's nearby footprint (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csNewTurn`): within **settlement-radius + 2** tiles, every human **colony** adds **2 + its population** and every human **offensive land unit** adds its (type) offence. So expanding your colonies and massing troops near a tribe steadily angers it, while the decay pulls back — alarm settles at a level proportional to how much you crowd them (equilibrium ≈ `100 × (pressure − 4)`, capped at 1000). The gain is damped by **Pocahontas**'s −50% (below). RNG-free and human-directed (foreign powers exert no native pressure in our model). *(Simplifications: no tile-ownership/control pressure and no missionary calming, neither of which we model.)*
- **Pocahontas** (Founding Father) calms the natives — see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md). On her election, **every settlement's alarm toward the human resets to 0 (Happy)** (`ResetAllNativeAlarm`, FreeCol `model.event.resetNativeAlarm`); thereafter the per-turn **ambient** alarm gain is **halved** (`ScaleNativeAlarmGain` folds her −50% `nativeAlarmModifier` into `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` — gains only; goodwill/decay are not slowed). This is the modifier's **faithful home** (FreeCol damps the ambient proximity alarm, not combat — combat tension is applied raw). Both are human-gated (alarm is toward the human) and ride on the persisted Congress. *Deviation:* the reset clears **all** settlements (FreeCol clears only contacted ones) and substitutes alarm-to-0 for FreeCol's native-nation `Stance.PEACE` (we have no native-nation stance).
- **Braves** (`Game.New`): each settlement is garrisoned by one native-owned `model.unit.brave` placed on a free land tile adjacent to it (deterministic, no RNG draw — the placement stream is unaffected). Braves are full `Unit`s (`OwnerNationId` = the settlement's nation, unarmed default role) so they round-trip through the save and can be the strongest defender on their tile. They are **excluded from the player's fog/visibility** (a brave never reveals tiles for the player) and cannot be selected or commanded by the player.
- **Native AI — raids & wandering** (slice 1b, `RunNativeTurn`): each turn, every native unit takes **one** action, in stable by-id order. A brave is **hostile** when its **home settlement** (the nearest surviving settlement of its own nation) is at alarm band **Displeased or worse** (alarm **> 600**) — FreeCol's seek-and-destroy gate (`NativeAIPlayer.secureIndianSettlement`: a settlement sends braves hunting only when its tension toward the player is *above* Content). A hostile brave first **pillages** an adjacent undefended pillageable human colony (`AdjacentPillageableHumanColony` → `PillageColony`, the high-value target — below); else, if it's beside a human colony it can't storm but can shake down (`AdjacentDemandableHumanColony`, and no demand already pending), it raises a **tribute demand** (below); otherwise it finds the **nearest human-owned unit** and, if adjacent, **attacks** it, else **steps one tile toward** it. A non-hostile brave (or a hostile one with no target) **wanders** one random legal tile. A brave with no movement left, or whose nation has lost all its settlements, idles/wanders. Every draw — the wander pick, the path tiebreak, the combat resolution, the pillage loot pick — comes from the **nation's own RNG stream** (`RandomFor(player)`), never the human's stream 0, so the human's seeded game is byte-stable however much the natives do (ADR-009). Combat runs through the internal `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` overload (see [combat](combat.md)); a raid records a transient `CombatNotice` (cleared each turn, never saved) that the UI surfaces to the player.
- **Native colony pillage** (`CheckPillageColony`/`PillageColony`, FreeCol `csPillageColony` / the `PILLAGE_COLONY` combat effect): a hostile brave adjacent to an **undefended** (no unit on its tile), **pillageable** human colony storms it (a transient unarmed-colonist defender, defence 1, on the nation's own stream). On a brave **win** it carries off `min(amount/2, 50)` of one **uniformly-chosen** lootable goods stack (`Colony.AddGoods(−take)`; loot-pick from the nation's stream) and a transient `ColonyRaidNotice` is recorded; the colony is **not** captured or destroyed (natives can't take colonies). On a **loss** the brave is slain (dispose-on-combat-loss). The gate is FreeCol `Colony.canBePillaged` (no stockade, the brave's `model.ability.pillageUnprotectedColony`, unprotected, something to loot). **Faithful subset:** we model the **goods-loot** outcome only — see the deviations below.
- **Native tribute demands** (`CreateNativeDemand`/`SelectDemand`/`AcceptPendingDemand`/`RefusePendingDemand`, FreeCol `IndianDemandMission` + `ServerPlayer.csCompleteNativeDemand`): a hostile brave adjacent to a human colony it **can't** pillage-as-a-step (defended, or holding only gold/food rather than a storable stack to grab — the case the pillage branch above leaves open) raises a **tribute demand** instead, ending its turn. The single pending demand is stored on `Game.PendingDemand` (transient — never saved, like the notice channels) and the human answers it next turn: **accept** transfers the demanded goods/gold out of the colony (consumed — capped at what the colony holds, never a negative balance) and **cools the nation's alarm by 150 across all its settlements** (FreeCol `-(5 - difficulty) × 50` at medium); **refuse** clears it with **no** transfer and **no** tension change (FreeCol changes tension only on accept — the refusal's bite is the brave's normal next-turn pillage/raid via the existing AI). An unanswered demand is **auto-refused** at the top of the next `EndTurn` (FreeCol's session timeout = reject). **What is demanded** (`SelectDemand`, FreeCol `selectGoods`, picked deterministically at the nation's alarm band): Content-or-calmer + enough food → that food at the cutoff; Displeased-or-calmer → the **priciest** non-food, non-military **storable** stack (by market *stack* value = amount × unit price); else the first present good in the order **military → building-material → trade → refined**; else the priciest storable stack; else **gold** = `gold/20` (or all the gold, or nothing if the human is broke). The amount is `clamp(count × dx / 6, 30, 100)` with `dx = nativeDemands + 1 = 3` at the default medium difficulty. **Faithful subset / deviations below.**
- **Speak with chief** (`Visit`): a person unit next to a settlement that hasn't been visited reveals the lands within `TalesRevealRadius` (3) of it and, unless the settlement is Hateful, gives a gold gift of **10–80** (FreeCol `GIFT_MINIMUM/MAXIMUM`). Once only; ends the unit's turn.
- **Learn skill** (`LearnSkill`): a **free colonist or indentured servant** next to a settlement whose skill isn't yet consumed and whose alarm is below Angry is taught the settlement's `LearnableSkill` expert type (the unit is replaced by one of that type, keeping its id). The skill is then consumed — **except at a capital**, which teaches indefinitely. Experts and petty criminals cannot learn.
- **Trade** (`SellToNatives`): a ship next to a settlement whose alarm is below Angry sells goods from its hold for gold (no European tax). Each settlement has up to **3 wanted goods**; the price it pays per unit is `(12 + trade-bonus) × wanted-premium`, where the premium is **150 / 125 / 110%** for its 1st / 2nd / 3rd wanted good (else 100%), with a ~10% markup on top (FreeCol `getPriceToSell`). `trade-bonus` is the settlement type's (camp 1 → city 4). A successful sale lowers the settlement's alarm; trading ends the ship's turn.

**Deviations from original 1994 / FreeCol behavior:**
- **Native AI is a flat priority switch, not FreeCol's mission planner.** FreeCol assigns braves typed missions (`UnitSeekAndDestroyMission`, `UnitWanderHostileMission`, `IndianDemandMission`, `IndianBringGiftMission`) scored over targets. We collapse this to: hostile → hunt the nearest human unit; else wander. Deliberate simplifications: (a) **per-settlement alarm stands in for nation-level tension** — FreeCol's attack gate reads a player-level `Tension`; we read the brave's nearest same-nation settlement's `Alarm` (we keep no nation-level native tension store). (b) **one action per brave per turn** (one step *or* one attack/pillage), matching the foreign-power AI's shape, vs FreeCol moving a unit its full allowance. (c) **target choice is the nearest human unit / adjacent colony (omniscient)** — no fog-limited or scored target selection; bounded because braves move one tile and only hunt when their own territory was provoked. (d) **Demanding tribute** (`IndianDemandMission`) **is now modelled** (see the tribute-demand rule above and its deviations below); still **deferred:** bringing gifts and braves re-equipping from settlement stock. Braves raid **human units and colonies only** (never settlements — the `CheckAttackSettlement` native gate stays closed — never foreign powers or rival tribes; the AI's target filter enforces this beyond the engine's owner-inequality rule).
- **Native colony pillage models the goods-loot outcome only.** FreeCol's `csPillageColony` picks one target *uniformly* from {burnable buildings, ships on the colony tile, lootable goods, +gold}; we pick uniformly among the colony's lootable **goods** stacks only — the **storable** stored goods (FreeCol `getLootableGoodsList`; hammers/bells/crosses are `storable="false"` and excluded). The looted goods are **destroyed**, not carried off — the brave gains nothing (FreeCol does `attacker.add(goods)`; we have no native goods-hauling/settlement-restock model). **Deferred:** stealing **gold** (FreeCol `max(1, colony.getPlunder/5)` — shares the deferred colony-capture plunder formula `(gold×(units+1))/(coloniesPop+1)`), **damaging a building** (no building-destruction model), **sinking/damaging ships** caught in the colony, the **great-win → colonist-kill / colony-destroy** path (we pillage on **any** native win, so a native great win is *gentler* than FreeCol's — a tribe never destroys a colony here), and the attacker's **tension easing** (`attackerTension −= NORMAL` — we keep no nation-level native tension store). Braves pillage the **human only** (FreeCol pillages any European colony) — the same sole-target contract as the unit raid.
- **Native tribute demands model the human-facing economics; native-side bookkeeping is abstracted.** Faithful: which goods/gold is demanded and how much (`selectGoods`/`capAmount`), the accept transfer + the alarm relief, refuse = no tension change, and the spec-order pick within each category rung. Documented deviations: (a) the paid tribute is **consumed**, not hauled to the settlement or added to a native store (we have no native goods/treasury model — same abstraction as pillage). (b) Demand **candidates are restricted to storable goods** (the pillage loot filter) — our model never demands the non-warehoused hammers/bells/crosses. (c) Pillage takes priority over demand (FreeCol scores them as separate missions); a demand fires only on the colony the pillage branch leaves open. (d) The selection's **first branch (food when the demanding nation is only Content-or-calmer) is unreachable** under our single per-settlement-alarm channel, because we only raise demands at Displeased+ — it is implemented and unit-tested for fidelity and would activate if a separate nation-level tension store is ever added. (e) **One human-facing demand per ring** (first brave wins; a second eligible brave falls through to its normal AI) — a blocking modal answers one at a time. (f) `nativeDemands` difficulty is the fixed medium default (2); making it a real difficulty option is ADR-018 ruleset-constant debt (`86d3bb1x3`). (g) The refuse-escalation is the brave's **existing** next-turn pillage/raid AI, not a dedicated attack; if the brave dies before the human answers, there is simply no specific escalation. Braves demand of the **human only**. (h) **The building-material rung omits food.** FreeCol derives `isBuildingMaterial` over *all* buildables (buildings + units + roles), so the freeColonist's 200-food growth cost makes **food** a building material — and under Angry/Hateful, where the priciest-non-military branch is skipped, FreeCol commonly demands food via this rung. We derive `BuildingMaterials` from building build-costs only (hammers + tools; we don't model unit/role required-goods), so the rung never matches food and we instead demand the priciest *valued* storable stack (food has no market value, so a trade/raw good wins; a colony holding **only** food still gets a food demand via the priciest fallback). A faithful food-demand is a follow-up. (i) **Accept alarm relief is flat (−150 on every settlement of the nation).** FreeCol lands −150 on the home settlement and a halved −75 on the rest (via player-level tension); we apply the full −150 nation-wide — the same single-alarm flattening `ApplyNativeCombatTension` already uses.
- **Trade is sell-only and coastal-only (first cut).** FreeCol models a full session (buy / sell / gift, haggling up to 3×, settlement goods stock and production that drive prices and what they'll sell). We implement selling cargo to a settlement; **buying from settlements** needs a settlement goods-stock/production model (deferred), and **inland settlements** need wagon trains (deferred) — only coastal settlements are reachable by ship today. Wanted goods are 3 random tradeable goods fixed at placement (FreeCol re-picks the top-3 by buy-price each turn from settlement stock, which we don't model). Pricing assumes the settlement still wants the good (no stock-fill price decay). No haggling. Alarm-reduction-per-sale approximates FreeCol's `ALARM_BONUS_SELL` game option.
- **Speak-with-chief is simplified.** FreeCol's full scout chief-speak has weighted outcomes (larger "beads" from the settlement-type `<gifts>` RandomRange, a chance to learn for free, and death at a hateful settlement) and requires the scout role. We give a flat 10–80 gift + a tales reveal on first contact for any colonist, no scout role, no death — the scout-specific behaviour is a later slice. No alarm change occurs on speaking (matches FreeCol).
- **Learn-skill gating.** FreeCol lets you *attempt* a learn at an angry/hateful settlement (the unit learns nothing, or dies). We gate it in the `Check` (Angry+ is refused) — no random death yet. The learnable-colonist set (free colonist / indentured servant) is hard-coded pending FreeCol `unit-change-types` (NATIVES) data.
- **Settlement counts & placement regions.** FreeCol assigns each nation a named map *region* and scales settlement counts by landmass and difficulty. Our map has no named regions yet, so we place settlements greedily on suitable tiles with a fixed per-band count (`NativeSettlementGenerator.TargetCount`). The *suitability* rule (settleable + ≥50% land neighbourhood) and the *capital-first* rule are kept. Counts/regions can be tuned toward FreeCol when the map gains regions.
- **Aggression / skills are parsed but not yet used.** They feed the tension and learn-skill slices (P5 slices 3–4); stored now so the data layer is complete.
- **`<plunder>` / `<gifts>` not parsed yet.** Combat plunder and gift-giving arrive with their slices; only the settlement's core attributes + defence modifier are parsed today.

## 3. Technical design

**Domain model:**
- `Specification.NativeNationType` (record) — one per concrete nation: its non-capital + capital `SettlementType` ids, `SettlementNumber` band, `NativeAggression`, the weighted `NativeSkill` list, and preferred region ids. Abstract templates (`default`/`camp`/`village`/`city`) are resolved away, not exposed.
- `Specification.SettlementType` (record) — a camp/village/city template (capital variant is a separate record): sizes, claimable radii, trade bonus, convert threshold, and the parsed `model.modifier.defence` percentage (used by the combat slice).
- `Natives.NativeSettlement` — a placed settlement: id, owning `NationTypeId`, `SettlementTypeId`, `IsCapital`, `Position`, `Size`, `LearnableSkill`, plus interaction state `Alarm` (0–1000) / `AlarmLevel` (computed band) / `HasBeenVisited` / `SkillConsumed` / `WantedGoods` (up to 3) / `WantedSlot` (all internal-set). (A minimal "owner" concept — the full multi-player `Player`/`Nation` refactor is deferred to the foreign-European slice.)
- `Natives.AlarmLevel` (enum Happy→Hateful) — the hostility band, mapped from `Alarm` by the FreeCol `Tension.Level` limits.

**Data sources:** `specification.xml` → `<indian-nation-types>` (parsed in `Ruleset.ParseNativeNationTypes`). `extends` chains are resolved nearest-wins for attributes and the settlement template; **skills and regions accumulate** down the chain (so the Inca inherit the abstract `city` skills and add their own). A null section (minimal test rulesets) yields no native nations.

**Algorithms & formulas:** `World/NativeSettlementGenerator.Place(ruleset, map, random, excluded)` — suitable tiles are gathered and Fisher–Yates-shuffled (seeded), capitals placed first, then non-capitals round-robin; `TakeTile` enforces the min-distance against placed settlements and the excluded set. Sizes/skills drawn from the RNG.

**Integration points:** generated once in `Game.New` on a **dedicated RNG stream** (`NativeStreamId = 1`, ADR-009) so placement cannot shift the economy/father/immigration draws (stream 0). Exposed via `Game.NativeSettlements` and `Game.NativeSettlementAt(pos)`. Rendering: `GameController.SyncNativeMarkers` makes one `NativeSettlementMarker` per settlement **on an explored tile** (fog-gated), in the scene's `MapView/NativeLayer`; art selected per settlement type (FreeCol `indian_camp` / `indian_village` / `inca_city` / `aztec_city`, ADR-014).

**Interaction flow** (`Game.cs`): `CheckVisit`/`Visit`, `CheckLearnSkill`/`LearnSkill`, and `CheckSellToNatives`/`SellToNatives` (+ `NativeSalePrice`) follow the standard `Check…`-oracle pattern (ADR-006); `Visit` uses the main saved RNG (`_random`) for the gift roll (so save/resume stays deterministic — no extra stream needed). Learning replaces the unit via `UpgradeUnitType` (keeps id/position/location/cargo, since `Unit.Type` is immutable). Selling credits gold with no tax and lowers alarm. `Game.ChangeNativeAlarm` is the public alarm mutator; `DecayNativeAlarm` runs for every settlement in `EndTurn` (see [turns](turns.md)). Wanted goods are assigned in a post-placement pass in `NativeSettlementGenerator` (after the placement RNG draws, so placement output is unchanged).

**Native AI** (`Game.cs`, slice 1b): `RunPlayerTurn` dispatches a `PlayerType.Native` player to `RunNativeTurn(player)` (the colonial/foreign path is unchanged). `RunNativeTurn` iterates `_units.Where(u => u.OwnerNationId == player.NationId).OrderBy(u => u.Id)` over a `.ToList()` snapshot (a raid can remove the prey or the brave mid-loop). Per unit: skip if off-map or out of movement; resolve `HomeSettlement` (nearest same-nation `NativeSettlement`, Chebyshev + position tiebreak) and compare `home.AlarmLevel >= RaidAlarmThreshold` (`AlarmLevel.Displeased`); if hostile and `AdjacentPillageableHumanColony` (a human colony where `CheckPillageColony` allows — adjacent, undefended, lootable goods) exists, `PillageColony` it (the high-value branch, before the unit-hunt); else if hostile, no demand is already pending, and `AdjacentDemandableHumanColony(player, brave)` (a human colony adjacent to the brave for which `SelectDemand` finds something to take) exists, `CreateNativeDemand` against it; else if `NearestHumanUnit` (nearest on-map `IsHumanOwned` unit) exists, attack when adjacent (via `RaidHumanUnit`) else `StepToward` it; otherwise `Wander` one legal neighbour. `StepToward(player, unit, goal)` is the shared pathing helper (factored out of `StepTowardNearestUnexplored`): the legal adjacent move minimising Chebyshev to the goal, ties broken by position then `RandomFor(player)`. `RaidHumanUnit` calls the internal `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` overload (the nation's own stream) and appends a `CombatNotice` to the transient `_combatNotices`. `PillageColony(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` resolves combat vs a transient unarmed-colonist defender, on a win steals `min(amount/2, 50)` of one uniformly-picked lootable goods stack and appends a `ColonyRaidNotice` (raw attacker-nation id, colony name, goods id, amount, position) to the transient `_colonyRaidNotices` (skipped if the picked 1-unit stack yields 0); on a loss `ResolveLoserOutcome` slays the brave. The owning native player resolves by `NationId == OwnerNationId` (a brave's `OwnerId` stays 0/unused). **Tribute demands** (`CreateNativeDemand`/`SelectDemand`/`AcceptPendingDemand`/`RefusePendingDemand`): `CreateNativeDemand(nation, brave, colony)` runs `SelectDemand(colony, homeAlarmLevel)` (the FreeCol `selectGoods` ladder — food-at-cutoff / priciest-non-food-non-military storable stack / military→building→trade→refined / priciest / `gold/20`→all→empty; amount `Math.Clamp(count × dx / 6, 30, 100)` with `dx = NativeDemandsDx = 3`), and on a non-null result ends the brave's turn and stores the single transient `NativeDemand` on `_pendingDemand` (no-op if one is already pending). `AcceptPendingDemand` re-looks-up the colony by id (no-op false if it's gone or no longer human-owned), transfers `min(demand, held)` goods (`Colony.AddGoods(−…)`) or `min(demand, gold)` gold off the human, then `ChangeNativeAlarm(s, −NativeDemandAcceptAlarmRelief)` (= −150) across every settlement of the demanding nation; `RefusePendingDemand` just clears it. `EndTurn` calls `RefusePendingDemand()` at the top (auto-refuse-if-ignored) beside the notice-channel clears. **Determinism:** no native path touches `_random` — and the **tribute path draws no randomness at all** (`SelectDemand`/`CreateNativeDemand`/accept/refuse are RNG-free; `Market.BidPrice` is a pure read) — so demands never perturb the human's stream 0; `_pendingDemand` is **not** serialized (save v21 unchanged). Iteration is stable by id/position — verified by `NativeAiTests` + `NativePillageTests` + `NativeDemandTests` (same-seed byte-identical; the human's stream 0 untouched by raids/pillage/demands; the 30-turn demand stream-0 test) and the L5 soak.

**Persistence:** save format **v14** added `SavedNativeSettlement[]`; **v16** added the interaction state (`Alarm`, `HasBeenVisited`, `SkillConsumed`); **v17** added `WantedGoods`. Settlements round-trip verbatim (placement is not replayed, so the native RNG stream is not saved). Pre-v14 saves load with no native settlements; pre-v16 settlements load peaceful/unvisited; pre-v17 with no wanted goods.
