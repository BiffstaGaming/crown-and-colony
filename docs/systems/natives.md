## 1. How it works (plain English)

The New World is already inhabited. When a game begins, the indigenous nations — the Apache, Sioux, Tupi, Arawak, Cherokee, Iroquois, Inca and Aztec — already have settlements dotted across the map. You discover them as you explore; a settlement you have never seen stays hidden under the fog.

**The rules, in plain words:**
- There are **eight** native nations. Three live in **camps** (Apache, Sioux, Tupi), three in **villages** (Arawak, Cherokee, Iroquois), and two in great **cities** (Inca, Aztec).
- Each nation has **one capital** (bigger, better defended) plus a few ordinary settlements. Some nations spread more widely than others.
- Settlements sit on land, never crowd each other, and never spawn right on top of your landing site — you always get room to settle.
- Each settlement quietly knows a **skill** it could teach a visiting colonist (e.g. expert farmer) — visiting, trading and learning come in later slices.

**Worked example:**
> You sail in and found your first colony. A few turns later your scout walks north-east and the fog peels back to reveal an Iroquois village — a cluster of longhouses with "Iroquois" on its name plate. Press on and you might find their capital, marked with a ★.

**Interacting with a settlement.** Move a colonist next to a settlement and you can **speak with its chief**: the first time, they tell tales of nearby lands (revealing the map around the settlement) and usually hand over a small gold gift. (First contact is **per nation** — each European power, you included, gets its own one-time audience with a given chief; a rival visiting "their" first doesn't use up yours. The **foreign powers now do this too** as they explore.) A plain colonist (a free colonist or indentured servant) can also **learn the settlement's skill** — it is taught the settlement's expert profession (e.g. an expert farmer), a free shortcut to a specialist. An ordinary settlement teaches its one skill just once; a nation's **capital** teaches forever.

Each settlement also has an **alarm** level toward you — Happy, Content, Displeased, Angry, or Hateful. It starts peaceful and cools over time, but hostile acts raise it: **attacking one of its braves** adds a lot of alarm, and **killing the brave** adds a lot more. An **angry** settlement won't teach you; a **hateful** one gives no gifts and is dangerous to be near. Some nations are **naturally better with the natives**: the **French** anger them only **half as fast** — a permanent national trait (like a built-in half-Pocahontas), so a French player can crowd a tribe far longer before it turns hostile.

**A tribe judges each colonial power separately.** A settlement doesn't hold one mood for "the Europeans" — it keeps a **separate alarm** toward *you* and toward *each rival power*. So the Cherokee can be your firm friends while seething at the Spanish next door: trade with them and *your* standing rises, while a Spanish massacre nearby drives up *Spanish* alarm without touching yours. Each power's alarm rises and cools on its own. A settlement also remembers **who it hates most** — the rival power it's angriest at (ignoring anyone it's still happy with) — which matters when you eventually declare independence (the tribe you've wronged most can be swung behind your rebellion). In a one-power game (just you), this behaves exactly as a single alarm always did. *(Today the native war-party AI still only marches on the human's colonies — natives don't yet attack rival powers' colonies — so a rival's alarm is tracked and remembered but doesn't yet send braves at that rival. The groundwork is laid for that and for foreign-power native relations.)*

**The whole tribe has a mood too.** Beyond each settlement's own alarm, a nation now also carries a **tribe-wide tension** toward each power — the mood of the nation as a whole, not just one camp. Anger one of their settlements and a share of that anger **ripples out to the whole tribe**; a deal like paying a chief to make war on a rival turns the **entire nation** against that power at a stroke. This tribe-wide mood cools over time on its own, the same way a single camp's alarm does. The Native Affairs Advisor's per-nation heading reads this tribe-wide figure, so the heading reflects the nation's true overall standing rather than just its angriest known village.

**The land is theirs.** Each native settlement **owns the land around it** — the tiles within its reach belong to that nation (a camp or village claims the ring one tile out; an Inca or Aztec city claims two). This is the natives' territory: stepping onto it, settling on it, or digging up what lies beneath it are things the tribe notices. For now this ownership is what tells the game whose land a tile is — it powers the **burial-ground** danger when you investigate a ruin on sacred ground. Their territory isn't drawn on the map yet (that's a later visual touch), and it shifts only when settlements come and go.

**Buying — or taking — native land.** A tile a tribe owns can be made yours in one of two ways: **pay for it** (a gold price the tribe sets, based on how productive the land is) or **take it by force** (free, but it angers that nation — every one of their settlements grows much more hostile toward you). The price is roughly *60 × the land's yearly trade-goods output, plus 100* — so a rich tile costs more than a barren one. If you've recruited **Peter Minuit**, native land is simply **free** to you, with no anger. Once bought or taken, a tile is yours for good — it never reverts to the natives, even if their settlements move or fall. *(Note: the natives keep no treasury in this game, so your payment just leaves your purse rather than filling theirs.)*

**You can't just walk onto their land for free.** Founding a colony **on** a native-owned tile, or putting a colonist to **work** one of your colony's surrounding tiles that the natives own, now stops and asks you the same question: **buy it, take it by force, or abandon the attempt?** You can't take their land for nothing any more — you either pay the price, or steal it and anger the tribe (the land-taken hit is *nation-wide*: every settlement of that nation grows more hostile **toward you specifically** — a rival who steals from the same tribe raises their own standing, not yours). Choosing *abandon* simply cancels the founding or the work order. The **AI** powers face the exact same gate when they expand onto native ground: they decide without hesitation — **pay if they can afford it, otherwise steal** — so an aggressive land-grab now genuinely sours a tribe against the power that did it. *(There's a "buy / steal / abandon" pop-up for you, the human; the AI's choice is automatic and always the same for a given situation.)*

**Braves.** Every settlement is guarded by a **brave** — a native warrior standing on the land next to it. Braves are the natives' fighting units (and your targets): you can attack one with an armed unit, and combat plays out by the rules in [combat](combat.md).

**Braves now act on their own.** A calm tribe's braves simply **roam** their lands a tile at a time. But anger a tribe — by attacking its people — and once a settlement turns **Displeased** (or worse) its braves leave camp to **hunt your colonists and soldiers**: they march toward a target and **attack** when they reach it. They're not mindless — a war party sizes up the people nearby and goes for the **soft, valuable** target (a lone unarmed colonist, a treasure escort) over a dug-in soldier at the same distance, rather than always the closest body. So mistreating the natives now has teeth: their warriors come for you. A beaten unarmed colonist is killed, an armed one is knocked back (and the brave may grab his muskets). When a brave attacks you, the game tells you in the status bar ("The Apache raided your Free Colonist!" / "Your Soldier fought off an Apache raid.").

**Threatened tribes arm their braves — and field native dragoons.** A settlement that turns hostile doesn't just send out bare-handed warriors: if it has **muskets or horses** in its own store, its braves **take up arms before they march**. A plain brave becomes an **armed** (musket) or **mounted** (horse) warrior; and if the camp has **both** muskets and horses it makes the strongest warrior of all — a **native dragoon** (armed *and* mounted), much tougher in a fight. A brave that's already armed (or already mounted) isn't left half-equipped either: the camp **promotes it to a full dragoon** by handing it the half it lacks — and only that half, so an armed brave costs just the horses to upgrade, not a fresh set of muskets. When equipment is **scarce**, the camp arms its **strongest braves first** (an already-armed brave is promoted to dragoon before a bare brave is armed at all), so the limited weapons go where they matter most. A settlement only does this when it's angry enough to go to war (a peaceful camp keeps its weapons in store), and only as far as its stock stretches. A tribe also **shares its arms around**: at the start of each turn a calm, well-stocked camp **ships spare muskets or horses to a frontier camp under threat that has none** — so a tribe's weapons reach the warriors who need them rather than sitting idle in a peaceful village far from the fighting, and the camp that receives them can arm its braves that very turn. **Where do the weapons come from?** Every settlement starts the game holding a modest store of muskets it keeps to arm its braves; the bigger, better settlements — capitals and large villages/cities — also keep horses, so they can field mounted braves and dragoons while small frontier camps can only arm. (This stands in for the original game's slow native manufacture of arms: rather than model a full native workshop, we simply give each camp the store it would have built up, sized by how big and important it is.) The upshot in an ordinary game: a tribe you anger enough to go to war **will** send armed braves — and dragoons from its larger camps — at you, not bare-handed warriors. Arming is entirely deterministic, and the braves of each tribe act using their own hidden dice, entirely separate from yours, so your game still plays out identically for a given starting seed.

**Hostile braves now raid your colonies too.** A brave standing next to one of your colonies that has **no defender garrisoned in it** will **storm it and carry off goods or gold** — it grabs up to half of one warehouse stack (at most 50), or, instead, raids the colony's strongbox for a slice of your gold, and runs. Unlike a European rival, a tribe never *takes* your colony or burns it down (in this slice) — it raids and leaves; the colony, its people and its buildings stay yours. You're told what was taken ("The Apache raided Jamestown and carried off 50 tobacco!" / "…made off with 120 gold!"). The fix is the same as for unit raids: keep a soldier garrisoned in any colony near an angry tribe — a defended colony can't be pillaged (the brave must beat the garrison in the field first). Even an *un*garrisoned colony resists far better behind walls: a **stockade** (or fort/fortress) boosts the defending colonist, so a fortified colony often beats the raid off. A brave that loses its raid is killed.

**Angry braves can also demand tribute.** A brave from a tribe that's Displeased (or worse) standing beside one of your colonies it *can't* simply storm — a colony with a soldier garrisoned, or one whose warehouse holds nothing it could carry off but you have gold or food — will instead **demand tribute**: it names some of your goods (or a sum of gold) and waits for your answer. You choose: **pay** (the goods or gold leave that colony, and the tribe is noticeably calmer for it) or **refuse** (you keep everything, but the warriors are still at your gate — a refused brave may simply raid you next turn). If you ignore the demand and end your turn, it counts as a refusal. What they ask for depends on how angry they are and what your colony holds — an angry tribe eyes your muskets and your **food stores** (a hungry war party demands provisions, just as in the original game), then your most valuable warehouse stack; a colony with nothing worth taking is shaken down for gold. Paying buys you breathing room; refusing keeps your goods but courts a raid.

**Trading.** Sail a ship loaded with goods next to a coastal settlement and you can trade **both ways** for gold — no European tax. You can **sell your cargo** to it: each settlement most wants three particular goods and pays a premium for them (half as much again for its favourite). Bring what they crave and you'll do far better than at the European docks — though the natives knock a fixed cut off what they'll pay a *ship* (they reckon a sea-trader is a middleman), so an overland trader would do better. You can also **buy from their store** — the wares the tribe has gathered (sugar, tobacco, cotton, furs, ore). The more of a good a settlement holds, the cheaper it sells it and the less it will pay you for more of the same — so a tribe swimming in furs sells furs cheaply but won't want yours, while a tribe short of cloth pays top dollar for cloth. That's why their three wanted goods aren't fixed: they shift as their stores rise and fall, always pointing at what they most lack. **Their store refills over time.** A settlement doesn't only get goods from your sales — between your visits its people **gather more of the raw goods their land yields** (sugar, tobacco, cotton, furs, ore), so a camp you bought out last visit will have built its stock back up by the time you return. A good it already has plenty of grows slowly; a good it's short of refills faster — so over a game a settlement is a renewing source to trade with, not a one-off. Every sale or purchase is also a chance to **haggle** — name a price and the chief either takes it, counters with his own, or (the longer you push your luck) loses patience and breaks off the deal. **And the haggle now sticks:** when the chief accepts your price, the deal closes at *that* figure — push a sale up and you genuinely pocket the extra; talk a purchase down and you genuinely pay less. Trading is friendly: each exchange lowers their alarm a little. (Reaching inland settlements with wagon trains comes later — only coastal settlements are reachable by ship today.)

**Gifts.** Stay on good terms and the natives repay it: a brave from a **happy** tribe standing beside one of your colonies will now, now and then, **leave a parcel of goods** in your warehouse — a free delivery, no strings attached. The gift is **real goods from the tribe's own store**: they only give away something they have a genuine surplus of, and the parcel (anywhere from 10 to 80 units) is **taken out of their stock**, so a tribe can't conjure endless gifts from nothing — once they've given, they need to gather more before they can give again. There's also a **cooldown**: a settlement that has just sent a gift won't send another for a while, so windfalls arrive every so often rather than every turn. It's the friendly mirror of a raid: keep a tribe content (trade with them, don't crowd or attack them) and your frontier colonies pick up the occasional windfall.

**Missionaries.** A colonist dressed as a **missionary** (or a trained **Jesuit**) can move onto a native settlement and **establish a mission** there. If the tribe is **Displeased or calmer**, your missionary moves in and lives among them — the tribe warms to you a little, the land around the settlement is revealed, and from then on the mission **slowly wins converts**: each turn it builds up goodwill (faster with a Jesuit, and faster the more wary the tribe is), and when enough has built up a **brave converts** — it leaves the settlement and walks into your **nearest colony** (within 10 tiles) as a free **Indian Convert** worker. But if the tribe is already **Angry or Hateful**, they **kill** your missionary instead — so don't preach to a tribe that hates you. There's only ever one mission per settlement — and if a **rival European** already has a missionary there, yours can't just walk in and replace it: it must **denounce** the rival's mission first. Denouncing is a gamble that turns on faith and numbers — the more colonists *you've* drawn to the New World versus your rival, and the holier your missionary (a Jesuit denounces more readily; a Jesuit *defender* is harder to oust) — and it can go either way. Win and you **expel the rival and take over** the mission; lose and **your** missionary is thrown out for nothing. (And even a winning denounce can't survive a Hateful tribe — they'll kill your missionary the moment he steps in, rival or no rival.) *To do it: select your missionary, click the native settlement to open its panel, and press **Establish mission** — or, when a rival European already holds the mission there, **Denounce mission** instead. The panel tells you what happened (the mission was founded, the tribe killed your missionary, the rival was ousted, or your denunciation failed).*

**Inciting a tribe against a rival.** A missionary can do more than preach — standing at a settlement, he can **pay the chief to make war on a rival European power**. Pick the rival you want the tribe to attack, and the chief names his price in gold: how much depends on how the tribe feels about *you* versus about *them*. If the tribe already hates that rival, the bribe is cheap (down to a floor of 650 gold); if the tribe likes the rival but is cool toward you, it's dear (10,000 gold and up). Pay it and the whole tribe turns on that power — every one of their settlements becomes war-level hostile toward the rival, so their braves will march on the rival's colonies, not yours. The rival also learns who put the natives up to it, and resents you for it. (As with everything native, no gold actually fills a native treasury — it just leaves your purse.) *To do it: select your missionary, click the settlement, press **Incite against a rival**, then pick the power to set them on.*

**Demanding tribute from a tribe.** An **armed** unit (a soldier, dragoon or artillery) standing beside a settlement can **shake the chief down for gold** instead of attacking. A calm tribe pays up (a friendly one pays the most, a wary one half as much), but an angry or hateful tribe refuses outright. Either way it's an insult — the demand raises the tribe's alarm toward you — and a tribe you've just shaken down won't be milked again for a few turns. *To do it: select an armed unit beside the settlement, click it, and press **Demand tribute**.*

**What the player sees and does:** native settlements draw on the map (camp / village / Inca-city / Aztec-city art) with the nation's name beneath, capitals starred. **Clicking a discovered settlement opens an interaction panel** — with a colonist standing beside it you can *speak with the chief* (first contact: reveals nearby lands + a small gold gift), *learn its skill* (a free colonist trains into the settlement's expert), or *attack it*. **With a ship beside (or on) a coastal settlement you can also trade**: a *Sell goods* button lists what your ship is carrying — pick a good and the chief names what he'll pay; a *Buy goods* button lists what the settlement has gathered — pick a lot and he names his asking price. Either way you can *accept* the price on the spot or *haggle* once more (ask a little more / offer a little less) — the chief takes it, names a new figure, or loses patience and breaks off the deal. Each option appears only when it's allowed (Sell only when your ship carries goods, Buy only when it has free hold space and the tribe has wares it'll part with, and neither when the tribe is too hostile); if you have no unit beside the settlement, the panel just shows the settlement's mood and what it teaches. A refused trade never crashes — the reason shows in the status bar.

**The Native Affairs Advisor (the empire-reports "Natives" tab).** The reports screen has a **Natives** tab that lists every settlement you have discovered, **grouped by the nation that owns it**. Each nation gets a heading — its name, how many of its settlements you've found, and the tribe's overall mood toward you (its **tribe-wide tension** band, e.g. *Displeased*, plus a one-word stance: *at peace*, *uneasy*, or *at war*) — and underneath it, a row per settlement showing that camp's alarm toward you, its teachable skill, any mission, what it wants to trade for, and the **colonial nation it most hates** (the rival power it's angriest at, or "—" if it's calm toward everyone). This mirrors the original game's Native Affairs Advisor: at a glance you see which tribes are turning against you and which rival you can hope to turn a wronged tribe against. *(The heading now reads the nation's **tribe-wide tension** channel — the mood of the whole tribe — rather than just its angriest known settlement; the per-settlement rows still show each camp's own alarm. The count is "settlements you've discovered" — we have no spy-report total of a tribe's full settlement count.)*

| `Game.NativeDemandMin / NativeDemandMax` | 30 / 100 | 30 / 100 | FreeCol `IndianDemandMission.GOODS_DEMAND_MIN` / `GoodsContainer.CARGO_SIZE` | ✅ |

**Why the two mismatches mattered:** both feed the won-settlement-assault path on a mission settlement (`AttackSettlement`): `NativeConvertProbability` was the capture-convert base chance (too high at 50% vs the spec's 30%), and `NativeBurnProbabilityPercent` was the burn-missions chance (too low at 2% vs the spec's 6%). The fix changes only the *probability constants*; the default game stays byte-identical (the soak's free-colonist AI never staffs missionaries, so neither path fires — ADR-009 verified). FreeCol reads both options directly as a percentage (`0.01f * spec.getInteger(...)`), and our constants are used identically (`percent/100.0`), so the integer option *is* the percentage.

**Not directly comparable (intentional abstractions, already documented as deviations):** the per-sale alarm relief (`price/50`, an approximation of the `alarmBonusSell` 20% game option — there is no exact hardcoded constant); the bring-gift parcel (`NativeGiftAmount` 25 / `NativeGiftChanceDenominator` 8 — our no-native-goods-store simplification of FreeCol's stock-driven `getRandomGift`); `BraveEquipGoods` 25 (the role goods requirement, a ruleset value not a difficulty option). These were reviewed and left as-is — they are not faithful re-statements of a single spec value.

**Settlement types** (pinned from the spec):

| Type | Size (min–max) | Capital size | Claimable radius | Defence bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camp | 4–6 | 6–8 | 1 (+2) | +50% (capital +100%) |
| Village | 6–8 | 8–10 | 1 (+2) | +50% (capital +100%) |
| City (Inca/Aztec) | 8–12 | 10–12 | 2/3 (+2/3) | +100% (capital +200%) |

- Every placed nation gets exactly **one capital**; the rest are non-capital settlements.
- **Per-nation count** (a tuned simplification — see deviations): low band → 1 settlement, average → 2, high → 3. Capitals are placed first (all nations) so every nation appears even when space is tight.
- A settlement tile must be **settleable land** (not water, not mountains/arctic) with **at least half its neighbours land** (no settlements on thin spits/islets).
- Settlements are at least **3 tiles apart** (Chebyshev) and at least 3 tiles from the player's landing tile and its neighbours (so the nearest a settlement can be to the start is 4 tiles — outside the starting colonist's sight).
- Each settlement's **size** is a random value in its type's range; its **taught skill** is a weighted random pick from the nation's skill list (or none if the nation lists no skills).

**Native land ownership** (FreeCol `Tile.owner` / `Settlement` claim):
- Each settlement **owns the land tiles within its `ClaimableRadius`** (Chebyshev): camps/villages **1** (the 3×3 around it), Inca/Aztec cities **2**, including the settlement's own tile. Only **dry land**, **in-bounds** tiles are claimed (no water, no off-map).
- Where two settlements' claims **overlap**, the tile goes to the **nearest** settlement; an exact tie goes to the **lower settlement id** (deterministic).
- A tile's owner is the owning **nation** (e.g. `model.nationType.apache`). Queryable via `Map.IsNativeOwned(p)` / `Map.NativeOwnerOf(p)`.
- Ownership is **derived**, not saved: it is a pure function of settlement positions + radii, recomputed at game start **and on load**, so it never needs a save field and round-trips byte-identically. (When land purchase lands, a *bought/cleared* tile becomes a saved override on top of this baseline.)
- FreeCol's wider **`ExtraClaimableRadius`** (further tiles a tribe will only cede under pressure / by purchase) is parsed but **not modelled** here — only the base owned radius is.

**Native land purchase** (FreeCol `Player.getLandPrice` / `ServerPlayer.csClaimLand`):
- **Price** = `landPriceFactor × Σ(tile potential yield of every good except the primary-food aggregate) + 100`, then the buyer's `landPaymentModifier`. `landPriceFactor` comes from the difficulty level (`Ruleset.Difficulty.LandPriceFactor`; 40/50/60/70/80 by level, default medium **60**) — see [difficulty](difficulty.md). Only the **`model.goods.food` aggregate** is excluded (FreeCol's `gt != getPrimaryFoodType()`); **grain and fish ARE counted** — farmland and fisheries are genuinely worth paying for (terrain carries no direct `model.goods.food` potential, so the exclusion only ever drops the aggregate itself). The base **+100** means a native tile always costs ≥ 100. *(Fixed `86d3dme0z`: an earlier version wrongly dropped all `is-food` goods including grain & fish.)*
- **Peter Minuit** carries `model.modifier.landPaymentModifier −100%`, which zeroes the price → native land is **free** (and, paid free, draws no anger).
- **Buy** (`ClaimLandByPaying`): pay the price in gold (refused if you can't afford it); the tile becomes yours. *(FreeCol credits the owning nation the gold; we keep no native treasury, so the gold simply leaves the buyer — a documented deviation.)*
- **Take** (`ClaimLandByStealing`): no gold, but the robbed nation's settlements each gain **+200 alarm** (FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_LAND_TAKEN`). *(Applied nation-wide because our ownership is tracked per nation, not per settlement — a minor deviation from FreeCol, which targets the specific owning settlement.)*
- A tile a native **settlement sits on** is **not for sale** (FreeCol `tile.hasSettlement()` → not for sale).
- A claimed tile is recorded as a **saved override** so the native-ownership re-derivation never re-claims it — it stays yours across save/load and even if the owning settlement is later destroyed.

**Forced buy-or-steal-or-abandon trigger** (`86d3e4bj7`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csClaimLand` invoked from `InGameController.claimLand` before `BuildColonyMission` builds):
- **Founding** a colony on a native-owned centre tile, or **assigning a colonist to work** a native-owned ring tile, is no longer free — it forces a claim *first*. `CheckFoundColony`/`CheckAssignWork` stay pure legality gates (founding/working remains *Allowed* on native land); the claim is enforced by `FoundColony`/`AssignWork` themselves and queried via **`RequiredLandClaim(player, tile)`** (`Required` + `BuyPrice` + `OwningNation`).
- **The choice** is a `LandClaimChoice` — **Buy** (pay the `LandPrice`, free under Minuit), **Steal** (take it + raise the owning nation's per-player alarm by **+200**, FreeCol `TENSION_ADD_LAND_TAKEN`), or **Abandon** (don't proceed — no state change). Buy/Steal reuse the voluntary `ClaimLandByPaying`/`ClaimLandByStealing` paths, so the two stay in lock-step.
- **Per-player alarm (C1):** stealing applies the land-taken hit on the **acting player's channel** (`ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, player.PlayerId, …)`), nation-wide. A rival stealing from the same tribe raises *its* channel, never the human's — ADR-009 channel isolation.
- **The human** must supply the choice: the no-choice `FoundColony(unit)` / `AssignWork(colony, tile, good)` overloads throw **`LandClaimRequiredException`** (carrying the buy price + owning nation) so the presentation raises its buy/steal/abandon dialog and re-calls the `LandClaimChoice` overload. The colony screen's auto food-assignment (`AutoAssignIdleToFood`) **skips** the human's native ring tiles silently (no surprise dialog) — they're claimed deliberately via the tile-work picker.
- **The AI** resolves deterministically (RNG-free): **`AiResolveLandClaim`** = pay if it can afford the price, else steal (FreeCol `BuildColonyMission`: `price==0 ? 0 : checkGold(price) ? price : STEAL_LAND`; FreeCol's optional 1-in-4 gold-cheat is omitted — it would draw RNG and break twin-determinism). So an AI founding/working on owned land claims it automatically, on its own alarm channel; the default soak therefore now has its AI pay/steal where it once took free — **deterministic and byte-identical across twins** (no save bump).
- **Resolved synchronously** — there is **no pending-claim state**: the claim happens on the click (human) or inline (AI/engine), so the save format is unchanged (still v55).
- **Still out of scope:** FreeCol's `ExtraClaimableRadius`, crediting the native owner the gold, and a presentation `.tscn`/L3 panel for the dialog (it's a code-built `ConfirmationDialog` for now).

**Alarm bands** (FreeCol `Tension.Level`, `Tension.java`) — alarm is 0–1000:

| Band | Alarm | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Happy | 0–100 | friendly |
| Content | 101–600 | tolerant |
| Displeased | 601–700 | wary |
| Angry | 701–800 | won't teach its skill |
| Hateful | 801–1000 | no gifts; dangerous |

- Settlements start at alarm **0** (Happy). Each turn alarm cools toward 0 by `value/DecayDivisor + DecayBase` (= `value/100 + 4`, FreeCol `ServerPlayer` decay). `Game.ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, delta)` (clamped 0–1000) is the public alarm mutator. Combat now calls it: attacking a brave adds **+200** (`AddNormal`) to its nearest settlement, and **+400** more (`AddUnitDestroyed`) if the brave is killed — see [combat](combat.md). The hostile-act tension deltas (the `TENSION_ADD_*` family), the land-taken/surrender alarms, the decay terms, and the first-contact gift range / tales radius are all **data-overridable** as of `86d3drpgg`: they read from `Ruleset.Difficulty.NativeTension` (`NativeTensionOptions`), which defaults to the classic FreeCol `Tension.java`/`IndianSettlement.java` values (so the default game is byte-identical). The FreeCol-source `TensionAdd*` consts stay on `NativeSettlement` as the drift-guarded source of truth the record references.
- **Ambient alarm — natives resent encroachment.** Each turn (just before the decay), `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` raises a settlement's alarm by the human's nearby footprint (FreeCol `ServerPlayer.csNewTurn`): within **settlement-radius + 2** tiles, every human **colony** adds **2 + its population** and every human **offensive land unit** adds its (type) offence. So expanding your colonies and massing troops near a tribe steadily angers it, while the decay pulls back — alarm settles at a level proportional to how much you crowd them (equilibrium ≈ `100 × (pressure − 4)`, capped at 1000). The gain is damped by **Pocahontas**'s −50% and, for a **French** player, the `cooperation` nation-type's own −50% (both below; they stack multiplicatively). RNG-free and human-directed (foreign powers exert no native pressure in our model). *(Simplifications: no tile-ownership/control pressure and no missionary calming, neither of which we model.)*
- **Pocahontas** (Founding Father) calms the natives — see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md). On her election, **every settlement's alarm toward the human resets to 0 (Happy)** (`ResetAllNativeAlarm`, FreeCol `model.event.resetNativeAlarm`); thereafter the per-turn **ambient** alarm gain is **halved** (`ScaleNativeAlarmGain` folds her −50% `nativeAlarmModifier` into `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` — gains only; goodwill/decay are not slowed). This is the modifier's **faithful home** (FreeCol damps the ambient proximity alarm, not combat — combat tension is applied raw). Both are human-gated (alarm is toward the human) and ride on the persisted Congress. *Deviation:* the reset clears **all** settlements (FreeCol clears only contacted ones) and substitutes alarm-to-0 for FreeCol's native-nation `Stance.PEACE` (we have no native-nation stance).
- **French nation-type advantage** (`86d3df4u1`, FreeCol `model.nationType.cooperation`): the **French** carry the same `model.modifier.nativeAlarmModifier −50%` as a permanent national trait. `ScaleNativeAlarmGain` folds the human's **nation-type** modifiers (`NationTypeModifiers`, the same reusable seam as the religious-unrest bonus) alongside any father modifier — so a French player's ambient alarm gain is halved, and a French Pocahontas stacks to ×0.25. Like religious-unrest, the human defaults to **no nation** (×1), so a default game is byte-identical; foreign powers exert no native pressure, so it only matters for a French human.
- **Braves** (`Game.New`): each settlement is garrisoned by one native-owned `model.unit.brave` placed on a free land tile adjacent to it (deterministic, no RNG draw — the placement stream is unaffected). Braves are full `Unit`s (`OwnerNationId` = the settlement's nation, unarmed default role) so they round-trip through the save and can be the strongest defender on their tile. They are **excluded from the player's fog/visibility** (a brave never reveals tiles for the player) and cannot be selected or commanded by the player.
- **Native AI — raids & wandering** (slice 1b, `RunNativeTurn`): each turn, every native unit takes **one** action, in stable by-id order. A brave is **hostile** when its **home settlement** (the nearest surviving settlement of its own nation) is at alarm band **Displeased or worse** (alarm **> 600**) — FreeCol's seek-and-destroy gate (`NativeAIPlayer.secureIndianSettlement`: a settlement sends braves hunting only when its tension toward the player is *above* Content). A hostile brave first **pillages** an adjacent undefended pillageable human colony (`AdjacentPillageableHumanColony` → `PillageColony`, the high-value target — below); else, if it's beside a human colony it can't storm but can shake down (`AdjacentDemandableHumanColony`, and no demand already pending), it raises a **tribute demand** (below); otherwise it picks the **best-scored human-owned land unit** to raid (`PickRaidTarget` — FreeCol's `UnitSeekAndDestroyMission` value−distance heuristic over the 8/12/16 Chebyshev range ladder, the same `ScoreUnitTarget` the foreign-power war AI uses, so a weak/valuable target at equal range beats a dug-in soldier — falling back to the **nearest** at any distance when none is in range) and, if adjacent, **attacks** it, else **steps one tile toward** it. A non-hostile brave (or a hostile one with no target) **wanders** one random legal tile. A brave with no movement left, or whose nation has lost all its settlements, idles/wanders. Every draw — the wander pick, the path tiebreak, the combat resolution, the pillage loot pick — comes from the **nation's own RNG stream** (`RandomFor(player)`), never the human's stream 0, so the human's seeded game is byte-stable however much the natives do (ADR-009). Combat runs through the internal `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` overload (see [combat](combat.md)); a raid records a transient `CombatNotice` (cleared each turn, never saved) that the UI surfaces to the player.
- **Native colony pillage** (`CheckPillageColony`/`PillageColony`, FreeCol `csPillageColony` / the `PILLAGE_COLONY` combat effect): a hostile brave adjacent to an **undefended** (no unit on its tile), **pillageable** human colony storms it (a transient unarmed-colonist defender, defence 1, on the nation's own stream). On a brave **win** it carries off `min(amount/2, 50)` of one **uniformly-chosen** lootable goods stack (`Colony.AddGoods(−take)`; loot-pick from the nation's stream) and a transient `ColonyRaidNotice` is recorded; the colony is **not** captured or destroyed (natives can't take colonies). On a **loss** the brave is slain (dispose-on-combat-loss). The gate is FreeCol `Colony.canBePillaged` (no stockade, the brave's `model.ability.pillageUnprotectedColony`, unprotected, something to loot). **Faithful subset:** we model the **goods-loot** outcome only — see the deviations below.
- **Native bring-gifts** (`86d3dfcfe`, then **store-backed + cooldown** `86d3fpzx1`; `TryBringGiftFromStore` in `RunNativeTurn`, FreeCol `IndianBringGiftMission` + `getRandomGift`): the goodwill mirror of pillage. A brave whose home settlement is **Happy** (the friendliest band) toward the human, **off cooldown** (`LastGiftTurn + NativeGiftCooldownTurns(10) ≤ turn`), **holding a giftable surplus**, and standing beside one of its colonies has a per-turn chance (1-in-`NativeGiftChanceDenominator` = 8, drawn from the nation's **own** stream) to **draw a good + amount from its own `GeneralStock`** and leave it in the colony's warehouse, recording a transient `ColonyGiftNotice`. The gift good is any tradeable non-military good the settlement holds above `NativeGiftThreshold` (75 = FreeCol `KEEP_RAW_MATERIAL` 50 + `GIFT_THRESHOLD` 25); the amount is `NativeGiftMinimum(10)`..`NativeGiftMaximum(80)` (FreeCol `GIFT_MINIMUM`/`GIFT_MAXIMUM`), capped at the stock, and is **deducted from the settlement's store** (then its wanted goods re-price). Pure goodwill — no alarm change, no brave consumed, no combat. The chance + the good/amount picks are rolled **only** when the brave is already eligible, so an ineligible brave never perturbs the native stream. **Deviations:** the cooldown stands in for FreeCol's gift-probability + one-mission-in-flight throttle (FreeCol has no explicit per-settlement cooldown); the brave doesn't physically carry the goods (the parcel teleports, as before). The old fixed-25-tobacco abstraction (no store, no cooldown) is **retired**.
- **Equip & promote braves from settlement stock** (`86d3c9vzp`, `EquipBravesAtThreatenedSettlements`/`TryEquipBrave`/`CanAffordUpgrade`/`EquipFromSettlement` in `RunNativeTurn`, FreeCol `NativeAIPlayer.secureSettlements`→`equipBraves`): at the **start** of a nation's turn, before any brave acts, each **threatened** settlement (alarm band Displeased+ — the same gate that sends braves raiding) secures its braves from its **own goods stock**. `EquipBravesAtThreatenedSettlements` iterates the nation's alarmed settlements (deterministic, by position) and, for each, equips its braves — the ones whose `HomeSettlement` is that camp — in **military-strength order**, strongest first (`OffenceBase + DefenceBase`, ties by id; FreeCol's `getMilitaryStrengthComparator`), so under scarce stock the best warriors are armed first. Per brave, `TryEquipBrave` takes the **strongest role the camp can afford in one step** (FreeCol `Settlement.canImproveUnitMilitaryRole` — the best superior military role whose *extra* equipment is in stock): an **unarmed** (default-role) brave becomes a **native dragoon** if the camp holds ≥ 25 muskets **and** ≥ 25 horses, else **armedBrave** (muskets) or **mountedBrave** (horses) for whichever single half it has; an **already-armed or already-mounted** brave is **promoted to native dragoon** when the camp holds the **missing half** (`CanAffordUpgrade` checks the delta). `EquipFromSettlement` charges only the **goods difference** over the brave's current role (`RoleGoodsDelta` — so an armed→dragoon promotion costs just the horses, not a fresh set of muskets), then `ChangeRole`s the brave (the same path combat capture uses). Equipping is **free** — it never consumes a brave's action, so an armed brave still pillages/demands/raids/wanders this same turn — and the upgrade persists via the brave's existing serialized role field (**no save change**). The equip is **entirely RNG-free** (the strongest-affordable choice is deterministic), so it never touches the human's stream 0; a settlement holding zero stock never fires and stays byte-stable (ADR-009). The musket/horse stock lives on `NativeSettlement` as `_militaryStock` (public `StockOf`/`AddStock`/`MilitaryStock`), now **seeded at generation** (`NativeSettlementGenerator.SeedMilitaryStock`, `86d3e49gq`) and **serialized** (save **v54**, `SavedNativeSettlement.MilitaryStock`, omit-when-empty), so in an **ordinary game** a threatened camp's brave actually arms itself — the equip chain fires on live data, not only on stock a test deposits. **Bounded subset / deviations below.**
- **Spread arms to threatened camps** (`86d3c9vzp` arms-spreading, `RedistributeArmsToThreatenedSettlements` in `RunNativeTurn`, FreeCol `NativeAIPlayer.secureSettlements`→`tradeGoodsWithSettlement`): run at the **very start** of a nation's turn, **before** `EquipBravesAtThreatenedSettlements`, so a camp can arm its braves from a fresh delivery the same turn. For each military good in stable order (**muskets** then **horses**), a **donor** is a settlement that is **not threatened** (alarm band below `RaidAlarmThreshold` = Displeased) and holds a **surplus** — at least `2 × BraveEquipGoods` (50) so it keeps one half-unit for itself — and a **recipient** is a **threatened** settlement (alarm Displeased+) holding **less than one half-unit** (< 25) of that good (it can't equip a brave from it). One half-unit (`BraveEquipGoods` = 25) is shipped from the **richest** eligible donor to the **neediest** recipient (donor ranked by stock desc then position; recipient by stock asc then position), repeating until no eligible pair remains (bounded by the finite stock). A one-settlement nation is skipped. The transfer is **entirely RNG-free** (deterministic ranking, no `RandomFor` draw on any stream), and it moves only the same `_militaryStock` the equip arms from (now seeded + serialized, v54) — so it adds **no save field of its own**, and a game whose camps hold zero stock (e.g. every camp emptied, or a pre-v54 save) has no donor → no transfer and stays byte-stable (ADR-009). **Bounded subset / deviations below.**
- **Native tribute demands** (`CreateNativeDemand`/`SelectDemand`/`AcceptPendingDemand`/`RefusePendingDemand`, FreeCol `IndianDemandMission` + `ServerPlayer.csCompleteNativeDemand`): a hostile brave adjacent to a human colony it **can't** pillage-as-a-step (defended, or holding only gold/food rather than a storable stack to grab — the case the pillage branch above leaves open) raises a **tribute demand** instead, ending its turn. The single pending demand is stored on `Game.PendingDemand` (transient — never saved, like the notice channels) and the human answers it next turn: **accept** transfers the demanded goods/gold out of the colony (consumed — capped at what the colony holds, never a negative balance) and **cools the nation's alarm by 150 across all its settlements** (FreeCol `-(5 - difficulty) × 50` at medium); **refuse** clears it with **no** transfer and **no** tension change (FreeCol changes tension only on accept — the refusal's bite is the brave's normal next-turn pillage/raid via the existing AI). An unanswered demand is **auto-refused** at the top of the next `EndTurn` (FreeCol's session timeout = reject). **What is demanded** (`SelectDemand`, FreeCol `selectGoods`, picked deterministically at the nation's alarm band): Content-or-calmer + enough food → that food at the cutoff; Displeased-or-calmer → the **priciest** non-food, non-military **storable** stack (by market *stack* value = amount × unit price); else the first present good in the order **military → building-material → trade → refined**; else the priciest storable stack; else **gold** = `gold/20` (or all the gold, or nothing if the human is broke). The amount is `clamp(count × dx / 6, 30, 100)` with `dx = nativeDemands + 1 = 3` at the default medium difficulty. **Faithful subset / deviations below.**
- **Speak with chief** (`Visit`): a person unit next to a settlement **its owner hasn't yet visited** (`86d3dk5mm` — per-player first contact: `CheckVisit` gates on `HasBeenVisitedBy(unit.OwnerId)`, so each colonial power gets one audience per chief) reveals the lands within `TalesRevealRadius` (3) of it and ends its turn. **Foreign powers now take this audience too** while exploring (`86d3dk5mm`, AI scout-chief — `RunForeignPowerTurn`: a colonist that can't found and is beside an unvisited settlement `Visit`s it on the power's own stream). A **scout-role** unit gets FreeCol's full `scoutSpeakToChief` outcomes (`ScoutSpeakToChief`): a **Hateful** tribe **slays** the scout; otherwise one "scouting" roll yields — **trained** into a seasoned scout (always if the chief teaches scouting, else 1-in-10), **tales** (a wider reveal; taken when there are no beads or ~1-in-3 of the time), or **beads** (gold from the settlement type's `<gifts>` range — FreeCol's *continuous* RandomRange `rnd[0,(Max−Min+1)×Factor) + Min×Factor`, finer-grained than the discrete plunder roll, **+10%** for an already-expert scout). A **non-scout** colonist gets the basic visit: a flat **10–80** gift (FreeCol `GIFT_MINIMUM/MAXIMUM`) unless the settlement is Hateful. Once only.
- **Learn skill** (`LearnSkill`): a colonist whose type the spec marks **learnable** (the `model.unitChange.natives` change-type — classic: free colonist or indentured servant), next to a settlement whose skill isn't yet consumed and whose alarm is below Angry, is taught the settlement's `LearnableSkill` expert type (the unit is replaced by one of that type, keeping its id). Eligibility is **data-driven** (`86d3fpxaw`): `CheckLearnSkill` calls `Ruleset.CanLearnSkillFromNatives(unitType, settlement.LearnableSkill)` — the from→to gate FreeCol's `learnFromIndianSettlement` uses (`getUnitChange(NATIVES, skill) != null`). The skill is then consumed — **except at a capital**, which teaches indefinitely. Experts and petty criminals have no `natives` row and cannot learn.
- **Two-way trade** (`86d3e4bh2`): a ship next to a settlement whose alarm is below Angry trades **both ways** for gold (no European tax), drawing on the settlement's **general goods stock** (`NativeSettlement.GeneralStock`/`GeneralStockOf`/`AddGoods` — the FreeCol `IndianSettlement` goods container, distinct from the military stock that arms braves).
  - **Sell** (`SellToNatives`/`NativeSalePrice`): the price it pays per unit is the **stock-fill base** `(12 + trade-bonus) × (capacity − current) / capacity` (FreeCol `getNormalGoodsPriceToBuy`'s unit price — the price falls as the settlement's store of that good fills toward capacity, `capacity = 100 × claimable-radius`: camp 100, capital/village 200, city capital 300) times the **wanted premium** — **150 / 125 / 110%** for its 1st / 2nd / 3rd wanted good (else 100%) — with a ~10% markup (FreeCol `getPriceToSell` ≈ `amount + 11·getPriceToBuy/10`). `trade-bonus` is the settlement type's (camp 1 → city 4). The whole price is then cut by the **ship-trade penalty** — `−30%` at medium (`model.option.shipTradePenalty`, harsher on higher levels: −20/−25/−30/−35/−40) — because the trader arrived by sea (FreeCol charges this only to a naval trader; our native trade is ship-only, so it always applies). The sold goods **join the settlement's store** (re-pricing future trade), and the sale lowers alarm.
  - **Buy** (`BuyFromNatives`/`NativeBuyPrice`/`GoodsToSell`): `GoodsToSell` lists what the settlement offers — the general-stock goods it holds at least **20** of (FreeCol `TRADE_MINIMUM_SIZE`), each capped at one hold (100), priciest first. The asking price is its own valuation (the same stock-fill base × wanted premium) marked up ~10% (FreeCol `getPriceToSell` from the settlement's side), with **no** ship-trade penalty (FreeCol applies that only to the player's sale). Buying **drains the store** (so the next unit costs more) and credits a little goodwill. Refused if the store is short, the player can't afford it, or the settlement is Angry+.
  - Either action ends the ship's turn (FreeCol zeroes moves on opening a trade session).
- **Stock-driven wanted goods** (`RecomputeWantedGoods`, FreeCol `IndianSettlement.updateWantedGoods`): a settlement's up-to-3 wanted goods are **derived from its store**, not fixed at placement. Of the storable, non-military, tradeable goods, the three it values highest per full hold — the stock-fill base (above) **halved for farmed goods** (FreeCol `getNormalGoodsPriceToBuy` — so manufactured wares it cannot make and holds none of out-rank the raw goods it gathers, and among the raw goods the scarcest wins) — most-wanted first, dropping any below the trade-minimum floor (`100 × TRADE_MINIMUM_PRICE(3)`). Recomputed at generation and after every buy/sell, so cravings track stores. **RNG-free** (ties break by goods id) → never the human's stream 0.
- **Haggling** (`TryHaggleSell`/`TryHaggleBuy` → `NativeHaggleResult`, FreeCol `NativeAIPlayer.handleTrade`): each offer is checked against the settlement's fair price walked **down ×9/10 per round** when selling (`haggleDown`) or **up ×11/10** when buying (`haggleUp`). A good-enough offer is **accepted** at the player's price; otherwise the natives **counter** at their price *or* — with rising probability the longer it drags on (`randomInt(HAGGLE_NUMBER(3) + round+1) ≥ HAGGLE_NUMBER`) — **lose patience** and break off (`Done`). The patience roll is drawn on a dedicated **native-trade RNG stream** (104), never the human's economy stream 0 (ADR-009); since haggling is an interactive, human-only action that isn't replayed, that stream's running state is **not serialized**.

**Deviations from original 1994 / FreeCol behavior:**
- **Native AI is a flat priority switch, not FreeCol's mission planner.** FreeCol assigns braves typed missions (`UnitSeekAndDestroyMission`, `UnitWanderHostileMission`, `IndianDemandMission`, `IndianBringGiftMission`) scored over targets. We collapse this to: hostile → hunt the nearest human unit; else wander. Deliberate simplifications: (a) **per-settlement alarm stands in for nation-level tension** — FreeCol's attack gate reads a player-level `Tension`; we read the brave's nearest same-nation settlement's `Alarm` (we keep no nation-level native tension store). (b) **one action per brave per turn** (one step *or* one attack/pillage), matching the foreign-power AI's shape, vs FreeCol moving a unit its full allowance. (c) **target choice is now scored** (`86d3c9vzp`) — a hostile brave's unit raid uses the FreeCol `UnitSeekAndDestroyMission` value−distance heuristic (`PickRaidTarget`/`ScoreUnitTarget`) over the 8/12/16 range ladder (soft/valuable over dug-in), falling back to the nearest at any distance; it is still **omniscient** (not fog-limited) and bounded (braves move one tile and only hunt when provoked). Colony pillage/demand target choice stays "adjacent colony". (d) **Demanding tribute** (`IndianDemandMission`) **is now modelled** (see the tribute-demand rule above and its deviations below); **bringing gifts** (`IndianBringGiftMission`) and **braves equipping from settlement stock** (`equipBraves`, see the equip rule above + its deviations below) are **now modelled** too. Braves raid **human units and colonies only** (never settlements — the `CheckAttackSettlement` native gate stays closed — never foreign powers or rival tribes; the AI's target filter enforces this beyond the engine's owner-inequality rule).
- **Native colony pillage models the goods-loot and gold outcomes.** FreeCol's `csPillageColony` picks one target *uniformly* from {burnable buildings, ships on the colony tile, lootable goods, +gold if the owner can be plundered}; we model the **goods stacks** + the **gold** option. Goods candidates are the **storable** stored goods (FreeCol `getLootableGoodsList`; hammers/bells/crosses are `storable="false"` and excluded), and looted goods are **destroyed**, not carried off — the brave gains nothing (FreeCol does `attacker.add(goods)`; we have no native goods-hauling/settlement-restock model). The **gold** option is the last in the pick (matching FreeCol's order), added only when the human has gold; it steals `max(1, ColonyPlunderAmount/5)` (FreeCol `colony.getPlunder/5` — the colony-capture plunder formula `rnd[0, gold×(pop+1)/(coloniesPop+1)]+1`), capped at the owner's purse (conservation-safe; FreeCol doesn't cap), and records a `ColonyRaidNotice` with a **null** `GoodsId`. **Deferred:** **damaging a building** (no building-destruction model), **sinking/damaging ships** caught in the colony, the **great-win → colonist-kill / colony-destroy** path (we pillage on **any** native win, so a native great win is *gentler* than FreeCol's — a tribe never destroys a colony here), and the attacker's **tension easing** (`attackerTension −= NORMAL` — we keep no nation-level native tension store). The pillage **gate stays goods-based** (`CheckPillageColony` requires lootable goods), so a goods-less but gold-holding colony is not pillageable — a minor deviation from FreeCol `canBePlundered`. Braves pillage the **human only** (FreeCol pillages any European colony) — the same sole-target contract as the unit raid.
- **Native tribute demands model the human-facing economics; native-side bookkeeping is abstracted.** Faithful: which goods/gold is demanded and how much (`selectGoods`/`capAmount`), the accept transfer + the alarm relief, refuse = no tension change, and the spec-order pick within each category rung. Documented deviations: (a) the paid tribute is **consumed**, not hauled to the settlement or added to a native store (we have no native goods/treasury model — same abstraction as pillage). (b) Demand **candidates are restricted to storable goods** (the pillage loot filter) — our model never demands the non-warehoused hammers/bells/crosses. (c) Pillage takes priority over demand (FreeCol scores them as separate missions); a demand fires only on the colony the pillage branch leaves open. (d) The selection's **first branch (food when the demanding nation is only Content-or-calmer) is unreachable** under our single per-settlement-alarm channel, because we only raise demands at Displeased+ — it is implemented and unit-tested for fidelity and would activate if a separate nation-level tension store is ever added. (e) **One human-facing demand per ring** (first brave wins; a second eligible brave falls through to its normal AI) — a blocking modal answers one at a time. (f) `nativeDemands`, `nativeConvertProbability` and `burnProbability` are now **difficulty options** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.NativeDemands`/`NativeConvertProbability`/`BurnProbability`, classic-medium 2/30/6; the demand-amount `dx = nativeDemands + 1` and the accept relief `(5 − nativeDemands)·50` transforms stay in code) — read from the spec `model.difficulty.natives` group, default game byte-identical (`86d3bb1x3`). The accept relief's `·50` and the demand-amount `clamp(…,30,100)` bounds remain code-level; the `TENSION_ADD_*` tension deltas, land-taken/surrender alarms, the alarm decay (`value/100 + 4`) and the gift range / tales radius are now the data-overridable `Ruleset.Difficulty.NativeTension` (`NativeTensionOptions`, `86d3drpgg`) — FreeCol keeps these as engine consts (no spec option), so they default to the classic values across every level. See [difficulty](difficulty.md). (g) The refuse-escalation is the brave's **existing** next-turn pillage/raid AI, not a dedicated attack; if the brave dies before the human answers, there is simply no specific escalation. Braves demand of the **human only**. (h) **Food is demanded under Angry/Hateful** (`86d3c18n8`, fixed). `BuildingMaterials` is now derived over *all* buildables (buildings + units + roles) — FreeCol's `GoodsType.isBuildingMaterial` — so the freeColonist's `required-goods food=200` makes **food** a building material (and the armed/mounted roles make muskets/horses ones). At Angry/Hateful the priciest-non-military branch is skipped, so the category ladder runs and the **building-material rung claims food** (food precedes a raw trade stack like tobacco in the rung order), exactly as FreeCol does; a remaining edge is that food still loses to any non-food storable stack at Displeased-or-calmer (branch 2 excludes food first), matching FreeCol's own branch ordering. (i) **Accept alarm relief is flat (−150 on every settlement of the nation).** FreeCol lands −150 on the home settlement and a halved −75 on the rest (via player-level tension); we apply the full −150 nation-wide — the same single-alarm flattening `ApplyNativeCombatTension` already uses.

- **Equip-braves models the full strength-ordered promotion (incl. native dragoon); `secureIndianSettlement`'s defend-missions are out of scope.** FreeCol's `equipBraves` sorts a settlement's braves by military strength and promotes each as far as its stock allows — including partially-equipped braves up to the full **native dragoon** — and is followed by the much larger `secureIndianSettlement` (threat evaluation + `DefendSettlementMission` assignments). We **now** model the equip facet faithfully: strength-ordered securing (strongest brave first), the dragoon role for both-halves-affordable, and **promotion of an already-armed/mounted brave to native dragoon** charging only the missing half (the `86d3c9vzp` deepening). We still implement **none** of `secureIndianSettlement`'s threat-scoring / defend-mission pathing (a brave's actual movement stays the flat raid/wander switch above). (a) **Stock is seeded, not produced** — rather than model FreeCol's full native goods-container with per-turn military-goods *production* and retention (`IndianSettlement.getMaximumProduction` / `KEEP_RAW_MATERIAL`), we **seed** each settlement's muskets/horses store once at generation (`NativeSettlementGenerator.SeedMilitaryStock`, `86d3e49gq`), sized by type/capital/size, as a faithful stand-in for the built-up stock the original game would have on hand by war-time. The stock is the `_militaryStock` field on `NativeSettlement` (`StockOf`/`AddStock`/`MilitaryStock`), **serialized** (save **v54**, omit-when-empty). So an ordinary game's threatened camps *do* arm their braves — the chain is no longer dead in practice. FreeCol's `tradeGoodsWithSettlement` **arms-spreading between camps is also modelled** (`RedistributeArmsToThreatenedSettlements` — a calm stocked camp ships a half-unit to a threatened bare one, see the rule above). What is still out of scope: any **ongoing** source that *refills* native muskets/horses over the game (per-turn production, buying arms via trade) — once a camp spends its seeded stock arming its braves it is not replenished, and the inter-camp spreading only redistributes the seeded stock. (b) **The equip is now entirely RNG-free** — FreeCol promotes to the strongest affordable role with no random pick (the old muskets-vs-horses coin-flip was our simplification; both-halves-affordable now deterministically yields a dragoon, so the path draws nothing on any stream). (c) **Strength comparator** uses `OffenceBase + DefenceBase` (ties by id) as a faithful stand-in for FreeCol's `getMilitaryStrengthComparator`. (d) Equipping charges only the **goods difference** over the current role (`RoleGoodsDelta`), consumed from the settlement, reusing the colony-equip draw-down shape (`ChangeRole`) without the `Colony`/`CheckEquipRole` path.

- **Missionaries — ordaining one** (`86d3c9p3p`): a colonist becomes a missionary by being **equipped into the `model.role.missionary` role at a colony with a church or cathedral** (the building provides `model.ability.dressMissionary`; a chapel cannot) — the player-facing entry to this whole feature; see [colonies](colonies.md) (ordaining a missionary). The role grants `model.ability.establishMission`, used next.
- **Missionaries — establish a mission** (`CheckEstablishMission`/`EstablishMission`, FreeCol `InGameController.establishMission`): a unit in the **missionary role** (it carries `model.ability.establishMission`), on or adjacent to a settlement with movement left, can **establish a mission**. The **alarm does not gate the command** — establishing at an Angry/Hateful tribe is a legal action that simply gets the missionary killed (mirrors how a hateful tribe legally kills a visiting scout). The outcome turns on the settlement's alarm band (FreeCol `Tension`): **Happy/Content/Displeased** (alarm ≤ 700) → the mission is **installed** (the settlement records the owning player + whether the missionary was a jesuit, its **alarm eases by 100** as goodwill — FreeCol `ALARM_NEW_MISSIONARY`, clamped at 0 — and the surrounding tiles are revealed at the missionary's line of sight), and the missionary is **consumed into the settlement** (FreeCol holds it as the settlement's resident missionary, not an on-map unit); **Angry/Hateful** (alarm > 700) → the missionary is **destroyed** with no mission. One mission per settlement; establishing replaces any existing one (FreeCol kills the old missionary first). **Draws no randomness** (the whole mission mechanic is RNG-free). The human-facing *establish-mission UI* is a later presentation slice. **Deviations:** the missionary line-of-sight reveal is a fixed radius = the unit's base LoS (FreeCol's non-enhanced `getMissionaryLineOfSight()` = 1; the `ENHANCED_MISSIONARIES` option is out of scope); the missionary is modelled as settlement state (owner + expert-ness), not a held unit object.
- **Missionaries — convert accrual** (`ProcessMissions`, FreeCol `ServerIndianSettlement.csStartTurn`): each turn (in `EndTurn`, after the ambient-alarm pass and **before** the alarm decay — so it reads the alarm the turn produced, matching FreeCol) every settlement holding a mission gains **convert progress** = `(missionarySkill + 6) + 2% of alarm`, where the missionary skill is **3 for a jesuit, 0 for an ordinary colonist** (`conversionSkill` +6 and `conversionAlarmRate` +2% are FreeCol modifiers on the colonist base type) — **unless the mission's owner has Father Jean de Brébeuf in the Congress, which makes *every* one of that player's missions count as a jesuit (skill 3) even when staffed by an ordinary colonist** (the continuous `model.ability.expertMissionary`; see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)). A more-alarmed tribe converts *faster*. When the progress reaches the settlement type's **convert threshold** (classic **100**) — and the settlement is still **bigger than 2**, and a colony of the mission's owner is within **10** tiles (Chebyshev) — a brave **converts**: the progress resets to 0, the settlement **shrinks by one**, and a `model.unit.indianConvert` musters at that colony for the owner (lifting the owner's fog). Otherwise the progress **banks** and carries to next turn. **RNG-free** (ADR-009). *(Converts can also be **captured in combat** — winning an assault on a settlement you hold a mission in may convert a brave; that path lives in [combat](combat.md) (capture-convert) and is where Juan de Sepúlveda's `nativeConvertBonus` applies. The same won-assault roll has a small **burn-missions** chance — the natives burn your missions across that nation instead — also in [combat](combat.md).)* **Deviations:** FreeCol picks a *specific random brave* to convert and removes it from the settlement's unit list; we don't model individual braves as residents, so we **decrement the settlement size by one** and spawn the convert without a die — keeping the whole mechanic deterministic (the human's stream 0 untouched). The convert musters **on the colony tile** (units stack there) rather than FreeCol's tile-search. The alarm term caps at our `MaxAlarm` 1000 (FreeCol caps at 1100 — a ≤2/turn difference only at the very top of the band). The within-10 colony search (`NearestColonyOf`) is a plain Chebyshev range, **without** FreeCol's same-landmass (`isConnectedTo`) requirement — immaterial today (the map grows a single continent and we model no runtime landmass/connectivity), and the same simplification `NearestHumanColony` already makes.
- **Trade is two-way and coastal-only; goods stock is seeded, not produced.** FreeCol models a full session (buy / sell / gift, haggling up to 3×, settlement goods stock **driven by per-turn tile production** that sets prices and what they'll sell). We **now** model **selling to** *and* **buying from** a settlement, a **stock-driven** price (the fuller the store, the cheaper it sells and the less it pays — FreeCol's `getNormalGoodsPriceToBuy` stock-fill term), **stock-driven wanted goods** (`updateWantedGoods`), and a **haggle** offer/counter loop (`86d3e4bh2`). Remaining simplifications: (a) **stock is seeded *and* produced** (`86d3fpzvy`) — each settlement's general goods store is seeded at generation (`NativeSettlementGenerator.SeedGeneralStock`, native stream) **and replenished every turn** by `ProduceNativeGoods` (called from `RunNativeTurn` via `ProduceNativeNationGoods`): the settlement gathers its raw storable tradeable New-World goods, `NativeProductionPerResident(2) × Size × headroom/ceiling + rng.Next(0, Size+1)` units (a stock-fill curve — a good near the 100-unit ceiling grows slowly, one at the ceiling not at all), on the nation's stream. Selling **grows** it, buying **drains** it, and production **refills** it between visits — so a bought-out camp recovers over a few turns rather than staying low until the player sells back. Production is **abstracted** (we don't model the natives' tiles, so it deliberately under-produces FreeCol's full per-tile `getTotalProductionOf` to keep trade worthwhile); consumption / horse-breeding / new-resident births (FreeCol `csNewTurn`) are not modelled. (b) The sell-price stock-fill base keeps the **un-halved** `(12+trade-bonus)` per unit (so the established empty-store sell price is unchanged), while the **wanted-goods ranking** does apply FreeCol's farmed-halving — a documented split (FreeCol uses the halved `getNormalGoodsPriceToBuy` for both; the difference is a small absolute price gap on raw farm goods). (c) FreeCol's `getNormalGoodsPriceToBuy` also **fakes manufactured-good production** from local raw materials and adds a trade-goods bump; we model neither (we have no native production), so manufactured goods price purely off the stock-fill term. (d) **Inland settlements** still need wagon trains (deferred) — only coastal settlements are reachable by ship today. (e) **AI-side native goods trading** (FreeCol braves/wagons trading between settlements and with rival colonies) is **out of scope** (Chris's 2026-06-22 call). The **ship-trade penalty** (`model.option.shipTradePenalty`) **is** modelled on the player's sale (FreeCol `getShipTradePenalties`, `sense=true`); since our native trade is ship-only it always applies (FreeCol also exempts overland wagon trades, which we don't have). The sibling `missionaryTradeBonus`/`enhancedMissionaries` modifier is **default-off** in classic, so it is **not** wired. Alarm-reduction-per-trade approximates FreeCol's `ALARM_BONUS_SELL`/`ALARM_BONUS_BUY` game options. The buy/sell/haggle flow is now **reachable in the UI** (`86d3f62qh`): `NativeSettlementPanel` offers *Sell goods* / *Buy goods* when the acting unit is a carrier at/adjacent to a coastal settlement, lists the goods + price from the oracles, and surfaces the haggle offer/counter — see §3 (trade UI) and [presentation](../modules/presentation.md). The haggle loop now **honours the named price** (`86d3fpzqd`): the price-carrying `SellToNatives`/`BuyFromNatives(…, agreedPrice)` overloads transact at the figure the chief accepted (validated to the haggle band so the UI can't mint gold — see §3 Natives-depth), so a successful haggle genuinely changes the gold moved. The old advisory behaviour (engine charged the standard price regardless) is retired.
- **Speak-with-chief: scout outcomes shipped; some FreeCol detail abstracted.** A scout-role unit now gets the full weighted outcomes (beads from `<gifts>`, expert-train chance, tales, death at Hateful); a non-scout still gets the simplified flat 10–80 gift (FreeCol reserves chief audiences for scouts — a documented interim that keeps the existing visit usable). Documented deviations from FreeCol `scoutSpeakToChief`: (a) **no native-gold deduction** — the beads add to the player but aren't subtracted from a native treasury (we model none, as with treasure plunder). (b) The reveal uses the fixed `TalesRevealRadius` (3) rather than the scout's line-of-sight max'd with `TALES_RADIUS`. (c) **"already-scouted → nothing"** is represented by `CheckVisit` **refusing** a re-visit (HasBeenVisited) rather than allowing a no-op audience — same null effect, but it doesn't burn the scout's move. (d) The expert-train trigger uses `rnd==0` **or** the chief literally teaching `model.unit.seasonedScout`; FreeCol's broader "the learnable skill *has* the EXPERT_SCOUT ability" is approximated by that id check. No alarm change occurs on speaking (matches FreeCol).
- **Learn-skill gating.** FreeCol lets you *attempt* a learn at an angry/hateful settlement (the unit learns nothing, or dies). We gate it in the `Check` (Angry+ is refused) — no random death yet. The learnable-colonist set is now **read from the spec's `model.unitChange.natives` change-type** (`86d3fpxaw`, `Ruleset.CanLearnSkillFromNatives`), not hard-coded — classic free colonist / indentured servant, and a variant ruleset can restrict who learns what without code.
- **Settlement counts & placement regions.** FreeCol assigns each nation a named map *region* and scales settlement counts by landmass and difficulty. Our map has no named regions yet, so we place settlements greedily on suitable tiles with a fixed per-band count (`NativeSettlementGenerator.TargetCount`). The *suitability* rule (settleable + ≥50% land neighbourhood) and the *capital-first* rule are kept. Counts/regions can be tuned toward FreeCol when the map gains regions.
- **Aggression / skills are parsed but not yet used.** They feed the tension and learn-skill slices (P5 slices 3–4); stored now so the data layer is complete.
- **`<plunder>` and `<gifts>` are parsed** (`SettlementType.Plunder` for combat plunder, `SettlementType.Gifts` for a scout's beads). Both are FreeCol `RandomRange`s read off the settlement type.

## 3. Technical design

**Domain model:**
- `Specification.NativeNationType` (record) — one per concrete nation: its non-capital + capital `SettlementType` ids, `SettlementNumber` band, `NativeAggression`, the weighted `NativeSkill` list, and preferred region ids. Abstract templates (`default`/`camp`/`village`/`city`) are resolved away, not exposed.
- `Specification.SettlementType` (record) — a camp/village/city template (capital variant is a separate record): sizes, claimable radii, trade bonus, convert threshold, and the parsed `model.modifier.defence` percentage (used by the combat slice).
- `Natives.NativeSettlement` — a placed settlement: id, owning `NationTypeId`, `SettlementTypeId`, `IsCapital`, `Position`, `Size`, `LearnableSkill`, plus interaction state — **per-player alarm** (`_alarm`: `Dictionary<int,int>` keyed by `Player.PlayerId`, FreeCol `IndianSettlement`'s `Map<Player,Tension>`; `AlarmFor(playerId)` reads, default 0; internal `SetAlarm(playerId, value)` drops a zero channel; `AlarmChannels` enumerates non-zero channels ordered by id, for the save; the legacy `Alarm` int property is sugar for channel 0 = the human) / `MostHated` (`int?`, FreeCol `getMostHated`, recomputed by `Game.UpdateMostHated`) / `AlarmLevel` (the human's computed band) / **per-player chief visits** (`_visitedBy` set; `HasBeenVisitedBy(playerId)`/`MarkVisitedBy`; `HasBeenVisited` kept as the human/player-0 convenience; `VisitedByPowers` for the save) / `SkillConsumed` / `WantedGoods` (up to 3, **stock-driven** — recomputed from the general stock by `Game.RecomputeWantedGoods`) / `WantedSlot` (all internal-set) / a muskets/horses **military stock** (`StockOf`/`AddStock`/`MilitaryStock` — the source the native AI's `TryEquipBrave` arms braves from; **seeded at generation** by type/capital/size and **serialized** at save v54, omit-when-empty, `86d3e49gq`) / a **general goods stock** (`GeneralStockOf`/`AddGoods`/`GeneralStock` — the non-military wares a trader buys and the basis of the stock-driven wanted goods, FreeCol's `IndianSettlement` goods container; **seeded at generation** and **serialized** at save v57, omit-when-empty, `86d3e4bh2`). (A minimal "owner" concept — the full multi-player `Player`/`Nation` refactor is deferred to the foreign-European slice.)
- `Natives.AlarmLevel` (enum Happy→Hateful) — the hostility band, mapped from a player's alarm by the FreeCol `Tension.Level` limits. The band limits + the alarm ceiling are **data-overridable** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.NativeTension.HappyMax/ContentMax/DispleasedMax/AngryMax/MaxAlarm`, `86d3drpgg`): the rules engine bands via `NativeSettlement.AlarmLevelFor(playerId, tension)` (a private `Game.AlarmLevelOf(settlement, playerId)` wraps it with `Ruleset.Difficulty.NativeTension`; a `Game.AlarmLevelOf(settlement)` overload defaults to the human's channel for the raid AI), while the parameterless `NativeSettlement.AlarmLevel` / `AlarmLevelFor(tension)` band the **human's** channel against `NativeTensionOptions.ClassicMedium` for ruleset-free callers (presentation) — byte-identical to the old hardcoded thresholds. `ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, playerId, delta)` clamps the acting player's channel to the routed `MaxAlarm`; the 2-arg `ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, delta)` overload targets the human's channel.

**Data sources:** `specification.xml` → `<indian-nation-types>` (parsed in `Ruleset.ParseNativeNationTypes`). `extends` chains are resolved nearest-wins for attributes and the settlement template; **skills and regions accumulate** down the chain (so the Inca inherit the abstract `city` skills and add their own). A null section (minimal test rulesets) yields no native nations.

**Algorithms & formulas:** `World/NativeSettlementGenerator.Place(ruleset, map, random, excluded)` — suitable tiles are gathered and Fisher–Yates-shuffled (seeded), capitals placed first, then non-capitals round-robin; `TakeTile` enforces the min-distance against placed settlements and the excluded set. Sizes/skills drawn from the RNG.

**Integration points:** generated once in `Game.New` on a **dedicated RNG stream** (`NativeStreamId = 1`, ADR-009) so placement cannot shift the economy/father/immigration draws (stream 0). Exposed via `Game.NativeSettlements` and `Game.NativeSettlementAt(pos)`. Rendering: `GameController.SyncNativeMarkers` makes one `NativeSettlementMarker` per settlement **on an explored tile** (fog-gated), in the scene's `MapView/NativeLayer`; art selected per settlement type (FreeCol `indian_camp` / `indian_village` / `inca_city` / `aztec_city`, ADR-014).

**Interaction flow** (`Game.cs`): `CheckVisit`/`Visit`, `CheckLearnSkill`/`LearnSkill`, `CheckSellToNatives`/`SellToNatives` (+ `NativeSalePrice`) and `CheckBuyFromNatives`/`BuyFromNatives` (+ `NativeBuyPrice`/`GoodsToSell`) follow the standard `Check…`-oracle pattern (ADR-006); `Visit` draws from the owner's stream and branches on the unit's role — a scout takes `ScoutSpeakToChief` (die/expert/tales/beads via `GiftsAmount` off `SettlementType.Gifts`), any other colonist `VisitAsColonist` (the flat gift). An internal `Visit(player, unit, settlement, IGameRandom)` overload threads a scripted RNG for tests (the `Attack` pattern); the public/3-arg paths pass `RandomFor(player)` (the human's `_random`), so save/resume stays deterministic — no extra stream needed. Learning replaces the unit via `UpgradeUnitType` (keeps id/position/location/cargo, since `Unit.Type` is immutable). **Selling** credits gold with no tax, adds the goods to the settlement store, lowers alarm and recomputes wanted goods; **buying** drains the store, loads the ship, debits gold, lowers alarm a little and recomputes wanted goods — both end the ship's turn (the public 4-arg `SellToNatives`/`BuyFromNatives` route to the human, an internal `(player, …)` overload to any colonial owner). The price helpers `NativeNormalUnitPrice` (the stock-fill base, shared by buy/sell/ranking), `NativeWantedMultiplier`, `NativeGoodsCapacity` (`100 × claimable-radius`) and `WantedRankingValue` (farmed-halved, ranking only) sit beside them. **Haggling** is `TryHaggleSell`/`TryHaggleBuy` returning `NativeHaggleResult(Accepted, CounterPrice, Done)`, with `HaggleUp`/`HaggleDown` and the shared `ResolveHaggle` patience roll drawn on the lazily-seeded `_nativeHaggleRandom` (`NativeTradeStreamId` = 104, never serialized — interactive, not replayed). `Game.ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, playerId, delta)` is the public per-player alarm mutator (every gate above keys on the acting player — `player.PlayerId` / `unit.OwnerId` / `ship.OwnerId`); `DecayNativeAlarm` runs for every settlement in `EndTurn` (see [turns](turns.md)). **Wanted goods are stock-driven** (`Game.RecomputeWantedGoods`, FreeCol `updateWantedGoods`): the general store is seeded in a post-placement pass in `NativeSettlementGenerator` (after the placement RNG draws, so placement output is unchanged), then `Game.New`/`Restore` derive each settlement's wanted goods from it (RNG-free) and every buy/sell re-derives them.

**Trade UI seam** (`presentation/NativeSettlementPanel.cs`, `86d3f62qh`): the on-map panel renders the trade actions over the same oracles (ADR-006 — presentation reads oracles + forwards commands; no engine change). `BuildActions` adds a **Sell goods** button when the acting unit is a carrier carrying any good the `CheckSellToNatives` gate would allow, and a **Buy goods** button when the carrier has free hold space (`CargoSlotsFree > 0`) and some entry of `GoodsToSell` passes `CheckBuyFromNatives`. **Per-player tension (`86d3e4bfb`, save v55).** A settlement's alarm is a **map keyed by colonial `Player.PlayerId`** (FreeCol `IndianSettlement.getAlarm(Player)` / its `Map<Player,Tension>`), so a tribe can be friendly to one power and hostile to another. Each interaction/combat hook keys on the acting player: visit/learn/trade/mission/tribute on the acting unit's owner; combat tension (`ApplyNativeCombatTension(nation, playerId, delta)`) and the capital-surrender reset (`SetAlarm(attacker.OwnerId, Surrendered)`) on `attacker.OwnerId`; land-taken/burial-ground on the acting player; the declaration-of-independence native re-stancing on `rebel.PlayerId`. **`MostHated`** (`NativeSettlement.MostHated`, `int?`) mirrors FreeCol `getMostHated`: among the colonial players whose band is **not Happy**, the highest-alarm one (ties → lowest id), else null; `UpdateMostHated` runs after every change. **Human-centric paths are unchanged** and read channel 0 via the no-player overloads: the per-turn **ambient pressure** (`ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` — only the human's colonies/units stir alarm), the **raid/demand AI** (braves only raid the human, so `AlarmLevelOf(home)`/`AdjacentDemandableHumanColony` read channel 0), and `ResetNativeAlarm` (forgets the human's anger only). **Native AI** (`Game.cs`, slice 1b): `RunPlayerTurn` dispatches a `PlayerType.Native` player to `RunNativeTurn(player)` (the colonial/foreign path is unchanged). `RunNativeTurn` iterates `_units.Where(u => u.OwnerNationId == player.NationId).OrderBy(u => u.Id)` over a `.ToList()` snapshot (a raid can remove the prey or the brave mid-loop). Per unit: skip if off-map or out of movement; resolve `HomeSettlement` (nearest same-nation `NativeSettlement`, Chebyshev + position tiebreak) and compare `home.AlarmLevel >= RaidAlarmThreshold` (`AlarmLevel.Displeased`); **before any brave acts, `RedistributeArmsToThreatenedSettlements(player)` ships a half-unit of surplus muskets/horses from a calm stocked camp to a threatened bare one, then `EquipBravesAtThreatenedSettlements(player)` arms each threatened camp's braves from its (now possibly topped-up) stock** (the securing facet — see below); equipping is free, so it doesn't consume a brave's action; then, per unit, if hostile and `AdjacentPillageableHumanColony` (a human colony where `CheckPillageColony` allows — adjacent, undefended, lootable goods) exists, `PillageColony` it (the high-value branch, before the unit-hunt); else if hostile, no demand is already pending, and `AdjacentDemandableHumanColony(player, brave)` (a human colony adjacent to the brave for which `SelectDemand` finds something to take) exists, `CreateNativeDemand` against it; else if `PickRaidTarget` (the best-scored human land unit-tile within the 8/12/16 ladder by `ScoreUnitTarget`, falling back to `NearestHumanUnit` at any range — `86d3c9vzp` displeasure weighting) yields a tile, attack when adjacent (via `RaidHumanUnit`) else `StepToward` it; otherwise `Wander` one legal neighbour. `StepToward(player, unit, goal)` is the shared pathing helper (factored out of `StepTowardNearestUnexplored`): the legal adjacent move minimising Chebyshev to the goal, ties broken by position then `RandomFor(player)`. `RaidHumanUnit` calls the internal `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` overload (the nation's own stream) and appends a `CombatNotice` to the transient `_combatNotices`. `PillageColony(brave, target, RandomFor(player))` resolves combat vs a transient unarmed-colonist defender; on a win it picks one option uniformly over `loot.Count + (HumanPlayer.Gold > 0 ? 1 : 0)` — a goods stack (steals `min(amount/2, 50)`, skipped if a 1-unit stack yields 0) or, on the **last** index, **gold** (`min(max(1, ColonyPlunderAmount/5), HumanPlayer.Gold)` off the human) — and appends a `ColonyRaidNotice` (attacker-nation id, colony name, **goods id or null for gold**, amount, position) to the transient `_colonyRaidNotices`; on a loss `ResolveLoserOutcome` slays the brave. The gold-plunder draw (`ColonyPlunderAmount`) uses the nation's `random`, never stream 0. Iteration is stable by id/position — verified by `NativeAiTests` + `NativePillageTests` + `NativeDemandTests` (same-seed byte-identical; the human's stream 0 untouched by raids/pillage/demands; the 30-turn demand stream-0 test) and the L5 soak.

**Native land ownership** (`World/NativeLandClaimGenerator.cs` + `GameMap`): `GameMap` holds a sparse `Dictionary<Position,string>` `_nativeOwners` (tile → owning nation type id), parallel to `_resources`/`_rumours`, exposed by `IsNativeOwned`/`NativeOwnerOf`/`NativeOwners` with internal `SetNativeOwner`/`ClearNativeOwner` (the clear/transfer mutators land-purchase will use). `NativeLandClaimGenerator.Claim(map, settlements, ruleset)` is a **pure** function: for each settlement (ordered by id) it walks its `ClaimableRadius` Chebyshev box, skips off-map/water tiles, and keeps the best claimant per tile (min distance, then min id) — returning `tile → nation`. `Game.ClaimNativeLand()` is **idempotent** (it clears the map's claims first, then repopulates), so it can be re-run safely whenever the settlements change: it runs in **`Game.New`** (after native settlements are placed), **`Game.Restore`** (after settlements are restored), and **when a native settlement is destroyed** in `AttackSettlement` (the razed settlement releases its claim; surviving same-nation settlements keep theirs). Because the claim draws **no randomness** and is recomputed on load, ownership is never serialised (no save bump) and cannot perturb any RNG stream — the L5 soak stays byte-stable. Consumed by: the Lost City Rumour burial-ground gate (next) and native land purchase (below).

**Native land purchase** (`Game.cs`, `86d3c9tha`): `LandPrice(player, tile)` = `LandPriceFactor(60) × Ruleset.GoodsTypes.Where(!IsFood).Sum(g => TileYieldPotential(tile, g.Id)) + LandPriceBase(100)`, then the player's `model.modifier.landPaymentModifier` via `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, LandPaymentModifierId, raw)` (which stacks every matching modifier by index; Peter Minuit's −100% → 0). The sum uses **`TileYieldPotential`** — the terrain's attended output + on-tile resource bonus **without** any founding-father goods modifier (FreeCol `getPotentialProduction` with a null owner) — so e.g. Henry Hudson's +furs never inflates what land *costs* (only what a colony *produces*; `TileYield` = `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …, TileYieldPotential(…))` keeps that split). Returns 0 off native land. `CheckClaimLand` gates off-map / non-native / a native-settlement tile (the cost carries the price). `ClaimLandByPaying` checks affordability, deducts the price (nothing credited — no native treasury), and `Map.ClaimFromNatives(tile)`; `ClaimLandByStealing` records the owning nation, `Map.ClaimFromNatives(tile)`, then `ChangeNativeAlarm(s, LandTakenAlarm=200)` for every settlement of that nation. **The saved override:** `GameMap._claimedFromNatives` (a `HashSet<Position>`, public `IsClaimedFromNatives`/`ClaimedFromNatives`, internal `ClaimFromNatives` which both records the tile and clears its live native owner) is the one piece of native ownership that **is** saved — `ClaimNativeLand` subtracts it after every re-derivation (New/Restore/destruction), so a bought tile never reverts. Save **v26** (`SaveGame.ClaimedTiles`, row-major indexes, additive + omitted-when-empty → a no-purchase game is byte-identical to v25). No RNG is drawn, so the soak stays byte-stable.

**Missionaries — establish** (`Game.cs`, `86d3c9t6e` slice 1): `NativeSettlement` gains `MissionOwnerId` (`int?`, the colonial player whose missionary resides here; null = none), `MissionIsExpert` (`bool`, jesuit vs ordinary colonist — captured at establish-time so the per-turn skill term is recoverable without a unit reference), and the `HasMission` helper. `CheckEstablishMission(unit, settlement)` (the ADR-006 oracle) gates on-map + `Ruleset.Role(unit.RoleId).GrantedAbilities[model.ability.establishMission]` + movement-left + on/adjacent — **not** alarm. `EstablishMission` (public 1-arg → human; internal `(player,…)`; **no `IGameRandom` overload — RNG-free**): if `settlement.AlarmLevel >= AlarmLevel.Angry` → `_units.Remove(unit)` (the missionary is killed) and return false; else set `MissionOwnerId`/`MissionIsExpert` (the latter via `unit.Type.Id == Ruleset.Role(unit.RoleId).ExpertUnit` — variant-faithful, not a hardcoded jesuit id), `ChangeNativeAlarm(settlement, -100)` (goodwill, FreeCol `ALARM_NEW_MISSIONARY`), `RevealAround(player, settlement.Position, LineOfSightOf(unit))`, `_units.Remove(unit)` (consumed into the settlement), return true. **Missionaries — denounce a rival mission** (`Game.cs`, `86d3c9t7z`, FreeCol `InGameController.denounceMission`): when a missionary tries to establish at a settlement that **already holds a rival player's mission**, `EstablishMission` routes to `DenounceMission` instead of silently overwriting (FreeCol splits the two via `MissionaryMessage`'s `denounce` flag; re-establishing over your **own** mission stays the plain RNG-free install). **Missionaries — convert accrual** (`Game.cs`, `86d3c9t6e` slice 2): `NativeSettlement.ConvertProgress` (int). `ProcessMissions()` runs in `EndTurn` after `ApplyAmbientNativeAlarm` and **before** the `DecayNativeAlarm` loop: for each settlement with a mission whose owner still exists, `ConvertProgress += (skill + ConversionSkillBonus[6]) + min(Alarm, MaxAlarm) * ConversionAlarmRatePercent[2] / 100` (skill = `JesuitConversionSkill[3]` if `expert` else 0, where `expert = settlement.MissionIsExpert || HasAbilityFor(owner, ExpertMissionaryAbility)` — so **Father Jean de Brébeuf** (`model.ability.expertMissionary`, the first *continuous* per-turn father ability, folded per-owner exactly like the combat/yield abilities) makes an ordinary mission accrue at the jesuit rate; see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)). **Incite a native settlement against a rival** (`Game.Natives.cs`, `86d3fq00w`/`86d3fq0bt`, FreeCol `InGameController.incite`): a **missionary** (a unit whose role grants `model.ability.inciteNatives` — `model.role.missionary` does, alongside establish/denounce) at/adjacent to a settlement can pay the chief to turn the tribe against a chosen European rival. **Natives-depth fidelity pass** (`Game.Natives.cs` + `NativeSettlement.cs`, wave "Natives-depth": `86d3fpzqd`/`86d3fpzvy`/`86d3fpzkq`/`86d3fpzx1`). Four faithful deepenings, all engine logic in the `Game.Natives.cs` partial (the trade/alarm core in `Game.cs` is read/called, not edited, save three single-line orchestration hooks noted below):

1. **Haggle honours the named price** (`86d3fpzqd`, FreeCol's price-carrying `NativeTrade`/`handleTrade` ACK). New **price-carrying overloads** `SellToNatives(unit, settlement, goods, amount, agreedPrice)` / `BuyFromNatives(…, agreedPrice)` (public → human, internal `(player,…)`) transact at the **agreed** figure, not the standard `NativeSalePrice`/`NativeBuyPrice`. The agreed price is validated by `ClampHaggledSalePrice`/`ClampHaggledBuyPrice` against the haggle band — a sale is floored at the standard offer and capped at `HaggleSellPriceCeilingPercent` (200%) of it; a buy is capped at the standard ask and floored at `HaggleBuyPriceFloorPercent` (50%) — an out-of-band figure from the UI degrades to the standard price, so the panel **cannot mint gold** (ADR-006: the engine is the authority, the UI only presents a figure the haggle oracle could have reached). Goodwill (`ALARM_BONUS_SELL`/`BUY`) scales with the gold actually moved. The `NativeSettlementPanel` haggle flow now passes `_haggleCounter` (the chief's standing/accepted price) to these overloads; on a successful push `_haggleCounter` is set to the player's accepted offer before committing.
2. **Settlement goods store driven by native production over time** (`86d3fpzvy`, FreeCol `ServerIndianSettlement.csNewTurn` producing tile goods into the store). `ProduceNativeGoods(settlement, rng)` replenishes the **general goods store** one turn: for each raw storable tradeable New-World good (`IsFarmed && IsNewWorldGoods && IsStorable && IsTradeable && !IsMilitary && !IsFood`, spec order), it gathers `NativeProductionPerResident(2) × Size × headroom / ceiling + rng.Next(0, Size+1)` units (a **stock-fill curve** — a good already near the `NativeProductionStoreCeiling` (= `NativeTradeCargoSize` 100) grows slowly; a good already at the ceiling produces nothing), then re-prices wanted goods. Production is **abstracted** (we don't model the natives' tiles): a documented simplification, deliberately **under-producing** the full tile potential so trade stays worthwhile. `ProduceNativeNationGoods(nation)` is the per-nation driver — settlements in id order, drawing only on the nation's stream (`RandomFor(nation)`).
