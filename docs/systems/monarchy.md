## 1. How it works (plain English)

Back home, your **King** keeps an eye on your colonies. Each turn — once the colony is a few decades old — he weighs his options and may do something: most turns he does **nothing**, but as the game goes on he grows bolder. He can **raise your taxes** — or, when he's in good humour, do you a kindness instead: **lower your tax rate** by a little. He can also build up the army he'd send to crush a rebellion, declare war or peace on a rival on your behalf, or **offer you mercenaries and military support**. Early on, and while you have no colonies, he leaves you alone entirely.

**Taxes climb gradually, not in a rush.** When the King raises your tax, he then **leaves it alone for a while** — at the normal difficulty, about **9 turns** must pass before he can demand another rise. So your sales tax creeps up over the course of the game and tends to sit in the 40s–50s by the late game, rather than being hammered up to its 65% ceiling within a few decades. (This is a deliberate departure from FreeCol, whose King has *no* such cooldown and can demand a rise turn after turn — the FreeCol team themselves note their monarch is "more aggressive with tax increases than the original Colonization monarchs were." Our cooldown restores the original game's manageable, gradual climb.) You can't dodge the cooldown by saving and reloading — the King remembers when he last taxed you.

**Your tax rate also sets the King's treasure cut.** Your sales-tax rate does double duty: besides skimming your European sales, it is exactly the cut the King takes to ship a **treasure train** home from a colony for you (`86d3fb5mj`). At 25% tax he keeps 25% of the haul; the higher he's pushed your taxes, the more expensive it is to let him carry your gold — which is one more reason to keep taxes in check (or to sail the treasure home yourself, fee-free and tax-free). See [treasure-train](treasure-train.md).

**The goodwill tax action.** A *tax cut* (LOWER_TAX) shaves a few points off your sales tax there and then — no choice for you, just a happier purse. There is also a WAIVE_TAX action, but it is **purely a message** ("We graciously waive a tax increase") with **no effect** — the King simply forbore a tax rise this turn; nothing in your colonies or treasury changes. (This matches the original game / FreeCol exactly. An earlier build of ours had WAIVE_TAX forgive your back-taxes; that extension was removed to stay faithful.)

This first piece is the King *deciding* — the weighted dice-roll each turn that picks what (if anything) he does. What each decision actually *does* to your game (the tax demand, the mercenary offer, the war fleet) is being added piece by piece.

**What the player sees and does:** the King's two *answerable* moves now pop up a **dialog** asking you to decide. When he **raises your taxes**, the dialog names the proposed new rate and the goods he's eyeing — you can **pay the tax** or **refuse and hold a tea party** (dumping the goods and triggering a boycott). When he **offers you mercenaries**, it shows the force and the gold price — you can **hire them** or **decline** (and a spurned King turns cold, sending no more offers). His other moves — cutting your tax, declaring war or peace for you, granting free troops, or quietly growing his army back home — still happen **under the hood** with no prompt, because in the original game you don't get a say in those. One detail that matters for fairness: if the King has made an answerable demand (a tax rise, or a mercenary offer) and you save the game before answering, the demand is **kept in the save** — you can't dodge a tax hike by saving and reloading; the prompt is still waiting when you load back in.

**Lifting a boycott.** Once a good is under **boycott** (after a tea party), you can pay the King his back-tax to trade it again. The **colony screen** grows a **Boycotts** section listing each boycotted good, the arrears owed, and a **Pay** button; paying lifts the boycott. The button is greyed (showing the cost) when you can't afford it. (Electing Jacob Fugger lifts *all* boycotts at once instead — see [founding-fathers.md](founding-fathers.md).)

## 2. Detailed rules

- The King acts at most **once per turn**, decided in a single weighted pick. He does nothing at all when **any** of these hold (the *grace gate*, no dice rolled):
  - the turn number is below the **grace period** = `(6 − dx) × 10` = **30** at medium (`dx = 1 + monarchMeddling`, medium meddling = 2 → `dx = 3`);
  - the player has **no colonies**;
  - the player has already **declared independence** (is no longer a plain colonial power).
- Past the gate, the chooser offers **NO_ACTION** plus every *valid* action, each at its FreeCol weight, and picks one in proportion:

  | Action | Weight (medium) | Offered when valid… |
  |---|---|---|
  | NO_ACTION | `max(200 − turn, 100)` | always (dominates early, floors at 100) |
  | RAISE_TAX (act / war) | `5 + dx` = 8 each | tax < 65 **and** not within the inter-raise grace (see below) |
  | LOWER_TAX (war / other) | `5 − dx` = 2 each | tax > 30 |
  | ADD_TO_REF | `10 + dx` = 13 | always (grows the [Royal Expeditionary Force](royal-expeditionary-force.md)) |
  | DECLARE_PEACE | `6 − dx` = 3 | a rival is at war with you |
  | DECLARE_WAR | `5 + dx` = 8 | a rival is at peace with you |
  | MONARCH_MERCENARIES | `6 − dx` = 3 | at war, not displeased, ≥ 200 gold |
  | SUPPORT_LAND | `3 − dx` | **only when `dx < 3` AND gold < 200** — *never offered at medium* (matches FreeCol's own `getActionChoices` gate exactly) |
  | SUPPORT_SEA | `6 − dx` = 3 | raided by privateers, not yet granted, not displeased |
  | HESSIAN_MERCENARIES | `6 − dx` = 3 | ≥ 5000 gold, has colonies |

- `FORCE_TAX` and `DISPLEASURE` are **never** chosen by the dice — they are consequences of a player *response* (later items).

### Inter-raise tax grace period (the runaway-tax fix, `86d3f674b`)

- **The chooser withholds both RAISE_TAX actions for a grace period after the King makes a tax demand.** The grace is `(6 − dx) × 3` turns = **9 at medium** (`dx = 1 + monarchMeddling`); a more-meddlesome King (higher `dx`) has a shorter cooldown, mirroring the `(6 − dx)` scaling of the initial grace, floored at 1 turn. The clock starts the turn the demand is *made* (`Player.LastTaxRaiseTurn`), **whatever the player answers** — accept, FORCE_TAX, or a tea party — so the King cannot pile demands turn-on-turn. Everything else (the per-turn probability, the `5 + dx` weight, the increment formula) is unchanged; only *when* a raise may be offered is gated.
- **Why this deviates from FreeCol.** FreeCol has **no** inter-raise cooldown: its monarch re-runs the same weighted roll every turn, so the RNG produces clusters of consecutive raise demands that can slam the tax into its 65% cap by the mid-game. The FreeCol team acknowledge this — "FreeCol monarchs are currently more aggressive with tax increases than Col1 monarchs were" — and the community's own proposed fix is exactly a grace period between raises (FreeCol forum threads `fba2e12b` / `74d6d78a`). Since *Colonization* (Col1) is our fidelity target and FreeCol concedes it overshoots here, we add the cooldown to restore Col1's gradual climb. Measured over 300 turns on fixed seeds it cuts the raise count from ~25–31 (cap reached by the mid-game) to ~12–18 (tax in the 40s–50s late-game) with zero consecutive-turn clustering — see the L2 cadence scenario test.
- The grace is **persisted** (`Player.LastTaxRaiseTurn`, save **v60**, omit-when-null) so a reload mid-cooldown cannot reset it (ADR-009). It draws nothing itself — it only changes which actions the ephemeral monarch RNG is offered — so the human's stream 0 is untouched and the soak stays twin-deterministic.

### Tax actions (item 2)

- **RAISE_TAX** opens a *demand* the player answers (accept / reject). The proposed new rate is `min(tax + 1 + rnd[0, 3 + turn/40), 65)`. The King names the player's **most valuable tradeable stockpile** (highest sale-value across colonies, one cargo's worth); a raise demand is only made when there is such a good.
  - **Accept** → the tax rises to the proposed rate.
  - **Reject, goods already gone** (sold/moved before answering) → the King raises it anyway, **+3** (FORCE_TAX).
  - **Reject, goods still in the colony** → a **Boston Tea Party**: the colony dumps the demanded goods overboard, the good is **boycotted** (back-tax = its sale price × 300 — it cannot be sold until paid off), rebel sentiment **surges** (+50% bell output for 25 turns, decaying −2%/turn), and the **tax is not raised**. Pay the back-tax (`PayArrears`, costs the full arrears) to lift the boycott. The **colony screen** surfaces this (`86d3fpyu0`): a **Boycotts** section (rendered only when some good has `Market.Arrears > 0`) lists each boycotted good with a **Pay (N)** button gated on `CheckPayArrears(goodsId)` (greyed with the reason when unaffordable) that forwards to `GameController.PayBoycott` → `Game.PayArrears(goodsId)`. Pure presentation (ADR-006); the boycott is read from the shared human `Game.Market`.
- **LOWER_TAX** (war / other goodwill) applies immediately, no player choice: `max(tax − 1 − rnd[0, 8), 20)` (`LowerTaxAmount`). Validity gate: `tax > MINIMUM_TAX_RATE + 10` (= 30), FreeCol exactly.
- **WAIVE_TAX** — **message-only, no mechanical effect** (FreeCol-exact). The King announces "We graciously waive a tax increase" and **nothing in game state changes** — no arrears forgiven, no tax change. It is **always valid** (`MonarchActionIsValid` returns true unconditionally, mirroring `Monarch.actionIsValid`) but FreeCol **omits it from `getActionChoices`**, so the dice **never picks it** — it only exists as the server's framing for a King who *forbore* a tax rise. We match FreeCol exactly: not in the chooser, dispatched as a no-op.

### Mercenary offers + the King's displeasure (item 4)

- **MONARCH_MERCENARIES** (offered at war, not displeased, ≥ 200 gold) and **HESSIAN_MERCENARIES** (≥ 5000 gold) offer a force of **veteran soldiers** (armed or mounted — 2-3 groups of 1-2), priced at the European purchase price **× 65%**, **trimmed to what your treasury can afford** (no affordable units → no offer).
- **Accept** → pay the gold, the veterans appear on your **Europe dock**. **Decline an offer you could afford** → the King is **displeased** (`DISPLEASURE`): he offers **no more mercenaries or military support** for the rest of the game.
- *Faithful-subset:* the mercenary force is veteran soldiers (the classic land mercenary); the naval man-o-war mercenary and full ability-driven type selection are simplified. The mercenary **price percentage** is difficulty-data-driven (`mercenaryPrice`, medium 65 — see [difficulty](difficulty.md)); the force **composition** is still a hardcoded faithful-subset.

### Free military support (item 5)

- **SUPPORT_SEA** (offered after a **privateer raid**, if not already granted and the King isn't displeased) grants **one free naval ship** on your Europe dock — a **one-shot** (`SupportSeaGranted`). **SUPPORT_LAND** grants free land troops (medium support level 2 = **2 mounted veterans**). Both are free and immediate (no demand).
- *Reachability:* `SUPPORT_LAND` is **never offered at medium** (the chooser only lists it when `dx < 3`, and only in the gold-poor `else` branch, gold < 200). **This is FreeCol-faithful, not a simplification:** FreeCol's own `Monarch.getActionChoices` gates the SUPPORT_LAND *offer* on `dx < 3` (the meddling-derived dx), NOT on the `monarchSupport` difficulty option — `monarchSupport` only sizes the *granted* force in `getSupport`. So vanilla FreeCol at medium (dx = 3) also never offers SUPPORT_LAND. Our `GetMonarchActionChoices` reproduces that gate byte-for-byte (tested: offered at meddling 1 / dx = 2 with gold < 200 and at-war; withheld at medium). `SUPPORT_SEA` needs a privateer raid on the human — **now reachable** (`86d3fq0b0`): when a privateer (`model.ability.piracy`) attacks a human-owned unit, `Game.Attack` sets the human's `AttackedByPrivateers` flag (FreeCol `csCombat`), which is exactly the SUPPORT_SEA validity gate (`MonarchActionIsValid`). So after a privateer raid the King can offer his one free warship in the normal monarch tick. Both handlers + validity gates are implemented and tested (SUPPORT_SEA now end-to-end from the privateer hook; SUPPORT_LAND still forced via the handler since it's never offered at medium). See [combat](combat.md).

### Declare war / declare peace (item `86d3c9r7j`)

- **DECLARE_WAR** (offered when at least one European rival is at **peace** with you) — the King drags you into his war: he picks one peace-standing rival and sets your stance to **War**. No player choice (it's the King's decree, immediate).
- **DECLARE_PEACE** (offered when at least one rival is at **war or cease-fire** with you) — the King makes peace for you: he picks one such rival and sets your stance back to **Peace**.
- The King's target is chosen on **his own RNG** (never your stream 0), so a default game stays byte-identical. Stance change only — no tension delta is applied (the war simply *exists*), and no save change (stances already persist). A no-op if, despite the gate, no eligible rival remains.
- **Benjamin Franklin stops the meddling.** Once you elect **Benjamin Franklin** to your Continental Congress (his `ignoreEuropeanWars` ability — see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)), the King can **no longer declare war on your behalf**: the potential-enemy list is empty, so DECLARE_WAR is never offered and a stray dispatch does nothing. He can **still make peace** for you — Franklin stops wars, not peace (faithful to FreeCol's `collectPotentialEnemies` vs `collectPotentialFriends`).
- **War support (FreeCol `getWarSupport`).** When the King *does* declare a war for you, he may also send a one-off **war-support force** (a band of veteran soldiers, landed on your Europe dock) plus **war-support gold** to your treasury — the Crown helping fund the fight it picked. Whether he helps is the FreeCol strength calculation: he **always** helps when the rival is at least as strong as you (strength ratio ≤ 0.5), **never** when you are clearly stronger (ratio > 0.6), and a sliding chance in between (a cautious, stingy Crown). The amounts read from the [difficulty](difficulty.md) data (`warSupportForce` = **4** veteran soldiers, `warSupportGold` = **1500** at medium), the force's per-unit count varied ±1 (a single token unit against a defenceless rival) and the gold varied ±20% by the King's own roll. The grant is free and immediate (units + gold; no new save state).
- **The tax rate doubles as the treasure-transport cut (`86d3fb5mj`).** In the Col1 cash-in model the King's cut to ship a treasure train home **from a colony** is the player's **current `TaxRate`** (`Game.TransportFee` = `taxRate × amount / 100`, waived by Hernán Cortés) — not FreeCol's flat 60%. So a tax rise the King wins here also makes his treasure transport dearer. Treasure the player carries home themselves to Europe is fee-free *and* tax-free. The mechanics live in [treasure-train](treasure-train.md) §2/§3; this is noted here because the **tax rate is the shared input**.
- **You are told what the King decreed (`86d3e4buh`).** Each of these immediate actions — lowering or waiving your tax, declaring war or peace for you, sending free support, growing his expeditionary force — now produces an **end-of-turn notice** ("👑 The Crown declared war on the French on your behalf.", "…lowered your tax rate to 18%.", "…reinforced the Royal Expeditionary Force with 2 units.", and so on) that appears in the turn-events panel and is kept in the re-openable message log. The two demands you actually answer (a tax rise, a mercenary offer) still come up as the King's dialog instead.

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** the chooser, weights, grace and validity gates are FreeCol's exactly (WAIVE_TAX included — it is message-only and out of the chooser, exactly as FreeCol). The monarch's **tuning numbers** — meddling, tax cap/spread, mercenary price, support size, boycott factor, the REF base composition and the **war-support force + gold** — are now **difficulty-data-driven** (`Ruleset.Difficulty.Monarch`, default = classic medium; see [difficulty](difficulty.md), [royal-expeditionary-force](royal-expeditionary-force.md)), so they are transposable per game-mode / variant. The war-support **strength yardstick** is our land-power `StrengthRatio` (the same one the AI diplomacy uses), a faithful subset of FreeCol's full `calculateStrength` (which also folds naval + settlement strength) — the cautious-Crown thresholds (always ≤ 0.5, never > 0.6) are FreeCol's exactly.

- **WAIVE_TAX is FreeCol-exact (message-only).** In FreeCol, `WAIVE_TAX` is a **message-only** action ("In Our wisdom, We have graciously decided to waive a tax increase") that changes **no** game state, and FreeCol **does not list it in `getActionChoices`** — the dice never picks it; it only appears as a server framing for a King who *forbore* a tax rise. We match this exactly: `MonarchActionIsValid(WaiveTax)` returns `true` unconditionally (as `Monarch.actionIsValid`), the chooser **does not add it**, and `DispatchMonarchAction` treats it as a **no-op** (the presentation surfaces the King's announcement; nothing else happens). *(An earlier build gave it a tangible goodwill — forgiving boycott back-taxes — gated on arrears; that extension was removed `86d3drphx` to stay faithful to FreeCol.)* LOWER_TAX is the genuine tax goodwill and is fully FreeCol-faithful (formula, weight and `tax > 30` gate).

- The only deliberate value-preserving deviation: the **boycott back-tax factor** stays the classic-game **300** (the spec's medium `arrearsFactor` is 500), kept on `MonarchOptions.ArrearsFactor` rather than read from the spec id. The remaining monarch numbers (`MINIMUM_TAX_RATE` 20, `MONARCH_MINIMUM_PRICE` 200, `HESSIAN_MINIMUM_PRICE` 5000, the FORCE_TAX +3 surcharge, the 25-turn tea-party bell duration) are FreeCol **code** constants (not difficulty options) and stay as C# consts.

## 3. Technical design

- `MonarchAction` (enum): the 15 FreeCol actions.
- `Game.MonarchActionIsValid(action)`: a pure, RNG-free predicate over the human's state (FreeCol `actionIsValid`). `ADD_TO_REF` is gated off until the REF Force is modelled (item 6).
- `Game.GetMonarchActionChoices(turn)`: the weighted `(weight, action)` list — empty before the grace gate; otherwise NO_ACTION + each valid action at its weight.
- `Game.RunMonarchTick()`: called once per round in `EndTurn` after `UpdateColonialStances`. If the chooser is empty it returns having drawn nothing; otherwise it picks one action via `RandomChoice.WeightedRandom` and dispatches it via `DispatchMonarchAction(action, monarchRng)`.
- **Tax (item 2 + goodwill):** `DispatchMonarchAction` handles `NO_ACTION`, `RAISE_TAX_*` (build a `PendingMonarchDemand` from `RaiseTaxAmount(rng)` + `GetMostValuableGoods`), `LOWER_TAX_*` (`SetTax(LowerTaxAmount(rng))` immediately), and `WAIVE_TAX` (**a no-op — message-only, FreeCol-exact**). `PendingMonarchDemand` (the ADR-006 oracle the UI reads — now surfaced by `MonarchDialog`, see below) is answered by `RespondToMonarch(bool accept)`, and is **persisted across save/load since v46** (see below). `RaiseTaxAmount`/`LowerTaxAmount` are the FreeCol formulas; tax reuses the existing `Player.TaxRate` save field. `WAIVE_TAX` is **always valid** (`MonarchActionIsValid` returns `true`, as `Monarch.actionIsValid`) but is **not added to `GetMonarchActionChoices`** — exactly FreeCol, where the dice never picks it. No save bump.
- **Inter-raise tax grace (`86d3f674b`, save v60):** `Game.TaxRaiseGraceTurns` = `max(1, (6 − (1 + Meddling)) × 3)` (9 at medium) is the cooldown; `Game.TaxRaiseOnCooldown(turn)` is `_human.LastTaxRaiseTurn is { } last && turn − last < TaxRaiseGraceTurns` (always false before the first raise). `GetMonarchActionChoices` skips adding `RAISE_TAX_ACT`/`RAISE_TAX_WAR` when `TaxRaiseOnCooldown(turn)`; `MonarchActionIsValid` is **unchanged** (its `tax < max` semantics still hold for the existing tests — the cooldown is a *chooser-offer* gate, matching FreeCol's "tax demands cannot be made" framing). `DispatchMonarchAction`'s `RAISE_TAX_*` case stamps `_human.LastTaxRaiseTurn = Turn` at the moment the demand is created (only when a demand is actually made — i.e. a taxable good exists), so the clock starts regardless of the player's later accept/FORCE_TAX/tea-party answer. `Player.LastTaxRaiseTurn` (`int?`) persists at **v60** (`SavedPlayer.LastTaxRaiseTurn`, omit-when-null → byte-identical to v59 until the first raise; pre-v60 saves load with no cooldown active). The gate draws no randomness; it only filters the chooser, so the ephemeral monarch RNG and stream 0 are untouched (soak twin-deterministic).
- **Pending demand persistence (`86d3c9rk6`, v46):** the `PendingMonarchDemand` (a RAISE_TAX demand, or a mercenary/Hessian offer) is now saved (FreeCol persists the `MonarchSession`), so a game saved while the King's prompt is open reloads with the demand still pending and answerable. `SaveGame.PendingMonarchDemand` (a `SavedMonarchDemand` mirroring the live record, incl. a mercenary offer's `SavedForceEntry` force) is **omitted when none is pending** — the common case — so a game with no open demand is byte-identical to v45; `Game.RestorePendingMonarchDemand` re-installs it on load and pre-v46 saves load with none. The King's other persisted state (tax/arrears/displeasure/support/REF) already rode v37–v40; the privateer-raid flag `AttackedByPrivateers` stays **transient by design** — it is set by the privateer-combat hook (`86d3fq0b0`, in `Game.Attack`) but not persisted: a privateer that has raided this session is a within-game condition, and the one-shot grant it enables (`SupportSeaGranted`) **is** saved, so a reload can't double-grant. (FreeCol likewise treats it as a derived combat-session flag, not part of the saved monarch session.)
- **Boston Tea Party / boycott (item 3):** `RespondToMonarch(false)` → if the goods are gone, FORCE_TAX (`SetTax(taxRaise+3)`); else `HoldTeaParty` — `colony.AddGoods(good, −amount)`, `Market.SetArrears(good, salePrice × 300)`, `colony.TeaPartyBellTurns = 25`. `Market` carries per-good `Arrears` (>0 = boycotted; `CanTrade` gates both `SellColonyGoods`/`SellShipCargo`). `Colony.TeaPartyBellTurns` adds `+2%`/turn to bell output (`AccumulateLibertyAndElectFathers`) and ticks down each turn. `CheckPayArrears`/`PayArrears` (ADR-006) lift the boycott for the full arrears. Save **v37**: `SavedPlayer.Arrears` (omit-when-empty) + `SavedColony.TeaPartyBellTurns` (omit-when-0). Remaining actions stay no-op until their slice.
- **Declare war / peace (`86d3c9r7j`):** `DispatchMonarchAction` wires `DECLARE_PEACE` to `ImposeMonarchStance(stance, candidates, rng)` and `DECLARE_WAR` to `MonarchDeclareWar(rng)`. Both order their candidate rivals (`MonarchPotentialEnemies` = peace-standing, for war; `MonarchPotentialFriends` = war/cease-fire, for peace) by player id, draw the King's pick on the **monarch RNG** (`rng.Next`, never stream 0), and call `SetStance`. Empty candidate set → safe no-op. No tension delta, no save change (stances persist via the existing player-stance field). See [diplomacy](diplomacy.md).
  - **Franklin block (`ignoreEuropeanWars`):** `MonarchPotentialEnemies()` returns empty when `HasAbilityFor(_human, model.ability.ignoreEuropeanWars)` — Benjamin Franklin in Congress — so DECLARE_WAR is gated off (`MonarchActionIsValid`) and never offered, and a stray dispatch is a no-op. `MonarchPotentialFriends()` is **not** gated (FreeCol: "he stops wars, not peace"), so the King can still make peace. The Franklin father + ability already exist in the spec (`model.foundingFather.benjaminFranklin`); this is the first of his three effects wired (the other two — `alwaysOfferedPeace`, `peaceTreaty +50%` — stay deferred pending an AI diplomatic-trade flow; see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)).
  - **War support (FreeCol `getWarSupport` + `InGameController`):** `MonarchDeclareWar` picks the enemy, then `GetWarSupport(enemy, rng)` decides whether to help on the **monarch RNG**: with `ratio = StrengthRatio(human, enemy)` (land power, the diplomacy yardstick) and `p = 10·(0.6 − ratio)`, support is granted when `p ≥ 1` (always, no draw), declined when `p ≤ 0` (never, no draw), else with probability `p` (one `NextDouble`). When granted, each `MonarchOptions.WarSupportForce` block's count is varied `+rnd[0,3)−1` (floor 1; a single token unit against a defenceless enemy), the force is delivered to Europe via the existing `GrantSupport`, and `WarSupportGold` is added to the treasury varied ±20% (`gold/10·(rnd[0,5)−2)`). Order matches FreeCol (support before the stance flips). `MonarchOptions.WarSupportForce` (a `MonarchSupportUnit` list) + `WarSupportGold` are **difficulty-data-driven** (parsed from `model.option.warSupportForce`/`warSupportGold`; medium = 4 veteran soldiers / 1500 gold). The grant is immediate units + gold — **no new save state** (stances + Europe units + gold already persist).
- **Player-facing dialog (`86d3c9xdb`):** `game/presentation/MonarchDialog.cs` (a hidden-by-default `PanelContainer` modal in `main.tscn`, the same dim-parchment pattern as `NativeDemandPanel`/`MoundsDecisionPanel`) surfaces the King's **two answerable** demands. `GameController.RefreshView` opens it (gated `!IsHumanDefeated`, once via `!Visible`) whenever `Game.PendingMonarchDemand` is set; the dialog reads the demand and forwards Accept/Decline to `Game.RespondToMonarch(bool)` (ADR-006 — the panel holds no rules). A **RAISE_TAX** demand renders the proposed rate + the eyed goods/colony with *Pay the tax* / *Refuse (tea party)*; a **mercenary offer** renders the force size + gold price with *Hire them* / *Decline*. The King's other actions apply immediately in GameLogic with no pending seam, so they never open the dialog — instead they now record a **King's-decree notice** (see the next bullet). Hidden by default → no visual-golden churn; presentation-only → no GameLogic/save/RNG change.
- **King's-decree notices (`86d3e4buh`):** the **immediate** (no-choice) monarch actions — `LOWER_TAX_*`, `WAIVE_TAX`, `DECLARE_WAR`, `DECLARE_PEACE`, `SUPPORT_SEA`, `SUPPORT_LAND`, `ADD_TO_REF` — now record a transient `MonarchDecreeNotice(Action, RivalNationId?, TaxRate, UnitCount, Gold)` (`GameSession/MonarchDecreeNotice.cs`; `Game.MonarchDecreeNotices`) at the point each effect resolves in `DispatchMonarchAction` (so the notice carries the *actual* parameters — the new tax rate, the picked rival's nation id, the units/gold granted, the REF units added). `ImposeMonarchStance` now returns the chosen `Player?` so `DECLARE_PEACE` can name the pacified rival, and `MonarchDeclareWar` records the war + any support inline. The list is cleared at the top of every `EndTurn` and is **never saved** (per-turn UI scratch — no save-format change, v57 unchanged); it stays empty before the monarch grace period, so the default game is byte-identical (the tick already runs on the ephemeral monarch RNG, never stream 0). The presentation formats each via `GameController.FormatMonarchDecreeNotice` into the End-Turn message panel + the re-openable session message log. The two *answerable* demands (RAISE_TAX, mercenary offer) do **not** record a decree notice — they surface through `MonarchDialog` instead.
- `RandomChoice.WeightedRandom(rng, choices)`: one weighted pick, one RNG draw (shared by the chooser, mercenary/support rolls, REF composition).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** the Monarch is the human's King, but its roll must not shift the human's **stream 0** (that would change every existing seeded game past turn 30). So the tick seeds an **ephemeral** `Pcg32Random` from the human's *current* stream state (read non-destructively via `SaveState`) **+ the turn**, on a reserved stream id — it consumes nothing from stream 0, yet is fully reproducible across save/load (the human state and turn are persisted). A gated-out turn draws nothing. The *decision* is derived (no save change for the tick itself); the *outcome* persists — tax/arrears/displeasure/support/REF (v37–v40) and, since v46, an unanswered `PendingMonarchDemand`.
