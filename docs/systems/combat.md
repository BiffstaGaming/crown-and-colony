## 1. How it works (plain English)

When one unit attacks another, the game weighs the attacker's **offence** against the defender's **defence**, then rolls the dice. The chance of winning is simply the attacker's strength as a share of the total: a power-3 attacker against a power-1 defender wins three times out of four.

Strength is the unit plus its **equipment**. A bare colonist can't fight — it has no offence. Arm it with 50 muskets and it becomes a **soldier** (+2 offence, +1 defence); add 50 horses and it's a **dragoon** (+3/+2). Equipment comes from a colony's warehouse: standing in the colony, a colonist trades muskets and horses for a military role. Native **braves** are the same idea from the other side — a settlement's warriors, who can pick up captured muskets to become armed braves.

The defender is also helped by rough ground (a hill doubles defence, a forest adds half again), by digging in, and by sheltering in a settlement. The roll is **graded** — a clear win versus a narrow one — which decides how badly the loser comes off.

**What happens to the loser**, in order:
- A unit that is **doomed on any loss** (a brave, a scout) is **killed**.
- An **armed** loser is **disarmed** first: it loses one step of equipment (a dragoon drops to a soldier, a soldier to an unarmed colonist) and the *winner can capture that equipment* — a brave that beats your soldier walks away armed with his muskets. A disarmed unit usually survives.
- An **unarmed but capturable** colonist is **taken prisoner** (it switches sides).
- Some units **demote** instead — artillery that loses becomes damaged artillery, and damaged artillery that loses is destroyed.
- Otherwise the loser is **killed**.

**The winner** may be **promoted** one step up the soldier ladder: a **petty criminal** soldier who wins becomes an **indentured servant**, a servant a **free colonist**, a free colonist a **veteran soldier**, and a veteran a **colonial regular** — so a hard-luck conscript can fight their way to a respectable soldier. Normally only a *decisive* win promotes, and only by chance — but with **George Washington** in your Congress, *every* win promotes. **Paul Revere** is the defensive counterpart: if natives ever attack a colony whose last colonist is unarmed, that colonist grabs muskets from the warehouse to defend.

**Attacking natives makes them angry.** Striking at a brave raises its settlement's alarm; killing the brave raises it a lot more. Enough hostility and the settlement turns from wary to hateful.

**Artillery is a siege weapon, not a field unit.** A cannon is devastating battering a **colony** — it attacks settlements and their garrisons at full strength — and, dug in behind a colony's walls, it's a brutal defender against a native raid (its defence **doubles**). But caught out **in the open field**, artillery is clumsy and exposed: both attacking and defending in the open, its strength is cut by three-quarters. Keep your guns in your colonies, or escort them; don't let them get jumped on open ground. (A cannon beaten in battle is downgraded to **damaged artillery** rather than destroyed.)

**Natives ambush from the trees.** **Forest and hills** are concealing terrain. A native raider attacking out of (or onto) such ground gets to **ignore the cover** the defender would otherwise enjoy — it fights as if the defender were standing in the open, so don't count on a forest's defensive bonus to save a colonist from a native war party. The one exception: a unit that has **fortified** (dug in) can't be ambushed, and keeps its full defence.

**Ships fight too.** An armed warship (a frigate, privateer or man-o-war) can attack an adjacent enemy ship on the water. Naval battles play a touch differently: there's no terrain to hide behind on open water, and a ship riding low with a full hold fights worse (every cargo load is a handicap). A beaten ship is usually **damaged** — it loses everything aboard (cargo and passengers) and limps off to repair, **sinking outright only on a heavy loss**; a ship can also **evade** a weaker attacker and slip away unharmed. A damaged ship repairs in **its own port** if you have one — the nearest of your colonies with a **drydock** (or shipyard) — sitting in the water beside it for a few turns; otherwise it limps all the way back to **Europe**. Foreign powers you're at war with will send their warships after your ships, and you'll be told when one is sunk.

**And now they strike back.** Once a tribe is angry enough (Displeased or worse) its braves come hunting — they march on your nearest colonist or soldier and attack it. A brave that beats an unarmed colonist kills it; one that beats an armed soldier knocks him back a step (and may take his muskets). When you're raided, the status bar tells you. Braves don't sack your colony buildings or take prisoners in this slice. (The full story of *who* the natives target and *when* lives in [natives](natives.md).)

**Colonies can change hands both ways.** You can march a soldier up to a rival's undefended colony and seize it — buildings, stores and all. Now the rivals can do the same to *you*: a European power you're at war with will, if one of its soldiers ends up next to one of your colonies that has **no defender standing in it**, walk in and take it. (A colony with a unit garrisoned on its tile is safe — the rival must beat that defender in the field first, which leaves the colony open only if it loses.) When you lose a colony this way the status bar tells you which power took it and which colony fell — so keep a soldier in any colony near an angry neighbour. **Whoever captures a colony also loots its treasury** — you carry off the rival's gold when you take their colony, and a rival empties yours when it takes one of yours (a small colony with a big treasury can be a juicy target). **Any ships moored at a colony go down with it** — when a colony falls, the former owner's ships sitting in its port are crippled and limp off to the nearest place they can repair (a drydock colony, or home in Europe), or are sunk outright if they have nowhere to go. So don't leave your fleet docked at a colony you might lose — and a raid that takes an enemy port can cripple the ships caught there.

**Walls help defend a colony.** Building a **stockade** (then a **fort**, then a **fortress**) makes a colony far harder to take or raid: a stockade adds +100% defence, a fort +150%, a fortress +200%. The bonus protects whoever is defending — a soldier garrisoned inside, or, if there's no garrison, the colonists themselves when a rival tries to capture it or a tribe tries to pillage it. So a walled colony can shrug off an attack that would overrun an open one.

**Sacking a settlement.** You can also attack a native settlement itself. It defends with its warriors *and* its walls — a camp adds half again to its defence, a capital doubles it — so you'll usually want artillery or massed soldiers. Win and you **sack it**: the settlement is destroyed and the treasure appears as a **treasure train** on the razed tile (a camp gives a few hundred gold, a rich village far more, and **Hernán Cortés** doubles the take and always finds treasure) — it's no longer instant gold; you must **escort the train home and cash it in** (see [treasure-train](treasure-train.md)). The whole nation seethes: its *other* settlements' alarm jumps. Lose, and your attacker is beaten back — disarmed or, for artillery, smashed to damaged artillery — while that settlement turns on you. (You can't simply walk onto a native settlement; you attack it, or trade/talk from beside it.)

**What the player does:** with a unit selected, **click an adjacent enemy brave or native settlement to attack it** — the same click that would move you onto an empty tile attacks an enemy that holds it instead. The result is shown in the HUD ("won the battle" / "beaten back"; "the native settlement was sacked!" / "assault was repelled"), and attacking ends the unit's turn. (Arming colonists still happens via the colony equip action; richer combat feedback/animation comes later.)

**Worked example:**
> Your free colonist, armed as a soldier (offence 0 + 2 = 2), attacks a native brave (defence 1) in the open. The soldier's offence with the +50% attack bonus is 2 × 1.5 = 3; the brave's defence is 1. The win chance is 3 / (3 + 1) = 75%. If you win, the brave (doomed on any loss) is killed and the settlement's alarm jumps. If you lose, your soldier drops his muskets and walks home a plain colonist — and the brave picks the muskets up.

## 2. Detailed rules

**Base power** comes from the unit type (`UnitType.Offence`/`Defence`) plus the unit's **role** (`RoleType.Offence`/`Defence`): free colonist 0/1, brave 1/1, artillery 7/5, veteran soldier 0/1.5 (its punch is the role); roles — soldier +2/+1 (50 muskets), dragoon +3/+2 (50 muskets + 50 horses), scout +1/+1 (50 horses), armed brave +2/+1 (25 muskets, native-only), mounted brave +1/+1 (25 horses), native dragoon +3/+2.

**Attacker percentage modifiers** (FreeCol `GENERAL_COMBAT_INDEX`): attack bonus **+50%**; movement penalty **−33%** (2 movement points left) / **−66%** (1 left); amphibious **−75%**; artillery-in-the-open **−75%** — an artillery (`bombard`) attacker is brittle in the field, but **only when the *defender* is not in a settlement** and the gun itself isn't in a settlement or dug in (FreeCol `getOffensiveModifiers`). Battering a colony or its garrison, artillery keeps full power — it is the colony-buster. **Ambush bonus** — a **native** attacker (the indian `ambushBonus`) striking in the open from concealing **forest/hills** (or *at* a defender standing on such terrain, `model.ability.ambushTerrain`) gains the **defender's own terrain defence percentage as offence** (FreeCol `Unit.canAmbush`), cancelling the cover — unless the defender is **dug in** (a fortified unit can't be ambushed). *(The mirror case — anyone ambushing a REF unit's `ambushPenalty` — arrives with the War of Independence, P6.)*

**Defender percentage modifiers**: terrain defence bonus (plains 0, marsh +25%, most forest +50%, rainForest +75%, hills +100%); **fortified +50%** — a dug-in land defender (`Unit.Orders == Fortified`, ordered via `Game.Fortify`; FreeCol `model.modifier.fortified`), read into `DefenceContext.Fortified` in the field-`Attack` path, see [units-movement](units-movement.md); native-settlement defence bonus (5c); **colony fortification defence bonus** — stockade **+100%**, fort **+150%**, fortress **+200%** (`BuildingType.DefenceBonus`, applied to any unit defending in the colony); **artillery-in-the-open −75%** — an artillery caught defending **outside** a settlement (and not dug in) is just as brittle as one attacking in the open; **artillery-against-raid +100%** — but an artillery defending a **colony against a native raid** doubles its defence (FreeCol `ARTILLERY_AGAINST_RAID`), so a gun behind the walls shreds raiders. All percentages combine multiplicatively in `DefencePower`.

**Odds & resolution**: win probability = `attack / (attack + defence)`; a draw `r ∈ [0,1)` grades it — `r < 0.1·win` great win, `r < win` win, `r ≥ 0.1·win + 0.9` great loss, else loss.

**Loser outcome precedence** (FreeCol `SimpleCombatModel.resolveAttack`, land open-field path):
1. **dispose-on-combat-loss** (type or role: brave, scout) → **slaughter**.
2. **offensive role** → disarm: the winner captures the equipment (`Unit.canCaptureEquipment` — the winner has `captureEquipment` and a military `role-change` matching `from = winner role`, `capture = loser role`, available to its side) and arms itself into that role; the loser steps to `Role.Downgrade ?? default`. Then, if the loser is *dispose-on-all-equipment-lost* with no downgrade it is slaughtered, or if *demote-on-all-equipment-lost* it is type-demoted.
3. **canBeCaptured + winner captureUnits** → **capture the unit**: it changes owner, taking the `capture` unit-change (veteran soldier → free colonist) if one exists, and is disarmed. *(FreeCol also requires `!combatIsAmphibious` here — a unit beaten by an assault fired straight off a ship is slain, not captured — but we model no amphibious assault yet (`CheckAttack` requires the attacker be on the map, not aboard), so this branch is never reached amphibiously; the guard lands with amphibious assault, `86d3c9tzv`.)*
4. a **demotion** unit-change exists → **demote the type** (artillery → damaged artillery).
5. otherwise → **slaughter**.

**Winner promotion** (FreeCol): if the winner's type has a `promotion` unit-change and either it has `automaticPromotion` (Washington) **or** the result was a *great* win and `100·r ≤ probability`, it promotes one rung up the ladder. `ApplyWinnerPromotion` is type-generic (`GetUnitChange(Promotion, winnerType)`), so the **full ladder** is walked: **petty criminal → indentured servant → free colonist → veteran soldier → colonial regular** (the promotion rows have distinct `from`s, so none is lost to the parse's by-`from` keying). The promotion roll is a **second draw** from the same RNG, taken only when needed (`great && !automatic`). *Combat is the route wired today for the criminal/servant rungs (`86d3c9q1z`); the same criminal/servant → free-colonist climb also comes via **schoolhouse education**, which lands with the education system (a separate task). On-the-job **experience** is a different branch (free colonist → its work expert; see [colonies](colonies.md)), not a criminal/servant rung.*

**Native alarm from combat** (FreeCol `defenderTension`, applied across the defender nation's *every* settlement via `csModifyTension`): tension is driven by the **outcome**, not the act of attacking. A European **win** raises it; a **repelled** attack *lowers* it (the natives prevailed, their tension toward you eases). Open-field (5b): killing a brave adds **+500** (`UNIT_DESTROYED` +400 + a minor insult `TENSION_ADD_MINOR` +100); a loss subtracts **−100** (−`MINOR`), or **−300** if your unit is slain (−`MINOR` − `NORMAL`).

**Settlement assault (5c).** A player land unit attacks a native settlement *tile* (`CheckAttackSettlement`/`AttackSettlement`). The settlement defends with an **implicit garrison**: a brave's defence (1) times its **settlement defence bonus** (camp/village +50%, capital/city +100%, city capital +200%); the open-tile terrain bonus, ambush, and the REF-only bombard bonus do **not** apply in a settlement. One graded combat draw resolves it:
- **Attacker wins** → the settlement is **sacked**: the attacker may promote (as above), takes **plunder** (next paragraph), the nation's alarm shifts (below), and the settlement is **destroyed** (removed from the map).
- **Attacker loses** → the attacker runs the loser-outcome precedence (disarmed / artillery → damaged artillery / slaughtered); the settlement survives and the nation's alarm *eases*.

**Plunder** (FreeCol `RandomRange.getAmount`, `continuous=false`): the sacked settlement's `<plunder>` range pays `(rnd[0, max−min] + min) × factor` gold, gated by a `probability`% roll (100 = always). Each settlement type carries a **base** range and a richer **extra** range; the attacker uses the extra range iff it has `model.ability.plunderNatives` — granted by **Hernán Cortés**. Pinned (gold): camp 200–300 @20% (Cortés 300–600 always); village 300–800 @50% (Cortés 400–1200); camp capital 400–800 (Cortés 600–1200); village capital 600–1600 (Cortés 900–2400). **As of `86d3c9ryj` the plunder is no longer credited straight to gold** — when it is > 0 it spawns a **treasure train** carrying that amount on the razed tile (FreeCol `csDestroySettlement`), which the attacker must escort home and cash in (see [treasure-train](treasure-train.md)). The plunder *draw* is unchanged, so this is RNG/byte-stable.

**Settlement-assault tension** (FreeCol `defenderTension`, nation-wide): sacking a **non-capital** settlement adds **+900** = `SETTLEMENT_ATTACKED` (+500, the slain in-settlement defender) + `MAJOR` (+300, the destruction) + `MINOR` (+100), applied to the nation's **other** settlements (the sacked one is removed; a lone-settlement tribe's alarm dies with it). Burning a **capital** instead makes the nation **surrender**: every surviving settlement's alarm is *set* to `SURRENDERED` = **350** (`(Content 600 + Happy 100)/2`). A **repelled** assault *lowers* the nation's alarm by **−100** (or **−300** if your unit is slain), across all its settlements.

**Native-initiated combat (slice 1b).** Braves now attack too: the native AI (see [natives](natives.md)) drives a hostile brave to attack an adjacent **human unit** through the *same* `Attack` action — but via the internal `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(nativePlayer))` overload, so combat draws from the **nation's own RNG stream**, never the human's stream 0 (ADR-009). A brave can only target **human-owned units** (the AI's target filter; the engine's `AreEnemies` owner-inequality alone would also allow rival powers/tribes); it cannot assault native settlements (the `CheckAttackSettlement` native gate stays closed). A brave attacking a human unit standing on a **colony tile** fights inside the colony — the **colony's fortification bonus** (stockade/fort/fortress, see below) applies *instead of* the tile terrain bonus (FreeCol suppresses terrain inside a settlement), and that defender auto-equips via **Paul Revere** if it is the colony's last unarmed colonist. Because the defender is the human (not a native), the native-alarm path is skipped — a raid never changes the raider's own alarm. A brave **cannot capture units** (`captureUnits=false` in the classic spec), so a beaten unarmed colonist is **slaughtered**, not taken — and `CaptureUnit` is hardened so that even a hypothetical native captor never mis-assigns the captive to the human (a brave's `OwnerId` is 0 = the human's id; `OwnerNationId` is the authoritative native owner).

**Native colony pillage (FreeCol `csPillageColony`).** A hostile brave beside an **undefended** human colony **pillages** it instead of capturing (natives can't take colonies). `CheckPillageColony`/`PillageColony` (the native sibling of `AttackColony`): the colony's last-resort defender is a transient unarmed colonist (defence 1); a brave **win** picks one option uniformly over the colony's lootable goods stacks **plus a gold option** (when the human has gold) — a goods stack carries off `min(amount/2, 50)`, the gold option steals `min(max(1, ColonyPlunderAmount/5), gold)` off the human (FreeCol's `colony.getPlunder/5`) — recording a `ColonyRaidNotice` (goods id, or **null for gold**); the colony keeps its buildings, people and ownership; a **loss** slays the brave (dispose-on-combat-loss). The whole path draws from the nation's own stream (combat band, the loot pick, and the gold-plunder draw) — never stream 0. Gated by FreeCol `Colony.canBePillaged` (no stockade, the brave's `model.ability.pillageUnprotectedColony`, the colony unprotected and with something to loot — our gate stays **goods-based**, so a gold-only colony isn't pillageable). **Faithful subset:** goods + gold options only — FreeCol also (uniformly) burns a building / sinks-or-damages a ship on the tile, and on a *great* win kills a colonist or destroys the colony; those, plus the attacker-tension easing (no nation-level native tension store), are deferred. Driven by the native AI (see [natives](natives.md)).

**Foreign-power-initiated combat (slice 1c-2).** Foreign powers now fight back: a power at `Stance.War` with the human (war starts when the human attacks it — see [diplomacy](diplomacy.md)) drives its **armed** units (offence > 0, e.g. its starting soldier) after the human's nearest unit via `RunForeignPowerTurn` → `AttackHumanUnit`, the foreign sibling of the native `RaidHumanUnit`. Identical seam to 1b: it targets **human units only** (`NearestHumanUnit`), attacks via the internal `Attack(unit, target, RandomFor(power))` overload so combat draws from the **power's own RNG stream** (never stream 0), and records a `CombatNotice` (attacker = the European nation id) surfaced in the HUD. It does **not** assault human *colonies* (1c-3) and does not yet *declare* war on its own. The war branch is dormant at peace, so the existing foreign-power determinism/goldens/soak stay byte-stable.

**Naval combat (slices 1c-3a / 1c-3b).** A ship attacks an adjacent enemy ship through the same `Attack`/`CheckAttack` spine, but resolution differs (`CombatModel.ResolveNaval`, FreeCol `SimpleCombatModel`): the win bands are the same (`r < 0.1·win` great win, `< win` win) but the **loss** range opens with an **evade** sub-band — `r < 0.8·win + 0.2` → the defender slips away unharmed (every ship has `evadeAttack`), then `r ≥ 0.1·win + 0.9` great loss, else loss. **Modifiers:** the +50% attack bonus and movement penalty still apply; **no terrain defence bonus** on open water; **no** fortify/amphibious/artillery-in-open; and a **cargo penalty** of **−12.5% per goods slot** (`CombatModel` `CargoPenalty`, applied to both offence and defence — a laden ship fights worse; goods slots only, via `GoodsSlotsUsed`, FreeCol `getGoodsSpaceTaken`). Ship offence/defence come from the unit type (frigate 16/16, privateer 8/8, man-o-war 24/24; transports like the caravel have **offence 0** so they can't initiate — the `OffenceBase ≤ 0` gate refuses them). Ships and land units **cannot attack each other** directly (cross-domain gate, FreeCol `Unit.canAttack`). An **evaded** attack changes nothing but spends the attacker's turn.

**Cargo loot (1c-3c, FreeCol `csLootShip`).** Before the beaten ship goes down, if the **winner** is a **naval raider** — it has `model.ability.captureGoods` (frigate, privateer, man-o-war) — and has **free hold space**, it **plunders** as much of the loser's cargo as fits (`LootShip`, by stable goods order; goods pack 100/slot, merging into any partial stack the winner already holds). Whatever doesn't fit is lost with the ship. A pure transport (caravel/galleon/merchantman, no `captureGoods`) takes nothing. The transfer is deterministic — we auto-take what fits (FreeCol's loot-cargo *chooser* is a single-player nicety with no AI equivalent). No save-format change — looted goods ride the existing `Cargo` serialization.

**Outcome — damage vs sink (1c-3b, FreeCol `resolveAttack`).** A defeated ship loses its **cargo and everyone aboard** (passengers drown either way — `RemoveAll(CarrierId == ship.Id)`), then:
- on a **great** loss (or with **no repair location**, or beached) the hull is **sunk** (`SinkShip`) — gone for good;
- otherwise it is **damaged** (`DamageShip`): the hull survives but limps to its **repair location** — a water tile beside the **nearest owned colony with a drydock/shipyard** if it has one (repairing **on the map**, in its own port), else off to **Europe** (see below) — where it sits **under forced repair** for `MaxHitPoints − 1` turns (every classic ship is 6 HP → **5 turns**), recovering one per turn (`AdvanceRepairs` in `EndTurn`). While `RepairTurnsRemaining > 0` the ship is pinned to 0 movement and **cannot sail, take on cargo, or be boarded** (FreeCol `isReadyToTrade`/forced repair — guarded in `SailToNewWorld`, `CheckBuyEuropeGoods`, `CheckBoard`). When it reaches 0 it is back in service **wherever it repaired** — already on the map at its drydock colony (free to act at once), or in Europe (sail it home as usual).

**Foreign warships** at war hunt the human's nearest **ship** and attack it (1c-3a′ — `RunForeignPowerTurn`, on the power's own RNG stream); AI targeting is **domain-matched** (`NearestHumanUnit` filters `IsNaval == hunter.IsNaval`, so ships hunt ships and land units hunt land). A naval raid on the human records a `CombatNotice` the HUD surfaces ("sank"/"evaded"). A foreign power's damaged ship limps to **its own** Europe and ticks down without acting, all off the human's stream 0.

**Shore batteries.** A coastal colony with a **fort or fortress** and **artillery parked on it** fires on any enemy ship that strays onto an adjacent sea tile — automatically, at the start of your turn, with no risk to the colony (the ship can't fire back). The more guns on the tile (each artillery adds its strength, up to a cap), the harder it hits; the ship is damaged (limps off to repair) or sunk outright. It only fires on ships you're **at war** with — and on **privateers**, which are fair game even at peace. A fort with no artillery, or an inland colony, never bombards.

**Colonial-colony assault + capture (1c-3e, FreeCol `csCaptureColony`).** A colonial **land** unit can assault a **rival colony tile** (`CheckAttackColony`/`AttackColony`). If a garrison unit stands on the tile you must defeat it first (the normal unit-attack path); once the colony is **ungarrisoned**, its last-resort defender is an **unarmed colonist** (the abstracted population, defence 1) and a win **captures** the colony (`CaptureColony`): its `OwnerId` flips to the attacker and its people, buildings and stores transfer intact — the new owner can now manage it (and enter it). A loss disarms/demotes the repelled attacker (the shared loser precedence). A win also **sacks the colony's treasury** — see plunder below. Assaulting a rival is an **act of war** (recorded both ways before the colony changes hands — this is the colonial-colony attack→war trigger deferred in FP-6a). *(Model note: our colony population is an abstract integer, so an ungarrisoned colony's only defender is the single transient unarmed colonist — there is no in-colony **armed** colonist that would fight first as in FreeCol. To defend a colony you station a real unit on its tile, which routes to ordinary unit combat.)*

**Colony capture plunder (FreeCol `Colony.getPlunderRange` / `csCaptureColony`).** A winning colony assault transfers gold from the former owner to the captor, computed *before* the handover (`PlunderColony` in the `AttackColony` win branch). The amount (`ColonyPlunderAmount`): with `upper = ownerGold × (colonyPop + 1) / (ownerColoniesPop + 1)`, the payout is `rnd[0, upper] + 1` (probability 100 — always pays) when `upper > 0`, else 0; 0 too when the owner is broke (FreeCol `canBePlundered = checkGold(1)`). It's **capped at the victim's purse** so gold never goes negative (a single-colony owner's `upper` equals its whole treasury, so the cap can bind; a multi-colony owner loses only that colony's share). The draw is on the **captor's** stream — the human's stream 0 for a human capture, the power's own stream for a foreign-AI capture (so foreign capture never perturbs the human's stream 0, ADR-009). *(Deviation: our cap is **conservation-preserving** — the captor gains exactly what the victim loses. FreeCol floors the victim at 0 but credits the captor the full raw amount, so at the boundary draw it can mint 1 extra gold; we don't. The native-pillage gold-steal — the smaller `getPlunder/5` — now reuses this same `ColonyPlunderAmount` (see §2 native colony pillage). Still deferred: FreeCol's goods-party-modifier cleanup.)*

**Foreign-AI colony capture (1c-3f) — capture is now two-directional.** The same `AttackColony` resolution now also runs from the AI side: in `RunForeignPowerTurn`'s war branch, a power at `Stance.War` with the human whose armed **land** unit is **adjacent to an undefended human colony** captures it (`AdjacentCapturableHumanColony` → `CapturePlayerColony` → the internal `AttackColony(attacker, target, RandomFor(power))`, the power's **own** RNG stream). Capturing a colony is the **decisive** move — it takes priority over chasing a field unit. A **garrisoned** human colony is excluded (`CheckAttackColony` refuses it); the AI hunts the garrison through the ordinary unit-hunt first, so the colony only becomes capturable once its defender has fallen. On a win the human is told through a transient **`ColonyLossNotice`** (capturing nation + colony name + position — the colony-loss sibling of `CombatNotice`, cleared each `EndTurn`, never saved); the HUD renders "⚑ The Dutch captured your colony Jamestown!". The AI captures the human only (the `IsHumanOwned` filter is the same sole contract as `NearestHumanUnit`; natives never reach this path — `RunNativeTurn` has no colony branch and `CheckAttackColony` refuses native attackers). The colony's defender (the transient last colonist, or a garrison) is shielded by the colony's **fortification defence bonus** (stockade/fort/fortress — see below), and a win **plunders the human's treasury** (on the power's own stream — see plunder above). **Deferred** (documented): Revere auto-equip of the last defender, and AI **pathing toward** colonies (seek-and-destroy targeting beyond adjacency).

**Ships caught in a falling colony (`86d3c9twd`, FreeCol `csDamageColonyShips` / `csSinkColonyShips`).** When a colony is captured (any direction — `CaptureColony`, the choke point all capture paths flow through), the **former owner's ships moored in its port** are dealt with by `ResolveCaughtShips`: each is **damaged** — it limps to its repair location (the nearest owned drydock/shipyard colony, else Europe — the same `DamageShip`/`RepairBerthFor` path as naval combat) — or **sunk** when it has nowhere to repair (FreeCol picks sink when `getRepairLocation()` is null). This runs **after** the ownership flip, so the just-lost colony is no longer a valid repair berth (a ship can't repair at the port just taken from it). Classic `model.option.captureUnitsUnderRepair` is **false**, so a ship already under repair in that port is processed too (re-damaged or sunk). Deterministic — ships are taken in id order and the damage/sink path draws no RNG (so foreign-AI captures stay off the human's stream 0, ADR-009). *(Model note: a naval unit can't occupy the colony's own land tile here (ships live on water), so "in port" is a ship on a **water tile adjacent to the colony** — FreeCol's `colony.getTile().getNavalUnits()` adapted to our ships-on-water representation. FreeCol additionally captures non-combatant **land** units sharing the tile; we resolve only the ships for now.)*

**Colony fortification defence bonus (FreeCol `model.modifier.defence`).** A colony's fortification building grants a percentage defence bonus to **whoever defends in it** — stockade **+100%**, fort **+150%**, fortress **+200%** (`BuildingType.DefenceBonus`, parsed from the building's own `model.modifier.defence`; the fort/fortress `delete="true"` markers are skipped so the redefined value is taken). `Game.ColonyDefenceBonus(colony)` sums its buildings' bonuses — a colony holds only one fortification tier (fort upgrades the stockade, fortress the fort), so the sum is the tier present (0 unfortified). It is applied through `DefenceContext.SettlementDefenceBonus` (multiplicative, like the native-settlement bonus) in **all three** colony-defence paths: the main `Attack` (a garrison on the colony tile — `ColonyDefenceBonusAt(target)`), `AttackColony` (capture's transient last colonist), and `PillageColony` (pillage's transient defender). A unit defending **in** a colony uses this bonus *instead of* the tile's terrain bonus (the main `Attack` suppresses terrain when the target tile holds a colony — `inColony`; `AttackColony`/`PillageColony` never applied terrain), mirroring FreeCol's settlement branch and our native-settlement assault. So a stockade can repel an assault or raid that would overrun an open colony — closing the colony-defence-bonus deferred since 1c-3e. A stockade is built normally (64 hammers) or granted free by **La Salle** at population 3 (see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)). (FreeCol's stockade `minimumColonySize` modifier and the "prevent Indian raids" ability are not modelled.)

**Privateer piracy (1c-3d-i, FreeCol `csCombat`).** A unit with `model.ability.piracy` (the privateer) can raid a rival colonial power **without declaring war**: when the attacker *or* the defender has piracy, `Game.Attack` skips the colonial War/tension hook (so neither side's stance changes — a privateer plunders deniably, and attacking *someone else's* privateer also stays peaceful). A privateer also **flies no flag** (FreeCol `isOwnerHidden` = the unit type has piracy): when a foreign privateer raids the human, the `CombatNotice`'s attacker is the sentinel `Game.UnknownEnemyNationId` (`model.nation.unknownEnemy`, a no-owner pseudo-nation) and the HUD names no nation — "A privateer sank your frigate." This is the *mechanic*; the human can use it now (raid rival shipping without war). **AI-initiated** piracy (a foreign power sending privateers at peace) is deferred — foreign fleets have no privateers yet, and the AI only attacks at war. The `attackedByPrivateers` player flag FreeCol sets is **not modelled** (no monarch/diplomacy consumer); the `CombatNotice` is the player's feedback.

**Deviations from original / FreeCol (deliberate, 5b/5c/1b/1c-2/1c-3 scope):**
- **Repair location: a home drydock, else Europe (`86d3c9p0q` follow-on).** A damaged ship limps to the **nearest owned colony with a drydock or shipyard** (`model.ability.repairUnits`, resolved down the `extends` chain — drydock grants it, shipyard inherits it), chosen by Chebyshev distance from where it was beaten (ties broken by colony id, `RepairBerthFor`); it teleports to a **water tile beside that colony** and repairs there, still **on the map**. With no such colony it falls back to **Europe** (FreeCol `Unit.getRepairLocation`). A drydock colony is always coastal (drydock requires `hasPort`, see [colonies](colonies.md)), so a water berth always exists. *(We berth the ship on an adjacent water tile rather than inside the colony's dock — our model has no "ship docked in a colony" location; the repair duration and "cannot act while repairing" rules are otherwise faithful.)* (A ship with no repair location at all — e.g. a hypothetical native-owned ship, of which there are none in the classic ruleset — still sinks.) Still deferred: **AI-initiated piracy** (foreign privateer fleets raiding at peace).
- **Francis Drake (1c-3d-ii)** makes privateers **+50% offence and +50% defence** (`model.modifier.offence`/`defence` scoped to `model.unit.privateer`) — the first **scoped** founding-father combat modifier; it folds the unit owner's Congress into the offence/defence base (see [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md)). A privateer with Drake fights at 12/12 instead of 8/8.
- **Repair counts down once per round, not per owner's turn.** `AdvanceRepairs` runs once at the end of `EndTurn` (after every player has acted), exactly like `AdvanceSailing`. So a ship damaged *during* a round (e.g. by an at-war rival in the same round) loses one repair turn at that round's end — FreeCol instead heals at the start of the *owner's* next turn. The total is ~5 turns either way; the divergence is a one-tick edge case and is consistent with how high-seas sailing already advances.
- Native colony pillage models the **goods + gold** outcomes (building/ship/destroy deferred) — native-AI follow-ups (see [natives](natives.md)). (Foreign-European unit, naval, and **colony** combat are now in — 1c-2 / 1c-3a–f; native **colony pillage** is in — goods + gold; **native tribute demands** are in — see [natives](natives.md).)
- **Implicit garrison settlement defence.** A settlement defends with one transient brave-strength defender (the settlement bonus applied), not the strongest of several in/on-tile braves; the adjacent garrison braves need not be cleared first, and a winning assault destroys the settlement in one resolution. A faithful multi-brave on-tile defence waits for in-settlement unit lists.
- **Plunder is credited straight to gold.** FreeCol spawns a treasure train the attacker must escort home; treasure-train logistics + Cortés's `treasureTransportFee −100%` are deferred. `CAPTURE_CONVERT`/`BURN_MISSIONS` are omitted (missionary-gated, no missionary system); `DESTROY_NATION`/atrocity-score bookkeeping is not modelled.
- **Native tension is stored per-settlement only.** We apply FreeCol's `defenderTension` (with `getSlaughterTension` location routing — open-field +400 vs in-settlement +500/+600) uniformly across the nation's settlements, but keep no separate nation-level `Player.tension` store for *natives*, and don't model the player's own tension *toward* natives (so a repelled attack's attacker-side tension is dropped). Native stance changes off these values are the native-AI slice.
- **Colonial-vs-colonial:** from FP-6a, attacking a *rival colonial player's* unit records mutual War + a tension spike on the player-level diplomacy model (see [diplomacy](diplomacy.md)) — this is recorded only, it does not change combat resolution or gate the attack. Native combat is untouched by it.
- **Colony capture omits the +300 capture-MAJOR tension.** FreeCol's `csCaptureColony` adds an extra one-directional `TENSION_ADD_MAJOR` (+300) to the *losing* player's tension toward the captor, on top of the act-of-war spike. We apply only `AttackColony`'s act-of-war spike (`TensionWar` = 1000, **symmetric**). Negligible in practice — the war spike already pins the pair at the HATEFUL band (our scale caps at 1100), so the missing +300 can't change the stance — but noted rather than silent; fold a per-capture MAJOR in if/when tension granularity matters.
- **Paul Revere applies the defence bonus without consuming/persisting the 50 muskets** (FreeCol's `AUTOEQUIP_UNIT` permanence is deferred); native auto-equipment is dormant until native settlements stock goods.
- Modifier *values*, tension deltas, the promotion probability, the great-win band and the plunder ranges are FreeCol-pinned constants in code (transposability-migration items — see [game-modes](game-modes.md)).

## 3. Technical design

- **Data:** `RoleType` (offence/defence summed from index-30 additive modifiers, required-goods, downgrade, granted/required abilities, `role-change` capture rules) and `UnitChange` (from→to + probability) parse in `Ruleset.ParseRoles`/`ParseUnitChanges`. `UnitType` gains combat-ability flags (`DisposeOnCombatLoss`, `CanBeCaptured`, `CaptureUnits`, `CaptureEquipment`, `DisposeOnAllEquipmentLost`, `DemoteOnAllEquipmentLost`, `Bombard`). `Ruleset.GetUnitChange` and `Ruleset.CaptureRole` are the lookups combat uses.
- **Model:** `Combat.CombatModel` is unchanged and still pure — roles fold into the **scalar base** passed to `AttackPower`/`DefencePower` (`UnitType.Offence + RoleType.Offence`), landing at FreeCol's index 30, before the index-50 situational percentages.
- **State:** `Unit.OwnerNationId` (null = player) + `RoleId`/`RoleCount` + `Orders` (the fortify/sentry state — [units-movement](units-movement.md)). `Game.CheckAttack`/`Attack` follow the `MoveCheck` + `CheckX`/`X` pattern; `Attack` builds the contexts (`DefenceContext.Fortified = !naval && defender.IsFortified`, so a dug-in land defender resists at +50%; the **artillery** flags — `AttackContext.ArtilleryInOpen` requires the attacker bombard + not-in-colony + not-fortified + the *defender* not-in-colony, and `DefenceContext.ArtilleryInOpen`/`ArtilleryAgainstRaid` key off the defender's bombard, whether it's in the colony, and whether the attacker `IsNative`; the **ambush** flag — `AttackContext.AmbushBonus` = the defender's `TerrainType.DefenceBonus` when the attacker `IsNative`, both units are in the open, the defender isn't fortified, and either tile is `AmbushTerrain`), resolves through `CombatModel`, then `ResolveLoserOutcome` + `ApplyWinnerPromotion` apply the precedence above and `ChangeNativeAlarm` raises tension. `EquipRole`/`CheckEquipRole` arm a colonist from a colony's stock (charging the role-goods delta). `EffectiveCombatRole` implements FreeCol `getAutomaticRole` (Revere). Braves spawn in `Game.New` via `SpawnUnit(type, pos, ownerNationId)` on `FreeAdjacentLand`, consuming no RNG draw.
- **Settlement assault (5c):** `Game.CheckAttackSettlement`/`AttackSettlement` mirror the attack pair but target a `NativeSettlementAt(target)`. The defender is a **transient** `Unit` of `BraveUnitTypeId` (id 0, never added to `_units`) on the settlement tile, fed a `DefenceContext(SettlementDefenceBonus: SettlementType.DefenceModifier)`. A win removes the settlement from `_nativeSettlements`, runs `ApplyWinnerPromotion`, then `ComputePlunder`; a loss runs `ResolveLoserOutcome(transientDefender, attacker)`. `ComputePlunder` re-implements FreeCol `RandomRange.getAmount`; `SettlementType.PlunderRange(hasPlunderAbility)` selects base vs extra by the `plunderNatives` ability (via `AbilityForOwner`). `CheckMove` rejects a non-native move onto a native settlement tile.
- **Naval outcome (1c-3b):** `ResolveLoserOutcome(winner, loser, greatLoss)` takes the result's `great` flag; for a naval loser it calls `SinkShip` when `greatLoss || !CanRepairAtEurope(loser)`, else `DamageShip`. `DamageShip` first resolves the repair berth from where the ship was beaten (`RepairBerthFor`), then drops passengers + cargo (`ClearCargo`): if the owner has a drydock/shipyard colony it sets `Location = OnMap` and `Position =` the berth (a water tile beside the nearest such colony, `ColonyRepairsShips` + Chebyshev/id order), otherwise `Location = InEurope`; either way `RepairTurnsRemaining = RepairTurnsFor(type) = max(1, MaxHitPoints − 1)`. `BuildingType.RepairsNavalUnits` parses the building's `model.ability.repairUnits` down the `extends` chain. `CanRepairAtEurope` is true for any **colonial** owner (every colonial power has a Europe); a ship is always colonial-owned, so the no-repair-location sink is defensive, and the drydock berth is strictly an upgrade on the Europe fallback (sink logic unchanged). `AdvanceRepairs` (called from `EndTurn` after `AdvanceSailing`, before the movement reset) decrements `RepairTurnsRemaining`; the reset loop pins a still-repairing ship to 0 moves (`IsUnderRepair ? 0 : InitialMovement`). `UnitType.MaxHitPoints` parses spec `hit-points` (default 1; all ships 6). The action guards — `SailToNewWorld` throws, `CheckBuyEuropeGoods`/`CheckBoard` return `No` — reject a repairing ship.

- **Defender selection (86d3c9tzv, FreeCol `Unit.betterDefender`):** `DefenderAt(attacker, p)` ranks the enemies stacked on a tile by their **full computed defence power** (`DefencePowerOf` — terrain / fortify / settlement / artillery / naval-cargo), not raw `DefenceBase`, so a dug-in or walled weaker unit can outrank a stronger one in the open; ties break by unit id (deterministic). `Attack` reuses `DefencePowerOf` for the chosen defender, so selection and resolution agree.
- **Colony bombardment (86d3c9tkk, FreeCol `csBombardEnemyShips`):** `BombardEnemyShips(player)` runs at the **start** of each colonial player's turn (in `RunPlayerTurn`, before colony production), drawing from the owner's stream (internal `(player, random)` overload for tests). For each of the player's colonies (ordered by id) that has a `BombardsShips` building (`BuildingType.BombardsShips` parses `model.ability.bombardShips` down the `extends` chain — the fort grants it, the fortress inherits), the **bombard power** = `min(MaxBombardPower=48, Σ OffenceBase of the artillery (`Type.Bombard`) units on the colony tile owned by the player)`; a colony with **0 power is skipped** (no spurious RNG). Targets are the enemy ships on adjacent water tiles — `IsBombardTarget` = a naval unit not the owner's own and either a **privateer** (`Type.Piracy`, fair game at any stance) or **at war** (`StanceBetween == War`) — collected to a list (neighbour-tile order, then unit id) before any hit. `BombardShip` resolves one-sided: `WinProbability(power, DefencePower(ship + cargo penalty))` → `ResolveNaval`; a **great win** (or a win with nowhere to repair) **sinks** the ship, a plain **win damages** it (reusing `SinkShip`/`DamageShip` — no loot, the colony isn't a raider), anything else leaves it unharmed (no counterattack — classic models no ship→colony bombardment). No save change. A bombard notice/message is presentation (deferred).
- **Colony capture (1c-3f):** `AdjacentCapturableHumanColony(unit)` returns the first **human-owned** colony where `CheckAttackColony(unit, pos).Allowed` (so adjacent + ungarrisoned + land + armed), ties broken by position; `CapturePlayerColony(power, unit, target)` reads the colony name, calls the internal `AttackColony(attacker, target, RandomFor(power))`, and on a win pushes a `ColonyLossNotice`. The war branch tries this **before** the unit-hunt and `continue`s — preserving the existing 1c-2/1c-3a′ unit-hunt for every case with no adjacent capturable colony. `ColonyLossNotice` is transient (a `List` cleared in `EndTurn` beside `_combatNotices`), surfaced by `GameController.OnEndTurnPressed`; it is **never** serialized (no save-format impact).
- **Determinism (ADR-009):** the public `Attack`/`AttackSettlement`/`AttackColony` draw from the game's **main saved RNG** (`_random`); the `internal …(IGameRandom)` overloads let tests force an outcome and script plunder draws — **and let the native/foreign AI resolve a raid or colony capture from that player's own stream**: `RaidHumanUnit` calls `Attack(brave, target, RandomFor(nativePlayer))`, `AttackHumanUnit`/`CapturePlayerColony` call the foreign overloads with `RandomFor(power)`, so no AI combat ever touches stream 0 (the human's byte-stability — guarded by `NativeAiTests`, `ForeignColonyCaptureTests` + the soak). `AttackColony` draws only its combat band and (on a win) the promotion roll, both from the passed RNG; the loser path draws none. The damage/repair path is **fully deterministic** (no RNG draw): `DamageShip`/`AdvanceRepairs` consume nothing, iterate `_units` in stable order, and never perturb stream 0 when a non-human ship is damaged. The settlement-assault RNG order is **combat draw → (win) promotion draw → plunder probability draw (only if `<100`) → plunder range draw**. `UpgradeUnitType` preserves owner/role/roleCount. `CaptureUnit` keeps `OwnerNationId` as the authoritative owner and never copies a native winner's `OwnerId` (0 = the human) onto the captive. Native units are excluded from `CurrentlyVisible`/`IsVisible`.
- **Save:** format **v18** adds `SavedUnit.Owner`/`Role`/`RoleCount` (default role omitted → v17 player saves byte-identical); braves round-trip via `Owner`. **v19** is a marker for settlement assault — a destroyed settlement is simply absent from the saved list and plunder folds into gold, so **no new field** and older saves load unchanged. **v21** adds `SavedUnit.RepairTurns` (ship damage state) — additive and **omitted when 0/healthy**, so an undamaged fleet serializes byte-identically and pre-v21 saves load as healthy. **v23** adds `SavedUnit.Orders` (the fortify/sentry standing order — [units-movement](units-movement.md)) — additive and **omitted when Active**, so a no-orders game stays byte-identical and pre-v23 saves load every unit Active; a *Fortified* defender's +50% bonus rides the persisted order.
