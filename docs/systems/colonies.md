## 1. How it works (plain English)

Select a colonist and press **B** to found a colony where it stands. The colonist settles down and becomes the colony's first inhabitant (it leaves the map). Colonies show as a small house with their name; click one to see its vitals in the status bar. Names come from a classic colonial list (Jamestown, Plymouth, …) in founding order.

**The colony screen.** Click your colony to open it. It fills the screen on FreeCol's **brown parchment** backdrop and is laid out like the original Colonization's (FreeCol's) colony view, drawn in **real FreeCol art**: a title and summary line (population, idle colonists, food toward the next colonist, defence bonus); a **production bar** along the top showing each good the colony makes or eats per turn (its icon + net amount); on the **left**, a row of **colonist portraits** (one per inhabitant) above an **isometric view of the surrounding tiles** — the colony's own settlement at the centre with the eight neighbours fanned out as overlapping diamonds, a colonist standing on each tile being worked (**drawn as its real type** — an expert lumberjack looks like a lumberjack, not a plain colonist — with the good and yield it produces **as that worker actually makes it** — an expert lumberjack's diamond shows the doubled lumber (8), the same figure the production overview shows, not the free-colonist base (4) — and a *✕* to release it) and a *Work…* picker on a free tile — sitting above the **construction** panel (the full **build queue** — what's building now, with its progress, then everything lined up behind it; each line has **▲/▼** to reorder and a **✕** to remove, an **Add to queue** menu that lists both **buildings and units** you can construct, and a **Clear queue** button); on the **right**, the colony's **buildings** as a grid of their real images, each showing **the actual colonists working it as little portraits** (one per slot — an expert in a building shows as that expert, empty slots faintly marked; click a worker to take it out) and a **+ / −** to staff or unstaff it; a row of the **units standing outside** the colony (join / arm / send-out); a **warehouse bar** showing every stored good as its icon and count — with **low/high warnings**: a good that is almost gone shows a red **▼** and a *running low* tooltip, and a good at or near the warehouse cap (so its surplus will be discarded on End Turn) shows an amber **▲** and a *will overflow* tooltip; and a **production overview** — a per-good table of what the colony **produces**, **consumes**, and the **net** each turn (food, raw + manufactured goods, bells, crosses, hammers), the full breakdown the top bar's net-only strip can't show. The left column's top shows the **Sons-of-Liberty band** — Rebels · Population · Royalists with the membership % and production bonus, over a gold/dark meter (see [sons-of-liberty.md](sons-of-liberty.md)).

**Putting colonists to work.** Each colonist either works one of the eight surrounding tiles (growing food, or a raw good — lumber, ore, cotton, furs) or staffs a building (turning raw goods into bells, hammers, cloth, …). New colonists report to the best food tile automatically. **Moving a colonist between tiles is click-to-move** (like dragging in the original): click the colonist on its tile to pick it up (it highlights), then click another tile to send it there (it takes that tile's best good), or click the **colony centre** to send it idle. You can also drop an idle colonist straight onto a free tile by clicking it. For fine control, each worked tile keeps a **✕** to release it and a **Work…** picker to choose *which* good a tile produces (e.g. **lumber** instead of food, which the carpenter then turns into hammers).

**Dragging colonists (drag-and-drop).** The colony screen has an **Idle colonists** row of portrait chips — one per colonist with no current job. You can **drag a chip onto a tile** to put that colonist to work there (it takes the tile's best-yield good; use the tile's **Work…** picker to choose a different good), or **drag it onto a building** to staff that building. A drag onto an already-worked tile, a fully-staffed building, or a tile the colony can't yet work (a sea tile before Docks) is quietly refused — nothing happens. Dragging onto **native-owned** land raises the same Buy / Steal / Abandon prompt the click flow uses. The drag is an extra convenience layered on top of the clicks: every click button still works exactly as before (it stays the accessible fallback).

**Moving a unit onto a colony.** Click your own colony with a **selected unit beside it** and the unit **walks onto the colony tile** — the same click that would move it onto any tile (you no longer have to use go-to to reach your own town). The unit **stands on the tile** at the colony: it does **not** automatically fold into the work force. From there a colonist can **Join** (via the explicit button below), a soldier can garrison and defend, and a **treasure train can be cashed in**. With **nothing selected** (or the selected unit can't reach the tile — it isn't adjacent, or it's already standing there), the click **opens the colony screen** to manage it as before. So the flow is: click to send the unit, then click again once it has arrived to open the colony.

**Moving colonists in and out.** The colony screen's **Colonists** section moves people across the colony wall: **Send a colonist out** detaches a free colonist onto the colony's tile (click that tile to select it and march it off, or arm it), and any of your colonists standing on or beside the colony gets a **Join colony** button to fold into its population (the unit leaves the map, +1 population). A colony must always keep at least one colonist.

**Renaming or abandoning the colony.** At the top of the colony screen a **rename** box (pre-filled with the current name) lets you type a new name and press **Rename** (the title updates at once; a blank name is refused). Beside it, **Abandon colony** gives the colony up entirely — its last colonist walks back out onto the tile and the colony vanishes. You can only abandon the **last** colonist (send the others out first), and a colony with a **fortification** (stockade/fort/fortress) can't be abandoned at all; the button is greyed with the reason until those conditions are met.

**Arming colonists.** A colonist standing **in** the colony can be equipped from the colony's own stores — **Arm soldier** (50 muskets), **Arm dragoon** (50 muskets + 50 horses), **Arm scout** (50 horses) or **Arm pioneer** (tools) — and **Disarm** back to a plain colonist (refunding the gear). The buttons appear only when the colony actually holds the goods. That's how you raise a defender at home: send a colonist out onto the colony tile, then arm it. And the reverse: if you **found or join a colony with an already-armed colonist** (a soldier, dragoon, scout or pioneer), it settles down as a plain worker and its gear — the soldier's 50 muskets, a dragoon's muskets + horses, a scout's horses, a pioneer's tools — drops into the colony's warehouse rather than being lost. A colony with a **church or cathedral** also offers **Arm missionary** — it dresses the colonist as a missionary (free of charge; only a church can ordain one), ready to walk out and establish a mission at a native settlement (see [natives.md](natives.md)).

**Clearing a speciality.** An **expert** standing on the colony tile (an expert farmer, master carpenter, …) gets a **Clear speciality** button that reverts it to a plain free colonist — useful when you'd rather have a flexible worker than a specialist. The button appears only for a unit that actually has a learned speciality (see [education-schools.md](education-schools.md)).

**Lifting a boycott.** If one of your goods is under **boycott** (after a Boston Tea Party — see [monarchy.md](monarchy.md)), the colony screen grows a **Boycotts** section listing each boycotted good, its back-tax owed, and a **Pay** button. Paying the back-tax lifts the boycott so you can trade the good again; the button is greyed (with the cost) when you can't afford it.

**Loading a ship or wagon (the Cargo section).** When one of your **carriers** — a ship *or* a wagon train — is standing **on or next to** the colony, a **Cargo** section appears showing each such carrier and how full its hold is ("hold 1/4"). A *Load goods…* picker puts a hold-lot (up to 100) of any warehouse good aboard, and each goods stack already in the hold gets an *Unload* button to drop a lot back into the warehouse. This is the manual, one-off way to move goods by sea or overland (trade routes do it automatically; the Europe market does it by buy/sell). If the hold is full or the warehouse runs short, the move is refused and the status bar says why — nothing is lost. See [transport.md](transport.md) for the carrier rules.

**Choosing what to build.** The construction panel shows a **queue**, not just one job. Pick from the **Add to queue** menu — it lists every **building** the colony can put up *and* every **unit** it can construct (a **wagon train** in any colony, **artillery** once you've built an armory, **ships** at a coastal shipyard), each with its hammer/tool cost. Whatever you add lines up behind the current job; the colony finishes one item per turn and moves on by itself, so you can plan several builds ahead and walk away. Re-order the line with **▲/▼**, drop a job with **✕**, or wipe the whole list with **Clear queue**. Finished units appear on the colony's tile (ships launch onto the water beside it); finished buildings join the colony.

**Ordaining a missionary.** A colonist standing in a colony that has built a **church or cathedral** can be made a **missionary** (no goods needed — the church does the ordaining). A colony with only a chapel can't: the chapel makes crosses but can't dress a missionary; you must upgrade it to a church first. Once ordained, the missionary can leave to **establish a mission** in a native settlement (see [natives](natives.md)).

**Spying on a rival colony with a scout.** Move a **scout** right up next to **another player's** colony and you can **spy** on it — your scout slips up to the gate and, for a single glimpse, you get to see the whole inside of that colony the way its owner does: which **buildings** it has put up, where its colonists are **working** (which tiles, which workshops), what's sitting in its **warehouse**, and how its **Sons of Liberty** are doing. You don't need to be at war — a scout can simply walk over and take a look. It's a peek, not a foothold: the view is a snapshot of that moment (you don't keep watching the colony afterwards), and **spying uses up the scout's whole turn**. There's also a chance the colony's watch **turns your scout away** before it sees anything — you still burn the turn, you just come back empty-handed. (Only a unit equipped as a scout can do this; an ordinary colonist or soldier can't.)

## 2. Detailed rules

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Unit type lacks the foundColony ability (e.g. ships) | rejected |
| Terrain is not settleable (mountains, water) | rejected |
| Tile already has a colony | rejected |
| Tile is adjacent to an existing colony | rejected (minimum colony spacing — colony footprints never touch) |
| Otherwise | colony founded, population 1, founding unit consumed |

**Economy tick (every EndTurn, first economy slice):**
| Step | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1. Colony square produces | the tile's unattended yield goes to the stores (plains: grain 3 + cotton 2) |
| 2. Colonists eat | 2 food per colonist; grain drains before fish; stores floor at 0 |
| 3. Growth | at ≥200 stored food: −200 food, +1 population |

Goods enter the warehouse under their spec `stored-as` id — grain/fish/meat all become **food** (one warehouse entry, matching FreeCol; legacy saves normalize on load). **Starvation:** a food shortfall starves one colonist that turn; assignments shrink to fit, pulling workshop workers before field workers. When the shortfall hits the **last** colonist — its food production plus stored carryover can no longer feed even one person — the **colony is destroyed**: it vanishes from the map (its tile clears), its last colonist dies with it, and any units that were garrisoned **on** the colony tile are left standing on the now-empty land (`86d3e4bnj`, FreeCol `model.colony.colonyStarved` → `csDisposeSettlement`). The player is **told** their colony starved out, and the loss is written into the game **history** (it will later cost score). In the **classic game this never happens in normal play** — every settleable centre tile yields at least 2 food, exactly a lone colonist's appetite — so a size-1 colony always feeds itself; destruction only becomes reachable once something can push food production below that (e.g. a future disaster).

The two food numbers above — **2 food eaten per colonist** and **200 food to grow one** — are no longer written into the code: they come from the rules file (`Ruleset.ColonyConstants`, `86d3drpgg`), alongside the **200 "liberty per rebel"** divisor behind the Sons-of-Liberty bar and the **50** a custom house keeps back before auto-selling. The classic numbers are unchanged (so colonies eat, grow, rebel and export exactly as before), but a variant could retune any of them in data — see [ruleset-data](ruleset-data.md).

**Warehouse capacity & overflow (`86d3c9nnp`).** A colony's warehouse holds only **100 of each storable good** — a **warehouse** raises that to 200, a **warehouse expansion** to 300 (FreeCol `model.modifier.warehouseStorage`, cumulative up the building chain; `Game.WarehouseCapacity`). Anything produced **past the cap each turn is wasted** (spilled after production, FreeCol's warehouse waste). Two kinds of goods are **exempt**: **food** (it's consumed/grown, not capped here) and the **non-storable** goods — bells, crosses and hammers, which accrue toward liberty/immigration/construction rather than sitting in the warehouse. So a colony churning out more cotton than it can ship loses the surplus until you build a warehouse or trade it away. **You are now warned of this** (`86d3e4buh`): when a human colony spills a good over its cap, an end-of-turn notice tells you which colony and how much was wasted, so you can build a warehouse or set up a trade route before it keeps happening. The same end-of-turn notices warn you when a colony **loses a colonist to famine** (a food shortfall starved one colonist but the colony survived) and announce when one **starves out of existence** entirely. And **before** a turn ends, the colony screen's warehouse bar warns you **live** (`86d3fpyze`): a good **at or near the cap** is flagged amber with a *▲ will overflow* tooltip so you can ship or sell it before the next End Turn wastes the surplus, and a good **almost gone** is flagged red with a *▼ running low* tooltip (so, say, the tools your blacksmith is about to run out of catch your eye). These are just on-screen warnings — they read the same stock-vs-capacity numbers the overflow rule uses and change nothing about the rules themselves. The marks use simple fixed thresholds (low = 10 or fewer in store; high = within 10 of the cap); a per-good *configurable* threshold like FreeCol's is a later nicety.

**Buildings (economy slice 4):**
- New colonies start with the **free base buildings** (no build cost, not an upgrade): town hall, carpenter's/blacksmith's/artisan houses, pasture, etc. Construction of costed buildings/upgrades is the next slice.
- Building production runs in the tick after tiles: **unattended** entries always run (town hall rings 1 bell/turn); **worker** entries convert warehouse inputs per assigned colonist (carpenter: lumber 3 → hammers 3), scaled down when inputs run short.
- **Horse breeding.** A **pasture** (free base building) — upgraded to a **stables** — grows the colony's horse herd each turn from spare food. You need at least **2 horses** to start a herd; from there the bigger the herd the more foals are born (a stables roughly **doubles** the rate), the herd stops growing at the **warehouse cap**, and breeding only ever eats **surplus** food, never the colonists' rations (so it can't starve the colony). With a pasture a small herd grows by 2/turn, climbing past 4/turn once you hold 50+ horses; a stables reaches those rates at half the herd size.
- `CheckAssignBuildingWork`/`AssignBuildingWork`/`UnassignBuildingWork` oracles; workplaces cap (default 3); idle accounting spans tiles + buildings.

**Expert competence in upgraded buildings (`86d3drmzf`).** An **expert** earns a **bigger bonus in an upgraded manufactory** than in the base house. A master carpenter adds **+3 hammers** in the carpenter's house, but the **lumber mill** has a **competence factor of 2**, so his bonus is **doubled to +6** there — the same colonist, the better workshop. The upgraded buildings carry it: lumber mill / blacksmith shop / tobacconist shop / weaver shop / fur trading post / distillery **×2**, and the factory tier (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, fur factory, rum factory) **×3**. Competence scales only an expert's **flat** bonus: a master distiller's **×2 rum doubling is unchanged** in the distillery (the doubling itself isn't amplified), and a **free colonist** — who has no bonus — is unaffected, so this only rewards staffing your best workshops with the matching expert. (It also scales the **penalties**: an indentured servant's −1 on a manufactured good becomes −3 in a factory.)

**"Experts have connections" — factories that run without raw materials (`86d3fpyja`, optional rule, off by default).** There's an **optional realism rule** that rewards putting the matching expert in a factory: with it switched on at New Game, an **expert worker in a factory** (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, fur factory, rum factory, arsenal — the buildings that "have connections") keeps making a **guaranteed trickle of goods even when the raw material runs out**. The idea is that a master craftsman has *connections* — an iron works' master blacksmiths can turn out **4 tools each per turn** with no ore in the warehouse, a rum factory's master distillers keep bottling rum with no sugar, and so on. The floor is **4 units of input per matching expert**, so more experts means more guaranteed output; a **free colonist** gets nothing from it (only true experts have the connections). The floor **only ever helps** — a well-stocked factory that already has plenty of raw material produces exactly as before, because the guarantee never *lowers* real production. One bookkeeping quirk, straight from FreeCol (`86d3hjz87`): the production ledger **charges the full recipe's input** for what the experts made, even when the warehouse can't cover it — the store simply **bottoms out at zero** (it never goes negative). So a rum factory distilling "on connections" shows the sugar it *would* have used in its consumption column, while the warehouse loses only what was actually there. In the **default game this rule is off**, so factories run purely on real inputs exactly as they always have — nothing changes unless you turn it on. (This is a dormant FreeCol mechanic we've now wired up; the classic game keeps it off, so every existing colony behaves identically.)

**Building upkeep (`86d3drmzk`).** Some buildings can **cost gold each turn to maintain** (the lumber mill 10, the upgraded workshops 5, the factory tier 15, the custom house 15, …); each turn a player pays the **sum of its colonies' building upkeep** out of the treasury. **The classic game charges no upkeep:** FreeCol's classic ruleset ships the `enableUpkeep` game option **off** by default, so by default no gold is deducted and the economy is unchanged — the mechanic is in place and data-driven (a ruleset that turns the option on starts charging immediately).

**Bankruptcy (`86d3c9ux4`).** If upkeep is switched on and a player **can't pay the bill** one turn, it goes **bankrupt**: its treasury is emptied and **every colony's building production is halved** (−50% to the goods buildings make — hammers, cloth, rum, bells, …) until the player can afford its upkeep again. The penalty bites the turn *after* the player misses payment and lifts the turn it pays in full — a money squeeze that snowballs into a production squeeze. Because the classic ruleset never charges upkeep, **a player is never bankrupt in the default game** (this is FreeCol's `model.disaster.bankruptcy` disaster, which only fires under `enableUpkeep`).

**Natural disasters (`86d3c9uu8`).** A ruleset can let **natural disasters** (floods, tornadoes, disease, drought, …) strike colonies. Each turn there's a set **percent chance** one of your colonies is hit; a stricken colony can **lose some gold, lose half of a stored good**, suffer a **temporary production slump**, have a **building razed**, lose a **colonist**, or have a **ship in its port wrecked**. That slump (`86d3fpyu9`) is a **−50% hit to the struck colony's output for three turns** — a flood halves what a hit colony makes (its tile crops, or its workshop goods) until it recovers — and it lands **only on the colony that was struck**, not your whole empire. **Saving and reloading doesn't cure it** (`86d3hz9ga`): a game saved mid-slump comes back still slumped, and the colony recovers on exactly the turn it would have anyway — you can't wash a flood away by quicksaving. **Which disasters can strike depends on the colony's land** (`86d3fq0ye`): the disaster is drawn from the list its **centre-tile terrain** allows — a plains colony gets a **tornado**, a swamp colony **disease or a flood**, a hills colony an **earthquake or landslide**, a tundra colony a **blizzard**, and so on (each terrain weights its own disasters). A colony on land that lists **no** disaster (e.g. grassland) is simply never struck. The heavier effects (`86d3fq0ye`) now bite too: **lose-a-building** razes or downgrades one of the colony's built buildings (a warehouse drops back to a depot, a stockade is levelled), **lose-a-colonist** removes one of the colony's people — and if it was the colony's **last** colonist, the colony is **lost** entirely — and **wreck-a-ship** damages a ship sitting in the colony's port (it limps off to repair, or sinks if it has nowhere to go), exactly as a native raid would. **The classic game has none:** FreeCol's classic ruleset sets the disaster chance to **0**, so no disaster ever rolls and the default game is unchanged — the data and the roll are in place (a ruleset that raises the chance starts rolling immediately).

**Construction (economy slice 5):**
- `SetBuild` sets construction to one buildable — a **building or a buildable unit** (validated: has a cost, population met, **required abilities satisfied**, and for a building not owned + upgrade prerequisite present; `Buildables`/`BuildableUnits` list legal targets). It **replaces the whole queue** with that single item (or clears it when given `null`).

**Build-ability gate (`86d3c9p0q`).** Some buildings only unlock once the colony earns the right (FreeCol `Colony.getNoBuildReason` → MISSING_ABILITY / COASTAL). A building type may carry **required abilities** (spec `<required-ability>`); a colony can build it only when it satisfies every one. The classic gates:
- **Coast** — **docks, drydock, shipyard** need `hasPort`: the colony must be a **port** — beside **open-sea** water (an ocean or high-seas tile). Inland colonies never see them in the build menu, **and neither does a colony beside only an inland lake** — a lake is enclosed water with no route to Europe, so a lakeside colony is land-locked from the sea and cannot build naval buildings (FreeCol `Tile.isCoastland` / `Settlement.isConnectedPort`: `isLand() && getHighSeasCount() > 0`, which excludes lakes). Our map already tags enclosed water as the `model.tile.lake` terrain (no sea route), so the gate simply requires an adjacent **non-lake** water tile (`86d3fpy78`).
- **Adam Smith** — the **factory tier** (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, rum factory, fur factory) **and the arsenal** need `buildFactory`, granted by electing **Adam Smith** to the Continental Congress.
- **Peter Stuyvesant** — the **custom house** needs `buildCustomHouse`, granted by **Peter Stuyvesant**.

The gate is checked in both `CheckSetBuild` and `CheckEnqueueBuild`, so neither the single-set nor the queue path lets a colony start something it can't qualify for. `hasPort` resolves to the colony's coastal status — `Game.IsColonyCoastal(colony)` is true when a neighbouring tile is water **and not the inland-lake terrain** (`model.tile.lake`), i.e. open-sea (ocean/high-seas) adjacency, the faithful `Tile.isCoastland` / `Settlement.isConnectedPort` rule that excludes land-locked lakes (`86d3fpy78`). Every other ability resolves to a Founding Father in the owner's Congress (`Game.ColonyHasAbility`). Required abilities are inherited down the `extends` chain, so drydock and shipyard keep docks' `hasPort` without restating it. When a build fails **several** gates at once, the message you see follows **FreeCol's priority order** (`86d3hjz87`): no cost → population too small → not coastal → missing ability → unit limit / no enabling building → already built / already queued / missing upgrade predecessor — e.g. an inland colony without Stuyvesant (customs-on-coast rule on) is told "needs a coastal colony", not "needs Stuyvesant". Whether the build is *allowed* is unaffected — only which reason is reported first.
- Completion is material-based: when the stores cover the full cost (hammers/tools), materials are consumed and the building appears — upgrades **replace** their predecessor, keeping its staff. No partial-progress tracking (matches accumulate-then-spend; FreeCol's per-turn hammer sink is a future cross-check).

**Ordered build queue (`86d3c9nxe`).** A colony holds an **ordered list** of buildables, not just one (FreeCol `Colony.buildQueue`). `CurrentBuild` is always the **front** of that list; the rest wait their turn.
- **Queue more work ahead.** `EnqueueBuild` appends a building behind whatever is already queued (`CheckEnqueueBuild` validates it the same way as `SetBuild` — costed, not already built or queued, population met — but an **upgrade prerequisite may be earlier in the queue** rather than already standing, so you can line up *lumber mill → then its upgrade* before either exists). `SetBuild` still replaces the entire queue with a single item; passing `null` clears it.
- **Auto-advance, one per turn.** Each turn construction completes the **front** item (when affordable) and the queue **advances** so the next item starts banking materials next turn — you no longer have to revisit the colony to pick the next building (FreeCol `csNextBuildable`). The colony **re-checks the front item only once its materials are in store** (FreeCol's affordability gate): a now-**impossible** item (already built, its upgrade predecessor is gone, a unit that's capped or lost its enabling building) is then **dropped without spending** them and the next item is tried — but an item you simply **can't build yet** (a building above the colony's current population, an upgrade whose predecessor is still queued) **stays put while the colony saves up**, rather than being silently discarded on an idle turn. Only one item is finished per turn.
- **Colony-screen queue editor (`86d3c9p63`).** The construction panel renders the whole `BuildQueue` (resolving each id with `Game.DescribeBuildable`, so a queued **unit** shows its sprite + name instead of mis-rendering as a building) and forwards edits to the oracles only (ADR-006): **Add to queue** appends via `EnqueueBuild` and lists **both** `Buildables` (buildings) and `BuildableUnits` (units); each row's **✕** calls `Game.RemoveFromBuildQueue(colony, index)` and **▲/▼** call `Game.MoveBuildQueueItem(colony, index, ±1)`; **Clear queue** is `SetBuild(colony, null)`. Reorder/remove are **unrestricted** — a resulting out-of-order upgrade isn't rejected, it's simply skipped when reached (the same self-heal as a queued item that became impossible), mirroring FreeCol's `BuildQueuePanel` (free drag/drop, validated at build time).
- **Colony-screen rename / abandon / clear-speciality / arm-missionary / pay-boycott commands (`86d3fq0aw`/`86d3fq0bg`/`86d3fpxge`/`86d3fpy7n`/`86d3fpyu0`).** `ColonyPanel` adds five buttons, each pure presentation (ADR-006) — it reads a `Check*` oracle to gate the button's visibility/enabled-state and on click calls the existing engine command; **no rule logic in the UI**.
  - **Rename** — a `LineEdit` (seeded with `Colony.Name`) + a *Rename* button (also fires on Enter) forward to `GameController.RenameColony` → `Game.RenameColony(colony, name)`. The engine rejects a blank name (`ArgumentException`), surfaced in the status bar; the confirm button is disabled while the field is blank (an input affordance, not a rule). The panel rebuilds (deferred) so the title reflects the new name.
  - **Abandon colony** — gated on `Game.CheckAbandonColony(colony)` (greyed with the reason when population > 1 or the colony is fortified, i.e. `ColonyDefenceBonus > 0`); on click forwards to `GameController.AbandonColony` → `Game.AbandonColony(colony)`, which removes the colony and **closes the colony panel** (the colony no longer exists). The departed colonist walks out onto the colony's old tile as its real type.
  - **Clear speciality** — per unit standing on/beside the colony, gated on `Game.CheckClearSkill(unit)` (appears only for an on-map expert with a `clearSkill` unit-change; FreeCol's no-active-teacher rule is covered by the on-map gate). On click calls `Game.ClearSkill(unit)` via the shared `Act(unitId, …)` helper, reverting the expert to a free colonist. See [education-schools.md](education-schools.md).
  - **Arm missionary** — added to the per-unit arm-roles list (`model.role.missionary`), gated on `Game.CheckEquipRole(unit, colony, "model.role.missionary")`, which only allows it when the colony has a `dressMissionary` building (a church or cathedral). On click calls `Game.EquipRole(...)` (the missionary role requires no goods — it consumes nothing from the store). Mirrors the existing soldier/dragoon/scout/pioneer arm buttons exactly. See [natives.md](natives.md).
  - **Pay boycott** — a **Boycotts** section (rendered only when some good has `Market.Arrears > 0`) lists each boycotted good with a *Pay (N)* button gated on `Game.CheckPayArrears(goodsId)` (greyed with the reason when unaffordable); on click forwards to `GameController.PayBoycott` → `Game.PayArrears(goodsId)`, lifting the boycott. The boycott is empire-wide (read from the shared human `Game.Market`), so this is the colony-screen surface of a market action — see [monarchy.md](monarchy.md).

**Building units (`86d3ce1bu`, `86d3c9p51`).** A colony's construction queue can hold **units** — land and naval — not just buildings (FreeCol `Colony.buildQueue` holds `UnitType`s too). The classic build-menu units are:
- **Wagon train** (40 hammers) — buildable in **any** colony: the **carpenter's house** (a free starting building) grants the build ability for it. A player may keep **at most one wagon train per colony** (FreeCol's `<limit>`: the player's wagon trains must stay fewer than their colonies); found another colony to earn another wagon train.
- **Artillery** (192 hammers + 40 tools) — needs an **armory** built first (the armory grants the build ability for artillery; magazine/arsenal inherit it). Uncapped — a colony with an armory can churn out artillery indefinitely.
- **Ships** (caravel, merchantman, galleon, privateer, frigate — each hammers + tools) — need a **shipyard** built first (it grants the naval build ability; only the shipyard does), and the colony must be **coastal** (the shipyard itself needs a port). A built ship **launches into the colony's port** — a water tile beside it — since a ship can't sit on the colony's land tile.

A finished **land** unit **musters on the colony's own tile**; a finished **ship launches onto an adjacent water tile** (the colony's port). Either way it's owned by the colony's player and **costs no colonist** (population is a requirement to build, never spent). Like FreeCol, a colony **set to a single unit keeps rebuilding it** every time it banks the materials (so a lone *artillery* order is a standing "make artillery" instruction); a unit followed by more queued items builds once, then advances. Units have **no "already built" limit** — you can queue or repeat the same type freely (unlike buildings, which are one per colony).

*Deferred (documented):* the **free colonist** is not a build item — its food cost is the [population-growth](colonies.md) threshold, a separate mechanism. (Ship construction is now in — see above; building a ship still places it on adjacent water rather than "moored at the colony", as our model has no in-colony ship berth.)

**Tile workers (economy slice 2):**
- Colonists work the 8 tiles around the colony, one colonist per tile, each producing **one chosen goods type** at the terrain's best attended yield (`Game.TileYield`); ocean tiles fish — **but only once the colony has built the Docks** (the classic rule that the sea is unworkable until you have a port to fish from). A dockless colony can put colonists only on its land tiles; building the **Docks** (or a **Drydock**, which is a Docks upgrade) opens its surrounding ocean/lake tiles for fishing.
- **Bonus-resource yields (slice 8):** a tile's special deposit boosts what's produced there — `TileYield` applies the resource's spec modifiers (e.g. minerals +3 ore, prime sugar ×2), then the player's Founding-Father goods modifiers (Henry Hudson +100% furs), in ascending modifier-index order. A resource never *enables* a good the terrain can't already make. Expert-scoped resource bonuses (an extra bonus when an expert works the tile) are parsed but **not applied** — we don't track which colonist works a tile yet. See [ruleset-data](ruleset-data.md) (`ResourceType`) and [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md) (the modifier system).
- `CheckAssignWork`/`AssignWork`/`UnassignWork` oracles; rejects: off-map, non-adjacent, tile taken, no idle colonist, **a water tile when the colony has no Docks** (the `model.ability.produceInWater` gate — FreeCol `ColonyTile.getNoWorkReason` `MISSING_ABILITY`), and terrain that can't produce the goods. **`TileWorkOptions(tile)`** lists what a tile can produce when worked — its terrain's attended outputs that yield > 0 (lumber/furs/grain on forest, ore on hills, grain/cotton on plains, fish on ocean), each with its yield, sorted by yield. **`ColonyCanWorkTile(colony, tile)`** is the colony-aware terrain gate (land always; water only with Docks) the colony screen uses so it never offers — and the click-to-move never targets — a sea tile the colony can't yet work. Both are rules queries (ADR-006). The AI colony planner (`PlanColonyTileWork`) honours the same gate, so a dockless coastal colony is planned with land tiles only.
- Founding and growth **auto-assign** to the best free grain tile (deterministic tie-break); the player can **rearrange via the colony screen** — release a worker, send idle colonists to food (the auto-assign shortcut), **or assign an idle colonist to a specific surrounding tile for a chosen good** (the per-tile picker — lumber/ore/cotton/furs/…, not just food). This unblocks the raw → refined chain (e.g. lumber → carpenter → hammers).
- Idle colonists produce nothing (building jobs are a later slice).

**Colonist membership (slice 9):**
- **Join** — a colonist (any person unit) on or next to a colony can `JoinColony`: population +1, the unit leaves the map, the newcomer is auto-assigned to a food tile. This is the payoff of immigration ([immigration](immigration.md)/[transport](transport.md)): ship a recruit home, disembark by a colony, and it grows the colony. **Wired into the colony screen** (a *Join colony* button per eligible adjacent/on-tile unit).
  - **Expert takes its specialty (the full expert-swap, `86d3drn5j`).** When the joiner is an **expert** (an expert ore miner, master fur trapper, …) and a **plain free colonist** is already working that expert's good (mining ore, trapping furs, …), the expert **bumps the free colonist off that tile and takes the job it's best at** — the displaced free colonist then reports to the best open food tile, exactly as if it had just arrived. So shipping home an expert ore miner doesn't leave it growing grain while a free colonist mines ore badly: the right worker ends up on the right tile. If nobody is doing the expert's specialty, nothing is swapped (the expert just goes to work like any colonist). This mirrors Colonization's behaviour where an arriving specialist slots into the job it suits.
- **Leave** — `LeaveColony` detaches a colonist onto the colony's own tile as its **real unit type** (`Colony.RemoveOneColonist`, 86d3b6nrz slice 6): a **free colonist** if the colony has one to spare (so specialists keep working), and only an all-specialist colony sends a specialist out — as its own type, never silently downgraded to a free colonist. It mirrors the colony's own count-reduction order (an idle colonist first, else the last building/tile worker) so the colonist emitted is exactly the one the roster loses, keeping the counts + overlay consistent. Population −1; a colony must keep ≥ 1 colonist, and a fully-staffed colony vacates one job to fit. **Wired into the colony screen** (the *Send a colonist out* button); the freed unit is selectable on the map (a tile-click selects a unit on the colony tile before it opens the panel).
- **Moving onto an own colony is a plain move, not a join (`86d3fzmove`).** `Game.MoveUnit` only sets the unit's position (and movement/orders/fog) — it has **no auto-join**: a unit that moves onto its own colony tile **stands on the map** at the colony (`CheckMove` already permits an own-colony tile as a legal target — Game.cs:4839 only blocks native units and *rival* colonies). Joining is **never** implicit; it happens only via the explicit `JoinColony` (the *Join colony* button). The "goes into it" the player saw was purely presentation routing: `GameController.HandleTileClick`'s own-colony branch opened the colony panel and offered no way to move a selected unit there. The fix is presentation-only (ADR-006) — that branch now **moves the selected unit onto the colony tile when `CheckMove` allows it** (an adjacent/reachable unit), and falls back to `OpenColonyPanel` only when there is no movable selection (nothing selected, or the unit can't reach the tile). A unit already on the tile is still handled first by the unit-selection branch (a tile-click selects an own unit standing there). Because the unit just stands on the tile, a **treasure train** walked onto its colony is then cashable on the spot: `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` accepts a train `IsOnMap` at its own colony (Game.cs:1384), so the SelectedUnitPanel's *Cash in treasure* button enables once the train is selected there (see [treasure-train.md](treasure-train.md)).

**Colony lifecycle (`86d3c9nzc`):**
- **Rename** — `RenameColony(colony, name)` sets the colony's display name (FreeCol `renameObject`); a blank name is rejected. The name persists (it was always saved).
- **Abandon** — `CheckAbandonColony`/`AbandonColony` give a colony up: it is removed from the map and its **last colonist walks out as its real type** on the colony's tile — a lone expert farmer leaves as an expert farmer, not a free colonist (`Colony.RemoveOneColonist`, slice 6; FreeCol `abandonSettlement`). Faithful to FreeCol, you abandon the **last** colonist — a colony with more than one must be thinned first via *Send a colonist out* (`LeaveColony`) — and a **fortified** colony (stockade/fort/fortress, `ColonyDefenceBonus > 0`) cannot be abandoned. Removal is by absence in the save (no format change); `DisposeColony` drops it from the colony list (its tile-work assignments go with it; any garrison units on the tile stay).
- **Starvation destruction (`86d3e4bnj`)** — when a colony's economy tick can't feed its **last** colonist (a food shortfall at population 1), the colony is **destroyed**: `Game.StarveColonyToDeath(owner, colony)` runs the same `DisposeColony` as abandon (tile cleared, colony removed, garrison units on the tile survive), but **no colonist walks out** — the last colonist starves to death with the colony. The economy tick **returns early** for that colony (it does not grow, teach, or export the turn it dies), exactly FreeCol's `ServerColony.csNewTurn` (`model.colony.colonyStarved` → `ServerPlayer.csDisposeSettlement`, then `return`). When the owner is the **human**, the loss is surfaced as a transient `ColonyStarvedNotice` (`Game.ColonyStarvedNotices`, the disaster-notice sibling) and recorded as a `HistoryEventKind.ColonyDestroyed` event (FreeCol `COLONY_DESTROYED`; carries no score today but feeds the later colony-lost score penalty). **Deterministic** — no RNG (ADR-009); **no save-format change** (a destroyed colony is simply absent from the save — no new persisted state). Unreachable on a classic map (every settleable centre yields ≥ 2 food), so the default game keeps every colony and the L5 soak's `Population ≥ 1` invariant + byte-identical round-trip stay green.
- *(Deferred: a player-facing rename/abandon **UI**, and a player-facing **starvation-destruction message UI** — these are GameLogic commands/oracles + the transient notice; the presentation surfaces them.)*

**Spy on a rival colony (`86d3drn4m`, FreeCol `InGameController.spySettlement`):** a **scout-role** unit (anything carrying `model.ability.spyOnColony`) standing adjacent to a colony owned by **another player** may spy on it.
| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Unit is at sea / in Europe, or has no movement left | rejected (`CheckSpyOnColony`) |
| Unit lacks the `spyOnColony` ability (not a scout) | rejected ("only a scout can") |
| Target tile is not adjacent to the unit | rejected ("move next to the colony") |
| Target is not a colony, or is the unit's **own** colony | rejected ("no rival colony to spy on") |
| Otherwise | a spy attempt: it **always succeeds** — the scout's turn ends, the colony tile is revealed, and a `ColonyInteriorSnapshot` is returned |

- The spy **does not require war or any stance** — FreeCol gates only on the ability + the foreign-colony move type, so a scout at peace may look. A **garrison** on the colony tile does **not** block the look (unlike an assault) — the scout enters as a visitor.
- The spy **always succeeds** — the player receives a `ColonyInteriorSnapshot` — a **read-only, one-shot** copy of the colony's interior (name, owner, population, buildings, current construction, building-worker counts, worked-tile→goods layout, warehouse stores, Sons-of-Liberty %, production bonus) for the presentation to render. It is a **snapshot**, not ongoing visibility (FreeCol reveals the interior for that one look only). The scout's **turn ends** (FreeCol `setMovesLeft(0)`) and the colony **tile is revealed**.
- **FreeCol-exact (no failure path):** FreeCol's `spySettlement` never fails, so neither does ours — there is **no rebuff/evasion roll**. The spy draws **no RNG at all**, so the human's economy stream 0 is never shifted — a default game (which never spies) is byte-identical (ADR-006/009).

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** ✅ **minimum colony spacing cross-check done (FP-5).** Founding is now blocked on a tile adjacent to an existing colony, matching FreeCol's `Player.canClaimToFoundSettlementReason` (`tile.getAdjacentColonies()` must be empty) and the original's no-touching-footprints rule. Native settlements do **not** block founding (FreeCol treats native proximity as a land claim/price, not a hard bar; we don't model land price yet).

## 3. Technical design

- `Colony`: id, name, position, population (mutable internal — grows in Phase 3), `OwnerId` (`{ get; internal set; }` — which player holds it; reassigned on **capture**, see [combat](combat.md)).
- **Spy on a rival colony** (`86d3drn4m`, FreeCol `InGameController.spySettlement`/`SpySettlementMessage`/`Ability.SPY_ON_COLONY`): `Game.CheckSpyOnColony(unit, target)` gates an on-map unit with the `model.ability.spyOnColony` role ability (read via `Ruleset.Role(unit.RoleId).GrantedAbilities`, mirroring `CheckEstablishMission`), movement left, **adjacent** (`Position.IsAdjacentTo`) to a colony owned by **another** player. No stance gate (FreeCol gates only on the ability + the `ENTER_FOREIGN_COLONY_WITH_SCOUT` move type) and **no garrison gate** (a visitor, not an assault). `Game.SpyOnColony` ends the scout's turn (`MovementLeft = 0`, FreeCol `setMovesLeft(0)`), reveals the colony tile (`RevealAround` at `ColonySightRadius`), and **always** returns `SpyResult.Revealed(ColonyInteriorSnapshot.Of(colony))` — **FreeCol-exact: the spy never fails**, so there is no rebuff roll and **no RNG is drawn at all**. The `ColonyInteriorSnapshot` (`Colonies/ColonyInteriorSnapshot.cs`) is a **defensive copy** of the colony's interior (buildings, current build, building-worker counts, tile-worker layout, stores, SoL%, production bonus) — the read-only oracle the presentation renders for a one-shot view; the player gains **no** ongoing visibility (FreeCol reveals the interior for the one look only). `SpyResult` is a thin wrapper carrying the (always-present) snapshot. Because spying draws no randomness, it **never shifts the human's economy stream 0** → a default game (no spying) is byte-identical (ADR-006/009). The public `SpyOnColony(unit, target)` routes the human; an internal `(player, …)` overload spies on any owner's behalf (no RNG parameter). **No save-format change** (the snapshot is a transient query result, not persisted state); presentation/UI is deferred (GameLogic oracle + command only).
- **Net-production read** (`86d3c9wz6`): `Game.ColonyNetProduction(colony)` is a public, pure read returning the colony's per-turn net production keyed by **stored** good — each tile worker's `TileYield` (human-fathers folded) + the colony-centre tile's unattended output, less food eaten (`Population × Ruleset.ColonyConstants.FoodPerColonist`). It is the single tested source behind both the colony screen's production bar (`ColonyPanel.NetProduction`) and the empire **colony report** (`ColonyReportPanel`), replacing a duplicated presentation-side fold (ADR-006). Building-worker conversions (e.g. a weaver's cloth) are deliberately excluded — it mirrors the long-standing colony-screen production bar; the survival-accurate food figure remains `ColonyNetFood`. No RNG, no save change.
- **Full production-summary read** (`86d3f674t`, FreeCol's colony-panel production summary): `Game.ColonyProductionSummary(colony)` is a public, pure read returning the colony's **full per-turn breakdown** keyed by **stored** good — a `Colonies/ColonyGoodFlow(Produced, Consumed)` record per good (with `Net = Produced − Consumed`). Unlike `ColonyNetProduction` (tiles + centre − food only), it also folds in **building production**: a staffed building's outputs (a weaver's cloth, carpenter's hammers), the unattended town-hall bells / church crosses, AND the raw inputs those buildings burn. A faithful **dry-run** of `RunColonyTurn`'s production order — (1) colony-centre unattended yield, (2) worked tiles (worker type + Sons-of-Liberty bonus, floored at 0), (3) each building in build order against a **running working copy** of the warehouse so input scarcity matches the live turn, then (4) food eaten — sharing the production maths with the live turn via the extracted pure `ComputeBuildingProduction(colony, building, entry, storeOf, ownerBankrupt)` (the same calculator `RunBuildingProduction` now applies, so the live path is byte-identical). Construction and horse breeding are excluded (a one-off material spend / a surplus-only auto-production special case — neither is steady-state production a summary should show). No RNG, no mutation, no save change; an L2 test pins the reported net of a steady-state good (lumber, no construction) to the warehouse delta over a real `EndTurn`.
- **Worker sprites on the colony screen** (`86d3f674f`/`86d3f6754`, display fix): the colony screen draws every tile and building worker as its **actual unit type** (`ColonyPanel.WorkerSprite(typeId)` → `UnitMarker.ResolveTexture(short, default-role)`, falling back to `ColonyArt.UnitIcon` — the identical `units/<type>.png` bare-type path). Previously every tile worker was hard-drawn as `freeColonist` and building workers showed only a bare count, so an expert was invisible. Tile workers read `Colony.WorkerTypeAt(tile)` (the `_tileWorkerTypes` overlay — free colonist when absent); building cells render one **per-slot portrait** per `Colony.BuildingOccupants(buildingId)` occupant (non-free overlay padded with free colonists to the worker count), each a click-to-remove button (`Worker_{building}_{slot}`) over the existing `UnassignBuildingWork` oracle, with empty workplaces faintly marked. Pure presentation (ADR-006) — the worker-type overlay + the 2× expert production bonus already existed and were already applied in `RunColonyTurn` (verified, not changed); this only makes them **visible**. No save/RNG change; the colony-panel visual golden shifts (new production-overview section + building portraits) and is regenerated on Linux CI.
- **Tile-diamond yield = the worker's actual yield** (`86d3f674x`, display bug — Chris playtest: an expert lumberjack's tile diamond read "4" lumber while the production overview correctly read "8"): the per-tile diamond badge (`ColonyPanel.cs`, the worked-tile branch) drew `EffectiveYield(_game.TileYield(tile, good))` — the **2-arg base/free-colonist** yield (`Game.TileYield(Position, string)`) plus only the Sons-of-Liberty bonus — so it **ignored the tile worker's index-30 production modifier** (the expert's 2× / +N), under-reporting an expert tile. The fix routes the badge through a new public presentation oracle `Game.TileWorkerNetYield(colony, tile, goodsId)` = `Math.Max(0, TileYield(owner, colony.WorkerTypeAt(tile), tile, goodsId) + colony.ProductionBonus)` — **the exact figure `ColonyProductionSummary` / `RunColonyTurn` bank for that tile** (the worker-type-aware 4-arg `TileYield`, then the SoL bonus, floored at 0), with `owner = PlayerById(colony.OwnerId) ?? _human` as the colony turn uses. The `EffectiveYield` wrapper is **dropped** at the badge — `TileWorkerNetYield` already folds the SoL `ProductionBonus` and floors at 0, so re-wrapping would **double-count** the bonus. So an expert lumberjack's diamond now shows 8 (matching the overview), a free colonist on the same tile still shows the base, and the colony-centre / unattended yields (drawn separately, not via this badge) are unaffected. **Pure presentation** (ADR-006 — one tested figure shared with the production engine); no engine/save/RNG change.
- **Data-driven colony scalars** (`86d3drpgg`): the per-colonist food appetite, the growth threshold, the liberty-per-rebel divisor and the default custom-house export level are parsed into `Specification.ColonyConstants` (`Ruleset.ColonyConstants`; see [ruleset-data](ruleset-data.md) §3). `Game` reads the **food pair** in the colony turn (`RunColonyTurn`'s `ConsumeFood(Population × FoodPerColonist)` + the `Food ≥ FoodForGrowth` birth) and the net-food oracles (`ColonyNetProduction`/`ColonyNetFood`/the worker planner's `NetFood`). The **liberty/export pair** is carried *onto each colony* as a plain value — `Colony.RebelLibertyDivisor` (the `SonsOfLiberty` divisor + the `AddLiberty` 100%-SoL cap) and `Colony.ExportRetainDefault` (the `ExportOf`/`SetExport` default) — set from the ruleset when a colony is **founded** (`Game.FoundColony`) or **loaded** (`SaveGame.Restore`), exactly the `Colony.Government` precedent (the pure colony model holds no ruleset reference; the divisor/retain default are **re-derived from the ruleset on load, not persisted**, so no save-format change). The old `Colony.FoodPerColonist`/`FoodForGrowth`/`LibertyPerRebel`/`DefaultExportLevel` are now `static readonly` aliases of `ColonyConstants.ClassicDefaults`, so presentation/test static reads (e.g. the colony panel's `Food: x/200` label) keep the classic figure. Classic values byte-identical; per-source fallback to the classic default keeps an incomplete variant working.
- **Build queue**: `Colony` holds a private ordered `_buildQueue`; `BuildQueue` exposes it read-only and `CurrentBuild` is the computed front (`null` when empty). Mutation is internal-only via `SetBuildQueue`/`EnqueueBuild`/`AdvanceBuild`, driven by `Game.SetBuild`/`EnqueueBuild`/`RunConstruction` — presentation never edits the list directly (ADR-006). A queued id is a plain string: it may name a **building** or a **buildable unit**, so the queue and its save (`SavedColony.CurrentBuild`/`BuildQueueRest`, v24) hold units with **no format change**.
- **Buildables abstraction**: `Game.ResolveBuildable(id)` turns a queued id into an internal `Buildable` (id, cost, required population, required abilities, `IsUnit`, `UpgradesFrom`, the `UnitType`), resolving a building via `Ruleset.FindBuilding` or a buildable unit (land **or naval**) via `Ruleset.FindBuildableUnit` (only the free colonist is excluded, as a non-material/person unit). `Game.BuildRefusal` is the single gate both buildings and units pass, its gates running in **FreeCol's exact reason order** (`Colony.getNoBuildReason`, `Colony.java:1092-1116`, `86d3hjz87`): NOT_BUILDABLE (no cost) → POPULATION_TOO_SMALL → COASTAL → MISSING_ABILITY → LIMIT_EXCEEDED → the `canBeBuiltInColony` tail (a unit's MISSING_BUILD_ABILITY; a building's already-built / already-queued / WRONG_UPGRADE) — the allowed/refused *outcome* is order-independent, only the reason reported under stacked failures follows it; for units it adds the **build-ability scope** (`Game.ColonyCanBuildUnit`, from `BuildingType.BuildableUnitTypeIds` — carpenter's house→wagon train, armory→artillery, collected down the `extends` chain; a **naval** unit instead needs a building with `BuildsNavalUnits` — the shipyard — **and a coastal colony**, since the ship launches into a water berth) and the **build limit** (`Game.UnitBuildLimitOk`, from `UnitType.BuildLimit`). A completed land unit is placed on the colony tile by `Game.SpawnUnit(type, colony.Position, colony.OwnerId)`; a **ship** is placed on the first water neighbour of the colony (an owner-aware overload either way). `RunConstruction` skips any front item `BuildRefusal` now rejects, builds the affordable front one per turn, and keeps a **lone queued unit** to repeat (FreeCol `REMOVE_EXCEPT_LAST`).
- **Equipping roles + missionary ordination** (`86d3c9p3p`): `CheckEquipRole`/`EquipRole` change a colonist's role from the colony's stores (soldier/dragoon/scout/pioneer via `RoleGoodsDelta`), gated on the unit being a person on the colony tile and the colony holding the role's required goods. The **missionary** role carries `model.role.missionary`'s required-ability `model.ability.dressMissionary`, surfaced as `RoleType.RequiresDressMissionary`; `CheckEquipRole` now additionally requires `ColonyDressesMissionary(colony)` — a building granting that ability. `BuildingType.DressesMissionary` parses `model.ability.dressMissionary` down the `extends` chain (the **church** declares it, the **cathedral** inherits it; the chapel does not), mirroring `RepairsNavalUnits`/`BombardsShips`. The missionary role needs **no goods**, so ordaining consumes nothing. No RNG, no save change. (The colony's **crosses** are produced by the chapel/church/cathedral as **unattended** building outputs in `RunBuildingProduction` and drained to immigration — already wired; this item adds only the ordination gate.)

- **Starvation destruction** (`86d3e4bnj`, FreeCol `ServerColony.csNewTurn` → `ServerPlayer.csDisposeSettlement`): the food step of `RunColonyTurn` branches on the `ConsumeFood` shortfall — `shortfall > 0 && Population > 1` loses one colonist (the existing path); `shortfall > 0` with `Population == 1` calls `Game.StarveColonyToDeath(owner, colony)` and **returns** (the dead colony skips growth/teaching/exports this turn, mirroring FreeCol's `return` after disposal). `StarveColonyToDeath` reuses `DisposeColony(colony)` (the same `_colonies.Remove` as abandon — tile cleared by absence, tile-work assignments dropped; units garrisoned on the tile are untouched and survive), and — **only when `owner.PlayerId == _human.PlayerId`** (like every other `RecordHistory`/notice call) — adds a transient `ColonyStarvedNotice` (`Colonies/ColonyStarvedNotice.cs`; `Game.ColonyStarvedNotices`, cleared at the top of every `EndTurn`, never saved) and records a `HistoryEventKind.ColonyDestroyed` event (`RecordHistory`, score 0). The colony-turn loop in `RunPlayerTurn` was changed to enumerate `ColoniesOf(player).ToList()` — disposal mutates `_colonies` mid-loop, so the lazy `Where` view must be materialised first. **No RNG** (deterministic disposal; ADR-009) and **no save-format change** — a destroyed colony is simply absent from the save, introducing no new persisted state (save **not** bumped). Unreachable on a classic settleable centre (≥ 2 food ≥ a lone colonist's appetite), so the default game keeps every colony (the L5 soak's `Population ≥ 1` invariant + byte-identical round-trip hold). - **Horse breeding** (`Game.RunBreeding`, FreeCol `BuildingProductionCalculator` auto-production): a building with `BuildingType.BreedingDivisor > 0` whose production output is a breeding good grows the herd by `((herd − 1) / divisor + 1) × factor` (integer division) each turn, clamped to the warehouse headroom and to the surplus food it may eat. `BreedingDivisor`/`BreedingFactor` parse off the `model.modifier.breedingDivisor`/`breedingFactor` modifiers via `ResolveScalarModifierUpChain` (additive-then-multiplicative up the `extends` chain: pasture 50/2, stables ×0.5 → 25/2).
