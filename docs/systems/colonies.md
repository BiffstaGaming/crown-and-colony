## 1. How it works (plain English)

Select a colonist and press **B** to found a colony where it stands. The colonist settles down and becomes the colony's first inhabitant (it leaves the map). Colonies show as a small house with their name; click one to see its vitals in the status bar. Names come from a classic colonial list (Jamestown, Plymouth, …) in founding order.

**The colony screen.** Click your colony to open it. It fills the screen on FreeCol's **brown parchment** backdrop and is laid out like the original Colonization's (FreeCol's) colony view, drawn in **real FreeCol art**: a title and summary line (population, idle colonists, food toward the next colonist, defence bonus); a **production bar** along the top showing each good the colony makes or eats per turn (its icon + net amount); on the **left**, a row of **colonist portraits** (one per inhabitant) above an **isometric view of the surrounding tiles** — the colony's own settlement at the centre with the eight neighbours fanned out as overlapping diamonds, a colonist standing on each tile being worked (with the good and yield it produces, and a *✕* to release it) and a *Work…* picker on a free tile — sitting above the **construction** panel (the full **build queue** — what's building now, with its progress, then everything lined up behind it; each line has **▲/▼** to reorder and a **✕** to remove, an **Add to queue** menu that lists both **buildings and units** you can construct, and a **Clear queue** button); on the **right**, the colony's **buildings** as a grid of their real images, each showing how many of its work slots are filled and a **+ / −** to staff or unstaff it; a row of the **units standing outside** the colony (join / arm / send-out); and a **warehouse bar** showing every stored good as its icon and count. The left column's top shows the **Sons-of-Liberty band** — Rebels · Population · Royalists with the membership % and production bonus, over a gold/dark meter (see [sons-of-liberty.md](sons-of-liberty.md)).

**Putting colonists to work.** Each colonist either works one of the eight surrounding tiles (growing food, or a raw good — lumber, ore, cotton, furs) or staffs a building (turning raw goods into bells, hammers, cloth, …). New colonists report to the best food tile automatically. **Moving a colonist between tiles is click-to-move** (like dragging in the original): click the colonist on its tile to pick it up (it highlights), then click another tile to send it there (it takes that tile's best good), or click the **colony centre** to send it idle. You can also drop an idle colonist straight onto a free tile by clicking it. For fine control, each worked tile keeps a **✕** to release it and a **Work…** picker to choose *which* good a tile produces (e.g. **lumber** instead of food, which the carpenter then turns into hammers).

**Moving colonists in and out.** The colony screen's **Colonists** section moves people across the colony wall: **Send a colonist out** detaches a free colonist onto the colony's tile (click that tile to select it and march it off, or arm it), and any of your colonists standing on or beside the colony gets a **Join colony** button to fold into its population (the unit leaves the map, +1 population). A colony must always keep at least one colonist.

**Arming colonists.** A colonist standing **in** the colony can be equipped from the colony's own stores — **Arm soldier** (50 muskets), **Arm dragoon** (50 muskets + 50 horses), **Arm scout** (50 horses) or **Arm pioneer** (tools) — and **Disarm** back to a plain colonist (refunding the gear). The buttons appear only when the colony actually holds the goods. That's how you raise a defender at home: send a colonist out onto the colony tile, then arm it.

**Choosing what to build.** The construction panel shows a **queue**, not just one job. Pick from the **Add to queue** menu — it lists every **building** the colony can put up *and* every **unit** it can construct (a **wagon train** in any colony, **artillery** once you've built an armory, **ships** at a coastal shipyard), each with its hammer/tool cost. Whatever you add lines up behind the current job; the colony finishes one item per turn and moves on by itself, so you can plan several builds ahead and walk away. Re-order the line with **▲/▼**, drop a job with **✕**, or wipe the whole list with **Clear queue**. Finished units appear on the colony's tile (ships launch onto the water beside it); finished buildings join the colony.

**Ordaining a missionary.** A colonist standing in a colony that has built a **church or cathedral** can be made a **missionary** (no goods needed — the church does the ordaining). A colony with only a chapel can't: the chapel makes crosses but can't dress a missionary; you must upgrade it to a church first. Once ordained, the missionary can leave to **establish a mission** in a native settlement (see [natives](natives.md)).

## 2. Detailed rules

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Unit type lacks the foundColony ability (e.g. ships) | rejected |
| Terrain is not settleable (mountains, water) | rejected |
| Tile already has a colony | rejected |
| Tile is adjacent to an existing colony | rejected (minimum colony spacing — colony footprints never touch) |
| Otherwise | colony founded, population 1, founding unit consumed |

**Economy tick (every EndTurn, first economy slice):**
| Step | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1. Colony square produces | the tile's unattended yield goes to the stores (plains: grain 3 + cotton 2) |
| 2. Colonists eat | 2 food per colonist; grain drains before fish; stores floor at 0 |
| 3. Growth | at ≥200 stored food: −200 food, +1 population |

Goods enter the warehouse under their spec `stored-as` id — grain/fish/meat all become **food** (one warehouse entry, matching FreeCol; legacy saves normalize on load). **Starvation:** a food shortfall starves one colonist that turn (population floors at 1 — colony destruction is a future rule); assignments shrink to fit, pulling workshop workers before field workers.

**Warehouse capacity & overflow (`86d3c9nnp`).** A colony's warehouse holds only **100 of each storable good** — a **warehouse** raises that to 200, a **warehouse expansion** to 300 (FreeCol `model.modifier.warehouseStorage`, cumulative up the building chain; `Game.WarehouseCapacity`). Anything produced **past the cap each turn is wasted** (spilled after production, FreeCol's warehouse waste). Two kinds of goods are **exempt**: **food** (it's consumed/grown, not capped here) and the **non-storable** goods — bells, crosses and hammers, which accrue toward liberty/immigration/construction rather than sitting in the warehouse. So a colony churning out more cotton than it can ship loses the surplus until you build a warehouse or trade it away. *(The player-facing "warehouse full / famine in N turns" warning messages are a follow-up — they need a turn-notification surface.)*

**Buildings (economy slice 4):**
- New colonies start with the **free base buildings** (no build cost, not an upgrade): town hall, carpenter's/blacksmith's/artisan houses, pasture, etc. Construction of costed buildings/upgrades is the next slice.
- Building production runs in the tick after tiles: **unattended** entries always run (town hall rings 1 bell/turn); **worker** entries convert warehouse inputs per assigned colonist (carpenter: lumber 3 → hammers 3), scaled down when inputs run short.
- **Horse breeding.** A **pasture** (free base building) — upgraded to a **stables** — grows the colony's horse herd each turn from spare food. You need at least **2 horses** to start a herd; from there the bigger the herd the more foals are born (a stables roughly **doubles** the rate), the herd stops growing at the **warehouse cap**, and breeding only ever eats **surplus** food, never the colonists' rations (so it can't starve the colony). With a pasture a small herd grows by 2/turn, climbing past 4/turn once you hold 50+ horses; a stables reaches those rates at half the herd size.
- `CheckAssignBuildingWork`/`AssignBuildingWork`/`UnassignBuildingWork` oracles; workplaces cap (default 3); idle accounting spans tiles + buildings.

**Expert competence in upgraded buildings (`86d3drmzf`).** An **expert** earns a **bigger bonus in an upgraded manufactory** than in the base house. A master carpenter adds **+3 hammers** in the carpenter's house, but the **lumber mill** has a **competence factor of 2**, so his bonus is **doubled to +6** there — the same colonist, the better workshop. The upgraded buildings carry it: lumber mill / blacksmith shop / tobacconist shop / weaver shop / fur trading post / distillery **×2**, and the factory tier (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, fur factory, rum factory) **×3**. Competence scales only an expert's **flat** bonus: a master distiller's **×2 rum doubling is unchanged** in the distillery (the doubling itself isn't amplified), and a **free colonist** — who has no bonus — is unaffected, so this only rewards staffing your best workshops with the matching expert. (It also scales the **penalties**: an indentured servant's −1 on a manufactured good becomes −3 in a factory.)

**Building upkeep (`86d3drmzk`).** Some buildings can **cost gold each turn to maintain** (the lumber mill 10, the upgraded workshops 5, the factory tier 15, the custom house 15, …); each turn a player pays the **sum of its colonies' building upkeep** out of the treasury, and if it can't cover the bill its gold is **floored at 0** (it never goes negative — a bankruptcy *penalty* is a separate future rule). **The classic game charges no upkeep:** FreeCol's classic ruleset ships the `enableUpkeep` game option **off** by default, so by default no gold is deducted and the economy is unchanged — the mechanic is in place and data-driven (a ruleset that turns the option on starts charging immediately).

**Construction (economy slice 5):**
- `SetBuild` sets construction to one buildable — a **building or a buildable unit** (validated: has a cost, population met, **required abilities satisfied**, and for a building not owned + upgrade prerequisite present; `Buildables`/`BuildableUnits` list legal targets). It **replaces the whole queue** with that single item (or clears it when given `null`).

**Build-ability gate (`86d3c9p0q`).** Some buildings only unlock once the colony earns the right (FreeCol `Colony.getNoBuildReason` → MISSING_ABILITY / COASTAL). A building type may carry **required abilities** (spec `<required-ability>`); a colony can build it only when it satisfies every one. The classic gates:
- **Coast** — **docks, drydock, shipyard** need `hasPort`: the colony must be **coastal** (a water tile beside it). Inland colonies never see them in the build menu. *(We treat "coastal" as "next to any water tile"; FreeCol's stricter `isCoastland` excludes inland lakes and needs a high-seas-connectivity graph we don't model yet — a documented simplification.)*
- **Adam Smith** — the **factory tier** (iron works, cigar factory, textile mill, rum factory, fur factory) **and the arsenal** need `buildFactory`, granted by electing **Adam Smith** to the Continental Congress.
- **Peter Stuyvesant** — the **custom house** needs `buildCustomHouse`, granted by **Peter Stuyvesant**.

The gate is checked in both `CheckSetBuild` and `CheckEnqueueBuild`, so neither the single-set nor the queue path lets a colony start something it can't qualify for. `hasPort` resolves to the colony's coastal status; every other ability resolves to a Founding Father in the owner's Congress (`Game.ColonyHasAbility`). Required abilities are inherited down the `extends` chain, so drydock and shipyard keep docks' `hasPort` without restating it.
- Completion is material-based: when the stores cover the full cost (hammers/tools), materials are consumed and the building appears — upgrades **replace** their predecessor, keeping its staff. No partial-progress tracking (matches accumulate-then-spend; FreeCol's per-turn hammer sink is a future cross-check).

**Ordered build queue (`86d3c9nxe`).** A colony holds an **ordered list** of buildables, not just one (FreeCol `Colony.buildQueue`). `CurrentBuild` is always the **front** of that list; the rest wait their turn.
- **Queue more work ahead.** `EnqueueBuild` appends a building behind whatever is already queued (`CheckEnqueueBuild` validates it the same way as `SetBuild` — costed, not already built or queued, population met — but an **upgrade prerequisite may be earlier in the queue** rather than already standing, so you can line up *lumber mill → then its upgrade* before either exists). `SetBuild` still replaces the entire queue with a single item; passing `null` clears it.
- **Auto-advance, one per turn.** Each turn construction completes the **front** item (when affordable) and the queue **advances** so the next item starts banking materials next turn — you no longer have to revisit the colony to pick the next building (FreeCol `csNextBuildable`). The colony **re-checks the front item only once its materials are in store** (FreeCol's affordability gate): a now-**impossible** item (already built, its upgrade predecessor is gone, a unit that's capped or lost its enabling building) is then **dropped without spending** them and the next item is tried — but an item you simply **can't build yet** (a building above the colony's current population, an upgrade whose predecessor is still queued) **stays put while the colony saves up**, rather than being silently discarded on an idle turn. Only one item is finished per turn.
- **Colony-screen queue editor (`86d3c9p63`).** The construction panel renders the whole `BuildQueue` (resolving each id with `Game.DescribeBuildable`, so a queued **unit** shows its sprite + name instead of mis-rendering as a building) and forwards edits to the oracles only (ADR-006): **Add to queue** appends via `EnqueueBuild` and lists **both** `Buildables` (buildings) and `BuildableUnits` (units); each row's **✕** calls `Game.RemoveFromBuildQueue(colony, index)` and **▲/▼** call `Game.MoveBuildQueueItem(colony, index, ±1)`; **Clear queue** is `SetBuild(colony, null)`. Reorder/remove are **unrestricted** — a resulting out-of-order upgrade isn't rejected, it's simply skipped when reached (the same self-heal as a queued item that became impossible), mirroring FreeCol's `BuildQueuePanel` (free drag/drop, validated at build time).

**Building units (`86d3ce1bu`, `86d3c9p51`).** A colony's construction queue can hold **units** — land and naval — not just buildings (FreeCol `Colony.buildQueue` holds `UnitType`s too). The classic build-menu units are:
- **Wagon train** (40 hammers) — buildable in **any** colony: the **carpenter's house** (a free starting building) grants the build ability for it. A player may keep **at most one wagon train per colony** (FreeCol's `<limit>`: the player's wagon trains must stay fewer than their colonies); found another colony to earn another wagon train.
- **Artillery** (192 hammers + 40 tools) — needs an **armory** built first (the armory grants the build ability for artillery; magazine/arsenal inherit it). Uncapped — a colony with an armory can churn out artillery indefinitely.
- **Ships** (caravel, merchantman, galleon, privateer, frigate — each hammers + tools) — need a **shipyard** built first (it grants the naval build ability; only the shipyard does), and the colony must be **coastal** (the shipyard itself needs a port). A built ship **launches into the colony's port** — a water tile beside it — since a ship can't sit on the colony's land tile.

A finished **land** unit **musters on the colony's own tile**; a finished **ship launches onto an adjacent water tile** (the colony's port). Either way it's owned by the colony's player and **costs no colonist** (population is a requirement to build, never spent). Like FreeCol, a colony **set to a single unit keeps rebuilding it** every time it banks the materials (so a lone *artillery* order is a standing "make artillery" instruction); a unit followed by more queued items builds once, then advances. Units have **no "already built" limit** — you can queue or repeat the same type freely (unlike buildings, which are one per colony).

*Deferred (documented):* the **free colonist** is not a build item — its food cost is the [population-growth](colonies.md) threshold, a separate mechanism. (Ship construction is now in — see above; building a ship still places it on adjacent water rather than "moored at the colony", as our model has no in-colony ship berth.)

**Tile workers (economy slice 2):**
- Colonists work the 8 tiles around the colony, one colonist per tile, each producing **one chosen goods type** at the terrain's best attended yield (`Game.TileYield`); ocean tiles fish — **but only once the colony has built the Docks** (the classic rule that the sea is unworkable until you have a port to fish from). A dockless colony can put colonists only on its land tiles; building the **Docks** (or a **Drydock**, which is a Docks upgrade) opens its surrounding ocean/lake tiles for fishing.
- **Bonus-resource yields (slice 8):** a tile's special deposit boosts what's produced there — `TileYield` applies the resource's spec modifiers (e.g. minerals +3 ore, prime sugar ×2), then the player's Founding-Father goods modifiers (Henry Hudson +100% furs), in ascending modifier-index order. A resource never *enables* a good the terrain can't already make. Expert-scoped resource bonuses (an extra bonus when an expert works the tile) are parsed but **not applied** — we don't track which colonist works a tile yet. See [ruleset-data](ruleset-data.md) (`ResourceType`) and [founding-fathers](founding-fathers.md) (the modifier system).
- `CheckAssignWork`/`AssignWork`/`UnassignWork` oracles; rejects: off-map, non-adjacent, tile taken, no idle colonist, **a water tile when the colony has no Docks** (the `model.ability.produceInWater` gate — FreeCol `ColonyTile.getNoWorkReason` `MISSING_ABILITY`), and terrain that can't produce the goods. **`TileWorkOptions(tile)`** lists what a tile can produce when worked — its terrain's attended outputs that yield > 0 (lumber/furs/grain on forest, ore on hills, grain/cotton on plains, fish on ocean), each with its yield, sorted by yield. **`ColonyCanWorkTile(colony, tile)`** is the colony-aware terrain gate (land always; water only with Docks) the colony screen uses so it never offers — and the click-to-move never targets — a sea tile the colony can't yet work. Both are rules queries (ADR-006). The AI colony planner (`PlanColonyTileWork`) honours the same gate, so a dockless coastal colony is planned with land tiles only.
- Founding and growth **auto-assign** to the best free grain tile (deterministic tie-break); the player can **rearrange via the colony screen** — release a worker, send idle colonists to food (the auto-assign shortcut), **or assign an idle colonist to a specific surrounding tile for a chosen good** (the per-tile picker — lumber/ore/cotton/furs/…, not just food). This unblocks the raw → refined chain (e.g. lumber → carpenter → hammers).
- Idle colonists produce nothing (building jobs are a later slice).

**Colonist membership (slice 9):**
- **Join** — a colonist (any person unit) on or next to a colony can `JoinColony`: population +1, the unit leaves the map, the newcomer is auto-assigned to a food tile. This is the payoff of immigration ([immigration](immigration.md)/[transport](transport.md)): ship a recruit home, disembark by a colony, and it grows the colony. **Wired into the colony screen** (a *Join colony* button per eligible adjacent/on-tile unit).
- **Leave** — `LeaveColony` detaches a colonist onto the colony's own tile as its **real unit type** (`Colony.RemoveOneColonist`, 86d3b6nrz slice 6): a **free colonist** if the colony has one to spare (so specialists keep working), and only an all-specialist colony sends a specialist out — as its own type, never silently downgraded to a free colonist. It mirrors the colony's own count-reduction order (an idle colonist first, else the last building/tile worker) so the colonist emitted is exactly the one the roster loses, keeping the counts + overlay consistent. Population −1; a colony must keep ≥ 1 colonist, and a fully-staffed colony vacates one job to fit. **Wired into the colony screen** (the *Send a colonist out* button); the freed unit is selectable on the map (a tile-click selects a unit on the colony tile before it opens the panel).

**Colony lifecycle (`86d3c9nzc`):**
- **Rename** — `RenameColony(colony, name)` sets the colony's display name (FreeCol `renameObject`); a blank name is rejected. The name persists (it was always saved).
- **Abandon** — `CheckAbandonColony`/`AbandonColony` give a colony up: it is removed from the map and its **last colonist walks out as its real type** on the colony's tile — a lone expert farmer leaves as an expert farmer, not a free colonist (`Colony.RemoveOneColonist`, slice 6; FreeCol `abandonSettlement`). Faithful to FreeCol, you abandon the **last** colonist — a colony with more than one must be thinned first via *Send a colonist out* (`LeaveColony`) — and a **fortified** colony (stockade/fort/fortress, `ColonyDefenceBonus > 0`) cannot be abandoned. Removal is by absence in the save (no format change); `DisposeColony` drops it from the colony list (its tile-work assignments go with it; any garrison units on the tile stay).
- *(Deferred: a player-facing rename/abandon **UI** — these are GameLogic commands + oracles. **Last-colonist starvation destruction** is also deferred to the disasters system: the classic colony-centre tile always yields ≥ 2 food — desert/arctic 2, plains 3 — exactly a lone colonist's appetite, so a size-1 colony never starves in normal play; FreeCol's "starved colony disposed" only becomes reachable once food production can drop below that.)*

**Deviations from original / FreeCol:** ✅ **minimum colony spacing cross-check done (FP-5).** Founding is now blocked on a tile adjacent to an existing colony, matching FreeCol's `Player.canClaimToFoundSettlementReason` (`tile.getAdjacentColonies()` must be empty) and the original's no-touching-footprints rule. Native settlements do **not** block founding (FreeCol treats native proximity as a land claim/price, not a hard bar; we don't model land price yet).

## 3. Technical design

- `Colony`: id, name, position, population (mutable internal — grows in Phase 3), `OwnerId` (`{ get; internal set; }` — which player holds it; reassigned on **capture**, see [combat](combat.md)).
- **Net-production read** (`86d3c9wz6`): `Game.ColonyNetProduction(colony)` is a public, pure read returning the colony's per-turn net production keyed by **stored** good — each tile worker's `TileYield` (human-fathers folded) + the colony-centre tile's unattended output, less food eaten (`Population × FoodPerColonist`). It is the single tested source behind both the colony screen's production bar (`ColonyPanel.NetProduction`) and the empire **colony report** (`ColonyReportPanel`), replacing a duplicated presentation-side fold (ADR-006). Building-worker conversions (e.g. a weaver's cloth) are deliberately excluded — it mirrors the long-standing colony-screen production bar; the survival-accurate food figure remains `ColonyNetFood`. No RNG, no save change.
- **Build queue**: `Colony` holds a private ordered `_buildQueue`; `BuildQueue` exposes it read-only and `CurrentBuild` is the computed front (`null` when empty). Mutation is internal-only via `SetBuildQueue`/`EnqueueBuild`/`AdvanceBuild`, driven by `Game.SetBuild`/`EnqueueBuild`/`RunConstruction` — presentation never edits the list directly (ADR-006). A queued id is a plain string: it may name a **building** or a **buildable unit**, so the queue and its save (`SavedColony.CurrentBuild`/`BuildQueueRest`, v24) hold units with **no format change**.
- **Buildables abstraction**: `Game.ResolveBuildable(id)` turns a queued id into an internal `Buildable` (id, cost, required population, required abilities, `IsUnit`, `UpgradesFrom`, the `UnitType`), resolving a building via `Ruleset.FindBuilding` or a buildable unit (land **or naval**) via `Ruleset.FindBuildableUnit` (only the free colonist is excluded, as a non-material/person unit). `Game.BuildRefusal` is the single gate both buildings and units pass (FreeCol `getNoBuildReason` order); for units it adds the **build-ability scope** (`Game.ColonyCanBuildUnit`, from `BuildingType.BuildableUnitTypeIds` — carpenter's house→wagon train, armory→artillery, collected down the `extends` chain; a **naval** unit instead needs a building with `BuildsNavalUnits` — the shipyard — **and a coastal colony**, since the ship launches into a water berth) and the **build limit** (`Game.UnitBuildLimitOk`, from `UnitType.BuildLimit`). A completed land unit is placed on the colony tile by `Game.SpawnUnit(type, colony.Position, colony.OwnerId)`; a **ship** is placed on the first water neighbour of the colony (an owner-aware overload either way). `RunConstruction` skips any front item `BuildRefusal` now rejects, builds the affordable front one per turn, and keeps a **lone queued unit** to repeat (FreeCol `REMOVE_EXCEPT_LAST`).
- **Equipping roles + missionary ordination** (`86d3c9p3p`): `CheckEquipRole`/`EquipRole` change a colonist's role from the colony's stores (soldier/dragoon/scout/pioneer via `RoleGoodsDelta`), gated on the unit being a person on the colony tile and the colony holding the role's required goods. The **missionary** role carries `model.role.missionary`'s required-ability `model.ability.dressMissionary`, surfaced as `RoleType.RequiresDressMissionary`; `CheckEquipRole` now additionally requires `ColonyDressesMissionary(colony)` — a building granting that ability. `BuildingType.DressesMissionary` parses `model.ability.dressMissionary` down the `extends` chain (the **church** declares it, the **cathedral** inherits it; the chapel does not), mirroring `RepairsNavalUnits`/`BombardsShips`. The missionary role needs **no goods**, so ordaining consumes nothing. No RNG, no save change. (The colony's **crosses** are produced by the chapel/church/cathedral as **unattended** building outputs in `RunBuildingProduction` and drained to immigration — already wired; this item adds only the ordination gate.)
- **Water-tile work gate (Docks)** (bug fix, FreeCol `Ability.PRODUCE_IN_WATER`): `BuildingType.ProducesInWater` parses `model.ability.produceInWater` down the `extends` chain (`ResolveAbility`, like `DressesMissionary`) — the **docks** declare it, the **drydock/shipyard** inherit it. `Game.ColonyCanWorkWater(colony)` (private) ⇔ any built building grants it. `CheckAssignWork` refuses a sea tile (`TerrainAt(tile).IsWater`) unless the colony can work water — FreeCol `ColonyTile.getNoWorkReason` → `MISSING_ABILITY` for `!hasAbility(PRODUCE_IN_WATER) && !tile.isLand()`. The AI tile planner `PlanColonyTileWork` applies its plan through `AssignWork`, so it filters sea-tile candidates with the same `CanWorkTile` predicate — otherwise a dockless coastal plan would throw mid-turn. `Game.ColonyCanWorkTile(colony, tile)` (public oracle: land always; water only with Docks) is the terrain-level gate the colony screen uses to decide whether to offer the per-tile picker / accept a click-to-move (ADR-006 — the panel reads the oracle, never re-derives the rule). The classic centre tile is always land, so only the 8 ring tiles are gated. **Save-boundary defence** (adversarial-review follow-up): a save written *before* this gate could hold a colonist fishing a sea tile on a dockless colony, and the forward gate doesn't see that historical state. `SaveGame.Restore` therefore drops any water-tile worker on a colony lacking `produceInWater` once its buildings are restored (the colonist returns to the idle pool) — a no-op for any valid save, so the byte-identical round-trip is preserved. **No save-format change** (rule data, not state); **no RNG** (a pure validation gate). *(`ColonyCanWorkWater` is building-sourced, mirroring `ColonyHasExportAbility`; the classic ruleset grants `produceInWater` only via docks — a founding-father source would extend it then, exactly as for the export ability.)*
- **Per-colonist worker types** (`86d3b6nrz`, building toward expert/specialist production): a **sparse overlay** over the count model records each **non-free** colonist's `UnitType` (absent ⇒ free colonist). `Colony` holds `_tileWorkerTypes` (tile→type), `_buildingWorkerTypes` (building→occupant types) and `_idleWorkerTypes` — each specialist in exactly one. The count model (`Population` + `_tileWorkers` + `_buildingWorkers`) stays the **authority** for how many/where; `ReconcileWorkerTypes` keeps the overlay ≤ the counts as a safety net after the count-reducing flows that don't pick an exact colonist (starvation, job trimming), while the deliberate **leave/abandon** path uses `RemoveOneColonist` to pick the departing colonist and emit its real type (slice 6). Membership threads types: `FoundColony`/`JoinColony` seed the unit's type into the idle pool; `AutoAssignIdleToFood`/`AssignWork` place a **matching expert** (its `UnitType.ExpertProduction` == the tile's good) or a free colonist via `Game.PickIdleWorkerFor`; `AssignBuildingWork`/`UnassignBuildingWork` carry/pop a type. Save **v30** (additive): `SavedWorker.UnitTypeId` + `SavedColony.BuildingWorkerTypes`/`IdleWorkerTypes`, all omitted when free, so a free-colonist-only game stays byte-identical to v29.
- **Expert tile yields** (slice 3): the colony turn folds the working colonist's type into its tile output via `TileYield(player, workerTypeId, tile, goodsId)` → `ApplyGoodsModifiers(player, …, ApplyWorkerProductionModifiers(workerType, …, TileYieldPotential(tile, …)))`. The worker's index-30 `ProductionModifiers` apply **after** the bonus-resource (index 10) and **before** founding-father modifiers (index 40), floored at 0 — faithful to FreeCol's modifier order. So an **expert farmer** makes +2 grain, an **expert fisherman** +3 fish, an **expert fur trapper** ×2 furs; a free colonist (or a specialist on a good it isn't expert at) yields the plain figure. Indentured servants / petty criminals carry **no raw-tile modifier**, so their tile yield is unchanged (their penalty lands in **building** production — slice 5). `LandPrice` still reads the type-independent `TileYieldPotential`, so land valuation is unaffected. *(The colony-screen tile picker still shows the base/free-colonist yield — a display follow-up.)*
- **Expert-scoped resource bonuses** (slice 4): a bonus-resource `<modifier>` with a `<scope type="…"/>` (e.g. a `game`/`oasis` resource gives an **expert farmer** a further +2 grain on top of the +2 every farmer gets) now applies via `Game.ApplyScopedResourceModifiers(workerType, tile, …)`, folded on the resource-boosted potential (index 10) before the unit's own modifiers (index 30). `TileYieldPotential` still skips the scoped ones (it stays worker-independent for `LandPrice`); a free colonist or a non-matching expert gets only the unscoped resource bonus. This unlocks the parsed-but-skipped scoped modifiers (`ResourceModifier` previously "not yet modelled").
- **Per-worker building production** (slice 5): `RunBuildingProduction` no longer treats every building worker as a free colonist. Faithful to FreeCol's `BuildingProductionCalculator`, each worker's output is **its base + the Sons-of-Liberty bonus** (additive, index 20), then **its unit type's index-30 modifier**, then floored at 0; the building total is the sum over `Colony.BuildingOccupants` (the non-free overlay padded with free colonists to the worker count): `outputTotals[g] = Σ ApplyWorkerProductionModifiers(type, g, baseOutput + rebelBonus)`. So a **master carpenter** makes 6 hammers (base 3 +3), an **indentured servant** 2, a **petty criminal** 1. The **SoL bonus** is `rebelBonus = floor(ProductionBonus × building.RebelFactor)` **per worker** — the **lumber mill / cathedral** carry rebel-factor 2 and the **factory tier** 1.5, so good government boosts those more — and folding it **before** the index-30 step means a **multiplicative** expert (master distiller ×2 rum) multiplies the bonus too: at SoL +1 a master distiller makes `(3+1)×2 = 8` rum (not the old `(3×2)+1 = 7`). The **per-worker floor** means a bad government (−2) can't drag a productive colonist negative: a free colonist + petty criminal at −2 make `max(0,3−2) + max(0,(3−2)−2) = 1 + 0 = 1`, not the old pooled `max(0,(3+1)+2·−2) = 0`. **Inputs scale with the (bonus-inclusive) modified output** (FreeCol `minimumRatio`): the requirement keeps the entry's input:output ratio against the primary output's modified total, with the required input **floored to an integer before** the short-supply test and a tiny **EPSILON** before the final floors — so a 1:1 conversion never loses a unit to representational error (a petty's 1-of-3 hammers consumes its 1 lumber, not 0) and a starved non-1:1 factory matches FreeCol exactly. The **unattended** entries (town-hall bell, church crosses) and **breeding** path are untouched. An **all-free, bonus-free** building sums to `base × workers` with an integer ratio, so output **and** consumption stay byte-identical to the pre-slice scalar path (locked by the existing `BuildingTests`/`ProductionChainTests`). *Known simplification:* the input ratio uses the **first** output good, so a hypothetical multi-output attended entry whose workers modify only a non-primary output would mis-scale its inputs — no classic attended building has that shape (flagged for the variant).
- **Expert competence factor** (`86d3drmzf`, FreeCol `BuildingType.getCompetenceModifiers`): `BuildingType.CompetenceFactor` (`competence-factor` attr; default 1, resolved up the `extends` chain like `RebelFactor`) **multiplies an expert's additive production modifier** inside `RunBuildingProduction`. `ApplyWorkerProductionModifiers` gained a `competenceFactor` param (default 1.0 — the no-op tile/base-building path stays byte-identical); when `> 1` it scales **only `ModifierType.Additive`** modifier values by it (`value × competence`), leaving **multiplicative/percentage** ones untouched — exactly FreeCol's `getCompetenceModifiers`, which maps over `unitType.getModifiers` and rewrites the ADDITIVE ones only. So a **master carpenter** in the **lumber mill** (factor 2) makes `base 6 + 3×2 = 12` hammers (was 9), while a **master distiller** in the **distillery** (factor 2) stays at `6 × 2 = 12` rum (the ×2 isn't amplified), and an **indentured servant** in the **rum factory** (factor 3) takes `9 − 1×3 = 6` rum (the penalty scales too). A **free colonist** carries no production modifier, so competence is a no-op for it — **competence-1 buildings and all-free buildings are byte-identical** to the pre-change path (only an expert in a competence>1 building shifts, the deliberate economy change). Applied per occupant inside the existing `outputTotals[g] = Σ ApplyWorkerProductionModifiers(type, g, base + rebelBonus, building.CompetenceFactor)` fold, so the SoL-bonus-before-modifier order and the per-worker floor are unchanged.
- **Building upkeep** (`86d3drmzk`, FreeCol `Colony.getUpkeep` → `ServerPlayer.csPayUpkeep`): `BuildingType.Upkeep` (`upkeep` attr; default 0, resolved up the `extends` chain) is the per-turn gold each building costs. `Game.PayBuildingUpkeep(player)` — called from `RunPlayerTurn` after immigration/liberty accrual, before trade routes — deducts `Σ over the player's colonies of Σ building Upkeep` from `Player.Gold`, **floored at 0** (can't-pay never goes negative; the bankruptcy production penalty is a separate backlog task, **`86d3c9ux4`**, left as a `TODO`). It is **gated on `Ruleset.UpkeepEnabled`** — the `model.option.enableUpkeep` boolean game option (`Ruleset.ParseBooleanOption`; classic ships it `defaultValue="false"`), matching FreeCol which only calls `csPayUpkeep` when the option is on. **Classic therefore charges nothing and the default game is byte-identical** (the Soak's twin comparison + save round-trip stay green); a ruleset that flips the option on starts charging immediately. RNG-free; **no save-format change** (no new persisted state — upkeep is recomputed from the ruleset each turn).
- **On-the-job experience upgrades** (`86d3c9pgj`, FreeCol `model.unitChange.experience`): a **free colonist** working a tile accrues that turn's production as experience (`Colony._tileWorkerExperience`, sparse per-tile, capped at the worker type's `UnitType.MaximumExperience` — classic: free colonist 200, every other type 0) and then rolls a per-turn chance to upgrade **in place** to the tile good's matching expert. `Game.AccrueAndRollExperience` (in the worked-tile loop, iterated **row-major** so the `RandomFor(owner)` draw order is a pure function of board state — ADR-009): chance `= experience / (100·maxExp/probability)`, i.e. `experience / 5000` classic, peaking at the spec **4%** once experience caps; the draw is `rng.Next(maxValue)` and upgrades on a strict `< min(experience, maxExp)`. The target expert is `Ruleset.ExpertForProducing(good)` (grain → expert farmer, fish → expert fisherman, …); `Colony.UpgradeTileWorker` flips the type overlay + clears the experience, leaving `Population`/the tile assignment/the idle pool untouched (FreeCol mutates a live `Unit`; our overlay needs the bespoke op for the same net effect). **Eligibility is data-driven** — a worker type with no `maximum-experience` or no experience `<unit-type-change>` to the good's expert (every classic type except the free colonist, and every building worker — classic defines no building experience rows) is ineligible and draws **no** RNG, so it never perturbs the stream. Experience is per-(tile, occupant): cleared on reassign / leave / send-out / abandon, so a new colonist never inherits it. Saved additively in **v31** (`SavedWorker.Experience`, omitted when 0 → byte-identical to v30; pre-v31 loads 0). Two ruleset gaps were closed for it: `UnitType.MaximumExperience` (the attribute was unparsed) and `Ruleset.ExperienceUpgradeProbability`/`ParseExperienceUpgrades` (the prior `<unit-type-change>` parse keyed only on `from`, collapsing the nine same-from experience rows to one).
- `Game.CheckFoundColony(unit)` → `MoveCheck` oracle; `FoundColony(unit)` enforces and mutates (removes unit, adds colony). Same oracle/command pattern as movement (ADR-006).
- **Horse breeding** (`Game.RunBreeding`, FreeCol `BuildingProductionCalculator` auto-production): a building with `BuildingType.BreedingDivisor > 0` whose production output is a breeding good grows the herd by `((herd − 1) / divisor + 1) × factor` (integer division) each turn, clamped to the warehouse headroom and to the surplus food it may eat. `BreedingDivisor`/`BreedingFactor` parse off the `model.modifier.breedingDivisor`/`breedingFactor` modifiers via `ResolveScalarModifierUpChain` (additive-then-multiplicative up the `extends` chain: pasture 50/2, stables ×0.5 → 25/2). Food consumption is `foals × inputRatio` (classic 1:1), capped at **half of the food the colony *produced* this turn** (`consumeOnlySurplusProduction` 0.5) — `RunColonyTurn` tallies the centre + worked-tile food output and threads it into `RunBuildingProduction`/`RunBreeding`. This matches FreeCol: breeding draws on this turn's production, **not** the stored carryover, so a herd can't be grown off a stockpile and breeding leaves the other half (plus carryover) for the colonists, never starving them. No RNG, no save field (horses are an already-stored good).
- Save format **v3** adds `Colonies`; pre-v3 saves load with none (tested).
- Rendering: `ColonyMarker` (`_Draw` house + name via `ThemeDB.FallbackFont`), one per colony under `MapView/ColonyLayer`, reconciled per refresh.
- UI handles zero units (founding consumes the last one): marker hidden, status shows the latest colony.
