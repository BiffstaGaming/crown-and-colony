## 1. How it works (plain English)

Europe is across the ocean. Move a ship to the **high seas** (the deep water along the map's east/west edge), then press its **Sail to Europe** order — and after **three turns** it arrives at the docks. There you **sell** the goods in its hold for gold (minus tax) and **buy** goods to bring back. Sail it home and three turns later it re-enters the map where it left. Ships carry cargo loaded from a coastal colony.

**Sailing to Europe from the map:** select a ship and you'll see a **Sail to Europe** button in the unit panel. It only lights up once the ship is standing on a high-seas tile (the outer-edge deep water) — so the trick is to sail west or east to the map edge first, then press it. (A land unit never shows the button.)

**Worked example:** a caravel loads 100 sugar at your port, sails to the edge, crosses to Europe over three turns, sells the sugar for 200 gold, and sails home — re-appearing on the high-seas tile it departed from.

**Where a freshly-bought ship lands:** a ship you *buy* (or are *given* by the King) in Europe never sailed from a real tile, so it has no departure spot to return to. It is assigned the deep-water tile **nearest your nearest colony** (or, before you've founded one, nearest your first unit on the map), so on its first crossing it arrives **beside your territory** — not stranded at the far corner of the map. (This was a bug: new ships used to appear at the map's top-left high-seas tile, often a long way from anything you owned.)

**The Europe screen:** click **Europe** (or press **E**) to open it. It is laid out like the original game's harbour, in **zones** rather than one long list:

- **Header** — your treasury, the next-recruit price, and an **immigration progress bar** (how close the next free emigrant is).
- **Recruit / train / purchase** — the **recruitment dock** (three colonists, each with its price and a *Recruit* button), a **Train a specialist** list (expert farmer, master carpenter… — a *flat* price each), and a **Purchase** list (ships and artillery; artillery gets dearer each time you buy one). Any button you can't afford is greyed out.
- **Goods market** — every tradeable good with its live **buy (ask)** and **sell (bid)** price and a **boycott** flag; with a ship in port you get a *Buy* button (loads 100 into the ship's hold) and a *Sell* button for each stack the ship is carrying. The *Sell* button shows the gold the sale will actually **bank — after the King's tax** and after the price slides as your goods flood the market — not the headline bid price. A *Buy* button you can't afford (no gold, no room, boycotted) is greyed out rather than hidden, just like the recruit/train buttons. With no ship in port it just shows the prices, and a **boycotted** good shows its *boycotted (back-tax arrears)* state instead of a price.
- **Ships in port** — each ship is a **card** whose **cargo hold is drawn out as little slot boxes** (one per slot the ship can carry): a filled slot shows the goods or passenger in it, an empty slot is blank — so you see at a glance how full the hold is. Passengers can be *put on the dock*, and a treasure train carried home on a galleon can be *cashed in* fee-free right from the hold. A ship damaged in combat shows its **repair countdown** and can't sail or trade until it's whole.
- **Sail to the New World** — a *Sail* button for each ship ready to leave; it crosses back over three turns and arrives beside your territory.
- **On the docks** — colonists waiting to sail (each with a *Board* button for every ship that has room) and any treasure trains put on the dock (cash in fee-free).

It's the one place you recruit colonists, train/buy units, trade goods, put colonists on a ship, and send them home. Units in Europe are *not* drawn on the map — they live on this screen.

**Drag-and-drop (Phase 2):** as well as the buttons, you can now **drag** things between the zones — usually faster than hunting for the right button:

- **Drag a waiting colonist onto a ship card** to put it aboard (same as the *Board* button).
- **Drag a ship card onto the "Sail to the New World" zone** to send it home.
- **Drag a good from the market onto a ship card** to buy a 100-lot into that ship's hold.
- **Drag a cargo slot from a ship's hold onto the market** to sell that whole stack.
- **Drag an aboard colonist back onto the docks** to put it ashore (same as *Put on dock*).

The cursor tells you whether a drop is allowed — it shows a "can't drop" cursor over an illegal target (e.g. a full ship, or a good you can't afford). Every button still works exactly as before, so dragging is an *extra* way to do things, never the only way.

**Choosing which ship trades (the ship picker):** when more than one ship is in port, each ship card has a **Select** button. The good market's *Buy* and *Sell* buttons act on the **selected** ship (the card is marked with a ▶ and "Selected"); if you haven't picked one, they default to the first ship that isn't under repair — exactly as before. (Dragging a good onto a *specific* ship card always targets that card, regardless of the selection.)

## 2. Detailed rules

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| Load cargo | ship must be on the map next to (or on) the colony; goods move warehouse → hold |
| Sail to Europe | ship must be a naval unit on a **high-seas** tile and **not under repair**; arrives in `SailTurns` (3) turns |
| In Europe | not on the map; can sell/buy; can't move on the map |
| Sell in Europe | hold → European market (price slides down as the sale floods the market, **tax withheld per chunk**) → treasury; the quoted/displayed proceeds are the **after-tax** net (see [market.md](market.md) `SaleValue`) |
| Buy in Europe | gold → hold at the **ask** price; **buying raises the ask** as it drains the market (chunked, FreeCol `buyInEurope`) — see [market.md](market.md) |
| Train vs Purchase | Europe units split: **train** a priced specialist (skill > 0 — expert farmer, master carpenter…) or **purchase** a priced ship/artillery (no skill) |
| Artillery price | each artillery you buy raises **your** artillery price by **+100** (500 → 600 → 700…); ships and specialists stay flat |
| Sail home | from Europe; arrives in 3 turns at the departure high-seas tile |
| Off-map units | a sailing/Europe unit cannot be moved on the map |

**Deviations / simplifications:** sailing departs only from the actual high-seas edge tile (FreeCol allows any "high-seas-connected" tile); sail time is the fixed default 3 (FreeCol adds ship modifiers). Recruitment & immigration ([immigration.md](immigration.md)), carrying colonists on ships ([transport.md](transport.md)), and buying units (the spec `price`) all exist. A bought ship is parked on the high-seas tile **nearest the buyer's nearest colony** (FreeCol's per-player entry tile near its territory) so it arrives beside the colony on its first crossing, not at the map's top-left default; a bought land unit waits on the dock to board a ship. Bought artillery already fights (Bombard / artillery-in-the-open offence, demotes to damaged artillery on a loss — see [combat](combat.md)); bought specialists don't yet produce **expert yields** in colonies (the remaining "actually special" gap). The **artillery price increase (+100)** now comes from the difficulty level (`Ruleset.Difficulty.ArtilleryPriceIncrease`, medium 100; see [difficulty](difficulty.md)). The Train/Purchase split is exposed as two GameLogic lists, and the **Europe screen now honours it** (`86d3f6…` Phase 1): a *Train a specialist* list trains (flat price) and a separate *Purchase* list buys ships/artillery (escalating) — the old screen routed every priced type through `BuyUnit`, mispricing specialists and applying no flat-price path.

## 3. Technical design

- `Unit` gains `Location` (`UnitLocation`: OnMap / SailingToEurope / InEurope / SailingToNewWorld), `SailTurnsRemaining`, and a goods `Cargo` hold (`AddCargo`/`CargoOf`). `IsOnMap` gates map interactions.
- `Game`: `SailTurns` (3, base); `SailTurnsFor(owner)` shortens the crossing by the owner's Congress `model.modifier.sailHighSeas` (**Ferdinand Magellan** −1, floored at 1) — applied when a ship departs (`SailToEurope`/`SailToNewWorld`). `CheckSailToEurope`, `LoadFromColony`, `CheckSellShipCargo`/`SellShipCargo`, `CheckBuyEuropeGoods`/`BuyEuropeGoods`, `UnitsInEurope`. **`CheckSellShipCargo(ship, goodsId, amount)`** (`86d3f6…`) is the SELL mirror of `CheckBuyEuropeGoods`: it gates the sale (ship `InEurope`, `Market.IsTradeable`, not boycotted via `Market.CanTrade`, `ship.CargoOf(goodsId) >= amount`) and — following the `CheckCashInTreasureTrain` pattern — carries the **after-tax proceeds** as its `Cost` (the non-mutating `Market.SaleValue`, see [market.md](market.md)), so the Europe screen labels + gates the Sell button from the engine without recomputing tax/price. `SellShipCargo` now re-checks through it (a stale call is a clean `InvalidMoveException`, never a partial sale). `AdvanceSailing` runs in `EndTurn` (decrement, then dock in Europe or re-enter the map). `CheckMove` rejects off-map units.
- **Ships repair in Europe (1c-3b).** A ship damaged in combat limps to Europe under forced repair for several turns (see [combat](combat.md)). While `Unit.IsUnderRepair`, it sits in port unable to **sail home** (`SailToNewWorld` throws), **take on cargo** (`CheckBuyEuropeGoods`), or be **boarded** (`CheckBoard`) — FreeCol `isReadyToTrade`; `AdvanceRepairs` (in `EndTurn`) heals it one turn at a time. The Europe panel shows its repair countdown and hides its sail/buy controls until it is whole.
- **Persistence:** save v11 stores each unit's location, sail turns, and cargo; pre-v11 units load on-map with empty holds.
- **Unit purchase:** `UnitType.Price` (spec `price`; `IsPurchasable = Price > 0`). `Game.CheckBuyUnit`/`BuyUnit` debit gold and dock the unit in Europe; a naval unit's `Position` is set to `EuropeEntryTileFor(player)` so it can sail home **and arrive beside the player's territory** (the high-seas tile nearest the player's first colony by id, or — before any colony — nearest its first on-map unit; ultimate fallback `EuropeEntryTile()`, the top-left default, only with no colony and no on-map unit). This mirrors FreeCol `Unit.getFullEntryLocation` → `Player.getEntryTile` (a unit with no recorded entry location uses its owner's entry tile near its start/colonies). Chosen by Chebyshev distance with a stable row/column tie-break — deterministic, RNG-free (ADR-009), and the fog is not consulted. The same `EuropeEntryTileFor` placement is used for a King's **naval support** delivery (`SpawnInEurope`, a man-o-war), which previously spawned at `(0,0)`. **`86d3f6…`** fix: a freshly-bought/delivered ship used to take `EuropeEntryTile()` (first high-seas tile, top-left) directly and so re-entered the New World far from the player's colonies; a *returning* ship is unaffected (it keeps the real high-seas tile it departed from, recorded in `Position`). No save bump — `Position` is already persisted and the derivation runs at purchase time. man-o-war (mercenary-only) and the free colonist (recruited) are not purchasable.
- **Train vs Purchase + artillery price escalation** (`86d3c9qgy`): `UnitType.Skill` (spec `skill`; 0 for colonist/ship/artillery, ≥1 for an expert) splits the priced units — `UnitType.IsTrainedInEurope` (skill > 0) vs `IsPurchasedInEurope` (skill 0), surfaced as `Game.UnitTypesTrainedInEurope()` / `UnitTypesPurchasedInEurope()`. Each player carries an escalated-price map (`Player.UnitPriceOverrides`, mutable `UnitPriceMap`); `EuropeUnitPrice(player, type) = override ?? Price` is used by `CheckBuyUnit`/`BuyUnit`. After buying the escalating unit (`ArtilleryUnitTypeId`, the only one in classic), `BuyUnit` sets the override to `pricePaid + Ruleset.Difficulty.ArtilleryPriceIncrease` (medium 100) — so the next costs +100, per player; ships/specialists never escalate. Persisted in `SavedPlayer.UnitPrices` (v29, omitted when empty → byte-identical to v28 until a price escalates). **Public UI seams (`86d3f6…`):** `Game.CheckTrain(string)` / `TrainUnit(string)` (human overloads delegating to the owner-scoped `_human` internals) and `Game.EuropeUnitPrice(string)` (the human's current price oracle) let the Europe screen route specialists to the flat-price train path and label each type without itself knowing the escalation rule (ADR-006). No save change (no new persisted state).
- **Europe screen UI (zoned harbour, `86d3f6…` Phase 1):** `EuropePanel` (a `PanelContainer`, like `ColonyPanel`, sharing its parchment skin + `ColonyTheme`) renders six zones built programmatically inside `VBox/Scroll/Dynamic` (a centred content card): **(1) header** — treasury + next-recruit price label + an `ImmigrationBar` `ProgressBar` (`Immigration`/`ImmigrationRequired`); **(2) recruit/train/purchase** — the three `RecruitDock` slots (`Recruit_{slot}` → `Recruit`), a *Train a specialist* list (`UnitTypesTrainedInEurope()` → `Train_{shortName}` → `TrainUnit(string)`, flat price) and a *Purchase* list (`UnitTypesPurchasedInEurope()` → `Purchase_{shortName}` → `BuyUnit(string)`, escalating), each labelled `EuropeUnitPrice(string)` and greyed via the `Check*` oracle (`GatedButton`); **(3) goods market** — every tradeable good with `Market.AskPrice` + a boycott flag (`Market.CanTrade`/`Arrears`), trading a 100-lot through the first non-repairing in-port ship: the *Buy* (`BuyGood_{shortName}` → `BuyEuropeGoods`) is a **`GatedButton`** labelled `Market.BuyCost` and greyed (with the refusal reason in its tooltip) when `CheckBuyEuropeGoods` refuses — matching the recruit/train zone, no longer vanishing (findings #5/#7); the *Sell* (`Sell_{shipId}_{shortName}` → `SellShipCargo`) is labelled + gated from **`CheckSellShipCargo(...).Cost`** — the **after-tax** proceeds — never the old raw `BidPrice × amount` (findings #1/#8); a **boycotted** good shows its `boycotted (arrears N)` state, not a misleading sell price (the old identical-arms `boycott ? "sell {bid}" : "sell {bid}"` ternary is gone, finding #4). **(4) ships in port** — each ship's hold drawn as `CargoCapacity` slot boxes (`HoldSlots`, filled = good×amount / passenger, empty blank; the per-stack slot count comes from the engine oracle `Game.GoodsSlotsFor(amount)`, not a reimplemented `(amount+99)/100` literal — finding #3, ADR-006), passengers with `Unload_{id}` → `DisembarkToDock` and the fee-free `CashIn_{id}`; a repairing ship shows its countdown, no sail/trade; **(5) sail** — `Sail_{id}` → `SailToNewWorld` per ready ship; **(6) on the docks** — waiting colonists with `Board_{person}_{ship}` → `Board` and carried-home treasure trains with `CashIn_{id}` → `CashInTreasureTrain`. Every action re-checks its oracle on press (a stale button no-ops, never throws) — all Game oracles, no rules in the scene (ADR-006). Opened from `GameController` (the **Europe** button / **E** key). The map view renders only on-map units; off-map units appear on this screen. The panel is re-anchored near-fullscreen with margins + a `Scroll` in `main.tscn` so the zones have room.
- **Europe screen UI — Phase 2 (drag-and-drop + ship picker, `86d3f6…`):** an **additive** drag layer over the Phase 1 buttons (every click button still works — fallback / accessibility / the simple L3 path). Two thin rule-free transport Controls in `game/presentation/EuropeDragDrop.cs` implement the Godot 4.6 three-override pattern: **`EuropeDragSource`** (`_GetDragData` → a `Godot.Collections.Dictionary` payload + a floating `SetDragPreview` label; returns `default` for "nothing draggable" — Godot issue 78507, the C# override is non-nullable) and **`EuropeDropTarget`** (`_CanDropData` validates the payload kind **and** the matching `Game.Check*` oracle Allows it, so the cursor shows can/can't-drop; `_DropData` re-checks the oracle then forwards the single `Game` command and triggers `Changed()`). **Payload schema** (the `EuropeDrag` constants): `{kind:"colonist", unitId}` (a dock person → ship Board, or a passenger → docks Disembark), `{kind:"ship", shipId}` (a ship card → sail-zone Sail), `{kind:"goods", goodsId, amount[, fromShipId]}` (a market row → ship **Buy** when there is **no** `fromShipId`; a hold stack → market **Sell** when there **is** one — the `fromShipId` presence disambiguates buy vs sell and rejects the wrong target). **Five drops wired:** (1) dock colonist `DockColonist_{id}` → ship card `ShipDrop_{shipId}` ⇒ `CheckBoard`/`Board`; (2) ship `ShipDrag_{shipId}` → `SailDrop` ⇒ `SailToNewWorld` (re-checked via the inline `CanSailToNewWorld` — in Europe, not aboard, not repairing — there is no `CheckSailToNewWorld` oracle, so the guard mirrors `SailToNewWorld`'s own throws, reading state only, ADR-006); (3) market good `MarketGood_{short}` → ship card ⇒ `CheckBuyEuropeGoods`/`BuyEuropeGoods` (a 100-lot); (4) hold cargo lead-slot `Cargo_{shipId}_{short}` → `GoodsMarketDrop` ⇒ `CheckSellShipCargo`/`SellShipCargo` (the whole stack); (5) aboard passenger `Passenger_{id}` → `DocksDrop` ⇒ `DisembarkToDock` (re-checked via the inline `CanDisembarkToDock` — aboard a ship that is itself in Europe — mirroring `DisembarkToDock`'s throws). A refused drop is a **graceful no-op**, never thrown to the UI. **Ship picker:** each ship card has a `Select_{shipId}` button (`EuropePanel.SelectTradeShip`, public for the L3); the goods market's Buy/Sell target the **selected** ship (`_selectedShipId`, marked ▶/"Selected" on the card), defaulting to the first non-repairing in-port ship when none is selected (`TradeShip(ships)` — Phase 1's behaviour). The selection is **UI-only session state** persisted across the deferred `Changed()` rebuild and **cleared automatically** when the chosen ship is no longer a tradeable ship in port (sailed/sold/under repair) — **no save change**. ADR-006 throughout: no rule logic in the drag layer; every accept/drop reads a `Game` oracle or an inline state-only guard. *(Phase 2 complete: Phase 1 buttons + this drag layer + the ship picker.)*
- **Map-side "Sail to Europe" order:** the selected-unit HUD (`UI/SelectedUnitPanel/VBox/Orders/SailToEuropeButton`, wired in `GameController`) forwards to the existing `CheckSailToEurope`/`SailToEurope` command via `ApplyUnitOrder` — the discoverable surface for *departing* to Europe (the Europe screen only handles ships already in port). It is shown only for a **naval** unit and enabled only when `CheckSailToEurope` passes (the ship is on a high-seas tile), with a tooltip pointing the player to the map edge. Presentation-only (ADR-006); the engine command and its 3-turn crossing are unchanged.
