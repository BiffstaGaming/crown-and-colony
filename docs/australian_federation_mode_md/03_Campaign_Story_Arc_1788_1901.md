# Campaign Story Arc: 1788–1901

## Campaign structure

The campaign is divided into eras. Each era unlocks new event pools, goods, Historical Figures, buildings, and political objectives.

## Era 1 — Survival and First Contact, 1788–1797

### Historical theme

The First Fleet establishes a British penal settlement at Sydney Cove. Early years are defined by food insecurity, dependence on stores, unfamiliar conditions, and first contact with the Eora and neighbouring peoples.

### Gameplay focus

- Found first settlement.
- Establish farms, fishing, timber, and stores.
- Manage convict labour and discipline.
- Make first contact with local First Nations communities.
- Avoid starvation crisis.

### Unlocks

- Government Stores.
- Basic farms and fishing boats.
- Convict Labourer, Emancipist, Soldier, Surveyor.
- Arthur Phillip and James Ruse figure pools.

### Threats

- Food crisis.
- Disease events.
- Poor morale.
- Tension from settlement expansion.

## Era 2 — Early Expansion and Wool, 1797–1830

### Historical theme

Merino sheep and pastoral settlement begin changing the colonial economy. Roads, towns, farms, and early public works become central.

### Gameplay focus

- Expand beyond initial coastal settlement.
- Establish sheep stations and wool production.
- Build roads and early towns.
- Manage frontier pressure.

### Unlocks

- Sheep, Wool, Wool Shed, Road parties.
- Elizabeth Macarthur.
- Lachlan Macquarie from 1810 if prerequisites are met.
- Matthew Flinders, Charles Sturt.

### Threats

- Livestock trespass on Country.
- Drought.
- Rum trade and governance instability.
- Convict unrest.

## Era 3 — Separate Colonies and Free Settlement, 1830–1851

### Historical theme

Australian colonial settlement becomes more regionally complex. New colonies and free-settlement ideas develop, including South Australia and Western Australia.

### Gameplay focus

- Establish new colonial regions.
- Shift from purely penal settlement to mixed/free settlement.
- Build ports and regional trade.
- Develop banking, commerce, and immigration systems.

### Unlocks

- Colonial Credit Office.
- Free Settler / Assisted Immigrant unit pools.
- George Fife Angas.
- Caroline Chisholm.
- Mary Reibey if not already unlocked.

### Threats

- Distance and shipping costs.
- Labour shortages.
- Regional rivalry.
- Increased First Nations Country pressure.

## Era 4 — Gold Rush and Democratic Pressure, 1851–1872

### Historical theme

Gold discoveries transform the colonies. Population surges, migration increases, mining towns form quickly, and political reform movements gain strength.

### Gameplay focus

- Discover and exploit goldfields.
- Absorb large immigrant waves.
- Manage mining licence unrest.
- Build newspapers, courts, and representative institutions.

### Unlocks

- Goldfield, Digger, Gold Escort.
- Edward Hargraves.
- Peter Lalor.
- Responsible Government event pools.
- Chinese migration event chains.

### Threats

- Gold rush inflation.
- Crime / bushranging.
- Mining licence unrest.
- Anti-Chinese discrimination events.
- Frontier conflict intensification.

## Era 5 — Infrastructure and Intercolonial Rivalry, 1872–1889

### Historical theme

The Overland Telegraph, rail, shipping, refrigeration, and pastoral routes make continent-scale coordination possible, but colonies remain politically separate.

### Gameplay focus

- Link colonies by ports, roads, rail, and telegraph.
- Reduce communication delay.
- Expand wool, meat, and frozen export economies.
- Lower intercolonial tariff friction.

### Unlocks

- Telegraph Office.
- Rail Depot.
- Freezing Works.
- Charles Todd.
- Thomas Sutcliffe Mort.
- Sidney Kidman.
- William Jervois.

### Threats

- Drought.
- Infrastructure cost.
- Rival rail gauges.
- Imperial defence concerns.
- Low Federation Support in distant colonies.

## Era 6 — Federation Movement, 1889–1901

### Historical theme

Public support, conventions, constitutional drafting, referendums, and negotiations lead to Federation.

### Gameplay focus

- Build Federation Support colony by colony.
- Convene Federation Conventions.
- Draft constitutional clauses.
- Win referendums at historical-inspired thresholds.
- Address hesitant colonies.

### Unlocks

- Federation League Hall.
- Convention Hall.
- Henry Parkes.
- John Quick.
- Samuel Griffith.
- Catherine Helen Spence.
- Edmund Barton.
- Mary Lee and Louisa Lawson if civic reform prerequisites are met.

### Threats

- Referendum failure.
- NSW quota failure.
- Western Australian hesitation.
- Small colony representation concerns.
- Tariff disputes.
- Exclusionary law controversy.
- Low legitimacy from frontier conflict.

## Victory screen framing

The victory screen should celebrate the Commonwealth being proclaimed but include a historically honest note:

> The colonies have federated into the Commonwealth of Australia. This is a major constitutional achievement, but not the end of Australia's story. Many people were excluded from the political settlement, including many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and non-European communities. Country, sovereignty, and representation remain unresolved questions.
