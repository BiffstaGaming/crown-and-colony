# First Nations Design Principles

## Purpose

This file defines how Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples should be represented mechanically and narratively.

## Core principles

### 1. Use specific peoples and Country where possible

Do not use one generic `Native` faction. Use regional First Nations cultural/language groups with broad locations.

### 2. Treat Country as more than territory

Country should not be just a tile-ownership overlay. It should represent connection, law, food systems, movement, ceremony, and responsibility.

### 3. Avoid conquest-as-profit loops

The player should not be rewarded for attacking communities, seizing land, or destroying settlements. Conflict should create long-term consequences.

### 4. Replace conversion mechanics

Do not simply copy missionary conversion from Colonization. Use:

- Interpreters.
- Mediators.
- Agreement councils.
- Knowledge exchange.
- Protectorate/protection-era policy debates with caution and historical critique.

### 5. Represent resistance and survival

Resistance should be a legitimate response to land pressure, violence, broken agreements, or resource damage.

### 6. Include truth-telling text

Major colonial progress should include historical notes about exclusion, dispossession, frontier violence, disease, and survival.

## Recommended mechanical variables

| Variable | Range | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| Respect | 0–100 | Trust and diplomatic relationship. |
| Tension | 0–100 | Conflict risk. |
| Country Pressure | 0–100 | Settlement, livestock, roads, extraction, and armed presence pressure. |
| Agreement Strength | 0–100 | Stability of negotiated arrangements. |
| Knowledge Exchange | integer/list | Unlocked skills and ecological knowledge. |
| Legitimacy Impact | integer | Effect on Federation legitimacy and reform politics. |

## Country Pressure sources

- Founding settlement on or near Country without agreement.
- Expanding farms, sheep stations, cattle stations, mines, roads, rail, or telegraph.
- Armed units near communities.
- Livestock damaging water or food sites.
- Broken agreements.
- Violent incidents.
- Forced movement or mission/protection policies.

## Respect sources

- Trade without coercion.
- Gifts during hardship.
- Keeping agreements.
- Avoiding expansion into sensitive areas.
- Returning escaped livestock / compensation.
- Mediator or interpreter presence.
- Shared water/route agreements.
- High-legitimacy policy choices.

## Tension sources

- Country Pressure.
- Violence.
- Disease events.
- Broken promises.
- Resource depletion.
- Police/military presence.
- Expansion without diplomacy.

## Knowledge Exchange examples

| Knowledge | Effect |
|---|---|
| Coastal fishing | +1 Food from coastal tiles. |
| Seasonal calendar | Reduces drought/famine impact. |
| Fire management | Reduces bushfire risk and improves pasture regeneration. |
| Desert water knowledge | Desert movement attrition reduced. |
| Canoe / river travel | River movement improved. |
| Tracking | Scouts gain sight and ambush avoidance. |
| Bush foods | Food from scrub/bush tiles. |
| Eel aquaculture knowledge | Wetland/eel trap food bonus where regionally appropriate. |

## Agreement types

| Agreement | Requirements | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Passage agreement | Respect 40+ | Units can pass with less tension. |
| Trade agreement | Respect 45+ | Trade routes open. |
| Seasonal harvest agreement | Respect 50+ | Food yield bonus without settlement expansion. |
| Shared water agreement | Respect 60+ | Drought effect reduced, tension lower. |
| Road/telegraph corridor agreement | Respect 65+ | Infrastructure causes less Country Pressure. |
| Town boundary agreement | Respect 70+ | Settlement expansion penalty reduced. |
| High treaty-style agreement | Respect 85+ | Major legitimacy bonus, hard to maintain. |

## Diplomacy posture options

### Respectful engagement

- Slower land expansion.
- Higher diplomacy cost.
- Better long-term stability and knowledge exchange.

### Pragmatic trade

- Moderate benefits.
- Tension remains manageable if expansion is limited.

### Neglect

- No immediate cost.
- Country Pressure rises over time.
- Resistance events more likely.

### Coercion

- Fastest expansion.
- High tension and legitimacy penalties.
- Resistance, reform backlash, and possible victory-grade penalty.

## Federation interaction

Federation Support should not ignore First Nations relations.

Suggested rules:

- High frontier violence reduces national legitimacy.
- Low legitimacy makes reform figures more important.
- Strong agreements can unlock `Treaty Commonwealth` victory grade.
- Exclusionary policies may boost some colonial support but damage legitimacy.

## Language and terminology

Use:

- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- First Nations peoples.
- Traditional Owners.
- Country, capitalised.
- Specific group names where possible.

Avoid:

- Generic `Aborigines` as a blanket noun.
- `ATSI` abbreviation in public text.
- Treating AIATSIS map areas as exact borders.
- `Native village conquered` style text.
