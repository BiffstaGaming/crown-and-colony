# Playable Powers and Scenario Modes

## Recommendation

Use **British Australia** as the canonical historical campaign. Other powers should be alternate-history scenarios.

## Why Britain should be the main campaign

By the late 19th century, Federation joined six British colonies: New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania, and Western Australia. A faithful 1788–1901 campaign depends on that British colonial framework.

## Mode 1 — Historical Federation Campaign

### Playable power

- Britain / Australian colonies.

### Storyline

The player begins with British settlement in New South Wales and gradually develops the six colonies into a federation.

### Advantages

- Historically coherent.
- Fits Federation as victory.
- Allows accurate use of Australian political figures.
- Works cleanly with six colony referendum mechanics.

### Disadvantages

- Less asymmetry at the European-power selection screen.
- Replayability must come from starting region, difficulty, map seed, policy choices, and figure choices.

## Mode 2 — Alternate-History Pacific Sandbox

### Playable powers

- Britain.
- France.
- Netherlands.
- Spain.
- Portugal.

### Storyline

The Australian continent becomes a contested colonial theatre. Federation becomes a broader `Colonial Union` or `Commonwealth Compact` victory.

### Historical hooks

- Dutch contact and mapping from the early 1600s.
- French scientific and naval interest in the Pacific.
- British permanent settlement from 1788.
- Spanish and Portuguese options are more speculative and should be clearly marked as alternate-history.

### Victory variants

| Power | Victory label | Suggested flavour |
|---|---|---|
| Britain | Commonwealth of Australia | Historical Federation. |
| France | Australie Française Compact | Scientific, naval, and republican/imperial tension flavour. |
| Netherlands | New Holland Confederation | Trade, ports, cartography, spice-route flavour. |
| Spain | Terra Austral Viceroyalty Reform | Mission/empire management flavour, clearly counterfactual. |
| Portugal | Southern Sea Charter | Maritime trade and waystation flavour, clearly counterfactual. |

## Mode 3 — Colony Start Scenarios

Instead of different European powers, replayability can come from which colonial region is emphasised.

| Start | Difficulty | Gameplay identity |
|---|---:|---|
| New South Wales 1788 | Standard | Survival, convicts, first settlement, later political centre. |
| Van Diemen's Land / Tasmania | Hard | Island logistics, seal/fish, harsher frontier history. |
| Swan River / Western Australia | Hard | Distance, low population, later gold and Federation hesitation. |
| South Australia | Medium | Free settlement, planning, reform, suffrage, agriculture. |
| Port Phillip / Victoria | Medium | Gold rush power, high immigration, democratic pressure. |
| Moreton Bay / Queensland | Medium-Hard | Distance, pastoral expansion, tropical resources, low Federation support. |

## European power traits for sandbox mode

### Britain

- Faster convict labour pipeline.
- Better imperial shipping.
- Easier access to Federation figures.
- Higher imperial oversight.

### France

- Stronger exploration/science bonuses.
- Better early relations through scientific expeditions.
- Slower settlement growth.
- Alternate civic victory text.

### Netherlands

- Stronger trade and maritime mapping.
- Earlier coastal knowledge.
- Smaller immigration pool.
- Strong port-commerce bonuses.

### Spain

- Stronger imperial administration and missions in a counterfactual mode.
- Higher bureaucracy costs.
- Must be heavily alternate-history to avoid false historical claims.

### Portugal

- Strong maritime route bonuses.
- Better tropical/coastal trade.
- Low settler growth.
- Strong exploration but weaker inland infrastructure.

## Design warning

Do not let sandbox powers overwrite the main historical narrative. The main mode should remain British Australia to preserve the 1788–1901 Federation story.
