# Balancing Notes

## Overall balance goals

Australian Federation Mode should not become a simple economic snowball. The strongest economies — wool, gold, frozen meat, ports — should create political, logistical, and ethical challenges.

## Main difficulty axes

| Axis | Easy | Hard |
|---|---|---|
| Food security | More generous starting stores | Severe early hunger. |
| Distance | Faster ships/roads | Long travel and high freight cost. |
| Federation | Lower support thresholds | Historical thresholds and turnout rules. |
| First Nations diplomacy | Slower tension growth | Higher Country Pressure effects. |
| Gold | More stable prices | Volatile prices and unrest. |
| Immigration | Predictable arrivals | Boom/bust migration. |
| Infrastructure | Cheaper telegraph/rail | Expensive materials and maintenance. |

## Historical Figure balance

### Early figures

Should solve survival problems but not create long-term dominance.

- Arthur Phillip: prevents early collapse.
- James Ruse: food security.
- Elizabeth Macarthur: wool economy, but needs land/transport.

### Mid-game figures

Should create strategic direction.

- George Fife Angas: finance/free settlement.
- Charles Sturt: river expansion.
- Caroline Chisholm: immigration stability.
- Edward Hargraves: gold boom with instability.

### Late figures

Should accelerate victory but require setup.

- Charles Todd: telegraph/Federation Support.
- Henry Parkes: Federation movement.
- John Quick: convention process.
- Samuel Griffith: constitution drafting.
- Edmund Barton: final referendum push.

## Anti-snowball rules

### Wool

Potential nerfs:

- Requires ports or inland freight.
- Drought risk.
- Country Pressure from pastoral expansion.
- Price fluctuation.

### Gold

Potential nerfs:

- Depleting deposits.
- Inflation.
- Unrest/licence crisis.
- Bushranger/crime events.
- Anti-Chinese discrimination/reform events.

### Telegraph

Potential nerfs:

- High upfront cost.
- Requires route survey.
- Requires corridor agreements or creates Country Pressure.
- Maintenance cost.

### Federation Support

Potential nerfs:

- Support decays in disconnected regions.
- Anti-Federation events in high-tariff colonies.
- Referendum failure reduces support temporarily.
- Frontier legitimacy crisis reduces reform-town support.

## Suggested difficulty modifiers

| Modifier | Easy | Normal | Hard |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Federation Support generation | +25% | baseline | -15% |
| Historical Figure cost | -20% | baseline | +25% |
| First Nations tension from Country Pressure | -25% | baseline | +30% |
| Gold unrest | -30% | baseline | +40% |
| Drought frequency | -25% | baseline | +25% |
| Immigration volatility | Low | Medium | High |
| Referendum target modifier | -5 points | historical-inspired | +3 points |

## Victory grade scoring

| Category | Score source |
|---|---|
| Federation | Number of colonies passed and margin. |
| Economy | Export diversity, debt, infrastructure. |
| Civic reform | Suffrage, representation, newspapers, schools. |
| First Nations relations | Respect, agreements, low violence. |
| Stability | Low unrest, low debt, food security. |
| Historical breadth | Number of eras meaningfully developed. |

## Exploit prevention

- Do not allow player to farm First Nations conflict for resources.
- Do not allow unlimited gold growth without population/inflation consequences.
- Do not allow one super-city to single-handedly federate all colonies.
- Require each colony region to build its own support base.
- Make distant colonies need infrastructure or local campaigners.

## Recommended playtest questions

1. Can the player win through economy alone without civic investment? If yes, increase Convention requirements.
2. Can the player ignore First Nations relations entirely? If yes, increase legitimacy consequences.
3. Is gold always the best strategy? If yes, increase volatility/unrest/depletion.
4. Are Federation figures too late to matter? If yes, reduce point costs or allow pre-1889 civic unlocks.
5. Does the game become unwinnable after one failed referendum? If yes, strengthen Quick/Barton recovery mechanics.
6. Can NSW/WA be too hard? If yes, add compromise events and regional campaigners.
